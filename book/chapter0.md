# Chapter 0

## The Walk Out

The last paper always feels different when you know it went wrong.

Everyone else was walking out in groups -laughing, comparing answers, already
planning where to eat. Arjun walked alone. Head down. Bag strap held tight
across one shoulder like he was carrying something heavier than books.

He didn't want to talk to anyone.

Not because he was angry at them. He just had nothing to say. *How did it go?
You okay?* The questions would come and he would have to answer them. He
couldn't do that right now. So he turned left out of the gate and started
walking toward the bus stand.

His mind wouldn't stop.

*Class six.* Teacher going around the room -what do you want to become? Doctor.
Engineer. Collector. IAS. When it reached him he had said -*successful person.*
A few people laughed. The teacher smiled and moved on. He hadn't minded. He had
meant it completely.

*Class nine.* Rajan uncle, two houses down, collapsed one afternoon. Everyone
stood outside. The doctor who stepped out of the ambulance -calm, certain,
knowing exactly what to do -had done something to Arjun that he still couldn't
explain properly. That night he told his mother: I want to become a doctor. She
said: then study. He studied.

*Class eleven.* A video going around in the class group. A hospital
inauguration. A man speaking about building something bigger than himself.
Naresh Trehan. Medanta. Arjun watched it four times. Something shifted. Not
just *I want to help people* -something bigger. *I want to do something for
this country.* He felt slightly embarrassed thinking it. It was the kind of
thing people said in speeches. But he had felt it for real.

Then class twelve results. And the silence at home that nobody spoke about but
everyone felt. The kind that sits at the dinner table and doesn't leave.

He had seen his father's face when the private medical college fee came on
screen. Just one second. His father looked away quickly. But Arjun had already
seen it.

He didn't apply.

---

He had found the CA idea almost by accident. A friend who had gotten a
compartment in 12th, whose family was pushing him toward CA. Arjun had looked
it up that night. Then found a video -PM Modi speaking at some function to a
room full of chartered accountants.

There was one line that made him stop scrolling.

*Accountants are partners in nation building.*

That same feeling from the Medanta video came back. And Pranav Bhaiya was a CA.
Had been saying for years that it was a serious profession, a real path. Arjun
had trusted that.

He had enrolled.

And now here he was. Walking to the bus stand after a paper that had taken
something out of him. The entries he had practiced had disappeared under
pressure. He had sat staring at one question for twenty minutes and couldn't
begin.

*Receptionist work,* he thought. *That's all it is. Debit this, credit that.
This came in, that went out. Any person sitting at a counter can do this.*

The thought sat heavy. He had left biology -the one subject that had actually
made sense to him, where everything had a logic, where he had felt like he
belonged -for this.

*Partners in nation building.*

It felt like a tagline now. Something said to fill a room with clapping.

---

He reached the bus stand and stopped.

The coaching institute across the road had put up a new banner. Big, bright, a
little faded at the edges from the afternoon sun.

*Congratulations to our AIR Rankers -Foundation & Intermediate.*

Six photographs. Six faces. All smiling the particular smile of people who had
cleared something hard and knew it.

Arjun looked at it for a moment.

These were supposed to be his people. His future. They looked like they had
always known they were in the right room.

He looked away.

And then -without fully knowing why -he thought of Pranav Bhaiya.

Not warmly. More the way you think of the person who recommended something that
didn't work out. Pranav Bhaiya had said CA was the right call. He was a CA
himself -Fortune 500, finance, the whole picture that had made this choice feel
safe two years ago.

Arjun found the contact. He called.

## The Blood Report of a Business

*(Outside, the city is thinning. The road is getting wider. Arjun is watching
it from the window, phone still at his ear.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Arjun, ek kaam kar. Ek simple sawaal -bata mujhe. Ek
business ka -koi bhi business -uska blood report kaisa dikhega? Matlab, agar
ek company ka bhi blood test hota, toh usme kya kya hota?

**Arjun:** *(frowning)* Business ka blood test? Bhaiya company koi insaan
nahi hoti. Blood test toh uske owner ka hoga na?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Sahi poochha. Pehle yeh bata -company aur uska owner —
dono alag hote hain ya ek?

**Arjun:** *(flatly, like reciting)* Alag hote hain. Business entity concept.
Padha hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul. Toh company ko ek alag insaan maan lo. Bilkul
alag. Apni pehchaan, apna haal, apna sab kuch alag. Owner alag, company alag.
Jab company ki baat karte hain toh sirf company ki baat karte hain.

**Arjun:** *(sarcastically)* Achha. Toh Bhaiya -yeh company kya khaana
khaati hai? Uska RBC, WBC hota hai kya? Haemoglobin hota hai?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** *(without missing a beat)* Haan. Hota hai.

*(Arjun blinks.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Company khaana khaati hai -uska khaana hota hai income aur
revenue. Jaise ek insaan ko vitamins, glucose chahiye hota hai energy ke liye —
waise company ko income chahiye hoti hai chalte rehne ke liye. Jis din income
aani band ho gayi, company waise hi girne lagti hai jaise ek insaan jise khaana
milna band ho jaaye.

**Arjun:** Okay. Theek hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Aur jaise ek insaan khaana kha ke bahut saari activities
karta hai -school jaata hai, cricket khelta hai, ghumne jaata hai -waise
company bhi apni income se bahut saare kaam karti hai. Salary deti hai,
bijli ka bill bharta hai, raw material kharidti hai. Yeh sab uske expenses hain.

**Arjun:** Matlab income minus expenses -net mein kya bachha woh profit ya
loss.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Exactly. Aur jaise mummy bolti hain na -zyada khelo toh thak
jaoge, pehle kuch khaa lo, energy aayegi -waise business mein bhi itna hi
kharcho jitna kamaate ho. Zyada thak gaye toh health kharab.

**Arjun:** *(small smile, almost involuntary)* Net loss ho jaata hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul. Ab aage bata -ek insaan ke paas sirf income aur
expense hi nahi hoti na? Kuch permanent cheezein bhi hoti hain. Ghar hota hai,
gaadi hoti hai, savings hoti hain.

**Arjun:** Haan.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Waise hi company ke paas bhi permanent cheezein hoti hain.
Inhe hum assets bolte hain. Aur tu biology ka student hai toh seedha samjha
deta hoon. *(pause)* Company ke paas jo building hai, machinery hai, equipment
hai -yeh sab uski haddiyan aur muscles hain. Bones structure dete hain, muscles
kaam karte hain -waise property aur machinery company ko structure deti hai aur
kaam chalati hai. Dono umar ke saath thakti hain -isi liye hum depreciation
lete hain. Waise hi jaise haddiyan aur muscles umar ke saath kamzor hoti hain.

**Arjun:** *(nodding slowly)* Okay. Yeh toh samajh aaya.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Ab -tune glycogen padha hai na biology mein?

**Arjun:** Haan. Stored glucose. Body mein store rehta hai, baad mein energy ke
liye use hota hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Wahi hoti hai company ki inventory. Raw material, stock —
abhi store hai, baad mein jab bikegi toh cash aayega, income aayegi. Abhi ka
glycogen, kal ka glucose.

**Arjun:** Aur cash?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Cash hai free flowing glucose. Abhi hai, abhi use ho sakta
hai. Koi conversion nahi, koi wait nahi. Seedha kaam aata hai.

**Arjun:** Aur investments jo books mein hote hain?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bone marrow. Body mein ek deep reserve hota hai -koi bada
kuch ho jaaye toh bone marrow se blood banta hai, body sambhal leti hai. Company
ke investments waise hi hain -daily use mein nahi hain, par kisi badi zaroorat
ke waqt kaam aate hain. Future ke liye rakhe hue hain.

*(Arjun is quiet for a moment. Looking out the window. The fields have started —
they are past the city now.)*

**Arjun:** Bhaiya -baat toh sahi bol rahe ho. *(pause)* Aur liabilities? Woh
bhi kuch hota hai body mein? Woh kharaab wala -urea, creatinine -woh sab?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Kuch liabilities haan, waise hoti hain -penalty, doubtful
debts, koi purana bura obligation. Woh sach mein waste products hain, jitna jaldi
niklo utna achha. Par Arjun -har liability kharab nahi hoti. Yeh samajhna
zaroori hai.

**Arjun:** Matlab?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Socho -agar kisi customer ne advance mein paisa de diya.
Tune kaam abhi kiya nahi, deliver nahi kiya -par paisa aa gaya. Toh yeh
liability hai books mein. Par yeh kharab hai kya? Nahi. Yeh toh income banne
wali hai -bas thoda wait karo jab tak kaam poora na ho. Jaise khaana khaaya
par abhi pacha nahi -digestive tract mein hai. Energy abhi nahi bani, thodi der
mein banegi. Woh burden nahi hai -woh process mein hai.

**Arjun:** Aur loan? Woh toh liability hi hota hai na -woh kharab nahi hota?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Depends. Loan lekar agar achi machinery kharid li, jo aage
chal ke income degi -toh woh loan ek carrier ki tarah kaam kiya. Resources
sahi jagah pahunchaye. Kharaab tab hota hai jab loan liya aur kuch productive
nahi kiya ussse -sirf interest bhar rahe ho, koi output nahi. Woh sach mein
burden hai. Toh liability ko hamesha poochho -yeh kya kar rahi hai system mein?
Kaam aayegi ya sirf daba ke rakhegi?

*(Arjun absorbs this. Looks back out the window.)*

**Arjun:** *(slowly)* Toh balance sheet mein jo assets hain woh company ki
haddiyan hain, muscles hain, glucose hai, bone marrow hai -aur liabilities mein
kuch useful hain, kuch waste. Aur jo difference bachha -assets minus liabilities
-woh company ki apni value hai. Net worth.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul sahi.

**Arjun:** *(after a pause)* Par Bhaiya -yeh sab toh bas classification hi hai na?
Matlab iss cheeez ko isme daalo, iss cheez ko isme classify kardo. bas khatam. Isme
nation building jesi kya baat ho gayi ab? Accountant ne BS PL banayi, owner ne dekhi,
baat khatam? aur koi padhta bhi hai inko? Kisi aur ko kya matlab inka?

*(Pranav Bhaiya pauses. This is exactly the question he was waiting for.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Yeh sawaal poochha tune -bahut achha poochha. Iska jawab hi
batayega ki ek accountant ka role kitna bada hai. *(pause)* Bata -jab tera
typhoid tha, tera blood report sirf teri mummy dekhti thi kya?

**Arjun:** Nahi. Doctor dekhta tha. Papa dekhte the. School me bhi submit hua tha.
Teachers ne dekha tha, to check fitness report.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Exactly. Ek insaan ka blood report -ek patient ke liye —
uske doctor ne dekha, ghar walon ne dekha, sab ne decisions liye ussi ke basis
pe. Par woh ek insaan ka report tha -sirf ek patient.

Ab socho -ek badi company ka financial report. Ek bank jab usse loan dene ka
sochta hai -woh kya dekhta hai? Wahi balance sheet. Itni takkat hai ki loan
chuka payegi ya nahi -woh blood report se pata chalta hai. Ek bada investor jo
stock market mein paisa lagaana chahta hai company mein -woh kya dekhta hai?
Wohi P&L, wohi balance sheet. Company ki health kaisi hai -woh report se
samajhta hai. Government jab decide karti hai ki kisi company ko koi scheme ka
benefit milega ya nahi -woh financial statements dekhti hai.

*(a beat)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Ek insaan ke blood report ka ek reader hota hai -doctor,
ghar wale. Ek company ke financial report ke readers hote hain -banks,
investors, government, employees, vendors -poora ek chain. Aur badi companies
toh har teen mahine mein apna blood report nikalti hain. Isko hum interim
financial reporting bolte hain.

**Arjun:** *(quietly, almost to himself)* Matlab yeh report sirf owner ke liye
nahi hoti.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul nahi. Yeh toh sab ke liye hoti hai. Aur isliye jo
insaan (woh accountant) jo yeh report banata hai -uski zimmedari sirf owner tak nahi rukti,
uske upar inn sab ka biswas tika hua hota hai. Unke decisions hote hai iss financials ke base pe.

## The Classification Problem
 
**Arjun:** Par Bhaiya -yeh sab toh easy hai na ultimately? Matlab jo aap
bata rahe ho -asset hai, liability hai, income hai, expense hai. Classify karo
aur likh do. Debit idhar, credit udhar. Itni importance kyun hai isme? Ek
pathologist ki report mein bhi toh seedha count aata hai -haemoglobin kitna
hai, WBC kitna hai. Woh bhi toh classify karta hai. Accounts mein aisa kya
mushkil hai jo itna important ban jaata hai?
 
*(Pranav Bhaiya is quiet for a moment. Not because he doesn't have an answer —
because he wants Arjun to feel the question before he answers it.)*
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Tu biology ka student hai. Ek cheez poochhta hoon. PAS
stain -padha hai?
 
**Arjun:** Haan. Periodic Acid Schiff. Glycogen ke liye use karte hain.
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Woh kaam kya karta tha exactly?
 
**Arjun:** *(thinking back)* Stored glycogen hai ya active metabolism ho rahi
hai -yeh differentiate karta tha. Liver functioning ke baare mein pata chalta
tha. Slide pe daag aata tha -magenta colour -aur ussi se pata chalta tha ki
glycogen ka kya haal hai.
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Aur sabse badi dikkat kya thi usme?
 
**Arjun:** *(slowly)* Dono ek jaise dikhte the. Stored glycogen aur active
glucose metabolism -slide pe same colour, same pattern. Bahut baariki se
dekhna padta tha. Ek extra step hota tha -diastase digestion -jo glycogen ko
tod deta tha pehle staining se. Agar woh step skip karo toh stored glycogen bhi
active jaisi dikhti thi. Galat result aata tha.
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Exactly correct. *(pause)* Arjun -accounts mein bhi
bilkul wahi hota hai. Ek paisa aaya company ke paas. Cash account mein dikha.
Ab sawaal yeh hai -kya yeh income hai? Ya kuch aur hai?
 
**Arjun:** *(carefully)* Depend karta hai. Agar customer ne advance diya hai
toh income nahi -liability hai. Abhi kaam nahi hua, toh earn nahi kiya.
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul. Same substance -paisa. Par classification alag.
Ek case mein income, ek case mein liability. Aur agar galat classify kar diya —
toh blood report galat ho gaya. Wahi PAS stain wali galti. Dono ek jaise dikhte
hain -par ek active hai, ek stored hai. Ek earn kiya hua hai, ek abhi bhi
obligation hai.
 
*(A pause. Arjun is leaning slightly toward the window now, not away from the
conversation.)*
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Ek real example deta hoon. Tujhe yaad hai -class 8, 9,
10 mein tu Byju's use karta tha?
 
**Arjun:** *(surprised)* Haan. Papa ne liya tha. Sales wala bahut convincing
tha. Aapne mana kiya tha tab -par unhone le liya. EMI pe. Teen saal ki fees
ek saath.
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Haan. Maine mana kiya tha. *(small pause)* Teen saal ka
course tha. Paisa poora ek saath liya -discount ke liye. Ab socho -Byju's ke
accountant ke paas woh paisa aa gaya. ₹90,000. Ek baar mein. Ab yeh paisa kya
hai?
 
**Arjun:** *(thinking)* Teen saal ka hai. Toh ek saal mein sirf ₹30,000 ka
kaam deliver hoga. Baaki do saal ka abhi deliver nahi kiya. Toh ₹60,000 toh
liability hai -advance receive kiya, performance abhi baaki hai.
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul sahi. Yahi IndAS 115 kehta hai -revenue tab recognize
karo jab performance obligation poori ho. Jab kaam actually deliver ho. Yeh wahi
diastase step hai -pehle check karo ki yeh earned hai ya stored. Earned hai
toh income. Stored hai -abhi bhi obligation hai -toh liability.
 
**Arjun:** Aur Byju's ne?
 
**Pranav Bhaiya:** Byju's ne woh poora ₹90,000 usi saal income mein book kar
liya. Teen saal ka paisa, ek saal mein. PAS stain kiya -par diastase step
skip kar diya. Glycogen ko glucose bol diya. Stored ko earned bol diya.

somewhere along the way.)*
 
*(Outside -it is properly night now. But not city night. This is the
outskirts. Open fields on both sides of the road. A few distant lights
from houses far away. The sky above is very large and very dark and has
more stars than the city ever shows.)*
 
*(The window is open. Cool air is coming in steadily. Arjun closes his
eyes for a moment.)*
 
*(The mental chatter -the noise that had been running since he walked
out of that exam hall -is gone. Not because anything got resolved. The
result is still unknown. The paper is still what it was. Nothing changed.)*
 
*(And yet -quiet.)*

 *(He sits with that for a while. Just that.)*
 
---
 
## Home
 
*(The bus stops. Arjun gets off.)*
 
*(He walks the last few minutes to his house. The lane is quiet. A dog
somewhere. The smell of someone cooking dinner.)*
 
*(He had decided, somewhere between the exam hall and the bus stand, that
he was going to tell his parents tonight. That this was a mistake. That
he needed to switch. That CA was not for him. He had the whole conversation
planned in his head.)*
 
*(He opens the door.)*
 
*(His mother is in the kitchen. His father is watching the news. Someone
has already set the table.)*
 
**Arjun's mother:** Aa gaya? Khaana lag jaaye?
 
**Arjun:** Haan Maa. *(puts his bag down)* Aa raha hoon.
 
*(He washes his hands. Sits down at the table. His father comes and sits
across. His mother brings the food.)*
 
**Arjun's father:** *(casually)* Paper kaisa gaya?
 
*(A pause. Just one second.)*
 
**Arjun:** *(simply)* Theek tha. Dekho ab result kya aata hai. Jo hoga
achha hoga.
 
*(His father nods. His mother says something about eating while it's hot.
The television murmurs from the other room.)*
 
*(Arjun eats.)*
 
*(The conversation he had planned -the one about switching, about wrong
decisions, about feeling worthless -it doesn't come. Not because he
forgot. Not because he's pretending.*
 
*Because right now, in this moment, sitting here -he doesn't need it
to.)*
 
*(He eats his dinner. He goes to sleep.)*
 
*(The result will come when it comes.)*
