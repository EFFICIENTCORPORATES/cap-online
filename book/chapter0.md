# Chapter 0

---

## The Walk Out

The last paper always feels different when you know it went wrong.

Everyone else was walking out in groups — laughing, comparing answers, already
planning where to eat. Arjun walked alone. Head down. Bag strap held tight
across one shoulder like he was carrying something heavier than books.

He didn't want to talk to anyone.

Not because he was angry at them. He just had nothing to say. *How did it go?
You okay?* The questions would come and he would have to answer them. He
couldn't do that right now. So he turned left out of the gate and started
walking toward the bus stand.

His mind wouldn't stop.

*Class six.* Teacher going around the room — what do you want to become? Doctor.
Engineer. Collector. IAS. When it reached him he had said — *successful person.*
A few people laughed. The teacher smiled and moved on. He hadn't minded. He had
meant it completely.

*Class nine.* Rajan uncle, two houses down, collapsed one afternoon. Everyone
stood outside. The doctor who stepped out of the ambulance — calm, certain,
knowing exactly what to do — had done something to Arjun that he still couldn't
explain properly. That night he told his mother: I want to become a doctor. She
said: then study. He studied.

*Class eleven.* A video going around in the class group. A hospital
inauguration. A man speaking about building something bigger than himself.
Naresh Trehan. Medanta. Arjun watched it four times. Something shifted. Not
just *I want to help people* — something bigger. *I want to do something for
this country.* He felt slightly embarrassed thinking it. It was the kind of
thing people said in speeches. But he had felt it for real.

Then class twelve results. And the silence at home that nobody spoke about but
everyone felt. The kind that sits at the dinner table and doesn't leave.

He had seen his father's face when the private medical college fee came on
screen. Just one second. His father looked away quickly. But Arjun had already
seen it.

He didn't apply.

---

He had found the CA idea almost by accident. A friend who had gotten a
compartment in 12th, whose family was pushing him toward CA. Arjun had looked
it up that night. Then found a video — PM Modi speaking at some function to a
room full of chartered accountants.

There was one line that made him stop scrolling.

*Accountants are partners in nation building.*

That same feeling from the Medanta video came back. And Pranav Bhaiya was a CA.
Had been saying for years that it was a serious profession, a real path. Arjun
had trusted that.

He had enrolled.

And now here he was. Walking to the bus stand after a paper that had taken
something out of him. The entries he had practiced had disappeared under
pressure. He had sat staring at one question for twenty minutes and couldn't
begin.

*Receptionist work,* he thought. *That's all it is. Debit this, credit that.
This came in, that went out. Any person sitting at a counter can do this.*

The thought sat heavy. He had left biology — the one subject that had actually
made sense to him, where everything had a logic, where he had felt like he
belonged — for this.

*Partners in nation building.*

It felt like a tagline now. Something said to fill a room with clapping.

---

He reached the bus stand and stopped.

The coaching institute across the road had put up a new banner. Big, bright, a
little faded at the edges from the afternoon sun.

*Congratulations to our AIR Rankers — Foundation & IPCC.*

Six photographs. Six faces. All smiling the particular smile of people who had
cleared something hard and knew it.

Arjun looked at it for a moment.

These were supposed to be his people. His future. They looked like they had
always known they were in the right room.

He looked away.

And then — without fully knowing why — he thought of Pranav Bhaiya.

Not warmly. More the way you think of the person who recommended something that
didn't work out. Pranav Bhaiya had said CA was the right call. He was a CA
himself — Fortune 500, finance, the whole picture that had made this choice feel
safe two years ago.

Arjun found the contact. He called.

---

## The Call

*(Phone rings twice. Arjun is still standing at the bus stand, back to the
banner, looking at the road.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Arjun? Bol, kya hua?

**Arjun:** Haan Bhaiya. Kaise ho?

*(A pause. Pranav Bhaiya knows this voice. This is not how Arjun calls when
things are fine.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Main theek hoon. Tu bata — paper kaisa gaya?

**Arjun:** *(quietly)* Theek tha.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Arjun.

**Arjun:** Haan.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Sach mein bata.

*(Arjun exhales. Looks down at the road.)*

**Arjun:** Bhaiya, aapko yaad hai — class six mein jab teacher poochti thi ki
bade hokar kya banna hai?

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Haan.

**Arjun:** Main bolta tha — successful person banna hai. Tab se lekar class
nine, ten, eleven — har baar kuch na kuch tha. Doctor banna tha. Phir Medanta
wala video dekha aur laga ki yaar desh ke liye kuch karna hai. Itna bada sapna
tha Bhaiya. Sach mein itna bada.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Haan, yaad hai mujhe.

**Arjun:** Aur aaj main yahan khada hoon bus stand pe. CPT ka paper deke. Aur
mujhe lag raha hai ki main galat jagah aa gaya hoon. *(stops)* Paise beech mein
aa gaye Bhaiya. Bas isliye. Medical nahi ho paya. CA mein aa gaya. Aur aaj woh
bhi — *(doesn't finish)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** *(after a moment)* Tu successful person banna chahta hai —
yeh abhi bhi hai?

**Arjun:** Haan. Woh toh hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Achha. Toh ek kaam kar — ek kahani sunata hoon. Socrates ke
baare mein —

**Arjun:** *(cutting in, tired)* Bhaiya please. Motivate mat karo mujhe. Sach
mein. Main us mood mein nahi hoon abhi. Jo bhi bologe nahi samjhunga. Please.

*(Silence on the line. Pranav Bhaiya doesn't push. He understood something just
now — this is deeper than a bad paper.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Theek hai. Koi baat nahi. *(pause)* Teri bus aayi?

**Arjun:** *(looking up)* Haan, aa gayi. Abhi board karni hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Chal, pehle chadh. Phir baat karte hain.

---

*(Arjun boards the bus. Finds an aisle seat, middle of the bus. Settles in, bag
on lap, phone back to his ear.)*

**Arjun:** Haan Bhaiya, chadh gaya.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Theek hai. Ek ek karke sawal puchta hoon — jawab dena. Okay?

**Arjun:** Haan bolo.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Class 10th mein — kaisa feel hota tha tujhe? Andar se, apne
liye?

**Arjun:** *(slightly surprised by the question)* Achha tha. Matlab topper
nahi tha, aap jaise toh bilkul nahi — lekin marks theek the, concepts samajh
aate the. Khush tha. Goal dikh raha tha — doctor banna hai, PCB padhni hai, bas
karna hai.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Aur class 11th 12th mein?

**Arjun:** Woh toh aur bhi achha tha. Naya school, naye dost, roz kuch naya.
Tab hi woh Medanta wala video dekha — aur laga ki sirf community ke liye nahi,
desh ke liye sochna chahiye. Woh inspiration wahan se mili thi. *(pause)* Exam
tak sab theek tha.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Phir kya hua?

**Arjun:** *(quietly)* Aapko toh pata hi hai na ki kya hua.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Haan. Par tu bata.

*(Arjun looks out the window. The city is moving past — autos, chai stalls,
people with somewhere to be.)*

**Arjun:** Result achha tha. But topper nahi tha. Government college cut-off
miss ho gaya. Private ka fee — *(stops)* Bhaiya aapko pata hai. Main nahi kar
sakta tha. Ghar mein itna tha nahi. Maine dekha tha Papa ko jab fee screen pe
aayi. Ek second ke liye. Unhone jaldi se nazar hataayi. Par main dekh chuka
tha.

*(Beat.)*

**Arjun:** Maine apply hi nahi kiya.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Phir CA ka idea kaise aaya?

**Arjun:** Ek dost tha. Usse 12th mein compartment laga tha. Uske ghar wale
usse CA suggest kar rahe the. Usne bataya. Maine socha — dekh lete hain.
Research ki thodi. Theek laga. *(pause)* Aur Bhaiya — ek video mila YouTube pe.
PM Modi ka. Ek line thi usme — aapne suni hogi. Woh famous wali — "CA ki
signature PM ki signature se bhi badi hoti hai."

**Pranav Bhaiya:** *(small laugh)* Haan, woh famous line.

**Arjun:** Woh line toh mujhe cliche lagi. Kaafi filmy. *(pause)* Par ek aur line thi
uss video me — "Chartered Accountants are partners in nation building." Woh line sunke — Bhaiya,
wahi feeling aayi jo Medanta wala video dekhke aayi thi. Wahi desh ke liye kuch
karna wali feeling. Aur aap the — aap CA ho, aap bol chuke the ki yeh serious
profession hai. Toh maine socha — haan, yeh karta hoon.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Aur aaj — woh line kaisi lagti hai?

**Arjun:** *(short exhale, almost a laugh but not quite)* Marketing lagti hai
Bhaiya. Kisi ko clap dilane ke liye boli gayi line. Accountants are partners in
nation building — kaunsa nation building? Hum toh bas debit credit karte hain.
Yeh aaya toh idhar likho, woh gaya toh udhar likho. Yeh toh hospital ka
receptionist bhi karta hai — jo bhi aaya register mein likho, jo bhi gaya woh
bhi likho. Itna hi toh hai. Itni badi baat kahan se aayi isme?

*(Pranav Bhaiya doesn't react immediately.)*

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Achha. Ek kaam kar — CPT mein char subject the. Sabse zyada
boring, sabse zyada bekaar kaunsa laga?

**Arjun:** Accounts. Aur Law.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Ek bata. Maine ek hi poochha.

**Arjun:** Accounts.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Accounts toh bahut hi bekaar, boring subject lagta hai —
samajh aata hai. *(pause)* Ek cheez poochh sakta hoon?

**Arjun:** Haan.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Class 11th ke exams ke baad — 12th shuru hone se pehle,
beech ke gap mein — tu bahut beemaar pada tha. Typhoid. Yaad hai?

**Arjun:** *(shifting)* Haan. Kaun bhoolega woh.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Kya kiya tha tab?

**Arjun:** Blood test karaya tha. Har teen din mein. Doctor report dekhta tha,
ussi se medicine change karta tha. Dheere dheere theek hua. Poore 15 din lage
the.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Theek hone ka asli karan kya tha?

*(Arjun thinks for a second, not sure where this is going.)*

**Arjun:** Woh... blood test report. Doctor ussi ke basis pe sab decide kar
raha tha. Kaunsi medicine, kitna rest, kya khana kya nahi. Sab ussi report se
aa raha tha.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Exactly. Ab bata — agar woh blood test report hi galat hoti?
Numbers hi galat hote? Toh kya doctor sahi se treat kar paata?

**Arjun:** Nahi. Obviously nahi. Galat report pe galat treatment hoti.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Bilkul. *(a beat)* Arjun — jo insaan accounts banata hai —
woh usi company ka blood test report bana raha hota hai. Bahar se company kaisi
bhi dikhe — news mein, ads mein, logon ke mann mein — company ka asli haal kya
hai, woh sirf us balance sheet mein, us profit and loss mein, us cash flow
statement mein hota hai. Woh uska blood report hai. Aur jaise tera blood report
galat hota toh tera sahi ilaaj nahi ho sakta tha — agar kisi company ka
financial statement galat ho, toh uske saath jo bhi decisions lene wale hain —
bank, investors, government — sab ke sab galat ho jaayenge.

*(The bus hits a pothole. A man sitting ahead gets up — his stop. Arjun slides
over and takes the window seat. Cool air comes in.)*

**Arjun:** *(settling in)* Nahi Bhaiya — bas woh seat mein baith gaya hoon ab.
Window seat mili. *(pause, then quieter)* Aap aage boliye.

**Pranav Bhaiya:** Toh ek accountant ka role — receptionist ka nahi hota,
Arjun. Pathologist ka hota hai. Jo woh blood report banata hai — jiske basis pe
sab kuch decide hota hai. Doctors, investors, banks, ghar wale — sab. Agar woh
report sahi nahi — toh duniya ka koi bhi sahi decision nahi le sakta us company
ke baare mein.

*(Arjun is quiet. Outside, the city is thinning. The road is getting wider.)*

**Arjun:** *(after a moment)* Achha.

*(He looks out the window. Just that one word. But something has shifted.)*
