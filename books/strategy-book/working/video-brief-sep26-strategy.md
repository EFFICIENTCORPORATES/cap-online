One source that covers everything for that subject. Physical book, PDF, audio notes, videos, summary charts — format doesn't matter. What matters: one source, decided, locked. This is your Layer 1.

Layer 1 + a Question Bank = your full working material.

### BLOCK 3 — The Three Sanjeevani Bootis (12–15 min) | Face + Slides

**These three things must exist for every chapter in your boundary. Deadline: 25th July.**
This is bare minimum. Very much doable in 40 days.

---

#### Sanjeevani Booti 1 — The Golden Nuggets Register

When you watch any revision video — One Shot, Exam Oriented, Guru Drona, any batch of any faculty — your faculty is handing you gold. Most students let it pass. You collect it.

For every chapter, maintain one place with:

```
GOLDEN NUGGETS — [Subject] — [Chapter]

Important Concepts:     (with reference: "see Layer 2, P01-002 p.14")
Exam Tips:              (how to build working notes for this chapter)
Examiner Tricks:        (what the examiner does to trap, what to watch for)
```

These are the coins your faculty gives you. One place, one chapter, all of it collected.

---

#### Sanjeevani Booti 2 — The Error Register

After watching the revision video, when you sit to practice — every mistake goes here.

```
ERROR REGISTER — [Subject] — [Chapter]

Question No:
Where is this question exactly? (book + page / drive + file)
Where is the solution exactly?
What was asked?
Where did you mess up?
Why did you mess up? (what you thought vs. what was actual treatment)
```

Chapter by chapter. Subject by subject. Every mistake, captured.

This register becomes your most targeted revision tool. Before you revise any chapter, read its error entries first. You're priming yourself for the exact traps that have already caught you.

---

#### Sanjeevani Booti 3 — Paste It Where You Live + AI

The concepts you keep forgetting shouldn't live only inside a notebook you have to open. Put them where your eyes already go.

Printout or plain A4 paper. Paste:
- Above the washbasin where you brush
- In the hallway between your study room and breakfast table
- Near the dinner table
- Near your study table (last resort — your eyes are already tired here)

When a sheet's content is genuinely locked, take it down. Put up the next weak one.

**This is exactly where AI changes what's possible:**

Use AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, KIMI, NotebookLM — for Hindi: Indus / Sarvam AI) for:
- Building mnemonics from your favourite character, movie, or domain
- Generating chapter summary sheets you can print and paste
- Making revision checklists per chapter
- Testing you on a topic (ask it to quiz you one question at a time)
- Explaining why your wrong MCQ option was wrong

**AI's best job here is memory: mnemonics, retention hooks, revision checklists.** What to study and the first real clarity on a concept — that comes from your faculty. AI drills and retains what your faculty taught. Don't flip that.

### BLOCK 4 — After 25th July: The 45-Day Phase (4–5 min) | Face + Slides

From 25th July to exam: study only from Layer 2 material. Start building Layer 3.

**Layer 3 = your flow sequence.** For each subject: what to revise, from which source, in which order.

Default sequence if you don't have your own order:
```
Category A chapters first (heavy, almost always asked)
Category B after that
Category C last
```

Category formula (quick reminder on slide):
```
A — chapter carried 50+ marks across last 10 papers
B — 20 to 50 marks
C — under 20 marks

Time split: A = 60%, B = 30%, C = 10%
```

**Main mission of the 45 days:**
- Confidence in your boundary — everything in it is revisable
- At least one full pass through your Error Register
- Layer 3 built and in your hand

## Critical Review — Honest Flags

These are the issues in this plan that need a decision before scripting begins.

---

### Flag 1 — Video length is 35–40 min, not 20–30

Counting the content blocks honestly:
Block 1 (opening): 3–4 min
Block 2 (boundary + anchor + Drive tip + template): 6–8 min
Block 3 (three Sanjeevani Bootis + AI): 12–15 min
Block 4 (post-25th July): 4–5 min
Block 5 (Bucket 0): 1.5 min
Block 6 (CTA): 2 min
**Total: 29–36 min. More likely 35 at actual speaking pace.**

This is not a problem — 35 minutes of dense, useful strategy is fine for this audience. But the mental target of "20–30 min" will cause rushed scripting. **Decision needed: accept 35 min, or decide what gets cut.**

---

### Flag 2 — Boundary fixation framing

Sep 26 students are 83 days out. Their boundary should already be partially set from Bucket 1. Saying "next 4 days — fix your boundary" might panic students who think they're starting from scratch, and confuse students who already have one.

**Cleaner framing:** "If you haven't fixed your boundary — next 4 days, non-negotiable. If you have — review it and tighten it. Remove anything you genuinely cannot revise before 25th July."

---

### Flag 3 — Sanjeevani Booti 1 vs. existing Layer 2

Golden Nuggets (important concepts, exam tips, examiner tricks per chapter) is essentially what a good Layer 2 notebook should already contain for a student who did Bucket 2 properly. Students with strong Layer 2 notes may feel they need to create something new.

**Cleaner framing:** "If your Layer 2 already has this — great, you're ahead. If it doesn't — this is what you add now, chapter by chapter, while watching revision videos."

---

### Flag 4 — Google Drive tip is Bucket 1 work, being introduced in Bucket 3

This is Strategy 6 from Bucket 1 (Digital Organisation). Introducing it now as a "tip" is fine for students who missed it, but the framing matters. It should feel like a 30-minute catch-up task, not a new system to build from zero at this stage.

---

### Flag 5 — "Gamify the preparation" — underdeveloped

You mentioned "let's gamify the preparation time" near the end but it isn't connected to any specific concept in the video. Either develop what this means (the 25th July checkpoint as a level-up? the three Bootis as collectibles?) — or remove the phrase. As a standalone throwaway line it lands nowhere.

---

### Flag 6 — Comment strategy placement

Explaining the full comment box strategy in the opening (before any value has been delivered) risks feeling like admin before the class has started. Students came for strategies — give them 2 minutes of the hook first, then the comment instruction.

**Suggested placement:** Move comment strategy to Block 6 (end), not Block 1. Keep the opening pure: positioning + central frame + 20-hours north star question.
