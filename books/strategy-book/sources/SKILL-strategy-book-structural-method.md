# SKILL — Strategy Book: Structural Method

> **Scope:** The organizing logic of the CA Intermediate Strategy Book. Read this before adding, moving, or writing any strategy — it governs *where* a strategy goes and *how* it is framed.

---

## 1. The Core Organizing Principle: Time, Not Topic

The book is organized by **phase of the journey**, not by activity type. The earlier topic-based version (Philosophy, Planning, Notes, Testing, Revision…) failed because a student reading it could not answer "when do I do what?" Time-based buckets answer that question directly.

The reader does **not** read front to back. A Routing Page sends them to their current phase. Each bucket is a phase.

---

## 2. The Seven Buckets (+ two standalone sections)

- **Bucket 0 — The Foundation.** Runs beneath every other bucket, every day of the journey. No start or end date. Read once, then live it.
- **Bucket 1 — Before the Journey Begins.** One-time setup.
- **Bucket 2 — While Taking Classes.** Starts per subject when the student enrolls.
- **Bucket 3 — After Classes: The Revision Phase.**
- **Bucket 4 — 45 Days Before Exam.** The boundary-navigation plan: how the student plans to cover the entire boundary.
- **Bucket 5 — The 15 Days of Exams + Few Days Before Exam.** Includes exam-hall perseverance and the MCQ-first approach.
- **Bucket 6 — After the Exam Till Result Day.** Track A (completed all papers) / Track B (missed papers).
- **AI Section** — standalone, placed after all buckets, before the Emergency Section.
- **Emergency Section — 10 Days Left.** Fully independent, subject-neutral. A panic-mode reader goes straight here.

**Each bucket follows one format:** End Goal of This Bucket (short; includes the ranker version; states the prerequisite; carries a grey box titled "Starting Late or Falling Behind? Bare Minimum to Continue") → strategies → closing checklist (rank items starred). Rank additions appear inline with a star, only where the action is genuinely different — not just "more of the same."

---

## 3. The 3-Layer Study Architecture

- **Layer 1 — Raw working material**, built during classes: anchor book, classroom notes, homework notes. Thick, messy, rarely opened once Layer 2 exists.
- **Layer 2 — Revision notebook**, built in the revision phase: one thin clean compressed source per subject (like ICAI Saaransh / faculty charts). Holds summarised concept notes + error register + exam tricks + mnemonics + important-question summaries. References Layer 1 page numbers where a concept already exists; adds detail only where missing. **Compression cuts TIME, not volume or chapters — never eliminate chapters.**
- **Layer 3 — Last-mile reference sheet**, built in the 45-day phase: readable in ~10 minutes for a practical subject, ~15–20 for theory/law. Carried to the exam centre. (Open decision: ~40 loose A4 sheets vs. one bound book — author leans toward a single bound book over scattered sheets.)

**Notebook system:** sequential numbers 001, 002, 003; insertions use 1A, 1B, 2A, 2B. Prefix `P01, P02` (Paper 01, 02) for both physical notebooks and digital files so the system stays unified. [RANK] addition: a Drill-Down Table of Contents — not just chapter + page, but important questions and key topics within each chapter for fast referencing.

---

## 4. The Boundary System

- **MIN** = chapters from the Study Material asked even once in the last 5 *attempts* (not years).
- **MAX** = all Study Material + last 5 attempts' papers.
- **RANKER** = cover 5-year RTP/MTP/PYQ.
- **Rule 1:** the student may remove any chapter from the boundary, but once fixed, every chapter inside gets a **minimum 3 revisions** — no skipping.
- **Rule 2:** weightage is quantified with percentage/mark ranges where possible, not just A/B/C labels.
- **Author's personal rule:** "If I can't revise this in the last 24 hours before exam, it won't be in my boundary." The boundary must be **100% revisable the day before** — whatever is promised is covered.
- One page per subject for the boundary, updatable. Digital organisation merges into the Boundary Fixing section.
- Rankers cannot lighten the boundary — 100% coverage, no shortcuts, unlike pass-students who may lighten C-category chapters.

## 5. Recurring Devices & Confirmed Mechanics

- **Error register** — the author's favourite strategy. Separate register OR 3–4 chapter-wise pages at each chapter's end (student's choice). Entry triggers: faculty flags importance, faculty flags a confusion point, or the student hits such a question. Format: Qn No, Source, what you did, why, what was true, why true. Exam-trick entries: question number, the trick, what to ensure.
- **Mnemonics warning** — they grow fast and start conflicting across chapters/subjects about a month before exams. Revise them alongside question hints and topic context. Mnemonics substitute for *recall*, never for *concept clarity*.
- **Suggested Answers + Examiner Comments compilation** — an often-missed key source; teaches presentation (the difference between 55 and 75 marks).
- **Both Groups vs One Group** — decided on industry-standard study hours per subject; to pass a subject, commit full class hours + 50%. Framed deliberately so privileged students realise their luck. One group if a full-time job/family-support hours/uncontrollable commitments; both groups if committing 10+ hrs/day for ~6 months.
- **Priority system:** Health > Study/Goal > Family (3 people only).
- **Practice volume** — never claim "solve the question bank 3–4 times in full" (impractical). Realistic: each revision day, solve *some* questions in full exam-pattern mode (proper ruling, working notes). [RANK] Bucket 3 = full exam mode (simulated MTP) at least once.
