**Purpose & context**

Efficient is CA Pranav P Tulshyan, an accounting educator and faculty member at VC Gurukul who achieved AIR 1 in both CPT and IPCC. Two major book projects are active simultaneously:

**Project 1 — CA Intermediate Exam Preparation Strategy Book**
A standalone exam strategy guide for 17–19-year-old Indian commerce students just out of CPT/Foundation. Target reader: not inherently serious, heavy social media exposure, short attention span. The book must also work for someone picking it up in panic mode 20 days before the exam. Tone model: Raj Shamani more than Alex Hormozi — raw, blunt, direct, warm underneath, never preachy, never lecturing, no cliché gyaan. The "older brother" voice, not a professor or Instagram coach. Every strategy must have a "How to Do This" block or it is classified as gyaan and removed.

**Project 2 — CA Intermediate Concept Book**
A concept-first teaching tool to accompany ICAI study materials (not replace them), structured around the exact teaching sequence Pranav uses. Features recurring characters, four conceptual lenses (medical/pathology, physics/universal law, practical corporate experience, philosophical/Vedanta — two lenses still being named), and Chapter 0 as a story-driven entry point. Chapter 0 is a dialogue chapter set during a bus ride between student Arjun and mentor Pranav Bhaiya (a CA at a Fortune 500 MNC), using biology and karma analogies to make accounting feel meaningful before any formal concept is introduced.

**Skill file in project:** `write-like-the-ca-inter-teacher-skill.md` — Core principles: analogy over definition; reader's background drives analogy choice; concepts via questions not statements; personal before technical; student summarises not teacher; one analogy per concept locked throughout; real cases always over hypothetical; nation building as chain not speech; short sentences; narrator never explains what scene shows; endings on quiet human moments, never motivational notes.

**Key learnings & principles**

- Every strategy without implementation guidance is gyaan. No exceptions.
- Compression in revision cuts time, not chapters or volume. Never eliminate chapters to reduce load.
- Mnemonics are a substitute for recall, not for concept clarity. They compound risk if not revised alongside context.
- The Socrates Story and Gita shloka must be narratively reconciled — desire for effort vs. non-attachment to fruit cannot be left contradicting each other.
- "Presence is not equal to function" — key phrase for the pathology analogy section.
- Avoid heavy or academic words (architecture, identity formation, resilience) — use language a 17-year-old would naturally use.
- The "Why Rank?" section needs the author's specific story, not abstract motivational language.
- Analogies are entry doors into concepts, not the subject itself — analogy index rejected on this basis.
- Claude's role in the concept book project: critic not cheerleader; commit to decisions rather than presenting options; flag concept bank violations silently unless structural; no flattery, no summarising back what was just said.
