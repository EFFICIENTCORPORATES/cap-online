PAPER – 1: ADVANCED ACCOUNTING  
Specific Comments  
Question 1 .(a)  The majority of examinees were unable to correctly account 
for the treatment of interest, particularly in computing and allocating 
borrowing costs between qualifying and non- qualifying assets. Errors were 
frequently observed in determining the portion of borrowing costs eligible for 
capitalisation. Many examinees also demonstrated a lack of understanding in 
identifying whether interest s hould be capitalised or charged to the Profit and 
Loss Account. Additionally, several examinees  failed to compute the correct 
weighted average rate of interest.  
(b) A significant number of examinees were unable to ascertain the expected 
and actual returns on plan assets and failed to apply the appropriate 
accounting treatment for employee benefits as prescribed under AS 15 
“Employee Benefits.” Errors were also noted in the computation of employee 
benefit liabilities. Many examinees failed to apply half -yearly compounding of 
interest and were unable to distinguish between actuarial gains/losses and 
actual returns on plan assets . 
(c) Many examinees were unable to compute Earnings Per Share (EPS) 
correctly in accordance with AS 20 “Earnings Per Share.” A common  error was 
the use of profit before tax instead of profit after tax for EPS calculation. 
Additionally, some examinees failed to determine the correct number of 
equity shares for computation. Mistakes were also observed in calculating 
profit after tax, the weighted average number of shares, and the bonus 
element in rights issues.  
Question 2 .  Most examinees were able to pass the  required journal entries 
and prepare the reconstructed Balance Sheet in accordance with Schedule III. 
However, some examinees comm itted errors in the utilisation of the Capital 
Reduction Account and in the treatment of certain capital adjustments. A few 
also failed to present the revised capital structure appropriately.  
Question 3 .(a)  This question pertained to buy -back of securiti es under 
Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. Several examinees were unable to 
determine the permissible amount of buy -back i n accordance with the 
relevant provisions. Errors were also observed in the computation of the
