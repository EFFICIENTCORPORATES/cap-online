### Strategy 1 — Fix Your Boundary [ALL]

If you haven't fixed your boundary, do it now — next 4 days, non-negotiable.
If you have one already — review it and tighten it. Remove anything you
genuinely cannot revise before the exam.

**What goes in the boundary:**
- Minimum: every chapter from Study Material asked even once in the last
  5 attempts.
- Maximum: all Study Material + last 5 attempts' papers.

**The one rule once it's fixed:** anything inside gets a minimum of 3 revisions.
No skipping something because it's hard. In or out — decide. Once it's in,
it gets done.

> **The 3-question filter — before you add anything new:**
> ```
> 1. Can I complete this before the exam?
> 2. Can I revise this before the exam?
> 3. Can I revise this MULTIPLE times before the exam?
> ```
> If any answer is No — don't add it. Smaller, fully-revised boundary beats
> bigger, half-covered boundary every single time.

**Document it — one page per subject:**

```
MY BOUNDARY — [Subject]            (use pencil — this will evolve)

Study Material:
Past papers (years):
Where it lives (Drive / book):
Chapters removed (if any):
= NOTHING ELSE beyond this.
```

Print it. Pin it where you study.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "My personal rule: if I can't revise something in the last 24 hours before the
> exam, it doesn't belong in my boundary. Whatever you promise yourself you'll
> cover — you must be able to revise all of it the day before the exam. If you
> can't, your boundary is too big."

> **IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK:**
> Tonight — 30 minutes. Open the index of every subject. For each chapter:
> IN or OUT — use the 3-question filter. Fill the boundary page in pencil.
> Print it. Pin it to your desk. = NOTHING ELSE.

### Strategy 2 — The 3-Layer Architecture [ALL]

Your revision material works in three layers. Each has a different job.
Get this clear before you start — or you'll build the wrong layer at the
wrong time and lose days.

**LAYER 1 — Raw Working Material** (built during classes)
Your anchor book + classroom notes. Thick, messy, complete. Source of truth.
Once Layer 2 exists, you'll rarely open this except to cross-reference.

**LAYER 2 — Revision Notes** (build now, in this phase)
One clean notebook per subject. Compressed concept notes, error register,
exam tricks, mnemonics. Where a concept is clear in Layer 1, Layer 2 just
points to it ("see P01-002, p.14") — don't rewrite. This is what you revise from.

**LAYER 3 — Exam-Day Flow** (build after Level 1 Cleared)
One thin bound book. Whole subject in around 10–40 pages. Everything needed to
revise the night before and at the exam centre. Build after Level 1 Cleared —
not now.

```
Layer 1  → built in classes          → raw, complete, messy
Layer 2  → build NOW                 → compressed, clean, references Layer 1
Layer 3  → after Level 1 Cleared     → thin, fast, carried to exam centre
```

Right now your only job is Layer 2 and the Three Sanjeevani Bootis.
Layer 3 comes after Level 1 Cleared.

> **WARNING:**
> Layer 3 is NEVER digital as primary. If your phone dies at 7 AM on exam day,
> your physical Layer 3 notebook doesn't care. It is still there. Always physical.
> No exceptions.

### THE THREE SANJEEVANI BOOTIS

Three things must exist for every chapter in your boundary, for every subject.
Every chapter. Every subject. That is the target for this phase.

They are not optional extras. They are the difference between revision that
lands and revision that slides off.

---

### Strategy 5 — Sanjeevani Booti 1: The Golden Nuggets Register [ALL]

Every time you sit to watch a revision video — One Shot, Exam Oriented, any
lecture — your faculty is handing you gold. Important concepts framed exactly
right. The traps the examiner sets. How to build working notes for that chapter.
Most students let this pass. You collect it.

For every chapter, one page — in your notebook or a separate section:

```
GOLDEN NUGGETS — [Subject] — [Chapter]

Important Concepts:    (with ref — "see Layer 1, p.14")
Exam Tips:             (how to build working notes for this chapter)
Examiner Tricks:       (what the paper-setter does to trap students here)
```

These pages fold naturally into your Layer 2 when you build it. If your Layer 2
already captures all of this — you already have Booti 1. You're ahead.
If not — build it now, chapter by chapter, while watching.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "The most valuable things a teacher says are the throwaway lines — 'students
> always mess up this step,' 'this is where the examiner puts the trap,' 'write
> your working note like this, not like that.' Those lines are gold. I wrote them
> down the moment they were said. Half my edge in the exam came from those lines."

---

### Strategy 6 — Sanjeevani Booti 2: The Error Register [ALL]

After watching the revision video, when you sit to practise — every mistake
goes here. Not in your head. Not "I'll remember this." Here. Written.
Chapter by chapter. Subject by subject.

Keep it as a **separate register**, or leave **3–4 pages at the end of each
chapter** in your existing notebook — your choice.

**Entry format — use these specific fields:**
```
Where is this question?    (book + page / Drive file + page)
Where is the solution?     (exact location)
What was asked?            (one line)
Where did I mess up?       (the exact step or concept)
Why did I mess up?         (what I thought vs. what it actually is)
```

The **"Why did I mess up?"** line is the most important. Writing it forces you
to understand the gap — not just note that one exists.

**While practising:** when you solve a question, mark whether it was
straightforward or had a trick worth rechecking.

**How you use this register:** before you revise any chapter, read its error
entries first. You are priming yourself for the exact traps that have already
caught you. This single habit separates students who keep repeating mistakes from
students who stop.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "Chapter revise karne se pehle — pehle uski galtiyan padho. Trap pehle se
> pata hoga."

---

### Strategy 7 — Sanjeevani Booti 3: The Visual Vault [ALL]

The concepts you keep forgetting don't need more reading. They need a different
format. For every chapter in your boundary, build a Visual Vault: a collection
of visual, printable sheets that make the hard-to-remember things stick.

**What goes in the Visual Vault, per chapter:**

```
Flowcharts          → the full process, step by step, one diagram
Tables & Formats    → statements, working note formats, ready to use
Skeletons           → the answer framework: what first, what next
Dates · Rates · Limits → Law / Tax: all on one sheet per topic
Mnemonics           → build using AI from your favourite character or film
```

Make each item on A4. Printable. Visual.

**The usage rule — paste it where you live:**
- Above the washbasin where you brush — you'll read it twice a day without trying
- In the hallway between your study room and the breakfast table
- Near the dinner table
- Near your study table (last resort — eyes are already tired here)

When a sheet's content is genuinely locked — you know it cold without looking —
take it down. Put up the next weak one.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "I pasted the GST place-of-supply rules in the hallway I walked through to eat.
> Both sides of the mirror where I brushed — the errors I made most and the
> concepts that confused me most. Once something was genuinely memorised, I'd pull
> it down and put up the next one."

> **IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK:**
> Pick your weakest chapter right now. Build its Visual Vault tonight:
> one flowchart, one table or format, one mnemonic. Print. Paste.
> Tomorrow — do the next chapter. One chapter's Visual Vault per session,
> until every chapter in your boundary is done.

### AI — YOUR MEMORY MACHINE

### Strategy 8 — AI: Your Memory Machine [ALL]

AI is the most powerful revision amplifier available to you right now. Use it for:

- **Mnemonics** — give it your topic and your favourite character, movie, or domain.
  Personal hooks stick far better than generic ones.
- **Visual Vault sheets** — "Generate a one-page printable flowchart for [topic].
  Include all key steps." Print and paste.
- **Revision checklists** — per chapter, per topic.
- **Quizzing** — "I'm revising [topic]. Ask me one question at a time. Wait for
  my answer before the next one."
- **Wrong-answer explanations** — "I chose [option]. Correct is [option]. Explain
  why each of the other three is wrong." The wrong options are where the exam
  catches you.

Tools: ChatGPT · Claude · Gemini · Perplexity · KIMI · NotebookLM
For Hindi: Indus · Sarvam AI

> **WARNING:**
> Concept clarity must come from your faculty. AI is a revision and practice
> tool — for mnemonics, quizzing, and Visual Vault sheets. Don't outsource your
> foundational understanding to AI.
> Use your teacher to understand. Use AI to drill, test, and retain.
> Never trust AI blindly on exact figures or the latest law — verify against
> your Study Material.

### Strategy 10 — Build Your Layer 2 [ALL]

One clean notebook per subject. Chapter by chapter, write your compressed notes.
It holds:
- Summarised concept notes (where Layer 1 is clear, point to it — "see p.14")
- Your Golden Nuggets and Error Register entries
- Exam tricks and mnemonics
- Important-question summaries

Table of contents at the front. Every page must earn its place.

**Write in the language your brain thinks in.** Hindi or Hinglish is not just
allowed — it is better. These notes are for you to revise from, not to submit.
In the exam your answers stay in English — but your notes are yours.

**Compression cuts time, not content.** Layer 2 is shorter so you can read it
faster. It does NOT mean dropping chapters. Every chapter in your boundary stays.

---

### Strategy 11 — Revise, Then Test [ALL]

Revision isn't just re-reading. Every time you sit to revise a chapter, have
a test plan ready before you start.

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> Before opening your notes, decide the test: "after this revision I'll solve
> these 3 questions / attempt these 10 MCQs / let AI quiz me."
> Do the test before moving on. This is how your RTP, MTP, and PYQ coverage
> happens — each chapter's revision is "done" only when its matching past-paper
> questions have been attempted as the test. Revision and past-paper coverage
> are the same activity, not two separate ones.

---

### Strategy 12 — Use Your Error Register [ALL]

Before each chapter's revision, read its error entries first. You are priming
yourself for the exact traps that have already caught you.

This single habit separates students who keep repeating mistakes from students
who stop. The register only works if you read it before every revision session.

---

### Strategy 13 — Rate Your Chapters [RANK]

Once a week, give every chapter a quick three-part rating:

```
1. Exam Relevant — "kitni baar exam mein aata hai?"   →  A / B / C
   (from your weightage analysis — reuse it)
2. Recall        — "topics kitne achhe se yaad hai?"  →  ___ / 10
3. Simulation    — "question solve ho raha hai?"      →  ___ / 10
```

The danger chapter is an **A** sitting at low Recall or low Simulation.
That gap is exactly where your next hours go. An A-chapter at 4/10 is a
mark-leak waiting to happen. Re-rate every week.

---

### Strategy 14 — Practise Like the Exam, Not Like a Stopwatch [ALL]

Every day you practise a practical subject, some questions must be done in full
exam-pattern mode — proper ruling, proper working notes, proper format — exactly
as you'd write in the hall.

A selection of questions in exam mode, speed-revision for the rest.

> **RANK ONLY:**
> Sit for at least one full examination-mode simulation in this phase — a
> complete MTP, 3 hours, 15-minute reading time, written exactly like the real
> exam. Analyse: Where did I lose marks? Concept, calculation, presentation,
> or time? Did I leave blanks?

---

### Strategy 15 — Solve It Yourself [ALL]

Solution videos show someone else solving. They build their skill, not yours.
Train your own brain to sit with confusion and crack it.
The exam hall has no solution video.

---

### Strategy 16 — Run Your Visual Vault [ALL]

Run Sanjeevani Booti 3 actively throughout this phase. Every concept you find
yourself forgetting repeatedly goes on a Visual Vault sheet. Every sheet that's
genuinely locked comes down.

By the end of Phase 1, your walls should be thinning — not growing. A wall that
keeps growing means you're not actually retaining. Fix the retention method,
don't just keep adding sheets.

---

### Strategy 17 — A Fixed Revision Sequence [ALL]

Pick one chapter order per subject and stick to it. Constantly reshuffling
wastes energy on re-orientation and feels like variety while delivering nothing.

---

### Strategy 18 — Recovery and Restart [ALL]

Bad days, illness, family disruptions — universal. The skill isn't avoiding
them. It's restarting fast. One missed day is a missed day, not a failed attempt.

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> Don't try to "make up" a lost day by cramming double the next day — that
> breaks two days instead of one. Adjust the plan forward, trim scope if you
> must, keep the system running.

### Strategy 1 — Build Your Layer 3 [ALL]

Layer 3 is the thin top layer that lets you move through a whole subject fast.
One bound notebook per subject (or one notebook with clear subject dividers).
**Bound beats loose** — pages can't fall out or get out of order.

Target size: **10–40 pages per subject.** If it's longer, it's Layer 2 in
disguise. Compress more.

**What goes in Layer 3:**
- Practical subjects: chapter-wise revision flow — what to revise, which
  questions to hit, from where. ("SM Illustration 7", "Layer 2, p.23")
- Theory subjects: question hints + mnemonics, in revision order.
- Law-type subjects: section numbers, penalty clauses, time limits, monetary
  limits, due dates — in the order you'll revise them.

**Default sequence if you haven't built your own:**

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "Use this default — it beats a blank page every time:
> ```
> A-category chapters first  (50+ marks across last 10 papers) → 60% time
> B-category chapters next   (20–50 marks)                     → 30% time
> C-category chapters last   (<20 marks)                       → 10% time
> ```
> Adjust after your first mock when you know where the gaps are."

> **WARNING:**
> Layer 3 is ALWAYS PHYSICAL. A thin bound book. Carried to the exam centre.
> Digital Layer 3 is not Layer 3.

### Strategy 3 — Depth Over Breadth [ALL]

A-category: 100%. These carry the bulk of the marks — you can't be shaky here.
B-category: solid, but not at the cost of A.
C-category: only after A and B are locked.

A student who owns A-category cold will out-score a student who did everything
at 60% depth — and won't understand why, after "preparing everything."

> **RANK ONLY:**
> Rankers don't get to choose. 100% across A, B, and C. No shortcuts.
> If it's in the syllabus, it's prepared.

---

### Strategy 4 — Daily Format Drills [ALL]

For practical subjects: 20 minutes each morning, draw the key formats from
memory — balance sheet, cash flow, consolidation, P&L, whatever applies.
Under exam pressure a half-remembered format costs you minutes and confidence.
An automatic one costs nothing.

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> Blank sheet each morning, draw the format from memory, check against Layer 2,
> log anything missed into your error register. Repeat tomorrow.

---

### Strategy 5 — The Mock Window [ALL]

Two rules — non-negotiable:

**Minimum 1 full mock. Maximum 2.**

One mock analysed properly is worth more than three mocks skimmed. Mock dena
taiyari nahi hai — taiyari check karna hai.

**Both mocks done by exam-minus-10 days.** Nothing new enters in the last 10 days.

Each mock: 3 full hours. Same time as your actual exam. Same discipline.
Same conditions. No looking anything up mid-paper.

> **RANK ONLY:**
> Track time per question across mocks. Find the question types eating
> disproportionate time and adjust your hall strategy.

---

### Strategy 6 — Mock Analysis Protocol [ALL]

Analysis happens the same day as the mock — not tomorrow, not this weekend.
Same day. Done alone or with a friend who checks your answer sheet.

For every question you got wrong, route it through this decision tree:

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> ```
> "I knew this — but couldn't recall it in the moment?"
>   → Add it to your Visual Vault. It needs a sheet, not more reading.
>
> "I didn't know this at all — and it was inside my boundary?"
>   → Add it to your Golden Nuggets Register. Gap in your notes.
>
> "This chapter wasn't in my boundary at all?"
>   → Decide: add it or leave it out for this attempt.
>   → If you add it: it goes into Layer 3 + revision material now.
>   → If you leave it: it does not exist for this attempt. Done.
> ```
> Roll everything into your error register.
> Finish analysis before exam-minus-10 so you have time to act on findings.

---

### Strategy 7 — Error Register + Important Questions [ALL]

In this phase the error register becomes your primary revision tool — start
every revision session with it. Alongside it, keep your important-questions
list: the questions you flagged through Bucket 3 to definitely recheck now.
Together they are the most targeted revision material you own.

---

### Strategy 8 — The Sunday Sweep [ALL]

Every Sunday, an hour flipping fast through all your Layer 2 notes.
Not deep study — maintenance. By exam week, nothing in Layer 2 should feel new.

---

### Strategy 9 — Print What You'll Revise Again and Again [ALL]

Physical beats screen in this phase. Print your summary charts, format
templates, key provision tables, mnemonic sheets.

**Skip a meal if you must — don't skip printing an important summary chart.**

---

### Strategy 10 — Hold Steady [ALL]

This is the highest-anxiety stretch. Comparison spikes; everyone around you
looks stressed. Go back to Bucket 0 — the Blinders and the emotion-parking
habits matter more here than anywhere else.

> **CHECKLIST — END OF BUCKET**
> Before moving to Bucket 5, confirm per subject:
> - [ ] Layer 3 built and in your bag (physical, bound)
> - [ ] 3+ full revisions done
> - [ ] Minimum 1 mock written and analysed (Protocol done)
> - [ ] Error register cover-to-cover pass complete
> - [ ] Important-questions list rechecked
> - [ ] Everything you'll re-revise: printed
> - [ ] Exam kit packed (see Bucket 5 Strategy 1)
> ★ [RANK] 2+ mocks with time-per-question data reviewed

### SET THIS UP BEFORE THE EXAMS BEGIN

---

### Strategy 1 — Your Exam Kit and Logistics [ALL]

An entire attempt can be lost to a dead calculator or a misread bubble — not
to weak preparation. Sort this out before the exams start.

**One-time, before the exams:**
- **Scout the centre** — visit once, time the commute, find your block.
  No surprises on Day 1.
- **Two identical calculators** — same brand, same model. Muscle memory carries
  over; a battery failure can't stop you. Identical matters — a different key
  layout mid-exam costs time and rhythm.
- **3–4 photocopies of your hall ticket** — keep spares; leave one with a parent.
- Stationery: **3–4 black ball pens of the same brand**, two pencils, eraser,
  small and big scale, a **stapler**. Compass box if your paper needs it.

**The night before each paper:**
- Pack the bag and full kit the night before — never the morning of.
  Zero morning decisions.
- For the **first paper, reach at least an hour early** to settle in.
- Wear **loose, comfortable clothing**.
- Eat light on exam day — idli/poha, two rotis. Carry **nimbu-paani /
  glucose / a couple of sweets** for the 3-hour window.

### THE DAY BEFORE A PAPER

---

### Strategy 2 — Error Register First [ALL]

Read the error register for tomorrow's subject. These are the exact spots you've
lost marks before. Most targeted revision there is. 30–45 minutes.

---

### Strategy 3 — Hotspots Only [ALL]

A-category areas, the hard bits you've prepared, the things you keep forgetting.
No new chapters. No new concepts.

---

### Strategy 4 — Stop and Breathe [ALL]

Stop studying about 2 hours before sleep. Walk, eat with family, rest.
Not scrolling. Let the mind settle.

---

### Strategy 5 — Last 10 Minutes [ALL]

Eyes closed, rehearse tomorrow: arrive, sit, read the paper, mark questions,
begin. Not cramming. Rehearsal.

### EXAM MORNING

---

### Strategy 6 — Layer 3 at the Centre [ALL]

Reach early. Flip through your Layer 3 book — 20–30 minutes, the whole subject.
Everything familiar. Nothing new. Just confirmation.

### INSIDE THE HALL

---

### Strategy 7 — Use the 15 Minutes Reading Time [ALL]

Before writing begins you get 15 minutes of reading time. This is not a break
— it is where the paper is half-won. Scan the whole paper and mark every
question: ✓ (know it well) · ? (know parts) · ✗ (skip). Then decide your
sequence and roughly how long each question gets.

> **RANK ONLY:**
> Pencil the time plan lightly on the question paper — "Q1: 25 min, Q2: 20 min"
> — so you're executing a plan, not improvising mid-paper.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "I always used the reading time to pencil-scan the whole paper and see exactly
> what's asked. The first job is to fix the sequence. Then I'd open with the
> question I was most confident in — a strong first answer sets the impression
> on the examiner."

---

### Strategy 8 — MCQs First [ALL]

Always start with the MCQ section. It warms up the brain, secures those marks
early, and builds confidence before the big subjective questions.

- For numerical MCQs: calculate first, then match — never eliminate by guesswork.
- Can't crack a numerical MCQ in ~90 seconds? Mark it, move on, return later.
- No negative marking — never leave an MCQ blank. A reasoned guess beats empty.

---

### Strategy 9 — Build Momentum [ALL]

After MCQs, start with the question you're most confident about. Strong early
answers steady your nerves. Hit the hard questions once you're in flow,
around 45–60 minutes in.

---

### Strategy 10 — Presentation and Submission [ALL]

Readable handwriting — not pretty, readable. Working notes numbered and labelled
right below each answer. Every statement and account gets a heading. Cite the
standard/section when you use it ("As per AS 16…"). Underline final answers and
key terms — only those.

The small mechanics that quietly cost marks:
- **Tick attempted-question boxes** on the cover — every one you attempt.
  An un-ticked answer can go unmarked.
- On the MCQ/OMR sheet, **align every bubble carefully** — one row out of place
  and every answer after it is wrong.
- Before submitting, **count your supplements** so every extra sheet is attached.
- Keep the **pen cap off the back** while writing — less weight, less fatigue.
- Save the **last 10 minutes to review** — check totals, underline final answers,
  confirm nothing is half-marked.

> **RANK ONLY:**
> Add disclosure notes even when not asked — each valid one can carry half a
> mark to a mark, and signals depth to the examiner.

---

### Strategy 11 — Never Leave It Blank [ALL]

Stuck? Show what you can — the opening entry, the format headings, working
note 1. State your approach in a line: "Proceeding with Purchase Method as per
AS 14." Partial marks are real. An empty page is a zero. A half-attempt is
3–5 marks.

---

### Strategy 12 — Time Discipline [ALL]

No single question gets more than 1.5× its allotted time before you move on.
Forty minutes on a 12-mark question while three others sit blank is an
expensive mistake.

---

### Strategy 13 — When the Balance Sheet Won't Tally [ALL]

Don't erase everything. Write: "Note: difference of ₹___, likely from [area];
approach and working notes are correct." Examiners reward honesty and award
step marks for correct working notes and schedules even when the final figure
is off.

---

### Strategy 14 — Perseverance, at the Highest Level [ALL]

There will be questions you read and your mind goes blank — even things you
revised yesterday. This is normal under pressure. Breathe, move to a question
you can do, and come back. The exam tests nerve as much as knowledge.
Hold steady through the full 3 hours.

---

### BETWEEN PAPERS

---

### Strategy 15 — Don't Discuss the Paper [ALL]

Walk out, don't dissect it with anyone. Finding out someone wrote something
differently only breeds anxiety you can't act on. That energy belongs to the
next paper.

---

### Strategy 16 — Keep Sleeping 7–8 Hours [ALL]

Exam week is the time to sleep most consistently, not least. Every paper
deserves a rested brain.

---

### Strategy 17 — Light Prep for the Next Paper [ALL]

After resting, do light evening revision for the next paper — Layer 3 + error
register for that subject. You're reminding, not relearning.

### THE DISCIPLINE BRIDGE

There are two types of students who read a strategy book like this.

The first type reads it, thinks "wow, this makes sense," and does nothing.
Because this all seems big. Overwhelming.

The second type reads it and starts tonight. Not everything. One thing.

**CA is a game of PREPARATION — not a game of talent.**

And preparation demands one thing above everything else: **DISCIPLINE.**

Not a quick motivation burst. Not a one-week fire that dies every Monday.
Not josh. **Unwavering commitment. Unshakable. Whatever is happening outside.**

Looks overwhelming? Start with **Atomic Habits.**

Chhoti, tiny habits. Roz. Any habit done for 21 days becomes automatic.
You don't need the willpower to study 12 hours tomorrow. You need the habit
to open your notebook for 25 minutes today.

**The buffer is still there. Go.**

---

This bucket has no start date and no end date. It runs from the day you begin
this book until the day your result comes. Read it once. Then live it.

No "Starting Late" box here. If you haven't been doing these — start today.
The day you realise this, put it into practice that same day — and then every
single day after.

> One line before we begin.
>
> There are two kinds of pain:
> 1. The pain of discipline NOW
> 2. The pain of regret at time of result
>
> You must choose 1 out of the above 2 today. CHOOSE WISELY.
>
> That is the only philosophy line in this book. We're done. Now the system.

Priority 1 — Health

Friends, relatives, teachers — all of them come after these three. Lock this

### Strategy 1 — The Second Space in Your Phone [ALL]

Your phone is your study tool now — your classes, notes, PDFs, and AI all live
on it. Split it.

Create a **Second Space / New User** on your phone. A clean study-only profile
inside your phone, for these preparation months.

In this space, install only what serves the exam:
- Your faculty's app
- Companies Act / GST / Income Tax apps
- AI tools
- A water reminder, a posture reminder
- A news app

Instagram, WhatsApp chats — they stay in your main space.
This space is study only.

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> Android: Settings → Users / Second Space → create new user → install only the
> apps above.
> iPhone / iOS: iOS doesn't support a second user. Hide and lock distracting
> apps, turn off their notifications, mute the groups, filter contacts so nothing
> pulls you mid-study.

---

### Strategy 2 — AI Tools, Installed and Ready [ALL]

Install in your Second Space: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, KIMI, and
an India-native option like Indus / Sarvam AI (good for Hindi).

The full AI playbook — what to ask, how to ask it — is in Bucket 3, Strategy 8.

---

### Strategy 3 — Sleep [ALL]

7–8 hours. Every night. Your brain reinforces everything you studied while you
sleep. Cut sleep and you cut the reinforcement.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "I have personally tried polyphasic sleep and multiple other sleep strategies.
> Nothing worked and nothing was required. The only thing that worked: a fixed
> sleep timing, without fail, and 6 hours fixed sleep at night plus a 20–30
> minute nap during the day. Timings may differ for everyone — but minimum 6
> hours and maximum 8 hours is non-negotiable."

---

### Strategy 4 — Move Your Body [ALL]

15–20 minutes of movement daily — walk, stretch, yoga, badminton, anything.
Every 30 minutes of sitting: roll your neck, stretch your back.

**Eyes — the 20-20-20 rule:** every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away
for 20 seconds.

> **DIAGRAM: Posture and Eye Exercise**
> One page of line-drawing diagrams: neck rolls, shoulder shrugs, back stretch,
> wrist rotations, palming, eye rolling.

---

### Strategy 5 — Hydration and Light Meals [ALL]

Water through the day. Dehydration kills focus before you even notice it.

Watch the plate before you sit to study. A heavy meal pulls blood to your
stomach and your eyes start closing inside twenty minutes. Eat lighter before
study blocks.

Sharp foods: sprouts, roasted chana, curd, banana, peanuts, soaked almonds,
paneer, makhana, light poha or idli.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "I never drank coffee or tea until my first year of articleship. My drinks
> during preparation were water, lemon-salt water, jeera-lemon-salt water, and
> coconut water. Simple. Cheap. Worked."

---

### Strategy 6 — Wear Your Blinders [ALL]

There is exactly one thing in front of you: your Dream Marksheet. No topper
celebrations, no comparing whose syllabus is further along, no doing the maths
on someone else's progress.

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> - Mute every WhatsApp group where batchmates compare progress. Mute, don't leave.
> - When family asks how prep is going, one answer: "On track." Don't elaborate.
> - When someone posts a celebration, reply "Congratulations." Then move on.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "In my Foundation internals, there was a girl who beat me about three times.
> I was the first to congratulate her every single time. Only I know how that
> felt. But I never let it turn into anything ugly inside me. She became one of
> my best friends. Your real competition is you."

---

### Strategy 7 — Park Your Emotions Before You Study [ALL]

Whatever is going on outside — family, a fight, a hard day — it stays outside
the study room. Not because it doesn't matter. Because you can't fix it in the
next two hours, and you can definitely lose those two hours to it.

> **HOW TO DO THIS:**
> Before you sit, take 2 minutes. Write down what's bothering you on a scrap
> of paper — complete detail, whatever comes to mind. Fold it. It's parked.
> Store it in paper, not in your mind. Open your notebook. Come back later.

---

### Strategy 8 — The 10-Minute Flashback (Before Sleep) [ALL]

Don't end the night doom-scrolling. Lie down, eyes closed, and replay your
whole day. What happened, what you studied, what you understood. Not judging
it good or bad. Just recalling, in detail. You're training your brain's recall
muscle — the exact muscle the exam tests.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "If you overthink like I do, here's my hack. Pick the one concept or problem
> you didn't understand that day and just turn it over in your head — no Google,
> no notes, only your brain — until you fall asleep. I solved a lot of maths
> problems this way during Foundation."

---

### Strategy 9 — Talk to Yourself [ALL]

Forget writing a diary. Make a WhatsApp group with only yourself. At the end of
the day, send a voice note — say whatever you're feeling, what went well, what
didn't. Just talk.

---

### Strategy 10 — Daily Targets, Not a Daily Lecture [ALL]

Morning: write today's actual targets. Not "study accounts." Specific:
"finish Consolidation illustrations 4–9, note errors, mark important questions."

Night: in your self-WhatsApp note, check what got done, what didn't, why.
Carry the misses forward honestly.

**The point is the target, not the hours.** Never measure your day by "I sat
for 12 hours." Measure it by "did today's targets get done."

> **RANK ONLY:**
> Keep a weekly tally — hours, chapters, questions. Review every Sunday.
> Adjust next week on data, not mood.

---

### Strategy 11 — Earn Your Breaks [ALL]

Parties, functions, outings — yes, but when your week's targets are on track.
This isn't punishment. It's deciding in advance that the fun follows the work.

---

### Strategy 12 — Keep a Hobby [ALL]

Continue at least one hobby through these preparation months.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "Keep a hobby. Otherwise the articleship interview gets very boring — mine was
> quite boring. Lolzz." (Said with a smile. But not fully joking.)

---

### Strategy 13 — Make It a Game [ALL]

Learning without a target is just punishment. Learning without fun is incomplete.

Your preparation is a game with levels:

- **Level 1** = Level 1 Cleared — all three Bootis done, 45 days out.
  Every chapter, every subject. Tab — aur sirf tab — Level 1 Cleared.
- **Final Boss** = the exam hall.

Every chapter you nail, every error register entry you write, every Visual Vault
sheet you paste — that is XP earned. The goal isn't to survive the preparation.
It's to play it well.

> **PRANAV'S TIP:**
> "I always kept a mental scoreboard — not against anyone else, just against my
> own yesterday. Did I do more today? Did I understand something I didn't
> understand last week? That small internal game kept the energy alive across
> months. Find your own version of 'princess ko bachana hai' — you just have to
> find your own princess."
