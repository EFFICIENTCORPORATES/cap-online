KAHAANI HOOKS 3 Stories — Before the Theory
Story 1 — 5 Pathology Labs
Same blood. 5 labs. 5 different haemoglobin readings.
Arjun's typhoid report — one blood sample, sent to 5 labs. Lab A: 11.2. Lab B: 12.8. Lab C: 13.5. Lab D: 10.6. Lab E: 14.1.

Who is right? Which doctor treats the patient? When the measurement method is different, the result is meaningless.

Every lab follows the same standard now — NABL accreditation. One method. One result. Trust restored.

Accounts ki duniya mein yahi GAAP karta hai.

Year 3: Accountant C — Weighted Average. Profit: ₹34L.

₹22 billion. Foreign investors. One problem.
Byju's needed capital from Prosus (Netherlands), Sequoia (USA), BlackRock (global). These investors speak IFRS. India had AS — a different grammar.

FW-1 Introduction — What the Framework Is Medium
The Framework for Preparation and Presentation of Financial Statements is NOT an Accounting Standard. It sets out the underlying concepts and principles on which all Accounting Standards are built.

Issued by: Accounting Standards Board (ASB) of ICAI — July 2000.

What the Framework Covers

Components of FS Objectives of FS Underlying Assumptions Qualitative Characteristics Elements (Asset · Liability · Equity · Income · Expense) Recognition Criteria Measurement Principles Capital & Capital Maintenance
Exam Rule — Hierarchy

In case of conflict between an Accounting Standard and the Framework — the Accounting Standard always prevails. The Framework is foundational, not overriding. Specific AS have been debated, tested, and refined; they override the Constitution when there's conflict.

FW-3 Status and Scope Low–Medium

In Scope ✓

General-purpose financial statements — prepared annually for external users, by all commercial, industrial and business enterprises (public or private sector).

Out of Scope ✗

Special-purpose reports — e.g., computations prepared for income tax purposes. The Framework may be applied to these, to the extent not inconsistent with their requirements.
Key Rule

Nothing in the Framework overrides any specific Accounting Standard. In conflict → AS wins. Always.

FW-4 Components of Financial Statements Medium
ComponentPrimary Information Provided
Balance Sheet Value of economic resources controlled by the enterprise; liquidity and solvency position; ability to meet financial commitments
Statement of Profit & Loss Results of operations for the period; profitability performance
Cash Flow Statement How cash was generated and used; analysis of operating, investing and financing activities
Notes & Other Statements Supplementary information — accounting policies, segment reporting, related party disclosures, EPS, other explanatory material
MCQ Trap — FW-4

All components are interrelated — they reflect different aspects of the same transactions. No single statement in isolation serves all purposes. Common MCQ option: "Human Responsibility Report" or "Social Responsibility Report" are NOT components of financial statements.

FW-5 Objectives & 7 Users of Financial Statements High
Objective: Provide information about financial position, performance and cash flows of an enterprise — useful to a wide range of users in making economic decisions.
#UserWhat They Are Looking For
1 Investors Risk and return on investment; ability of enterprise to pay dividends
2 Employees Stability and profitability; ability to provide remuneration, retirement benefits and employment continuity
3 Lenders Whether loans and interest will be repaid when due
4 Suppliers & Trade Creditors Whether amounts owing will be paid when due; shorter-term interest than lenders (unless dependent on enterprise as major customer)
5 Customers Continuance of the enterprise, especially for long-term product/service dependency
6 Governments & Agencies Allocation of resources; regulation of activities; determination of taxation policy; national income statistics
7 Public Contribution to local economy; employment provided; patronage of local suppliers; trends in enterprise prosperity

Financial statements provide information to suit common needs of most users — they cannot and do not provide all information (e.g., no non-financial data like management quality or brand strength).

FW-6a Fundamental Assumption 1 — Going Concern Very High
Financial statements are prepared on the assumption that the enterprise will continue in operation in the foreseeable future — with no intention and no need to materially curtail the scale of operations.

Going Concern → Not Going Concern: What Changes

ItemGoing Concern TreatmentNot Going Concern Treatment
PPE / Fixed Assets Cost less depreciation (SLM over remaining useful life) Net Realisable Value (what you'd get on forced sale)
Depreciation Based on useful life — spread over years Difference between book value and current NRV
Deferred Expenditure Amortised over agreed period (e.g., 4 years — ₹X per year) Entire remaining balance written off immediately
Doubtful Debts Provision on estimated doubtful amounts only Write off ALL dependent/uncertain receivables immediately
Loan Prepayment Penalty Not applicable (loan continuing) Recognised immediately as expense
Trade Payables At book value (no discount expected) At likely settlement value (deduct discount if likely)
Stock Lower of Cost and NRV (selling in ordinary course) NRV only (forced liquidation assumption)
TYK Q10 — Very High Priority

ICAI Illustration 1 and TYK Q10 (Anurag Trading Co.) both require full P&L and Balance Sheet under (i) Going Concern and (ii) Not Going Concern for the same data. Every single line changes. Master this. The most common trap: students forget deferred expenditure is written off in FULL under Not GC (not just current year's portion), and forget the loan penalty is recognised immediately.

Disclosure rule: If followed → no separate disclosure needed. If NOT followed → must specifically disclose which basis is used and why.

FW-6b Fundamental Assumption 2 — Accrual High
Revenues and costs are recognised when earned or incurred — not when cash is received or paid. They are recorded in the financial statements of the periods to which they relate.
Cash BasisAccrual Basis
Income recognised whenCash receivedEarned / service performed
Expense recognised whenCash paidIncurred (benefit received)
Outstanding incomeNot recordedRecorded as receivable (asset)
Prepaid expenseFull expense in period paidOnly current-period portion as expense; balance = asset
Mandatory for companies?NoYes — Section 128(1), Companies Act 2013
Kaam Ki Baat — PPAC / Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas
Subsidy Receivable — Earned Before Cash Arrives

At PPAC (under MoPNG), the Government pays subsidy to oil PSUs (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL) for selling fuel below cost. But the actual disbursement from the Union Budget happens months after the financial year ends.

Recognition: in the year the subsidy was earned — the year the fuel was sold below cost. Not when the cash arrived. IOCL shows "Subsidy Receivable from Government of India" as an asset — sometimes ₹10,000+ crore — waiting for cash.

The earnings event drives recognition. Not the payment event. This is accrual at scale.

Byju's Connection

₹90,000 received. 3-year course. Accrual says: recognise income only as service is delivered — 1/3rd per year. Booking full ₹90,000 in Year 1 = Accrual violation. The income was not yet earned; the obligation to deliver 3 years of education was still outstanding.

FW-6c Fundamental Assumption 3 — Consistency Medium
Accounting policies are assumed to be consistent from one period to another. Consistency improves comparability of financial statements across time.

When Can You Change an Accounting Policy?

1. Required by a statute 2. Required by an Accounting Standard 3. For more appropriate presentation of financial statements

If changed → disclose the financial effect of the change.

MCQ Trap — FW-6c

MCQ 4 option (d) says policy can be changed "by statute as well as accounting standards" (both simultaneously required). Wrong. The correct rule is: ANY ONE of the three grounds is sufficient. Option (c) "Both (a) and (b)" is the right answer — meaning by statute OR by AS OR for more appropriate presentation.

FW-7 Qualitative Characteristics of Financial Statements High

Four attributes that improve the usefulness of financial information — to be maintained as far as possible within limits of reasonable cost/benefit.

CharacteristicCore MeaningSub-points / Constraints
Understandability Information presented so it can be understood by users with reasonable knowledge of business, economics and accounting Complex information cannot be omitted just because some users may find it difficult — it must be explained clearly
Relevance Information that is likely to influence economic decisions of users — evaluating past/present/future events or confirming/correcting past evaluations Materiality: threshold for relevance — item is material if its misstatement (omission or error) could influence decisions. Judged by size and nature.

Constraint — Timeliness: Delayed info loses relevance. But rushing before all facts known impairs reliability. Balance required.

Constraint — Benefit vs Cost: Benefits of providing information must exceed the cost. A pervasive constraint — not a qualitative characteristic itself.
Reliability Free from material error and bias. Five sub-requirements: (a) Faithful representation — transactions reported as they actually occurred
(b) Substance over form — reported on economic reality, not just legal form
(c) Neutrality — free from bias; presentation does not skew conclusions
(d) Prudence — caution in uncertain outcomes: anticipate no profits, provide for all losses
(e) Completeness — no material information omitted
Comparability FS should permit inter-firm (same industry, different companies) and intra-firm (same company, different years) comparison Comparability ≠ Uniformity. Do not retain an inferior accounting policy just to maintain consistency. If a better, more relevant policy exists → change it, disclose the effect, move forward.
Theory Q7 — Neutrality

"One of the characteristics of financial statements is neutrality" — Yes, agree. Neutrality is a sub-component of Reliability. FS are not neutral if, by selection or presentation of information, the focus of analysis could shift from one area of business to another, arriving at a totally different conclusion.

Byju's — Reliability Violations (All 5)

Faithful representation: income booked before earned. Substance over form: cash received ≠ income earned. Neutrality: accounts designed to attract investors. Prudence: profits anticipated before earned. Completeness: 3-year delivery obligation not disclosed as liability. All five violated by one company, one transaction.

FW-8 True and Fair View Low
Financial statements are required to show a true and fair view of performance, financial position and cash flows.

The Framework does not define "true and fair view" directly. However: application of the four qualitative characteristics + appropriate Accounting Standards normally results in financial statements portraying a true and fair view.
FW-9 Elements of Financial Statements — 5 Elements High
Asset
Resource controlled by enterprise, from past event, generating probable future economic benefits
Liability
Present obligation from past event, settlement expected to cause outflow of economic resources
Equity
Residual interest in assets after deducting all liabilities
(A − L = E)
Income
Increase in economic benefits → inflows / asset enhancement / liability decrease → equity increase (excl. owner contributions)
Expense
Decrease in economic benefits → outflows / asset depletion / liability increase → equity decrease (excl. owner drawings)

Key Rules for Income and Expense

RuleIncomeExpense
Always accompanied by Increase in an asset OR decrease in a liability Decrease in an asset OR increase in a liability
Gains/Losses Gains (not in ordinary course) = part of Income element Losses (not in ordinary course) = part of Expense element
NOT a separate element Gains are included in Income (not a 6th element) Losses are included in Expenses (not a 6th element)
Byju's — ₹90,000 — Element Test

₹90,000 received for 3-year course, Year 1. Income? No. Income requires the economic benefit to have been earned. The service (3 years of teaching) is not yet delivered. First it is a Liability — a present obligation (to deliver education) arising from a past event (receipt of ₹90,000), expected to result in outflow of resources (faculty, content, infrastructure). As each year's course is delivered → portion converts to Income.

FW-9a Asset — 5 Conditions for Recognition Medium
#ConditionKey Point / Example
1 Need not have physical substance Patents, copyrights, trade receivables are assets — no physical form required
2 Controlled by enterprise (not necessarily owned) Finance lease: lessee recognises the asset (controls it) even without legal ownership. Lessor does NOT recognise it in their books.
3 Control must be sufficient Specific management talent / technical expertise of an employee cannot be recognised as an asset — insufficient control (employee can resign)
4 Probable future economic benefits beyond current period If benefit exhausted in current period → it is an expense, not an asset. E.g., machinery bought by machinery dealer for resale in current period = expense (not capitalised)
5 Cost or value must be reliably measurable Cannot recognise an asset if its value cannot be determined with reasonable reliability
FW-9b Liability — 3 Key Rules Very High
#RuleDetail
1 Present obligation — not a future commitment A management decision to buy an asset in the future does NOT create a present liability. Obligation arises when: (a) asset is delivered, OR (b) enterprise enters an irrevocable agreement to acquire it (binding contract)
2 Provisions as liabilities — careful distinction Provision for warranty, litigation = liability (probable present obligation). BUT: provision for doubtful debts, depreciation, impairment = NOT liabilities — they represent diminution in asset value
3 Liability recognised only when outflow is anticipated AND reliably measurable Both conditions must be met. If probability is too uncertain (contingent) or amount cannot be measured → disclose as contingent liability, do not recognise on balance sheet
TYK Q11 — Very High Priority (Binding Agreement)

A Ltd. has a binding (irrevocable) agreement to buy a custom machine for ₹1,00,000. Before delivery, the production method changes — machine will be scrapped on arrival (zero scrap value).

Step 1: Recognise a liability of ₹1,00,000 to Gamma Ltd. — binding irrevocable agreement creates present obligation.
Step 2: No future economic benefit → machine cost = expense immediately (not capitalised as asset).

Entry: Loss on change in production method Dr. 1,00,000 / To Gamma Ltd. 1,00,000
Then: P&L A/c Dr. 1,00,000 / To Loss on change... 1,00,000

Common wrong answer: students wait for delivery to recognise liability. Wrong — irrevocable agreement = present obligation now.

FW-9c The A − L = E Relationship High
Fundamental Accounting Equation
Assets − Liabilities = Equity
Expanded Equity Formula
Closing Equity = Opening Equity + Capital Introduced + Income − Expenses − Drawings
Profit (derived)
Profit = (Closing Assets − Closing Liabilities) − (Opening Assets − Opening Liabilities) − Capital Introduced + Drawings

Key insight: Profit depends on how assets and liabilities are valued. Change the valuation basis → reported profit changes. This is the conceptual foundation of Capital Maintenance (FW-11).

TYK Q9 — Transaction Tracking

Mr. A — opening A: ₹8L, L: ₹3L, E: ₹5L. Four transactions: (1) dividend earned, (2) creditor settled at discount, (3) rent outstanding, (4) drawings. Track each transaction through the A−L=E table. Common error: treating drawings as a liability decrease instead of an asset decrease (equity decrease).

FW-10 Measurement of Elements — 4 Bases Medium

Measurement = determining the money value at which an element is recognised in Balance Sheet or P&L. Four alternative bases:

1. Historical Cost
Assets: Amount paid / fair value at time of acquisition
Liabilities: Amount of proceeds received in exchange for the obligation
Primary basis used by most AS. Machine bought for ₹7L recorded at ₹7L even if replacement cost is now ₹25L.
2. Current Cost
Assets: Amount that would be paid to acquire the same/equivalent asset currently
Liabilities: Undiscounted amount required to settle the obligation currently
Note: liabilities under current cost = undiscounted (not present value of future payments)
3. Realisable (Settlement) Value
Assets: Amount currently realisable on orderly disposal/sale
Liabilities: Undiscounted amount expected to be paid on settlement in normal course of business
Example: NRV used for inventory valuation at year-end (AS 2). Assets not carried above what they can realise.
4. Present Value
Assets: Present value of future net cash inflows expected in normal course of business
Liabilities: Present value of future net cash outflows required to settle in normal course
PV = A ÷ (1+R)ⁿ. Called "Value in Use" for assets. Used in impairment testing.
Kaam Ki Baat — D2C Startup CFO Advisory
Same Formula, Four Different Values

A D2C beauty startup — proprietary skincare formula, 2 years of R&D, ₹40L spent.
Under Historical Cost: ₹40L (capitalised if AS 26 conditions met).
Under Present Value: ₹3.2 crore (estimated future cash flows over commercial life, discounted at required rate).

Same formula. Same product. ₹40L vs ₹3.2 crore.

Neither is wrong — they answer different questions. Historical cost: what did we spend to create this? Present value: what will this earn us? The Framework recognises all four bases because different users and different decisions need different answers.

Practice Standard — Historical Cost Dominates

In practice, Accounting Standards primarily use Historical Cost, with modifications where specific AS require it: Inventory → Lower of Cost and NRV (realisable value); PPE → Cost less depreciation (with optional revaluation); Financial instruments → Fair value in some cases.

FW-11 Capital Maintenance — 3 Approaches Very High
Capital = net assets of a business. Capital Maintenance = ensuring the business can replace its assets and continue operations at the same level — i.e., dividends should not exceed profit after adequate provision for asset replacement.
Retained Profit Formula (all three bases)
Retained Profit = Closing Capital (at chosen basis) − Opening Capital (at chosen basis) − Capital Introduced

If Retained Profit is negative → capital NOT maintained → drawings were excessive.

Basis How Opening Equity Is Restated What It Ensures Limitation
Financial Capital at Historical Cost Opening and closing assets at their respective historical costs. No restatement. Business has enough funds to replace assets at their original purchase prices Works only if prices do not rise. In inflation → insufficient to replace assets at current prices.
Financial Capital at Current Purchasing Power Opening equity restated to closing prices using an average price index
(Opening equity × Closing Index ÷ Opening Index)		 Business has enough to replace assets at average current prices Average index doesn't reflect specific asset price changes. Machine may rise 30% while average is 20%.
Physical Capital at Current Costs Opening assets restated using specific price indices for each individual asset Business has enough to replace each specific asset at its own current price Most realistic but most complex to compute.
Illustration 2 & TYK Q8 — Very High Priority

Common trap: Students apply the price index to closing equity rather than opening equity. The restatement is always of opening equity (or opening assets) to closing-period prices. Closing equity stays at its actual/book value.

TYK Q8: Mohan — 60,000 units × ₹20 = opening equity ₹12,00,000. Sold all at ₹30. Maximum drawings = Closing equity (₹18L) − Opening equity (₹12L) = ₹6,00,000 under Historical Cost basis.

  • 02Framework vs AS conflict: AS prevails. Always.

  • 11Four qualitative characteristics: Understandability · Relevance · Reliability · Comparability.
  • 12Materiality = threshold for Relevance. Material if misstatement could influence economic decisions. Judged by size AND nature.
  • 13Reliability sub-components: Faithful representation · Substance over form · Neutrality · Prudence · Completeness.
  • 14Comparability ≠ Uniformity. Do not retain inferior policies just for consistency.
  • 15Five elements: Asset · Liability · Equity · Income · Expense. Gains = Income. Losses = Expenses. Not separate elements.
  • 16Asset: Controlled (not necessarily owned) + past event + probable future economic benefit beyond current period.
  • 17Liability: Present obligation (NOT future commitment) + past event + expected outflow. Binding irrevocable agreement = present obligation.
