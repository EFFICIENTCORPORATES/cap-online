## T2.1 · Introduction {#M2-C6-U2-T2.1}

**Concept**
- In force for periods commencing on or after 1-4-2004.
- Aim: right recognition and measurement for provisions and contingent liabilities; notes that show nature, timing, amount; accounting for contingent assets.
- Problem it solves: **profit smoothing**: provision on arbitrary basis in a high-profit year, reversed in a low-profit year.
- A provision hits both Statement of P&L (expense) and Balance Sheet (liability).
- Management makes the estimate; the auditor vouches for it.
- Before AS 29, uncertain timing or amount was an excuse to skip a liability; now a qualifying item must be shown as a liability.

**Exam points**
- Manipulation both ways: (a) low-profit year: skip the provision that is due, to protect EPS; (b) high-profit year: create a provision that is not due, to cut profit and tax.
- AS 29 makes the provision follow the criteria and the right year, not the profit.

**Stories:** S1, S3 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 1 (XY Enterprise), Example 2 (AB Shops).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Profit dekh kar nahi, shart dekh kar provision banao.

## T2.2 · Scope {#M2-C6-U2-T2.2}

**Concept**
- AS 29 applies to provisions, contingent liabilities and contingent assets, **except** those from:
  - (a) financial instruments carried at fair value;
  - (b) executory contracts, **unless onerous**;
  - (c) insurance enterprises' contracts with policy-holders;
  - (d) items covered by another Accounting Standard.
- ICAI names AS 7, AS 9, AS 15, AS 19 and AS 22 as standards that deal with specific provisions; apply that standard instead.

**Exam algorithm: which standard?**
1. Does another Accounting Standard deal with this specific item? Yes → that standard.
2. Is it an executory contract? Not onerous → outside AS 29.
3. Onerous executory contract → inside AS 29.
4. Anything else → AS 29.

**Exam points**
- "Unless onerous" brings a loss-making executory contract back into AS 29.
- ICAI's chapter does not define "onerous". Class meaning (Pranav): a deal where you lose on every unit (ghate ka sauda); provision for the expected loss is allowed.

**Stories:** S5 · **Where ICAI tests it:** no illustration or Test Your Knowledge item tests scope on its own.

**Linkages**
- AS 15, Employee Benefits (M2-C6-U1-T1.8 and T1.12): AS 29's scope names AS 15, and AS 15 sends the uncertain voluntary-redundancy case back to AS 29 (see T2.9). Checked in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Doosre standard ka maal doosre ke paas; executory mein sirf ghate ka sauda aata hai.

## T2.3 · Definitions {#M2-C6-U2-T2.3}

**Concept**
- **Executory contract:** neither party has performed, or both partly performed to an equal extent.
- **Provision:** a liability measurable only with a substantial degree of estimation.
- **Liability:** present obligation from past events; settlement expected to cause an outflow of resources with economic benefits.
- **Obligating event:** creates an obligation; the enterprise has no realistic alternative to settling it.
- **Contingent liability:** (a) a *possible* obligation from past events, confirmed only by uncertain future events not wholly in the enterprise's control; or (b) a *present* obligation not recognised because (i) outflow is not probable, or (ii) no reliable estimate.
- **Contingent asset:** a possible asset from past events, confirmed only by uncertain future events not wholly in the enterprise's control.
- **Present obligation:** existence at the balance sheet date is probable, i.e. more likely than not. **Possible obligation:** existence is not probable.
- **Restructuring:** a programme planned and controlled by management that materially changes the scope of the business or the manner in which it is conducted.

**Exam algorithm: classify the item**
1. At the balance sheet date, is an obligation more likely than not to exist? Yes → present; no → possible.
2. Present + outflow probable + reliable estimate → provision (T2.4).
3. Possible, or present but not probable or not measurable → contingent liability (T2.9).

**Exam points**
- Contingent liability has two faces: a possible obligation, or a present obligation that fails a test.
- Executory is a test of *both* sides: if one party has already performed and the other has not, it is not executory and a liability exists (class: student did 3 weeks, Bhaiya had not paid).
- ICAI's own word for "provision" differs: the definition above says "measured with substantial estimation"; TYK MCQ 1's key says "liability of uncertain timing or amount". ICAI is inconsistent in wording; choose key (a).
- "More likely than not" means above 50 percent; exactly 50 percent is not enough (my reading of the words).

**Stories:** S1, S4, S5 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 1, MCQ 3.

**Linkages**
- Chapter 11 Unit 1, Final Accounts (M3-C11-U1-T1.5) quotes AS 29's definitions of provision, liability and present obligation to tell a provision apart from a reserve. Checked in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Provision = pakka liability, bas amount ka andaza; contingent = abhi pakka nahi.

## T2.4 · Recognition of provision {#M2-C6-U2-T2.4}

**Concept**
- Recognise a provision when **all three** hold:
  - (a) present obligation from a past event;
  - (b) outflow of resources probable;
  - (c) reliable estimate possible.
- If any condition fails, no provision.

**Exam algorithm: the master test**
1. Present obligation at the balance sheet date, from a past event? (T2.5, T2.6)
2. Outflow probable, i.e. more likely than not? (T2.7)
3. Reliable estimate, a range is enough? (T2.8)
4. All three yes → provision; expense to P&L, liability to balance sheet.
5. Any no → contingent liability or nothing (T2.9).

**Exam points**
- Theory answer (TYK Theory Q9) = the three conditions, in this order.
- Class diagram: the balance sheet shows "Provisions (current)" and "Provisions (long term)" (Pranav's drawing; ICAI's chapter text does not use the Schedule III headings).

**Stories:** S1, S9 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 1, 2, 5, 7; Theory Q9; Q10; Q12.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Teen shart: purana event, paisa jaane ka chance, andaza ban sake.

## T2.5 · Present obligation {#M2-C6-U2-T2.5}

**Concept**
- Decide at the balance sheet date using all available evidence, for example the opinion of experts.
- More likely than not that an obligation exists → provision (if the other tests are met).
- More likely that no obligation exists → disclose a contingent liability, unless outflow is remote.

**Exam algorithm**
1. Gather the evidence available at the balance sheet date (lawyer, expert).
2. Obligation more likely than not? Yes → test probable outflow (T2.7).
3. No → contingent liability, unless remote.

**Exam points**
- A sale with a free-repair warranty creates the obligation at the sale (Example 3).
- A lease that must be handed back in original condition creates the obligation at signing; provide the **present value** of the restoration cost (Example 4).
- A contract to deliver by a date, with a penalty, creates the obligation once delivery will probably be missed (TYK Q10).
- ICAI's Example 4 says "X Ltd" in its last line, though the entity is Z Ltd. ICAI's slip; read it as Z Ltd.

**Stories:** S4, S9 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 3 (X Ltd), Example 4 (Z Ltd); TYK MCQ 4, MCQ 7; Q10 (Sun Ltd).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Balance sheet ki tareekh par saboot dekho, bhavishya ki ummeed nahi.

## T2.6 · Past event {#M2-C6-U2-T2.6}

**Concept**
- **Obligating event:** a past event that leaves no realistic alternative to settling.
- Only obligations that exist **independently of future actions** (the future conduct of the business) are provisions.
- No provision for costs of operating in the future (for example fitting smoke filters), because the enterprise can avoid them by changing its method.
- Provision for damage already done: penalties or clean-up for unlawful environmental damage; decommissioning an oil installation to the extent damage is already caused.
- Damage that nobody must remedy becomes an obligating event when a **new law requires** the clean-up.
- A proposed law: obligation only when enactment is **virtually certain**.

**Exam algorithm: is there a past event?**
1. What has already happened? That is the event.
2. Can the enterprise avoid the spending by its own future action? Yes → no obligation.
3. No realistic alternative? Yes → obligating event.
4. Law: enacted, or virtually certain to be enacted → obligation now. Only proposed → none.

**Exam points**
- Class test: the cracked bucket that has already leaked oil gives a provision; the bucket that might crack later does not.
- Law not yet passed but virtually certain → provide in the year it becomes virtually certain.

**Stories:** S4 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK Q11 (oil company).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Jo ho chuka uska provision; jo ho sakta hai aur rok sakte ho, uska nahi.

## T2.7 · Probable outflow of resources {#M2-C6-U2-T2.7}

**Concept**
- A liability needs a present obligation **and** a probable outflow.
- **Probable:** the chance it occurs is greater than the chance it does not.
- Not probable that a present obligation exists → disclose a contingent liability, unless the outflow is remote.
- **Many similar obligations** (product warranties): judge the class as a whole. One item may be unlikely, yet some outflow for the class is probable → provision (if other tests are met).

**Exam algorithm**
1. Present obligation exists? (T2.5)
2. Many similar items? Judge the whole class.
3. Probable → reliable estimate (T2.8).
4. Not probable but not remote → contingent liability.
5. Remote → nothing.

**Exam points**
- A customer's claim whose result depends on a court decision is not probable → no provision, disclose (Example 6).
- Replacement warranty with past experience of replacements → outflow expected → provide (Example 5).
- ICAI gives no percentage for "remote". Class guide (Pranav, not ICAI): remote is below 5 percent.

**Stories:** S4, S8 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 5 (Kell Ltd), Example 6 (AB Ltd); TYK MCQ 4.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Ek unit par shak, poori class par pakka: warranty ka provision banta hai.

## T2.8 · Reliable estimate of the obligation {#M2-C6-U2-T2.8}

**Concept**
- Estimates are a normal part of financial statements; provisions need more of them.
- It should be possible to find a **range** of outcomes and reach a sufficiently reliable conclusion.
- A case with no reliable estimate is **extremely rare**.
- In that rare case a liability exists but is not recognised: disclose it as a contingent liability.

**Exam points**
- A range of possible outcomes still gives a best estimate (TYK MCQ 5): provide the best estimate in the range, not the claimed maximum and not nothing.
- ICAI's mining example: legally bound to restore the site after the licence ends in 10 years, cost estimable → provision.
- Class: a fixed per-litre amount is a certain liability; a guessed clean-up cost is a provision; an unmeasurable health hazard is a contingent liability.

**Stories:** S4 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 5.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Andaza kaafi hai; poora pakka amount nahi chahiye.

## T2.9 · Contingent liabilities {#M2-C6-U2-T2.9}

**Concept**
- **Never recognise** a contingent liability; **disclose** it unless the outflow is remote.
- Jointly and severally liable: the part others are expected to meet is a contingent liability; provide for the part where outflow is probable (barring the extremely rare no-estimate case).
- Assess continually. If outflow becomes probable, **provide in the period of the change**.

**Exam algorithm: decision tree** *(my reconstruction of ICAI's printed flowchart from its text; the chart is garbled in the converted file)*
1. Present obligation from an obligating event? Yes → step 2. No → step 4.
2. Outflow probable? Yes → step 3. No → step 5.
3. Reliable estimate? Yes → **provide**. No (rare) → **disclose** contingent liability.
4. Possible obligation? No → do nothing. Yes → step 5.
5. Outflow remote? Yes → do nothing. No → **disclose** contingent liability.

**Exam points**
- Present obligation but outflow not probable (lawyer says not probable yet) → contingent liability, disclose only (Example 7, Example 6).
- Illustration 1: first five cases 100 percent win → nothing. Other cases have a 50 percent chance of winning, so loss is not probable (50 percent is not "more likely than not": my reading). Disclose the **expected loss**, not the maximum: 68,000 per case in ten cases and 72,000 per case in five cases, total 10,40,000.
- ICAI's solution to Illustration 1 calls the outflow "remote" and then discloses it. ICAI is inconsistent here; follow the method and the figure in ICAI's answer.
- A later court judgment gives evidence about the balance sheet date; TYK MCQ 8's key (c) provides, using the same idea as adjusting events in AS 4 (my reading; this unit does not state that rule).

**Stories:** S4 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 7 (XYZ); Illustration 1; Illustration 2, EXOX Ltd, part (i); TYK MCQ 3, MCQ 4, MCQ 8.

**Linkages**
- AS 15, Employee Benefits (M2-C6-U1-T1.8 and T1.12): where it is uncertain how many employees will accept an offer of voluntary redundancy, a contingent liability exists and is disclosed as per AS 29. Checked in both texts.
- AS 4, Contingencies and Events after the Balance Sheet Date (M2-C7-U1): its footnote says that after AS 29 the paragraphs of AS 4 on contingencies stand withdrawn; AS 29's TYK MCQ 8 names AS 4 in an option. Checked in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Pakka nahi to provision nahi, par chhupana bhi nahi: note mein batao.

## T2.10 · Contingent assets {#M2-C6-U2-T2.10}

**Concept**
- Arise from unplanned or unexpected events that may bring an inflow, for example a legal claim with an uncertain outcome.
- **Never recognise**: income that may never be realised.
- If realisation is **virtually certain**, it is no longer a contingent asset: recognise it.
- **Not disclosed in the financial statements.** Where an inflow is probable, it is usually disclosed in the report of the approving authority (Board of Directors for a company).
- Assess continually; when it becomes virtually certain, recognise the asset and the income in the period of the change.

**Exam algorithm: contingent asset or asset?**
1. Does the inflow depend on an uncertain future event outside the enterprise's control? Yes → contingent asset.
2. Virtually certain? Yes → recognise asset and income now.
3. Probable but not virtually certain → no entry, no note in the statements; Board's report only.
4. Less than probable → nothing.

**Exam points**
- Asymmetry: a contingent liability goes in the notes; a contingent asset stays out of the statements.
- Class markers (Pranav): case won, only execution of the final order pending = virtually certain; an insurance claim is the usual case.

**Stories:** S7 · **Where ICAI tests it:** only inside TYK MCQ 1's wrong options; no illustration.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Liability pe shak ho to bata do; asset pakka ho tabhi book karo.

## T2.11 · Measurement: best estimate {#M2-C6-U2-T2.11}

**Concept**
- Amount = best estimate of the expenditure to settle the present obligation at the balance sheet date.
- Estimate by management judgment, experience of similar transactions, and sometimes independent experts' reports.
- **No discounting**, except for decommissioning, restoration and similar liabilities recognised as part of the cost of Property, Plant and Equipment.
- Discount rate: **pre-tax**; reflects current market view of time value and risks specific to the liability; not risks already counted in the cash flows.
- Unwinding of the discount goes to the **Statement of P&L**.
- The provision is measured before tax; tax effects are dealt with under AS 22.

**Exam algorithm: measure a provision**
1. Best estimate at the balance sheet date (from a range, a reasoned point).
2. Discount only if it is a decommissioning, restoration or similar item that forms part of Property, Plant and Equipment cost.
3. If discounting: pre-tax rate; unwinding each year to P&L.

**Exam points**
- Restoration due in 10 years in a lease: provide the **present value** (Example 4).
- A 25-year dismantling obligation is recognised at installation, at best estimate with time value in the measurement basis (TYK MCQ 7 key).

**Stories:** none yet · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 4 (Z Ltd); TYK MCQ 5, MCQ 7.

**Linkages**
- AS 10, PPE (M2-C5-U2-T2.7, Initial Recognition): the cost of dismantling and restoring the site is part of the cost of the asset, and AS 10 says these obligations are recognised and measured as per AS 29. Checked in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Discount sirf restoration jaise kharchon par; baaki sab seedha best estimate.

## T2.13 · Future events {#M2-C6-U2-T2.13}

**Concept**
- Only obligations from past events, independent of future actions, are provisions (T2.6).
- Future events that change the **amount** are built into the provision where there is **sufficient objective evidence** they will occur.
- Clean-up cost may fall with technology: reflect expected savings from existing technology, and expected extra cost for a bigger clean-up; do **not** assume a completely new technology without objective evidence.
- New law: counted when it is virtually certain to be enacted. Evidence needed on both what the law demands and that it will be enacted; often only once it is enacted.

**Exam points**
- Two different questions: does the obligation *exist* (T2.6), and what is the *amount* (this topic).

**Stories:** S4 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK Q11 (law virtually certain).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Amount badalne wale future ko tabhi gino jab saboot ho.

## T2.15 · Reimbursements {#M2-C6-U2-T2.15}

**Concept**
- Part or all of the expense may come back from another party: an insurance contract, an indemnity clause, a supplier's warranty.
- The reimbursement is an **asset separate from the obligation**.
- Recognise it only when **virtually certain** to be received once the obligation is settled.
- Usually the enterprise stays liable for the whole amount: provide the full amount, and show the expected reimbursement as a separate asset when virtually certain.
- If the enterprise is **not liable** when the third party fails to pay, that part is not in the provision.

**Exam algorithm**
1. Is the enterprise still liable if the other party does not pay? No → leave that part out.
2. Yes → provision for the **full** amount.
3. Reimbursement virtually certain? Yes → separate asset, not more than the liability.
4. Not virtually certain → no asset; disclose it.

**Exam points**
- Class cases (Pranav): R1 pay only if VCG pays = no provision, no asset. R2 VCG certain, Bhaiya has given his word = provision and asset. R3 VCG not certain = provision only, disclose the reimbursement.
- Never net the liability against the reimbursement in the balance sheet.

**Stories:** S6 · **Where ICAI tests it:** no item in this unit.

**Linkages**
- AS 10, PPE, Illustration 16, Entity A (M2-C5-U2): insured plant replaced by the insurer; the receivable and the gain are recognised separately once receipt is virtually certain, as per AS 29. Checked in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Paisa wapas aane wala asset alag, liability alag; pakka tabhi jab virtually certain.

## T2.17 · Changes in provisions {#M2-C6-U2-T2.17}

**Concept**
- Review at **each balance sheet date**; adjust to the current best estimate.
- No longer probable that an outflow will be needed → **reverse** the provision.

**Exam algorithm: year-end provision entry**
1. Required closing balance at the new best estimate.
2. Compare with the opening balance.
3. Higher → charge the difference to P&L. Lower → credit the difference (reversal).

**Exam points**
- A reversal is **income** (class: reversal of provision leads to increase in income).
- Reversing to flatter a bad year is exactly the profit smoothing of T2.1.
- Charge only the **increase**, not the whole closing balance (TYK Q12).

**Stories:** S2, S3, S9 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 2; Q12.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Har saal naya andaza; sirf farak P&L mein jaata hai.

## T2.18 · Use of provisions {#M2-C6-U2-T2.18}

**Concept**
- A provision is used **only for the expenditure for which it was recognised**.
- Setting other expenditure against it would hide two different events.

**Exam algorithm: payment against a provision** *(Pranav's class cases; ICAI gives no entries)*
1. Payment more than the provision: provision Dr to the extent made; the excess to expense.
2. Payment less, nothing more to come: provision Dr for the payment; the balance is reversed to income (T2.17).
3. Never debit the expense and leave the provision standing.

**Exam points**
- Class: payment is debited to the provision, not again to expense.

**Stories:** S1, S2 · **Where ICAI tests it:** no item tests it on its own.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Jis kaam ke liye provision, usi kaam par kharch.

## T2.19 · Application of the recognition and measurement rules {#M2-C6-U2-T2.19}

**Concept: 2.19.1 Future operating losses**
- They do not meet the definition of a liability or the recognition test: **no provision**.

**Concept: 2.19.2 Restructuring**
- Examples: sale or termination of a line of business; closing locations or moving activities to another region; changing management structure; fundamental re-organisations.
- Provision only when the normal recognition tests are met.
- No obligation to sell until committed: a **binding sale agreement**.
- Include only **direct expenditure** that is (a) necessarily entailed by the restructuring and (b) not associated with ongoing activities.
- Exclude: retraining or relocating continuing staff; marketing; investment in new systems and distribution networks; identifiable future operating losses up to the restructuring date; gains on expected disposal of assets.

**Exam algorithm: restructuring provision**
1. Does it fit the restructuring definition (T2.3)?
2. Is there an obligation at the balance sheet date? For a sale: binding agreement.
3. Count only direct expenditure that passes **both** tests.
4. Leave out the excluded items above.

**Exam points**
- TYK MCQ 6 key (b): board approval alone, with no announcement and no implementation step, does not give a provision. ICAI's chapter text speaks of a binding sale agreement and does not use "constructive obligation" or "announcement"; the key rests on them. ICAI is inconsistent here; choose the key in the exam.

**Stories:** S8 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 6.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Restructuring mein sirf seedha, zaroori kharcha; future ka nahi.

## T2.20 · Disclosure {#M2-C6-U2-T2.20}

**Concept: each class of provision**
- Carrying amount at beginning and end of the period.
- Additional provisions made, including increases in existing ones.
- Amounts used (incurred and charged against the provision).
- Unused amounts reversed.
- Brief nature of the obligation and expected timing of outflows.
- Uncertainties about the outflows; major assumptions on future events where needed.
- Expected reimbursement and the amount of any asset recognised for it.

**Concept: each class of contingent liability** (unless outflow is remote)
- Brief nature and, where practicable: estimate of financial effect; uncertainties; possibility of reimbursement.
- Not practicable → say so.
- Extremely rare: disclosure would seriously prejudice a dispute → give the general nature of the dispute, the fact that details are withheld, and why.

**Exam points**
- Provision movement: opening + additions - used - unused reversed = closing (my reading of ICAI's four items).
- Remote → no disclosure. Contingent asset → not in the notes (T2.10).
- ICAI notes that Small and Medium-sized Companies (SMCs) are exempt from these disclosure requirements.

**Stories:** none yet · **Where ICAI tests it:** Illustrations 1 and 2 are placed here by ICAI's mapping; they test recognition and disclosure together.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Provision ka hisaab note mein; contingent liability ka note; remote ka kuch nahi.
