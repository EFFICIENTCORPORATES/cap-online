## T2.1 · Introduction {#M2-C5-U2-T2.1}

**Concept**
- Objective: accounting treatment of PPE, so users see the investment in PPE and the changes in it.
- Principal issues: recognition of assets; carrying amount; depreciation charge and impairment loss.

**Exam algorithm: the whole standard in one line** *(Pranav's flow, NB01)*
1. Is it PPE? (T2.3, T2.4)
2. Recognise: probable benefit + reliable cost (T2.5).
3. Measure at cost: purchase, exchange, construct (T2.7 to T2.9).
4. Ready to use: capitalisation stops, depreciation starts.
5. Later: day-to-day to P&L; efficiency or major replacement capitalised (T2.10).
6. After recognition: cost model or revaluation model (T2.10).

**Exam points**
- The standard is AS 10 (Revised).
- Every question is a recognise, measure, depreciate, or derecognise question.

**Stories:** S2 · **Where ICAI tests it:** nothing directly; the map above is used by every illustration.

## T2.2 · Scope of the Standard {#M2-C5-U2-T2.2}

**Concept**
- Apply AS 10 unless another standard requires or permits a different treatment.
- AS 19 (leases): recognition follows risks and rewards; depreciation and the rest still follow AS 10.
- **Not covered:** biological assets other than bearer plants; produce on bearer plants (the fruit on the tree); wasting assets (mineral rights, exploration and extraction of minerals, oil, natural gas).
- Bearer plants are covered; so is PPE used to develop or maintain these assets.
- Investment property (AS 13): cost model only.
- Spare parts, standby and servicing equipment: PPE if they meet the definition, else inventory (AS 2). Pranav: AS 10 gets first priority.

**Exam points**
- Bearer plant yes, its produce no.
- Produce is inventory (AS 2); intangibles are AS 26.

**Stories:** none (the notebook has a diagram, no story) · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 1 (AS 19, leased PPE); TYK MCQ 2, MCQ 7.

**Linkages**
- Spares and standby equipment: AS 2 excludes them from inventory when they meet the PPE definition, and AS 10 sends the rest to AS 2. See AS 2, Inventories (M2-C5-U1-T1.2); verified in both texts.
- Investment property is carried on AS 10's cost model: see AS 13, Investment Properties (M2-C5-U3-T3.7); verified in both texts.
- Leased PPE: depreciation of a finance-leased asset follows AS 10: see AS 19, Accounting for Finance Leases (M2-C5-U5-T5.8); verified in both texts.

## T2.3 · Definition of PPE {#M2-C5-U2-T2.3}

**Concept**
- Tangible items ("touch and feel").
- Condition 1: held for use in production or supply of goods or services, **or** for rental to others, **or** for administrative purposes.
- Condition 2: expected to be used for more than 12 months.
- "Administrative" means all business purposes: selling and distribution, finance and accounting, personnel and other functions.
- Assets bought for safety or the environment qualify: they let the entity earn from its other assets.
- Intangible items: AS 26.

**Exam algorithm: is it PPE?**
1. Tangible?
2. Held for production or supply, rental, or administrative use?
3. More than 12 months?
4. Yes to all three: then test recognition (T2.5).

**Exam points**
- Pranav also asks for a material value and a reliable cost (recognition, T2.5): do not mix them with the two conditions of the definition. ICAI's two conditions are held-for and used-for-more-than-12-months.
- A safety or environmental asset is PPE even though it adds no benefit by itself; test it for impairment under AS 28.

**Stories:** none · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 2 (chemical manufacturer).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Chhoo sako, 12 mahine se zyada chalega, business ke liye hai: PPE.

## T2.4 · Other definitions {#M2-C5-U2-T2.4}

**Concept**
- **Biological asset:** a living plant or animal.
- **Livestock** that meets the PPE definition is covered by AS 10 until an Agriculture standard is issued (ICAI). *Pranav's notebook crosses AS 10 out under "Animal": ICAI's text is followed here; the difference is flagged for him.*
- **Bearer plant** (all three): used to produce or supply agricultural produce; expected to bear produce for more than 12 months; remote likelihood of being sold as agricultural produce except as incidental scrap (firewood).
- **Not bearer plants:** annual crops (maize, wheat); trees grown for lumber; trees grown for both fruit and lumber (more than a remote chance of being sold).
- **Agricultural produce:** the harvested product of the entity's biological assets.
- **Agricultural activity:** management of biological transformation and harvest of biological assets, for sale, for conversion into produce, or into more biological assets.

**Exam algorithm: is it a bearer plant?**
1. Used to produce agricultural produce?
2. Bears for more than 12 months?
3. Remote chance of the plant itself being sold (scrap aside)?
4. All three yes: bearer plant, AS 10. Any no: not a bearer plant.

**Exam points**
- Scrap sale of an old bearer plant does not spoil the definition.
- Before it is ready to bear, a bearer plant is costed like a self-constructed asset (T2.8). Pranav: all cost up to ready to use, litchi tree is his example.

**Stories:** none · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 7 and the bearer-plant part of the scope.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Ped chalega par becha nahi jayega: bearer plant.

## T2.5 · Recognition criteria for PPE {#M2-C5-U2-T2.5}

**Concept**
- Recognise the cost as an asset **if, and only if:** (a) probable future economic benefit will flow to the entity, and (b) cost can be measured reliably.
- Recognition means: record it in the books as PPE (capitalise), not straight to P&L.
- **It happens twice:** initially (acquire or construct) and subsequently (add, replace, service). The criteria are checked every time a cost is incurred.
- Insignificant items (moulds, tools, dies) may be aggregated and tested together.
- An entity may expense an item that could have been PPE if it is not material.
- Spares, standby and servicing equipment: PPE if the definition is met, else inventory.

**Exam algorithm**
1. Benefit probable? (a reason the benefit will come)
2. Cost reliably measurable?
3. Both yes: capitalise. Either no: expense.
4. Repeat for every later cost.

**Exam points**
- The reason for the benefit must be stated in the answer.
- Recognise first, then measure.

**Stories:** S2, S1 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Illustration 1 (supermarket remodelling).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Faayda pakka aur kharcha naapa ja sake: tabhi PPE.

## T2.7 · Initial recognition {#M2-C5-U2-T2.7}

**Concept**
- Measure at **cost = (A) purchase price + (B) directly attributable cost + (C) initial estimate of decommissioning, restoration and similar liabilities (DRoL).**
- **A. Purchase price:** import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes included; trade discounts and rebates deducted. Pranav: refundable tax (input credit) is deducted; **cash discount is not deducted** *(ICAI is silent: see open questions)*.
- **B. Directly attributable:** any cost of bringing the asset to the location and condition in which it can operate as management intends. Examples: employee benefits arising directly (AS 15), site preparation, initial delivery and handling, installation and assembly, testing (less net proceeds of samples), professional fees.
- **Ready to use** is the line: capitalisation stops and depreciation starts, whether or not the asset is actually used.
- **Not in cost:** costs while the asset is capable but not yet used or runs below full capacity; initial operating losses; relocating or reorganising operations; inauguration; advertising for a new product; staff training for a new location or customer class; administration and general overheads.
- **Incidental operations** (a car park on the site before building starts): income and expense go to P&L. Income that is directly attributable (sale of debris on demolition, samples from testing) reduces cost.
- **C. DRoL:** initial estimate of dismantling, removing and restoring the site, owed on acquiring the item or by using it for a period other than to make inventory; recognised and measured under AS 29. Pranav: future costs are taken at present value. Costs of using the item to make inventory go to AS 2.

**Exam algorithm: cost to capitalise (purchase)**
1. Invoice + non-refundable duties and taxes − trade discount and rebates − refundable tax.
2. + directly attributable costs, only up to ready to use.
3. + initial DRoL (at present value).
4. Leave out: inauguration, advertising, training, overheads, relocation, start-up losses, below-capacity costs, interest on deferred credit (T2.9).
5. Testing: its cost in, its sale proceeds out.

**Exam points**
- "Incremental" is not "directly attributable".
- A trial run is capitalised only while it is needed to find out whether the asset is ready.
- Substance over form: if the asset is capable of operating as intended, further costs are expenses.

**Stories:** S1, S3, S5, S6 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 3 (car park income); Illustrations 2 (Entity A), 3 (Omega Ltd), 4 (Entity A, supermarket), 5 (amusement park); TYK MCQ 1, MCQ 8; Q14 (classification), Q15 (ABC Ltd), Q18 (Akshar Ltd), Q20 (Preet Ltd), Q21 (Star Limited).

**Linkages**
- DRoL is measured under AS 29 and is the one provision AS 29 allows to be discounted: see AS 29, Measurement: Best Estimate (M2-C6-U2-T2.11); verified in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Ready to use tak ka kharcha andar, uske baad ka kharcha bahar.

## T2.8 · Cost of a self-constructed asset {#M2-C5-U2-T2.8}

**Concept**
- Same principles as an acquired asset.
- Cost = material + labour + overheads (fixed and variable) + directly attributable cost + initial DRoL + borrowing cost if the asset is a qualifying asset.
- If the entity makes the same assets for sale, cost is the cost of making them for sale (AS 2); **internal profit is eliminated.**
- Abnormal wastage of material, labour or other resources is not included.
- Bearer plants: costed as self-constructed items until they are ready to bear.

**Exam algorithm**
1. Material + labour + production overheads.
2. + directly attributable.
3. + borrowing cost (qualifying asset only, AS 16).
4. + DRoL.
5. − abnormal wastage; − internal profit.

**Exam points**
- Own use at cost, not at selling price (Pranav: Hyundai making a Creta for itself).
- Interest is capitalised only for a qualifying asset and only by the AS 16 rules.

**Stories:** S3, S7, S1 · **Where ICAI tests it:** no illustration is built on it (checked); the principle is used in the cost items of Q18 and Q21.

**Linkages**
- Borrowing cost: a PPE item is a qualifying asset only if it takes a substantial period to get ready: see AS 16, Definitions (M2-C5-U4-T4.2); verified in both texts.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Apne liye banao toh cost, bechne ka bhav nahi.

## T2.9 · PPE acquired in exchange, and special cases of cost {#M2-C5-U2-T2.9}

**Concept: exchange**
- Cost of an item acquired for non-monetary asset(s), or for a mix of monetary and non-monetary assets: **fair value** unless (a) the exchange lacks commercial substance, or (b) the fair value of neither asset is reliably measurable.
- If not measured at fair value: **carrying amount of the asset given up.**
- **Commercial substance:** the cash flows (risk, timing, amount) or the entity-specific value of the part of operations affected change, and the difference is significant against the fair value of what is exchanged. Pranav: if the balance sheet position does not change, there is no commercial substance.
- Fair value is reliable if its range is not significant or the probabilities within it can be assessed. If either fair value is reliable, the given-up asset's fair value is used unless the received asset's is more clearly evident.

**Exam algorithm: order of preference**
1. Commercial substance? If no: carrying amount of the asset given up. Stop.
2. If yes: fair value of the asset given up, unless the received asset's is more reliable.
3. If the given-up value is not available: fair value of the asset received.
4. Only if both are unavailable (remote): carrying amount of the asset given up.
5. Cash received or paid adjusts the entry; the difference is gain or loss.

*Pranav: points 2 and 3 cover about 90% of questions; point 4 about 10%.*

**Concept: special cases of cost**
- **Deferred payment beyond normal credit terms:** total payment less the cash price equivalent is interest, spread over the credit period, unless capitalised under AS 16. Pranav: normal terms are the benchmark; a cash discount for early payment is not deducted from cost (Pranav; ICAI is silent); extra interest for longer credit is not added.
- **Consolidated price for several items:** split in the ratio of fair values on the acquisition date; if not reliably measurable, estimated by competent valuers.
- **Finance lease:** cost under AS 19.
- **Government grant:** carrying amount may be reduced by the grant under AS 12.

**Exam points**
- Write the rule first, then say whether the exchange has commercial substance, then which fair value you used and why.
- Without commercial substance there is no gain.

**Stories:** S4, S1 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Illustration 6 (Entity A, land for cash and machinery); Illustration 7 (Entity A, car X for car Y and cash).

**Linkages**
- An intangible acquired in exchange is costed on AS 10's principles: see AS 26, Exchange of assets (M2-C5-U6-T6.11); verified in both texts.
- Cost of a finance-leased asset: AS 19, Accounting for Finance Leases (M2-C5-U5-T5.8); verified in both texts.
- Grants against an asset: AS 12, Presentation of Grants Related to Specific Fixed Assets (M2-C9-U1-T1.6). AS 10 names AS 12; AS 12's own text speaks of fixed assets and does not name AS 10 (checked).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Substance hai toh fair value; substance nahi toh purani book value.

## T2.10 · Treatment of subsequent costs, measurement after recognition, depreciation, derecognition and disclosure {#M2-C5-U2-T2.10}

*ICAI's heading 2.10 covers everything from subsequent costs to disclosure, so this section is split into lettered parts for revision.*

**Concept, part (a): subsequent costs**
- **Day-to-day servicing** (labour, consumables, small parts; repairs and maintenance): P&L as incurred.
- **Replacement of parts** (furnace relining, aircraft seats, conveyor belts) and **regular major inspections:** capitalise if the recognition criteria are met; **derecognise the carrying amount of the replaced part or the previous inspection**, even if its cost was never separately identified.
- Pranav's three kinds: day-to-day (P&L); raises efficiency or life beyond the original assessment, e.g. remodelling (capitalise); major replacement or overhaul (capitalise, remove the old part).
- **Carrying amount of the old part:** supplier's invoice breakup; else fair value of the part at purchase; if not practicable, the cost of the replacement (or estimated cost of a similar future inspection) as an indication; less depreciation to date.

**Exam algorithm: major replacement**
1. Replacement or day-to-day? Day-to-day: P&L.
2. Carrying amount of the whole asset.
3. Carrying amount of the old part (invoice, fair value, or replacement cost as indication, less depreciation).
4. Remove the old part (loss to P&L).
5. Add the new part.
6. Depreciate the revised amount over the remaining life.

**Concept, part (b): measurement after recognition**
- **Cost model:** cost − accumulated depreciation − accumulated impairment losses.
- **Revaluation model** (fair value reliably measurable): fair value at the revaluation date − later depreciation − later impairment.
- Choose per **class**, apply to the whole class. Class = group of similar nature and use (land; land and buildings; machinery; ships; aircraft; motor vehicles; furniture and fixtures; office equipment; bearer plants). Pranav: you can pick a model per class, never pick and choose inside a class.
- If one item is revalued, revalue the whole class, at a similar date (Pranav: all classes at year end, 31 March), so cost and value at different dates are not mixed.
- **Frequency:** regular enough that carrying amount does not differ materially from fair value; volatile items yearly; stable items every 3 or 5 years.
- **Fair value:** market evidence, usually by professional valuers; if none (specialised, rarely sold): income approach (discounted cash flows) or depreciated replacement cost. Pranav: DCF is rarely practical; depreciated replacement cost = price of a new one today less the depreciation for its age.

**Concept, part (c): accounting for a revaluation**
- **Technique 1:** restate gross carrying amount (market data, or in proportion to the change in carrying amount); accumulated depreciation becomes gross minus carrying amount (after impairment).
- **Technique 2:** eliminate accumulated depreciation against the gross amount; restate the asset to fair value.
- **Increase:** credit to revaluation surplus (equity); **exception:** if it reverses an earlier decrease of the same asset charged to P&L, credit P&L to that extent.
- **Decrease:** charge to P&L; **exception:** debit the revaluation surplus to the extent of its credit balance for that asset.
- **Surplus:** may be moved to revenue reserves (retained earnings) when the asset is derecognised (whole), or as the asset is used (depreciation on the revalued amount − depreciation on original cost). **Never through P&L.**

**Exam algorithm: revaluation up or down**
1. Carrying amount before; fair value now.
2. Increase: first make good any earlier loss of that asset in P&L; the rest to revaluation surplus.
3. Decrease: first use the surplus of that asset; the rest to P&L.
4. Depreciate the revalued amount over the remaining life.
5. On sale: gain or loss against the carrying amount; surplus to retained earnings separately.

**Concept, part (d): depreciation**
- **Components:** each part with a cost significant to the total is depreciated separately; parts with the same life and method may be grouped. Land with DRoL: the DRoL part is its own component. Aircraft: airframe and engines separately.
- **Charge** goes to P&L unless it is included in another asset's carrying amount (AS 2 conversion cost; AS 26 development cost).
- **Depreciable amount** = cost (or revalued amount) − residual value, spread systematically over the **useful life** (the period the entity expects to use it, or units of production).
- **Residual value:** what the entity would get today from disposal, net of disposal cost, if the asset were already as old and worn as expected at the end of its life.
- **Useful life factors (all four):** expected usage; physical wear and tear; technical or commercial obsolescence; legal or similar limits (lease expiry).
- **Review** residual value, useful life and method at least every year end. A change is a **change in accounting estimate (AS 5): prospective** over the carrying amount and remaining life.
- **Starts** when the asset is available for use, not when it is first used (Pranav: ready to use).
- **Stops** when (1) residual value reaches the carrying amount (zero charge until it falls below), or (2) at the earlier of retired from active use **and** held for disposal, or derecognised. An idle asset keeps depreciating unless fully depreciated (under a units method the charge can be zero).
- **Land** is not depreciated (quarries and landfill sites are); a **building** is, whatever happens to the value of the land under it. Land and building are separate assets even if bought together.
- **Method:** the pattern of consumption: straight line, diminishing balance, units of production; apply consistently. **A revenue-based method is not appropriate.**
- Depreciation continues even if fair value is above carrying amount, and repairs do not remove the need to charge it.

**Exam algorithm: change in estimate**
1. Carrying amount today.
2. Revised remaining life and residual value.
3. (Carrying amount − residual value) ÷ remaining life = new charge. No restatement of the past.

**Concept, part (e): changes in DRoL**
- Changes in the liability, price adjustments, duties, or revised estimates of dismantling and restoration change the cost of the asset.
- **Cost model:** add to or deduct from cost in the current period; a deduction cannot exceed the carrying amount (excess to P&L); an addition triggers a check for impairment (AS 28).
- **Revaluation model:** a decrease goes to revaluation surplus (exception: to P&L to the extent it reverses a deficit charged earlier; excess over what the cost-model carrying amount would have been goes to P&L); an increase goes to P&L (exception: debit revaluation surplus to the extent of its credit balance for that asset).
- At the end of the useful life, all later changes go to P&L. A change may also show that the asset needs revaluing.
- Depreciate the adjusted depreciable amount over the remaining life.

**Concept, part (f): impairment, compensation, retirement**
- Impairment of an item: AS 28. Derecognition: AS 10. Compensation from a third party for an impaired, lost or given-up item: to P&L when it becomes receivable. Cost of a replacement item: AS 10.
- **Retired from active use and held for disposal:** carry at the lower of carrying amount and net realisable value; the write-down goes to P&L at once.

**Concept, part (g): derecognition**
- On disposal (sale, finance lease, donation) or when no future benefit is expected from use or disposal.
- **Gain or loss = net disposal proceeds − carrying amount,** to P&L (sale and leaseback: AS 19). **Not revenue** (AS 9).
- **Exception:** an entity that routinely sells items it held for rental: move them to inventory at carrying amount when they stop being rented; the sale proceeds are revenue (AS 9).
- Date of disposal: the AS 9 test for sale of goods.

**Concept, part (h): disclosure** *(Pranav: four heads)*
- **General** (for each class): measurement basis; depreciation method; useful lives or rates (say so if different from the statute); gross carrying amount and accumulated depreciation at start and end; reconciliation of the carrying amount (additions, assets held for disposal, revaluations and impairments, depreciation, other).
- **Additional:** title restrictions and pledges; expenditure during construction; contractual commitments; compensation in P&L (if not shown on the face); assets retired and held for disposal.
- **Revalued assets:** effective date; independent valuer or not; methods and assumptions; how far fair value came from observable prices; the revaluation surplus and any restriction on distribution.
- **Voluntary:** temporarily idle assets; fully depreciated assets still in use; the carrying amount under the cost model for each revalued class; assets retired and not held for disposal.

**Exam points**
- Day-to-day repair: P&L. Major part: capitalise and derecognise the old part.
- Revaluation: asset by asset, loss against that asset's own surplus first; surplus out through reserves, never P&L.
- Change in method or life: estimate, prospective; do not restate.
- Ready to use starts depreciation; first use does not.
- Land: no depreciation. Building: yes, always.
- ICAI prints "a change in method" and "a change in life" under the same AS 5 rule: do not call either a change in policy.

**Stories:** S1, S2, S3, S8, S9, S10, S11, S12 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 4 (aircraft components), Example 5 (NS Limited, residual value); Illustrations 8 and 9 (replaced part), 10 (Entity A, class by class), 11 (depreciation policy), 12 (change of life), 13 (Entity B, machine ready), 14 (residual value), 15 (Entity B, depreciation method), 16 (insured assets); TYK MCQ 3, 4, 5, 6; Theory Q9 to Q13; Q16 (Arka Ltd), Q17 (Skanda Ltd), Q18 (Akshar Ltd), Q19 (Bharat Infrastructure Ltd).

**Linkages**

- Impairment of the asset and a reversal that would push the carrying amount above the cost-model amount (that part is a revaluation, governed by AS 10): see AS 28, Recognition and Measurement of an Impairment Loss (M2-C5-U7-T7.7) and Reversal of an Impairment Loss for an Individual Asset (M2-C5-U7-T7.14); verified in both texts.

- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: charge 10% of the original cost, or spread the revalued amount over 10 years. Right: revalued amount ÷ the 6 remaining years. Why: depreciation follows the revalued amount and the remaining life.

### TYK-Q17 · TYK Q17, Skanda Ltd

**Goal:** treatment on selling a revalued machine, at two sale prices.
**Concepts:** revaluation surplus [T2.10]; depreciation on the revalued amount over the balance life [T2.10]; gain or loss against carrying amount [T2.10]; surplus to retained earnings [T2.10]

### TYK-Q18 · TYK Q18, Akshar Ltd
**Difficulty:** Hard (computed from 8 concepts)
**Goal:** account for a plant with a separate motors component through replacement, revaluation and disposal.
**Concepts:** cost with dismantling at present value [T2.7]; interest and start-up losses excluded [T2.7]; motors as a separate component [T2.10]; replaced motors derecognised [T2.10]; new motors capitalised with their own life [T2.10]; revaluation of the plant excluding motors [T2.10]; surplus to retained earnings [T2.10]; disposal proceeds split by carrying amount [T2.10]
**Given:** one purchase price for the plant, motors identified as a part with a shorter life; motors replaced in year 4; whole class revalued; plant retired and sold as one in year 8.
**Approach:** cost; split off the motors in the ratio of their purchase price; derecognise the old motors; capitalise the new; revalue the rest; depreciate; at disposal split proceeds in the ratio of carrying amounts.
**Exam points:** total cost ₹33,80,000; motors ₹5,63,333; revaluation surplus ₹2,10,000; losses on the two parts about ₹24,425 and ₹1,28,909 (ICAI rounds the second to ₹1,28,907). Splitting proceeds by carrying amount is ICAI's preferred method.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register:** Wrong: discount nothing and use the undiscounted dismantling estimate. Right: use the present value the question gives. Why: DRoL is measured at present value.
- **Error Register:** Wrong: revalue the plant including the new motors. Right: revalue the plant excluding the motors (the question's total less the new motors). Why: the new motors are at cost and a separate component.

### TYK-Q19 · TYK Q19, Bharat Infrastructure Ltd

### TYK-Q21 · TYK Q21, Star Limited
**Difficulty:** Hard (computed from 7 concepts)
**Goal:** the value at which a machine is capitalised.
**Concepts:** purchase price less input credit [T2.7]; site preparation [T2.7]; labour apportioned by hours [T2.7]; supervisor and technical cost apportioned [T2.7]; test run and architect [T2.7]; depreciation on assets used in installation [T2.7]; delay costs after ready to use [T2.7]
**Given:** GST included in the price with full credit; labour and supervision shared between installation and other work; test run; delay between ready and first use.
**Approach:** apportion shared costs by the stated share; add everything up to the ready date; subtract the input credit; costs after the ready date go to P&L.
**Exam points:** ₹7,39,500 (recomputed); the ₹8,900 delay costs after the ready date are P&L.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: capitalise the delay costs, or leave the GST credit in cost. Right: delay costs to P&L; capitalise the price net of the credit. Why: ready date ends capitalisation; refundable tax is not a cost.
