## T1.1 · Introduction {#M2-C5-U1-T1.1}

**Concept**
- AS 2 (Revised) = the value at which inventories are carried in the financial statements, until the related revenue is recognised.
- Three jobs: the carrying value; the cost formulas that assign cost to units; the write-down to net realisable value.
- Pranav's frame for the unit: Recognition, Measurement, Presentation, Disclosure.

**Exam points**
- Cost stays in inventory (an asset) until the sale; then it becomes cost of goods sold.

**Stories:** S1 · **Where ICAI tests it:** nothing directly.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Jab tak bika nahi, cost inventory mein; bik gaya toh kharcha.

## T1.2 · Inventories {#M2-C5-U1-T1.2}

**Concept**
- Inventories are assets held (i) for sale in the ordinary course, (ii) in production for such sale, (iii) for consumption in production of goods or services for sale.
- Include materials, maintenance supplies, consumables, loose tools, goods bought for resale, finished goods, WIP.
- Exclude spare parts, servicing equipment and standby equipment that meet the PPE definition (AS 10).
- **Out of AS 2:** (a) WIP of construction contracts (AS 7); (b) WIP of service providers; (c) shares, debentures and other financial instruments held as stock-in-trade; (d) producers' inventories of livestock, agricultural and forest products, minerals, ores and gases measured at NRV by established practice.
- Pranav: the same item can be in AS 2 for a trader and out of it for a producer.

**Exam algorithm: is it in AS 2?**
1. Is it held for sale, in production for sale, or for consumption in production?
2. Spare, servicing or standby item that meets the PPE definition: AS 10.
3. WIP of a construction contract: AS 7. WIP of a service provider: outside.
4. Shares and debentures held as stock-in-trade: outside. Producer's farm, forest or mine output at NRV: outside.
5. Otherwise: AS 2.

**Exam points**
- Inventory held for use in construction (cement lying at the site) stays in AS 2; only the WIP of the contract is AS 7.
- Shares held as stock-in-trade are outside AS 13 as well; Indian practice values them at the lower of cost and fair value.
- TYK MCQ 1: delete the not-covered options first (Pranav's MCQ rule).
- An item held for sale in the ordinary course with a controlled record procedure is inventory, not scrap (TYK Q13).

**Stories:** S1, S2 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 1, TYK Q13.

**Linkages**
- Spares and standby items that meet the PPE definition go to AS 10, those that do not are inventory: see AS 10, Recognition Criteria for PPE (M2-C5-U2-T2.5). Both texts say it.
- Shares held as stock-in-trade are outside AS 13 as well: see AS 13, Introduction and Definitions (M2-C5-U3-T3.1, M2-C5-U3-T3.2).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Bechne ke liye, banane mein, ya banane mein khapane ke liye: teen mein se ek ho toh inventory.

## T1.3 · Measurement of inventories {#M2-C5-U1-T1.3}

**Concept**
- Value at the **lower of cost and NRV**.
- NRV = estimated selling price in the ordinary course less estimated cost of completion less estimated cost necessary to make the sale.
- Reason: no asset should be carried above what its sale or use will realise. If NRV is below cost, write down to NRV. If NRV is above cost, the profit is ignored and inventory stays at cost.
- ICAI's chart: raw materials at cost if the finished goods are sold at or above cost, otherwise at replacement cost; finished goods and WIP at the lower of cost and NRV.
- Pranav: "lower of cost or NRV for all three? No!" Raw material has its own test (T1.14).

**Exam algorithm: value one item**
1. Which kind: raw material and stores, WIP or finished goods?
2. Raw material: run the finished-product test first (T1.14).
3. WIP and finished goods: find cost (T1.4 to T1.9), then NRV (T1.12).
4. Value = lower; compare item by item (T1.13).
5. Present in a short table with working notes (Pranav: presentation is equally important).

**Exam points**
- Selling price is not NRV: selling cost comes off.
- NRV of WIP = NRV of finished goods less the cost still to be incurred to complete it.
- ICAI's reasoning (in its introduction to the unit): higher closing inventory means lower cost of goods sold and higher profit, so prudence says do not carry inventory above NRV.

**Stories:** S1, S5, S6 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Examples 1 and 4; Illustrations 1 and 2; TYK Q11, Q12, Q15.

**Linkages**
- The Framework says the same: inventory is recorded at historical cost when acquired and at the year end at the lower of cost and NRV: see Framework, Measurement of Elements (M1-C2-U0-T10).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Cost aur NRV mein jo kam, wahi value: profit ka sapna nahi, loss ka dar.

## T1.4 · Costs of inventory {#M2-C5-U1-T1.4}

**Concept**
- Cost = costs of purchase + costs of conversion + other costs of bringing the inventory to its **present location and condition**.
- Pranav: location (kahan par) and condition (kis halat mein). Anything that does neither is not cost.
- Costs to maintain the inventory (storage) give no extra benefit: not cost, unless production needs them before a further stage.

**Exam algorithm: is this item in cost?**
1. Does it bring the item to where it is (carriage inward)? In.
2. Does it bring it to the state it is in (conversion, packing)? In.
3. Neither (storage, selling, administration)? Profit and Loss.
4. Raw material carries only purchase cost; WIP carries conversion up to its stage; finished goods carry everything (Pranav's stage bar: 0%, 50%, 100%).

**Exam points**
- The same costing rule applies to every stage; only the stage differs.

**Stories:** S3 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 3.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Kahan aur kis halat: jo in dono mein nahi, woh cost nahi.

## T1.6 · Costs of conversion {#M2-C5-U1-T1.6}

**Concept**
- Direct labour + overheads, fixed and variable, incurred converting raw material into finished goods.
- Variable overheads: on the actual use of production facilities.
- **Fixed production overheads:** absorbed on **normal capacity**: the average production over several periods under normal circumstances, allowing for planned maintenance.
- Actual production may be used if it approximates normal capacity.
- Low production or idle plant: the overhead per unit is **not** increased; the unallocated overhead is an expense of the period.
- Abnormally high production: the overhead per unit is decreased so that inventories are not above cost.

**Exam algorithm: fixed overhead per unit**
1. Normal capacity: the days or units the plant normally runs (Pranav: 365 − 52 Sundays − 10 holidays − 3 maintenance days = 300 days).
2. Compare actual production with normal capacity.
3. Actual below or about equal to normal: total fixed overhead ÷ normal capacity; the unabsorbed part to Profit and Loss.
4. Actual above normal: total fixed overhead ÷ actual production.
5. Pranav's one line: divide by the **higher** of actual or normal.
6. Closing units × rate = overhead carried in inventory.

**Exam points**
- **Golden Mantra (Pranav): fixed overhead allocation can never be above cost.**
- A normal loss (Sundays, holidays, planned maintenance) is absorbed by inventory. A loss that is not normal (a strike) does not raise the cost per unit: it goes to Profit and Loss.
- Pranav's line: normal is the "bare minimum"; if production is more, use the larger figure.
- In Accounts only the basics are needed; costing goes deeper (Pranav).

**Stories:** S3, S8 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 2 (ABC Ltd); Illustration 4; TYK Q15 and Q16.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Kam bana toh normal pe baanto, zyada bana toh actual pe, cost se upar kabhi nahi.

## T1.7 · Joint or by-products {#M2-C5-U1-T1.7}

**Concept**
- Costs up to the split-off point are allocated on a **rational and consistent basis**, e.g. sale value at split-off.
- By-products, scrap and waste are usually not material: valued at **NRV**. The main product's cost = joint cost minus the NRV of by-products, scrap and waste.
- Pranav's definitions: joint product = one raw material, two or more main products; by-product = one main product, with waste generated that is sold as scrap. (His wording, not ICAI's.)

**Exam algorithm: joint cost to closing stock**
1. Total cost up to the split-off point (material, wages, fixed and variable overheads).
2. Less NRV of the by-product (selling price less separate processing and packing) and NRV of scrap and waste.
3. The balance is the net joint cost.
4. Sale value of each main product = output units × selling price.
5. Split the net joint cost in the ratio of sale values.
6. Cost per unit = allocated cost ÷ output units; closing units at the lower of cost and NRV.

**Exam points**
- Use **output units** for sale value, not units sold or closing units (Pranav: "sale units nahi lena").
- The profit on the by-product is noise; ICAI uses its NRV.
- Present with working notes: NRV of the by-product, the joint cost, then the allocation.

**Stories:** S9 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK Q14.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Pehle by-product ka NRV ghatao, phir bacha cost sale value ke ratio mein baanto.

## T1.8 · Other costs {#M2-C5-U1-T1.8}

**Concept**
- Other costs are included only if incurred to bring the inventory to its present location and condition (design cost of a custom-made unit).
- Borrowing costs: usually not cost; included where the inventory necessarily takes a **substantial period** to get ready for sale (ICAI's example: wine).
- Amortisation of intangibles: the standard is silent; ICAI says it appears that amortisation of production-related intangibles (patent right of production, publisher's copyright) belongs in cost.
- Exchange differences: not in inventory cost.

**Exam algorithm: borrowing cost on inventory**
1. Is the inventory routinely made in large quantities over a short time? Expense it.
2. Does it necessarily take a substantial period to be ready for sale? Include it (see the AS 16 link).

**Exam points**
- ICAI's amortisation point is its reading ("it nevertheless appears"), not a rule of the standard.
- Pranav: a corporate custom design: the extra design cost goes into inventory cost.

**Stories:** S4 · **Where ICAI tests it:** nothing directly.

**Linkages**
- AS 16 lists inventories that need a substantial period to reach saleable condition as qualifying assets, and routinely manufactured inventories as not: see AS 16, Definitions (M2-C5-U4-T4.2).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Byaaj aur exchange difference bahar, design ka kharcha andar, wine jaise maal mein byaaj bhi andar.

## T1.9 · Exclusions from the cost of inventories {#M2-C5-U1-T1.9}

**Concept**
- Expensed in the period: (a) abnormal wastage of materials, labour and other production costs; (b) storage costs, unless production needs them before a further stage; (c) administrative overheads that do not contribute to bringing inventory to its present location and condition; (d) selling and distribution costs.
- Pranav's selling and distribution list: warehouse guard, reels, influencers, website, courier, admin office.

**Exam algorithm: normal and abnormal wastage**
1. Normal wastage = normal % × input.
2. Cost per unit = total cost ÷ (input − normal wastage).
3. Abnormal wastage = actual wastage − normal wastage; abnormal units × the same rate go to Profit and Loss.
4. Stock = good units × that rate.

**Exam points**
- Fixed production overhead absorbed on normal capacity is **not** excluded (TYK MCQ 3: the "except" option).
- Storage needed in production before a further stage is included.
- TYK MCQ 4: the key picks (d) "always included" as the incorrect statement. (b) "never included" is also loose, because ICAI excludes only the overheads that do not contribute (my reading; ICAI does not say it). Follow the key.
- Normal wastage is a cost of the good output.

**Stories:** S3 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Illustration 3; TYK MCQ 3, 4, 7; TYK Q9, Q10.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Faltu waste aur bechne ka kharcha P&L mein, banane tak ka kharcha stock mein.

## T1.10 · Cost formula {#M2-C5-U1-T1.10}

**Concept**
- **Specific identification** where possible; otherwise **FIFO** or **weighted average**.
- The formula should give the fairest approximation of the cost incurred.
- Specific identification suits items not ordinarily interchangeable and goods produced and segregated for specific projects. For a large number of interchangeable items it can be used to manipulate profit (ICAI's TYK MCQ 6 answer).
- ICAI lists FIFO and weighted average only. Pranav: LIFO not allowed.

**Exam algorithm: which formula**
1. Each unit different and identifiable (Rolls Royce)? Specific identification.
2. Interchangeable units from different lots (naphthalene packets)? FIFO or weighted average.
3. Cost not known at all? Techniques in T1.11.

**Exam points**
- The formula used is a disclosure item (T1.15).
- Weighted average over opening stock and purchases is used in TYK Q15.

**Stories:** S7, S10 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 6; the weighted average step in TYK Q15.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Gaadi alag toh pehchaan lo, packet ek jaise toh FIFO ya average.

## T1.11 · Other techniques of cost measurement {#M2-C5-U1-T1.11}

**Concept**
- (a) **Standard cost**, if standards fairly approximate actual, set at normal levels of material, labour efficiency and capacity use, and reviewed regularly.
- (b) **Retail method:** for many rapidly changing items with similar margins; cost = sale value of unsold stock reduced by the average gross margin percentage.
- Pranav: both are shortcuts when you do not know the cost ("mereko cost hi nahi pata").

**Exam algorithm: retail method with a mark-up on cost**
1. Sale value of opening stock plus purchases = their cost × (1 + mark-up).
2. Less sales = sale value of unsold stock.
3. Cost of unsold stock = that value ÷ (1 + mark-up).
4. Cross-check: closing stock = opening + purchases − (sales ÷ (1 + mark-up)).

**Exam points**
- ICAI's note: margin is on sales, mark-up is on cost.
- The retail method may be used for interim reporting; TYK MCQ 8's key says so.
- The method works because gross margins are similar across the items.

**Stories:** S10 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 3; TYK MCQ 8.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Mark-up cost pe, margin sale pe: 125 se bhaag, 25% mat ghatao.

## T1.12 · Estimates of net realisable value {#M2-C5-U1-T1.12}

**Concept**
- Estimate on the most reliable evidence available when the estimate is made.
- Include price or cost changes after the balance sheet date only to the extent that they confirm the conditions at the balance sheet date.

**Exam algorithm: NRV**
1. Start from the expected selling price in the ordinary course.
2. Less estimated costs of completion (WIP).
3. Less costs necessary to make the sale (commission, brokerage).
4. If the cost to complete and sell is more than the price, the NRV is nil (Pranav's broken phone: repair 6,000 against price 4,500).

**Exam points**
- Commission or brokerage on the selling price is a deduction (4% of 250 is 10).
- Reassess at each balance sheet date.

**Stories:** S5 · **Where ICAI tests it:** Example 1; Illustration 2; TYK Q12.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Bikri ka daam, minus poora karne ka kharcha, minus bechne ka kharcha: yeh NRV.

## T1.13 · Comparison of cost and net realisable value {#M2-C5-U1-T1.13}

**Concept**
- Compare **item by item**.
- Sometimes it is appropriate to group similar or related items.

**Exam points**

## T1.14 · NRV of materials held for use or disposal {#M2-C5-U1-T1.14}

**Concept**
- Materials and supplies held for production are **not written down below cost** if the finished product they go into is expected to sell at or above cost.
- If the price of materials has fallen and the finished product's cost will exceed its NRV, the materials are written down to NRV; ICAI's answers use **replacement cost** as the best measure.
- NRV is assessed at each balance sheet date.

**Exam algorithm: raw material test**
1. Estimate the finished product's cost and its selling price.
2. Selling price at or above cost: raw material at cost; ignore replacement cost.
3. Selling price below cost: raw material at the lower of cost and replacement cost.
4. Repeat at each balance sheet date.

**Exam points**
- Pranav: replacement cost only for raw material, stores and spares, consumables. Raw material value = lower of actual cost or replacement cost, and only when the finished product sells below cost.
- ICAI is inconsistent in wording: its text says the selling price "exceeds" the cost, TYK MCQ 2 and MCQ 5 say "at or above". Follow the key in MCQs. Pranav writes "at or above" and circled "above" on his flowchart.
- A fall in replacement cost alone is not a write-down trigger.

**Stories:** S5, S6 · **Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 2, MCQ 5; TYK Q11, Q15 and Q16.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Final maal cost se neeche bike tab hi raw material ka aaj ka bhaav dekho.

## T1.15 · Disclosures {#M2-C5-U1-T1.15}

**Concept**
- Disclose: (a) the accounting policies for measuring inventories, including the cost formula; (b) the total carrying amount, classified in the way suited to the enterprise.
- Common classes: raw materials and components; work in progress; finished goods; stock-in-trade (goods bought for trading); stores and spares; loose tools; others (specify the nature).
- Pranav: manufacturer uses the first three, a trader uses stock-in-trade; "others" can hold stock in transit.

**Exam points**
- Part I of Schedule III requires the valuation method to be disclosed.
- ICAI places Illustrations 1 to 4 after this topic; they test measurement (T1.3, T1.6, T1.9, T1.13), not disclosure.
- Pranav's tip: FIFO or weighted average is disclosed; LIFO is not a choice.

**Stories:** S1 · **Where ICAI tests it:** nothing directly.

### EX-1 · Example 1, a partly finished unit
**Difficulty:** Medium (computed from 3 concepts)
**Goal:** value of the partly finished unit at year end.
**Concepts:** NRV of work in progress [T1.12]; lower of cost and NRV [T1.3]; brokerage on selling price [T1.12]
**Given:** cost so far, further cost to finish, expected selling price of the finished unit and brokerage as a percentage of selling price; the unit cannot be sold as it is.
**Approach:** NRV of the finished unit (selling price less brokerage), then less the cost to complete gives the NRV of the unit; compare with cost.
**Exam points:** NRV of WIP is NRV of finished goods less cost to complete; present as a short valuation table (Pranav's exam format).
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: take the selling price (250) as the NRV, or say the NRV is nil because the unit cannot be sold as it is. Right: 250 less 4% brokerage less 100 to complete is 140; the value is 140. Why: the NRV of WIP is what it will earn after completion and sale.

### EX-2 · Example 2, ABC Ltd
**Difficulty:** Medium (computed from 4 concepts)
**Goal:** the fixed overhead carried in inventory in three production cases.
**Concepts:** normal capacity [T1.6]; fixed overhead rate on normal capacity [T1.6]; unabsorbed overhead to Profit and Loss [T1.6]; high production on actual basis [T1.6]
**Given:** plant capacity 1 lakh units, fixed overhead 18 lakhs; production of 1 lakh, 90,000 and 1.2 lakh units.
**Approach:** use the normal-capacity rate when production is equal or lower; when production is higher, spread the same 18 lakhs on actual units.
**Exam points:** low production: 16.2 lakhs absorbed, 1.8 lakhs to Profit and Loss. High production: 15 per unit, total stays 18 lakhs. Pranav's rule: divide by the higher of actual or normal.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: divide 18 lakhs by actual production of 90,000 (20 per unit). Right: use normal capacity (18 per unit); the 1.8 lakhs loss goes to Profit and Loss. Why: low production or idle plant must not raise the cost per unit.
- **Error Register:** Wrong: keep 18 per unit when production is 1.2 lakh units. Right: 15 per unit. Why: otherwise inventory would carry 21.6 lakhs against 18 lakhs actually spent.

### EX-3 · Example 3, the retail method
**Difficulty:** Medium (computed from 3 concepts)
**Goal:** cost of closing stock.
**Concepts:** retail method [T1.11]; mark-up on cost [T1.11]; cost of goods sold from sales [T1.11]
**Given:** opening stock and purchases at cost, sales, an average mark-up of 25% on cost.
**Approach:** bring sales down to cost (÷ 1.25) and deduct from opening stock plus purchases; or reduce the sale value of unsold stock by the mark-up. Both give 16,000.
**Exam points:** mark-up is on cost, margin is on sales (ICAI's note). Closing stock = opening + purchases − cost of goods sold, never minus sales.
**Tricky points:**
