## T1 · Meaning of reconstruction {#M3-C14-U0-T1}

**In plain words.** A company that has lost money for years shows assets it no longer really has (fictitious assets, unrepresented intangibles) and a debit balance in the Profit and Loss account. Its capital is bigger than its real worth, so it is over-capitalised. Reconstruction reorganises the company by revaluing assets, reassessing liabilities, writing off losses, reducing the paid-up value of shares and/or varying the rights of share classes, so the company can carry on without being wound up.

**Two types.**
- **Internal:** the same company continues; capital and debt are changed; outside claimants may sacrifice; done under Sections 61 and 66; needs the court or tribunal's confirmation.
- **External:** the old company is liquidated and a new one formed whose shareholders are substantially the same; no reduction of capital; under Section 232; ICAI calls it amalgamation in the nature of merger (AS 14).

**Why.** *Why not just liquidate?* ICAI says the object is to bail the company out *without winding up*. ICAI does not argue the commercial reason beyond this.

**Exam points**
- Decide the type from two facts: is the existing company liquidated, and does capital reduce? Not liquidated and capital reduces → internal.
- Internal needs Sections 61 and 66; external is Section 232 and AS 14.

**Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 (all identify the type or the meaning).

**Linkages**
- ICAI Chapter 14 says external reconstruction is covered under amalgamation in the nature of merger: see AS 14, Amalgamation in the Nature of Merger (M2-C9-U2-T2.4), and Chapter 13 (M3-C13-U0). AS 14's own text does not use the word reconstruction (checked), so this connection is stated only in Chapter 14.

**Remember** *(agent draft, Hinglish)*: Internal mein company wahi rehti hai; external mein purani band, nayi shuru.

## T2 · Methods of internal reconstruction {#M3-C14-U0-T2}

**In plain words.** Five methods, often used together: alteration of share capital, variation of shareholders' rights, reduction of share capital, compromise or arrangement, and surrender of shares.

**Exam algorithm: which methods need a Capital Reduction (Reconstruction) A/c?**
1. Alteration of share capital → no.
2. Variation of shareholders' rights → no.
3. Reduction of share capital, compromise with outsiders, surrender of shares (and unrepresented reserves) → **yes**: the sacrifice or write-off passes through it.

**Why.** *Why no account for the first two?* Nothing is lost: only the label or the rights change, so there is no sacrifice to record.

**Exam points**
- The theory answer is the list of five methods; ICAI's own answer lists sub-division/consolidation and stock conversion together as the first.
- Which methods open the account is a favourite check.

**Where ICAI tests it:** TYK Theory Q11 (list the methods).

**Linkages:** the five methods are taken one by one in T3 to T7; the entries are in T8.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Pehle do tareeke mein paisa wahi; baaki teen mein sacrifice hota hai.

## T3 · Alteration of share capital {#M3-C14-U0-T3}

**In plain words.** Under Section 61 a company can (a) increase authorised capital, (b) consolidate or sub-divide shares, (c) convert fully paid shares into stock and back, and (d) cancel shares nobody has taken. Sub-division and consolidation keep the proportion of paid-up to unpaid the same, so total capital never changes; only the face value and the number of shares do. **Stock** is fully paid shares bundled together so any amount can be transferred; it has no share count.

**Exam algorithm: sub-division, consolidation, stock**
1. New face value and new number of shares from the ratio given (recount; do not carry the old count).
2. Paid-up per new share keeps the same proportion of face value.
3. Entry: old class account debited, new class account credited, for the **paid-up (called-up) amount only**.
4. Show authorised and issued capital in the notes under the new description.
5. Stock: only **fully paid** shares convert; show stock without a share count; recount shares when re-converting.

**Why.** *Why only the paid-up amount?* Uncalled and unissued capital is not in the books, so there is nothing to relabel there (ICAI note under Illustration 1).

**Exam points**
- No Capital Reduction A/c for any alteration.
- Some accountants pass no entry at all because the amount is unchanged; when an entry is passed it is on the called-up part only.

**Where ICAI tests it:** Illustration 1 (B Ltd), Illustration 2 (C Ltd); sub-division is the first step in Illustration 7 (Revise Ltd) and TYK Q17 (Z Ltd); conversion of partly paid into larger fully paid shares appears in TYK Q14 (Green Ltd).

**Linkages**
- Alteration (Section 61) is the opposite of reduction (Section 66, T5): alteration loses nothing, reduction writes off a loss.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Sirf label badla, paisa wahi.

## T4 · Variation of shareholders' rights {#M3-C14-U0-T4}

**In plain words.** Under Section 48 a company with different classes of shares can change the rights attached to a class (the dividend rate, cumulative to non-cumulative) without changing the amount of that capital. The entry debits the old description and credits the new one.

**Why.** *Why no Reconstruction A/c?* The amount is unchanged, so no one has sacrificed anything.

**Exam points**
- Only the description changes (for example, a lower preference dividend rate); the amount stays.
- Preference shareholders usually vary rights as part of a larger scheme (together with arrears and conversion), so this method rarely appears alone.

**Where ICAI tests it:** no illustration is about variation alone; it appears inside schemes where preference shareholders accept new securities (for example Illustrations 4 and 6).

**Linkages:** see T6 for how arrears of preference dividend are settled in a scheme.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Rights badle, amount nahi; isliye Reconstruction A/c nahi.

## T5 · Reduction of share capital {#M3-C14-U0-T5}

**In plain words.** Under Section 66, with a special resolution and confirmation by the tribunal, a company may reduce its share capital in three ways: (a) extinguish or reduce the shareholders' liability on unpaid amounts, (b) pay off paid-up capital that is more than the company needs, or (c) cancel paid-up capital that is lost or unrepresented by assets. Only (c) is a sacrifice, credited to the Capital Reduction (Reconstruction) A/c. Under (c) either the paid-up value alone is reduced (face value unchanged) or both nominal and paid-up values are reduced.

**Exam algorithm: reduction**
1. Identify which of the three modes the question describes.
2. Mode (a): relabel the partly paid shares as fully paid at the lower amount.
3. Mode (b): old share capital Dr; new share capital Cr; the amount paid back Cr to a Sundry Shareholders A/c, then paid by bank.
4. Mode (c): old share capital Dr; new share capital Cr for what remains; the difference Cr to Capital Reduction A/c.

**Why.** *Why a separate account for the sacrifice?* The write-offs of losses and overvalued assets are collected against it, so one account shows whether the scheme balances (T8).

**Exam points**
- Sacrifice to Capital Reduction A/c arises only in mode (c); mode (b) is the one that uses the Sundry Shareholders A/c.
- ICAI's text says confirmation by the **Tribunal**, but the answer key to TYK MCQ 5 says **Court**. ICAI is inconsistent here; in the MCQ choose the keyed answer.

**Where ICAI tests it:** TYK MCQ 5 (who permits it) and MCQ 8 (refund of excess paid-up capital); reductions inside Illustrations 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8.

**Linkages:** see T8 for the closing balance of the account.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Teen mode: unpaid maaf, zyada paisa wapas, ya lost capital cancel; sacrifice sirf teesre mein.

## T6 · Compromise and arrangements {#M3-C14-U0-T6}

**In plain words.** A compromise is an agreement between the company and its members or outside claimants (creditors, debenture holders, preference holders) when it is in trouble. Each party sacrifices something. Two ICAI cases: shareholders give up their right over reserves and accumulated profits (reserves Dr, Reconstruction A/c Cr), and outside liabilities are settled for less (the liability Dr by the sacrifice, any provision Dr, Reconstruction A/c Cr).

**Exam algorithm: one party at a time**
1. Compare what the party **gives up** (claim, arrears, shares) with what it **receives** (cash, shares, an asset).
2. Gives up more → the difference is a sacrifice, **credited** to the Capital Reduction A/c.
3. Receives more than it gives up → the extra is a loss, **debited** to the account.
4. Claims that are not in the books (preference dividend arrears, unprovided contingent liabilities) have no ledger balance, so the entry covers only the part actually settled.

**Why.** *Why does the direction change?* The account collects the net sacrifice of the whole scheme; a party that is paid more than its claim has made the company worse off.

**Exam points**
- An asset handed over for a claim **larger** than its value is a sacrifice; for a claim **smaller** than its value, a loss.
- Arrears: no entry for the waived part.
- A higher interest rate on new debentures needs no entry.

**Where ICAI tests it:** Illustrations 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8; TYK Q13, Q14, Q17 and Q18.

**Linkages**
- The contingent liability that was not provided for in Illustration 8 is an AS 29 contingent liability: see AS 29, Contingent Liabilities (M2-C6-U2-T2.9).

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Jisne chhoda woh credit; jisne zyada le liya woh debit.

## T7 · Surrender of shares {#M3-C14-U0-T7}

**In plain words.** Shares are first sub-divided into smaller shares; shareholders then surrender some of them to the company. The company reissues the surrendered shares to debenture holders and creditors to settle reduced claims. Whatever is not reissued is cancelled by transfer to the Reconstruction A/c.

**Exam algorithm**
1. Sub-divide (T3).
2. Surrender: the surrendered shares are held in a Share Surrender A/c.
3. Issue shares or other securities to the claimants **out of the surrendered shares** (not as fresh issues).
4. Unused surrendered shares → Reconstruction A/c.

**Exam points**
- Illustration 7 is the only one with a Share Surrender A/c.

**Where ICAI tests it:** Illustration 7 (Revise Limited); TYK MCQ 9.

**Linkages:** step 1 is T3; the claimants' sacrifices are as in T6.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Pehle surrender, phir reissue, bacha hua cancel.

## T8 · Entries in case of internal reconstruction {#M3-C14-U0-T8}

**In plain words.** The Capital Reduction (Reconstruction) A/c is used only for reduction of capital, unrepresented reserves, compromise with outsiders and surrender of shares. ICAI's rules: (1) alteration and variation need no such account; (2) an increase in the value of an asset or a reduction of a liability is credited to the account; (3) the debit balance of Profit and Loss and every overvaluation of assets are written off by debiting the account (reserves may be used); (4) any balance left goes to **Capital Reserve**; (5) if the write-offs exceed the balance, reserves cover the difference. In the balance sheet, add "and Reduced" to the company's name only if the court so orders, and show the amount written off against fixed assets for five years.

**Exam algorithm (my exam routine built from ICAI's points; not ICAI's own wording)**
1. List every credit to the account: share capital sacrificed, liabilities reduced, assets appreciated, reserves given up.
2. List every debit: losses, intangibles and other assets written off, assets given away for more than their value, expenses if the question says so.
3. Revalue assets **before** transferring them to claimants; write assets down **before** a cash payment reduces them.
4. The write-off the question never states (goodwill, trademarks, a machine) is the **balancing figure**.
5. Credit balance → Capital Reserve; debit balance → covered from reserves, and only for the shortfall.

**Exam points**
- Unnamed write-offs are computed, not looked for.
- Cash and overdraft: some solutions net the overdraft, some keep it. See the Error Register points in Illustrations 3 and 8.

**Where ICAI tests it:** every illustration and scenario question; TYK MCQ 2 (losses are written off against the Capital Reduction A/c) and MCQ 10 (credit balance to Capital Reserve); Q12, Q16 and Q18.

**Linkages**
- Reconstructed balance sheets are drawn in the Schedule III format: see Final Accounts in Chapter 11 Unit 1 (M3-C11-U1-T1.5).
- Chapter 13 Illustration 11 (an amalgamation after a capital reduction) treats the overdraft differently from Illustration 3 here: see M3-C13-U0.

**Remember** *(agent draft)*: Credit mein sacrifice, debit mein write-off; jo bacha woh Capital Reserve.

### ILL-1 · Illustration 1, B Ltd
**Difficulty:** Easy (computed from 2 concepts)
**Goal:** Pass the entries for a sub-division and a later consolidation of shares and show share capital in the notes for each year.
**Concepts:** sub-division and consolidation of shares [T3]; entry on the paid-up amount only [T3]
**Given:** shares with part paid up are sub-divided into smaller shares and later consolidated into larger ones; the paid-up proportion stays the same.
**Approach:** each entry only relabels the same paid-up amount (old class Dr, new class Cr). Recount the shares and re-express the authorised capital each time, and show the notes year by year.
**Exam points:** no Capital Reduction A/c; the entry is on paid-up capital, not authorised; total capital never changes.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: treat a sub-division or consolidation as a reduction and open a Capital Reduction A/c, or pass the entry on authorised capital. Right: relabel the paid-up amount only. Why: nothing is lost; only the denomination changes.
- **Error Register:** Wrong: carry the old share count forward. Right: recount from the new face value, and move authorised capital the same way.

### TYK-Q12 · TYK Q12, Parth Ltd
**Difficulty:** Easy (computed from 2 concepts)
**Goal:** Pass the journal entries for the sanctioned scheme.
**Concepts:** write-downs and appreciation of assets through the Reconstruction A/c [T8]; shortfall met from the General Reserve [T8]
**Given:** terms writing assets down and appreciating others, a reduction of capital, and a General Reserve available.
**Approach:** debit the account with the write-downs, credit it with appreciation and the capital sacrificed; if the debits exceed the credits, reserves cover the gap.
**Exam points:** use the reserve **only for the shortfall**.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: transfer the whole reserve (or the whole debit side) to the account. Right: only the shortfall. Why: ICAI's rule 5: reserves are used to the extent the write-offs exceed the balance.

**Exam points:** the new preference shares are sized as a fraction of the new equity; one entry, not three.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register:** Wrong: pass separate entries and lose the balancing figure. Right: one compound entry.

### TYK-Q14 · TYK Q14, Green Limited
**Difficulty:** Hard (computed from 6 concepts)
**Goal:** Pass the journal entries for the reconstruction.
**Concepts:** partly paid shares called up first [T5]; all shares converted into shares of a larger face value [T3]; two parties' several balances combined into one personal account each [T6]; cash brought in by one of them [T6]; debt part cancelled, part converted into debentures [T6]; order of write-offs, with computers as the balancing figure [T8]
**Given:** partly paid shares, two creditors who hold debentures and trade payables, and a stated order for the write-offs.
**Approach:** call up the unpaid amount, convert all shares, merge each party's balances into one account, apply the cancel-and-convert terms, then write off in the stated order.
**Exam points:** the reduction base includes the amount just called up; only part of the trade payables belongs to the two parties; the question lays down the order of write-offs.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register:** Wrong: reduce partly paid shares without calling up the unpaid amount. Right: call up first, then reduce.
- **Error Register:** Wrong: apply the scheme to each of the parties' balances separately. Right: first merge them into one personal account each.

### TYK-Q17 · TYK Q17, Z Limited
**Difficulty:** Hard (computed from 7 concepts)
**Goal:** Pass the journal entries, prepare the Capital Reduction A/c and the Bank A/c.
**Concepts:** sub-division followed by a percentage reduction [T3]; own debentures held as an asset, part sold and the rest cancelled [T6]; preference arrears part waived and the rest paid in cash [T6]; debenture holders take an asset worth more than their claim [T6]; remaining assets valued, netting the asset given away first [T8]; payables, receivables and inventory revalued in one entry [T8]; two accounts to prepare, the Capital Reduction A/c and the Bank A/c [T8]
**Given:** a long scheme with sub-division, own debentures, arrears, an asset handed over, and revaluations.
**Approach:** sub-divide then reduce; deal with the own debentures (sale and cancellation); settle arrears; hand over the asset; net it off before writing down the remaining assets; combine the revaluation of the current items into one entry; then build the two accounts.
**Exam points:** here the debenture holders get an asset worth **more** than their claim, the reverse of Illustration 6; interest is paid each 31 March and the scheme starts on 1 April, so "cum interest" does not matter.
**Tricky points:**
- **Error Register (most common):** Wrong: treat every asset handed to a claimant as a sacrifice. Right: an asset worth more than the claim is a loss (debit); worth less is a sacrifice (credit). Why: see Illustration 6.
- **Error Register:** Wrong: write down the remaining assets without first netting off the asset given away. Right: net it first.
