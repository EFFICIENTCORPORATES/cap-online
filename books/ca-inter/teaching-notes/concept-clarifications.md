## 1. Non-statutory reserves under the purchase method of amalgamation (AS 14 topic 2.10, p9.30; Ch 13 treatment of reserves)
They **disappear**: the identity of reserves other than statutory reserves is not preserved, and the transferor's general reserve and P&L balance "are not recorded at all". The net assets taken over are compared with the purchase consideration; the difference is **goodwill** (consideration higher) or **capital reserve** (net assets higher). Only **statutory reserves** keep their identity, with a matching debit to **Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve**, shown as a separate negative line (p9.30 – 9.31). AS 14 topic 2.12 (p9.32): under the purchase method the transferor's P&L balance, debit or credit, loses its identity.

## 2. Bonus shares issued by a subsidiary: "the source decides the effect" (AS 21 Note 5, p10.33; Illustration 9, pp 10.49 – 10.51)
The holding company records no entry (only the share count rises). Consolidation effect depends on which reserve funded the bonus:
- **Out of pre-acquisition (capital) reserves:** no effect on goodwill, minority interest or consolidated reserves.
- **Out of post-acquisition (revenue) reserves:** the parent's share moves from consolidated reserves into capital; goodwill falls (or capital reserve rises) by that amount and consolidated reserves fall by the same amount; minority interest is unchanged in total (reshuffled).
- Worked example in the Arjun Ltd / Bhim Ltd story (Bhim holds 80%, cost 120; Arjun equity 140 = capital 50 + pre reserves 80 + post 10; bonus of 10): pre-acquisition source -> goodwill 16, MI 28, consolidated reserves +8 unchanged; post-acquisition source -> goodwill 8, MI 28 (12 + 16 + 0), consolidated reserves +0.
- ICAI's own numbers (Illustration 9, P holds 70%, bonus ₹7,50,000 out of post-acquisition general reserve): cost of control goodwill ₹87,000 -> capital reserve ₹4,38,000; consolidated P&L share ₹15,85,500 -> ₹10,60,500 (both move by ₹5,25,000); minority interest ₹11,56,500 unchanged.

## 3. Cross-holding and Sec 19 (AS 21 Note-1 p10.2; topic 1.1 p10.4)
"Cross holding" appears only as a **syllabus exclusion** (p10.2) and is never defined. Sec 19 (p10.4): a subsidiary cannot hold shares in its holding company, no holding company may allot or transfer its shares to a subsidiary (void), with three exceptions: legal representative of a deceased member, trustee, or a shareholder before it became a subsidiary. Voting only for shares held as legal representative or trustee.

## 6. Amalgamation: why does the net assets method differ between merger and purchase? (AS 14 topic 2.7, Note-2 chart, p9.28; added 2026-11-10)
ICAI's chart: **merger** = Assets at book − Liabilities at book − Reserves and Surplus (= equity share capital + preference share capital); **purchase** = Assets at agreed value − Liabilities at agreed value, no reserves deducted.
- The logic is one identity: **Assets − outside liabilities = Share capital + Reserves.** So "− Reserves" just gets back to share capital.
- **Merger (pooling):** the businesses are treated as continuing together, so the transferor's shareholders carry their capital across. The shares issued are measured by that capital. The reserves come across separately in their own identity (2.8, p9.29), so including them in the consideration would count them twice. Any difference between shares issued and the transferor's share capital is adjusted in reserves (2.9).
- **Purchase:** the transferee buys assets and liabilities, usually at agreed values. Reserves are not carried (other than statutory ones), so there is nothing to subtract; the whole net assets figure is what is paid for. The difference between price and net assets is goodwill or capital reserve (2.10).
- Example (my numbers, not ICAI's): assets ₹100, liabilities ₹30, equity capital ₹40, preference capital ₹10, reserves ₹20. Merger: 100 − 30 − 20 = ₹50 = 40 + 10. Purchase with assets agreed at ₹120: 120 − 30 = ₹90, with the 20 of reserves and the 20 revaluation gain both inside it.
- Note-2 says to compute consideration by the **net payment method first**; the net assets method is the fallback. The chart and this reasoning are the study material's clarification; AS 14 itself defines consideration only as shares, securities and cash paid (2.7), and ICAI does not spell the logic out.

## 7. Amalgamation: why are statutory reserves not deducted from net assets in a purchase? (AS 14 topic 2.10, p9.30; Ch 13 Illustration 8; added 2026-11-10)
The statutory reserve is carried with a **matching debit to Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve** (shown as a separate negative line, p9.31), so shareholders' funds do not change. It is a legal label, not extra value, and it is not an asset or a liability, so the net assets figure never contained it.
- Ch 13 Illustration 8 (purchase): net assets 57,50, consideration 53,70, capital reserve **3,80** (ICAI's figure). Subtracting the 3,00 of statutory reserves from net assets would give 0,80, which is wrong and does not balance.
- Entry: Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve Dr 3,00 to Export profit reserve 2,00 and Investment allowance reserve 1,00. When the legal period ends, both are reversed (2.10).

## 8. Amalgamation: why is the Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve smaller than the statutory reserves shown? (Ch 13 Illustration 8; added 2026-11-10)
The AAR matches **only the statutory reserves brought in from the transferor (Y Ltd)**, not the transferee's own. In the purchase balance sheet of Illustration 8 (₹ thousand): export profit reserve 500 = X's own 300 + Y's 200; investment allowance reserve 100 = Y's 100 (X had none); AAR (300) = 200 + 100 only. X's own 300 was already in X's books, backed by X's own equity, so it needs no offsetting debit. Earlier wording that the AAR "cancels the reserve" is true only for the part brought in from the transferor.

## 9. Chapter 13 lists four consideration methods; AS 14 (Ch 9 Unit 2) gives a priority of two. Are they different? (Ch 13 section 4, p13.7-13.8; Ch 9 Note-2, p9.28; added 2026-11-11)
- **AS 14's own definition (2.7, p9.28 and Ch 13 para 3(g)) is on the payment side:** consideration is the shares, securities and cash given to the transferor's shareholders. Ch 9 Note-2 then gives a working order: **net payment first, net assets only if net payment cannot be computed.**
- **Ch 13's four methods are not four different prices.** My reading (ICAI does not say this): *lumpsum* and *intrinsic value / share exchange* are two ways the payment to shareholders is specified (a fixed sum, or a share ratio), so they sit inside the net payment idea; *net assets* is the other route, computed from the asset side. So there are really two routes: payment side and asset side. Ch 13 also says any method "or a combination" may be used and gives no priority.
- **The illustrations match the Ch 9 priority:** Ch 13 Ills 1 and 5 (net payment: cash and shares stated per share); Ill 3 (share ratio from intrinsic value) and Ill 4 (shares in profit ratio, preference to give an income) are payment forms; Ills 2 and 10 use net assets because the equity payment is not stated and is the balancing figure (cash in Ill 2, number of shares in Ill 10).
- **Why Ch 13's net assets method has no merger/purchase split:** its wording (assets less outside liabilities, at agreed values, else book) is the **purchase column** of the Ch 9 chart. Ch 13 sets consideration up as a purchase concept (section 4 is headed "Purchase Consideration"). In a merger Ch 13 never computes consideration from net assets: Ill 8(a) and Ill 11 take it as the shares issued. The merger formula (assets − liabilities − reserves = share capital) exists only in Ch 9 Note-2; see section 6 above. So the two chapters do not contradict each other, but Ch 13 is silent on the merger version.
