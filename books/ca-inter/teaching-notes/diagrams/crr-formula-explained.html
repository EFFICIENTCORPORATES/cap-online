Buy-back of securities · Sec 69 · Capital Redemption Reserve

How much goes to CRR? It is the face-value part of the cash paid

The same rule explains the CRR in every buy-back illustration of Chapter 12 (CA Inter Advanced Accounting).
x = y× Face valueOffer price
x = CRR transfer
y = total cash paid for the buy-back
Face value = nominal value per share (e.g. ₹10)
Offer price = buy-back price per share (e.g. ₹30)

1Look at ONE share bought back at ₹30

₹10 face value
₹20 premium
₹10 face value: the share is cancelled, so share capital falls by ₹10. ICAI requires an equal sum to be moved to CRR (when paid out of free reserves or securities premium).

₹20 premium: charged to Securities Premium / free reserves. No CRR on this part.

So of every ₹30 paid, ₹10, i.e. 10/30 = one-third, goes to CRR.

2Build the formula in two lines

Shares bought back  n = y ÷ Offer price
CRR  x = n × Face value
So  x = y × Face value ÷ Offer price
Shortcut to remember: CRR = cash paid × (face value ÷ price). At price ₹30 and face value ₹10 it is always one-third of the cash paid.

3Check with Illustration 5 (p12.23)

Offer price ₹30, face value ₹10
Maximum buy-back y = ₹38,85,000
Shares n = 38,85,000 ÷ 30 = 1,29,500
CRR x = 1,29,500 × ₹10 = ₹12,95,000
or x = 38,85,000 × 10/30 = ₹12,95,000 ✓
This is the "x" in ICAI's second equation: 3x = y, because 30 ÷ 10 = 3.
