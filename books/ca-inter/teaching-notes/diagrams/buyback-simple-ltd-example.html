Buy-back · the three tests · why the room must be split

Simple Ltd: how many shares can it buy back?

A small made-up company. All amounts in ₹ lakh. Follow the steps from top to bottom.
Equity share capital404 lakh shares of ₹10
Free reserves60General reserve
Loans (debt)152borrowed
Buy-back price₹20per share
Face value₹10per share
so half of each ₹20 is face value
1

What is our "equity"?

Paid-up capital + free reserves. This is the base for all tests.

40 + 60 = ₹100 lakh
2

Test 1: Resources (25% of equity)

Money limit: cash paid cannot exceed 25% of equity.

25% × 100 = ₹25 lakh
₹25 lakh ÷ ₹20 = 1.25 lakh shares
3

Test 2: Shares outstanding (25% of shares)

Count limit: at most one-quarter of the shares in issue.

25% × 4 lakh shares = 1 lakh shares
4

Test 3: Debt-equity (loans ≤ 2 × equity)

After the buy-back, equity must still be at least half the loans.

Minimum equity = 152 ÷ 2 = ₹76 lakh
Equity now 100, so room = 100 − 76 = ₹24 lakh
5

The trap: is the whole ₹24 lakh buy-back money?

No. Part of the equity is moved into CRR, and CRR is not a free reserve, so it also shrinks equity.

Each ₹20 paid: ₹10 face value goes to CRR, so CRR = half of cash paid.
Let cash paid = y, CRR = y ÷ 2
Equity lost = y + y ÷ 2 = 24
100 − 24 = 76

Solve the split

1.5 y = 24, so divide 24 by 1.5.

y = 24 ÷ 1.5 = ₹16 lakh cash
CRR = 16 ÷ 2 = ₹8 lakh
Shares = 16 ÷ 20 = 0.8 lakh (80,000 shares)
7

What goes wrong without the split

If we wrongly say "room is ₹24 lakh, so buy back ₹24 lakh ÷ 20 = 1.2 lakh shares", then CRR = 12 and equity falls to 100 − 24 − 12 = 64, below the minimum of 76. Loans 152 ÷ 64 = 2.4, which breaks the 2 : 1 rule. That is why the ₹24 lakh must be shared between cash paid and CRR.
Shortcut for exams: y = (Present equity − Minimum equity) ÷ (1 + Face value ÷ Price). Here (100 − 76) ÷ (1 + 10/20) = 24 ÷ 1.5 = 16. Same method as ICAI Illustrations 5 and 6 (pp12.23–12.29).
