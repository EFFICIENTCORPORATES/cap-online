## Illustrations

**Illustration 1 (p14.5): sub-division and consolidation of shares**
- The entry is passed only on the **paid-up** amount (₹1,60,000), not the ₹2,00,000 authorised (ICAI Note-1, p14.7).
- No Capital Reduction A/c is opened; capital is only re-labelled.
- Count the shares: 20,000 of ₹10 -> 40,000 of ₹5; consolidating 20 shares of ₹5 -> 2,000 shares of ₹100. The authorised capital changes the same way.

**Illustration 2 (p14.7): conversion into stock and back**
- Only **fully paid** shares can be converted into stock.
- Stock has no share count; it is shown as ₹4,00,000 of stock.
- On re-conversion into ₹10 shares the count is 40,000 (not 4,000). No Capital Reduction A/c.

**Illustration 3, A & Co. (p14.13): the full scheme with many items**
- **Preference dividend arrears** = 4 × 6% × ₹4,00,000 = ₹96,000. Not a liability in the books. Three-fourths is simply waived (no entry); only the ₹24,000 settled in shares is entered, debited to Capital Reduction A/c.
- **Property** is revalued before it goes to the debenture holders: appreciation ₹20,000 on the part handed over plus ₹62,500 on the rest = ₹82,500 credited to Capital Reduction A/c. Debentures reduce by the **valuation** (₹1,20,000), not book value.
- The **penalty for cancelling the capital commitment** (5% × ₹2,50,000 = ₹12,500) is paid in cash and debited to Capital Reduction A/c.
- **Directors' loan:** 90% in shares, 5% cash, 5% waived (credited to Capital Reduction A/c).
- **Bank overdraft:** the opening balance sheet shows an overdraft of ₹1,95,000; ICAI's solution has **no entry and no working for it**, and the "after" balance sheet shows no overdraft and cash of ₹35,000. The only reading that reconciles is that the overdraft is absorbed against the new cash: 1,30,000 + 1,40,000 − 22,500 − 5,000 − 12,500 − 1,95,000 = 35,000. This is a **reconstruction, not ICAI's words**. Tell students; show the working.

**Illustration 4, Rebuilt Ltd (p14.20): reduction plus fresh capital**
- Keep reduction and new money apart: old shares cut to ₹2.50; right shares (3 for 1) are separate cash receipts.
- **Preference shareholders receive more than they give up**: per ₹50 share they get 4 × ₹10 + 6 × ₹2.50 = ₹55. The extra ₹5 × 12,000 = ₹60,000 is **debited** to Capital Reduction A/c.
- Write-off of Trademarks and Goodwill is the **balancing figure** (₹1,66,500).
- Equity shares: 15,000 + 45,000 + 72,000 + 12,000 + 40,000 = 1,84,000.

**Illustration 5, Vaibhav Ltd (p14.25): sacrifices from every party**
- Debentures exchanged ₹100 -> ₹70: ₹24,00,000 sacrifice to Capital Reduction A/c. The higher interest rate needs no entry.
- The creditor **forgoes 40%** (₹16,00,000); the other 60% (₹24,00,000) is settled by **60,000 shares of ₹40**.
- Tax provision ₹2,00,000 settled at ₹3,00,000: the extra ₹1,00,000 is a **debit** to Capital Reduction A/c.
- Write assets down to the stated values **first**; the ₹3,00,000 tax payment then reduces current assets from ₹90,00,000 to ₹87,00,000.
- Surplus in Capital Reduction A/c (₹26,00,000) goes to **Capital Reserve**.

**Illustration 6, ABC Ltd (p14.30): settlement in kind**
- Debenture holders take **plant at book value** (₹9,00,000) against a ₹12,00,000 claim: ₹3,00,000 sacrifice.
- Creditors take inventory ₹5,00,000 against ₹5,92,000: ₹92,000 sacrifice.
- Preference shareholders give up 30%; the 70% (₹4,20,000) becomes **11% debentures**, so preference capital becomes a liability.
- Cash = ₹10,000 + ₹3,00,000 new debentures − ₹1,50,000 overdraft = ₹1,60,000.

**Illustration 7, Revise Ltd (p14.35): surrender of shares**
- The one illustration with a **Share Surrender A/c**. After sub-division, 50% of 1,00,000 shares are surrendered (₹5,00,000).
- The 75% reduction applies to the **debentures and the accrued interest** (₹1,50,000 + ₹18,000 = ₹1,68,000).
- Debenture holders get ₹1,00,000 of preference shares and creditors ₹36,000 of equity shares, **both issued out of the surrendered shares**; the unused ₹3,64,000 goes to Reconstruction A/c.
- Final equity shares: 50,000 + 3,600 = 53,600.

**Illustration 8, Recover Ltd (p14.39): many small adjustments**
- **Preference arrears** = ₹1,50,000 × 8% × 4 = ₹48,000, on the **original** capital; settled by issuing ₹48,000 of ₹1 shares; debit Reconstruction A/c.
- **Debenture holders take the freehold property at ₹75,000**, keep their ₹55,000 (debentures + interest) and **pay the company ₹20,000**. Loss on the property (₹1,20,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹45,000) goes to Reconstruction A/c.
- **Unprovided contingent liability** ₹7,000 paid, ₹6,300 recovered from insurance: only the net ₹700 is a loss.
- Here the **bank overdraft (₹90,000) stays a separate liability** and cash of ₹51,300 is shown as an asset: the opposite of Illustration 3. Follow the question's wording and ICAI's solution in each case.

## Test Your Knowledge: only what is new (not in any illustration)

**MCQs 1 – 10, theory Q11:** all theory. Two points: MCQ 8 is the third Sec 66 mode (refund of excess paid-up capital: Sundry Shareholders A/c) which no illustration works; MCQ 5's answer says "Court" while the chapter text says "Tribunal".

**Q12 Parth Ltd (p14.49): General Reserve covers the shortfall.** Reconstruction A/c debits ₹3,09,000, credits ₹1,83,000; the shortfall ₹1,26,000 is met from General Reserve (₹1,50,000 available), **only for the shortfall**.

**Q13 Win Ltd (p14.49): shareholders get new equity, preference shares and debentures.** Old equity ₹10,00,000 -> new equity ₹6,00,000 (3/5), preference ₹1,20,000 (1/5 of new equity), 8% debentures ₹40,000, balance ₹2,40,000 to Capital Reduction A/c, in **one compound entry**.

**Q14 Green Ltd (p14.50): the most new content.**
- **Partly paid shares:** ₹10 uncalled is **called up first** (cash ₹10,00,000), then all shares are converted into ₹20 shares. Reduction base is ₹75,00,000 (₹65,00,000 + ₹10,00,000).
- X and Y each hold first debentures, second debentures and trade payables: moved into **one personal account each**.
- **X pays ₹2 lakh cash** in: account ₹12,00,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹14,00,000; ₹7,00,000 cancelled, ₹7,00,000 converted into 14% debentures. Y: ₹6,00,000, ₹3,00,000 cancelled, ₹3,00,000 converted.
- Only ₹3,00,000 of the ₹5,00,000 trade payables belongs to X and Y; ₹2,00,000 stays.
- **Order of write-offs is laid down:** goodwill, then P&L loss, then **computers as the balancing figure** (₹15,00,000). No capital reserve.

**Q16 X Ltd (p14.54): expenses of reconstruction** (₹5,000 cash) are **debited to Capital Reduction A/c** (Illustration 3 said ignore the cost of the scheme). Also: appreciation/write-down given as **percentages** (30%).

**Q17 Z Ltd (p14.56): own debentures and several twists.**
- **Own debentures held as an asset** (nominal ₹6,00,000, book ₹5,76,000): ₹2,40,000 nominal **sold at 98** -> cash ₹2,35,200 vs cost ₹2,30,400, profit ₹4,800; the remaining ₹3,60,000 nominal **cancelled**: cost ₹3,45,600, profit ₹14,400. "Cum interest" is irrelevant (interest paid each 31 March, scheme dated 1 April).
- Sub-division then **reduction by 40%** (₹24,00,000 to Capital Reduction A/c).
- **Preference arrears paid in cash:** ₹21,00,000 × 8% × 4 = ₹6,72,000; 75% waived, **25% (₹1,68,000) cash**.
- **Debenture holders take an asset worth more than their claim:** machinery ₹9,00,000 for a ₹6,00,000 claim: **₹3,00,000 loss** (Illustration 6 had asset worth less).
- Remaining PPE valued ₹60,00,000: write-down = 72,00,000 − 9,00,000 − 60,00,000 = ₹3,00,000 (net the asset given away first).
- Payables, receivables, inventory revalued in **one combined entry** (net gain ₹69,000).
- Two extra requirements: **Capital Reduction A/c** and **Bank A/c** (closing ₹1,40,200).
