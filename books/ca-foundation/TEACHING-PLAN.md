# Foundation Accounting — teaching sequence, weightages and time constraints

Conversation proposals, recorded 2026-10-20. These recommendations are not a final faculty-approved schedule or an assertion of beginner-course sufficiency.

## Learning flow and story proposal

Start with essential theory and the accounting equation, then journal/ledger/trial balance/subsidiary books/cash book; BRS; inventory; depreciation; non-manufacturing final accounts; rectification; bills; manufacturing final accounts; incomplete records; NPO; partnership/LLP; company accounts. Revisit remaining theory during practical topics and consolidate at the end. Keep original ICAI chapter/unit IDs for navigation.

A suggested narrative is Arjun's stationery shop: invest capital, transact, reconcile the bank, value unsold stock, allocate equipment cost, measure profit, correct errors, handle credit bills, manufacture notebooks, take on partners and later raise company capital. A nearby shop with incomplete books and a community library provide side cases. Dissolution can be an alternative scenario, not a forced event before incorporation. This cast/story is a proposal, not approved manuscript content.

## Delivery assumptions and checks

- This is fast-track delivery for students with basic accounting knowledge, not a promise of comprehensive first-time learning in 61.5 hours.
- Suggested 90-minute format: 10 retrieval/homework review, 30 concept explanation, 40 representative practice, 10 recap/traps/homework.
- Suggested independent work: 60–90 minutes per class, adjusted to other subjects. Move repetition to homework; essential treatments/exceptions still need teaching.
- A full-paper mock needs a separate sitting/home assignment, not a 90-minute slot. Use class time to discuss errors.
- Use a diagnostic test first. If journal/ledger basics are weak, seek additional sessions or recorded support; do not silently remove concepts or consume both revision slots as a supposed complete remedy.
- Track every concept as explained, demonstrated, assigned and checked. Completion is evidence of learning coverage, not just arrival at a calendar date.
