## 2. The 3K Framework — The Backbone of Every Class

Every single class, for every chapter, follows the same three-phase structure. No exceptions.

---

### K1 — Kahaani (Story)

The first phase of every class is a story.

This is not entertainment. This is memory architecture.

The human brain does not retain abstract rules — it retains events, characters, and consequences. Every accounting concept has a real-world story: a mistake made, a consequence paid, a rule created. Teaching begins there.

**Two types of stories used:**

**Type 1 — The Arjun Universe (Real + Emotional)**
Arjun is every CA student. The Arjun stories are grounded in human reality — the anxiety before exams, the bus ride home, the conversation that changes how a student sees accounting. These are used primarily for foundational concepts and for moments where emotional connection matters more than technical detail.

**Type 2 — The 6-Character Fictional Universe (Fun + Conceptual)**
A full accounting ecosystem: two companies (BCPL and ECPL), one accounting firm (Diya and Rolly as Accounting Doctors), one audit firm (Gurpreet Sir + Pranav as articles). The loop in this universe is always the same: Diya or Rolly makes an accounting error → Pranav catches it in audit → Gurpreet Sir asks a surface question → Pranav explains the concept → error corrected → student understands the WHY. This universe runs as a continuous season across all 34 chapters, with callbacks, payoffs, and a spine story that resolves near the end.

**Type 3 — Pranav Sir's Real Experience**
For numerical and practical chapters, stories come directly from professional experience: IOCL (Bihar & Jharkhand Finance Operations, 2019–2024), PPAC / Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas (Additional Director — Finance), D2C startup consulting (CFO advisory, fundraising), and CA Practice (Muzaffarpur). These are the most credible — real problems, real stakes, real accounting decisions.

**Time allocated to Kahaani in a regular class:** 20–30 minutes maximum. The full story is available in the Concept Book. In class, a summary narration is given — students read the complete version from the book.

**What Kahaani achieves:** Students encounter the concept as a lived experience before they encounter it as a rule. When they read the rule in Layer 1, they already have a memory slot for it.

---

### K2 — Koncept (Theory)

The second phase is the theory — Layer 1 of the study material.

**Source:** ICAI Advanced Accounting study material (the official text). All teaching is aligned with ICAI material. No shortcutting, no paraphrasing of definitions that may cost marks.

**How it is taught:**
- The study material is read chapter by chapter, section by section.
- Key definitions, recognition criteria, conditions, and exceptions are highlighted.
- As each concept is read, it is immediately connected back to the Kahaani: "Remember when Diya did X? This is the rule that would have stopped her."
- Real-life references from professional experience are woven in: "At IOCL, this exact timing difference on depreciation appeared on the books every year."
- The goal is not to cover every word of the text. The goal is for every student to understand every key principle well enough to apply it independently under exam conditions.

**Layer system during Koncept:**

| Layer | What it is | When used |
|---|---|---|
| Layer 1 | ICAI Study Material | During Koncept phase — the authoritative source |
| Layer 2 | Chapter-wise summarised notes with illustration explanations | Given as the Concept Book — for revision before tests |
| Layer 3 | One-line summary of every topic (teacher-provided) | Read at end of every class — 5-10 minutes |

---

### K3 — Karma (Practice)

The third phase is practice — and it is the most important phase for exam results.

The name is deliberate. As the Gita teaches: the action (Karma) is within your control. The result is not. A student who does rigorous Karma will get the marks. A student who only listens to Kahaani and understands Koncept but does not practice will not.

**MCQ Practice (30/30 Target):**
- Advanced Accounting has 30 MCQ marks in the CA Inter exam. The system target is 30/30.
- Daily topic-based MCQs are given through an online platform. A student selects any topic and gets immediate MCQ practice.
- MCQs are written from both theory and PYQ/MTP/RTP bank.
- Relentless repetition. Every subtopic is tested multiple times in different question formats.

**Practical Questions:**
- Every practical (numerical) question is solved in exam format. No exceptions.
- This means: proper working notes format, correct journal/ledger headings, P&L and BS with correct Schedule III headings — exactly as a student must write in the answer sheet.
- This is practised from Day 1, not introduced at the end.

**Error Register:**
- Students maintain an Error Register during and after practice sessions.
- Every mistake made — a missed condition, a wrong classification, an incorrect journal entry — is recorded.
- The Error Register is personal. It becomes the most valuable revision tool before exams.

**Karma time in a standard 2.5-hour class:**
- Typical allocation: 45–60 minutes per class
- Chapter-wise tests: At chapter completion, a dedicated chapter test (MCQ + theory + 1-2 practical questions) is conducted. Results are reviewed in the next session.

## 3. The Layer Revision System

The layer system ensures that content is encountered multiple times in different forms, at reducing levels of detail. This is how retention is built.

| Layer | Description | When encountered |
|---|---|---|
| **Layer 1** | Full ICAI Study Material — complete text | During Koncept in class |
| **Layer 2** | Summary of theory + explanation of each illustration | Concept Book — for chapter revision before tests |
| **Layer 3** | One-line summary per topic (the entire chapter in one page) | Read at end of every class; used to open next class |

**Layer 3 is the compass.** At the end of every class, the Layer 3 summary of that class's topics is read. At the start of the next class, the previous class's Layer 3 is reviewed. This creates the "yesterday → today" revision habit automatically.

## 4. The Three Books

Students receive three physical books on the first day of class (A4, spiral-bound, colour printed):

### Book 1 — The Concept Book
- Chapter-by-chapter: Story (Kahaani, full version) + ICAI Theory highlights (Layer 1) + Layer 2 summaries + Layer 3 one-liners
- The complete reference for all theory content
- Updated after each chapter as new stories are written for forthcoming chapters

### Book 2 — The Question Bank
- Chapter-wise curated questions from PYQ, MTP, and RTP (7+ years of papers)
- For each question: the question, the answer, a "Possible Mistakes" section, and "Examiner's Tricks"
- Solved in the same exam format that is expected in the actual CA Inter paper

### Book 3 — The Exam Strategy Book
- Complete exam preparation guide from Day 0 to the day of the exam
- 100+ strategies, 20-30 implementation frameworks
- Advanced Accounts specific: how to prepare this paper from start to finish
- Monthly implementation plans, revision timetables, paper-solving strategy

## 5. Assessment System

### Daily MCQ (Online)
- Topic-based MCQ practice through the online platform (available 24/7)
- Students select any chapter/topic and get immediate MCQ sets
- Difficulty levels available: Foundation, Standard, Advanced
- Results tracked — student can see their accuracy per topic

### Chapter Tests (After each chapter/chapter group)
- Conducted after each major chapter or grouped chapters
- Format: MCQ + 1-2 short theory questions + 1 practical problem (exam format)
- Test and review in a single 2.5-hour session
- Errors discussed in class → students update their Error Registers

### ITD Tests (Initial Till Date — Comprehensive)
- Two comprehensive tests during the batch:
  - ITD Test 1: After Seq 1–26 (mid-batch, approximately September)
  - ITD Test 2: Full syllabus (end-batch, approximately October)
- Format: Full exam simulation (3-hour paper, same pattern as CA Inter)
- Designed to identify systemic gaps before the actual exam

## 6. Monthly Zoom Sessions

**Frequency:** Once per month, on the last Sunday of the month
**Duration:** 2–3 hours

**Before every Zoom:**
Students fill an anonymous form. The form asks:
- Subject-wise confidence rating (1–5) for all papers
- Biggest fear / biggest strength at this moment
- One thing you wish your teacher knew (anonymous)
- Optional: share something personal you're going through

The form responses are reviewed before the Zoom. Students who flag critical concerns are addressed specifically in the session.

**What happens in the Zoom:**
- Overall exam readiness check (all subjects, not just Accounts)
- Anonymous confessions / concerns discussed without naming anyone
- Mental health and study-life balance — openly discussed
- Accounts-specific: current chapter coverage check, upcoming chapter preview
- Motivational anchoring: connecting the present moment to the exam-day goal

**Scheduled Zoom sessions:**
- Zoom 1: Sunday, 30th August 2026
- Zoom 2: Sunday, 27th September 2026
- Zoom 3: Sunday, 25th October 2026

## 8. The Doubt Solving Protocol

**In-class doubts:** Addressed throughout the class. Specific slots for doubt clarification built into every Karma session.
**Between classes:** Students can submit doubts via the class group. Priority doubts addressed at the start of the next class.
**Last Sunday of the month:** The Zoom session's Karma portion is dedicated to doubt solving.
**Anonymous doubts:** Can be submitted via the Zoom pre-form. Addressed in the Zoom without naming the student.

## 9. What This System Produces

A student who completes this batch will have:
- Read every chapter of ICAI Advanced Accounts at Layer 1 level (in class) and Layer 2 level (revision)
- Practised every chapter's PYQ, MTP, and RTP questions in exam format
- Built an Error Register specific to their own mistakes
- Written all 34 chapters' Layer 3 one-liners from memory (or near-memory) by the end
- Done enough MCQ repetition across every subtopic to approach 30/30 with confidence
- A comprehensive exam strategy in their hands from Day 1, not just before the exam
