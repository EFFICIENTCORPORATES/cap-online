## Section 1 · Rescue before winding up

### [s01] Rescue before winding up — 6 steps
- **Open:** "A company is like a person: healthy, ill, or finished."
- **Step 1:** Healthy company. **Buyback (Sec 68)**: too much cash, so it is given back to shareholders. Real cash goes **out**. Normal business.
- **Step 2:** The red **WIND UP** box appears, with a stamp: "Not the first thing we try." Foundation taught winding up (the process) and dissolution (the outcome). That is the **end**.
- **Step 3:** **Rescue 1: internal reconstruction.** Same company continues. Sacrifices, no cash leaves. Sec 61, 66.
- **Step 4:** **Rescue 2: external reconstruction.** The business moves to a **new** company; the old one closes.
- **Step 5:** The stamp goes. Now WIND UP reads **"Last resort"**: only if both rescues fail.
- **Step 6:** The other direction: healthy companies **grow** (amalgamation, absorption, acquisition). "That is the rest of this block."
- **Ask:** "A company loses money for five years. Is the first thing to do to close it?"
- **Trap:** reconstruction does **not** mean closing the company. Internal reconstruction: same company continues.

## Section 2 · Internal reconstruction

### [s02] Internal reconstruction — 4 steps
- **Open (step 0):** "Sick Ltd's balance sheet is lying: a loss of 40 hanging on the left, machinery shown at 70 that is not worth it." (Illustrative numbers, ₹ lakh.) Equity & liabilities left, assets right.
- **Step 1:** Who can help? Shareholders accept a cut of **30**, debenture holders waive **10**, creditors accept **20** less. Nobody is paid cash; they **sacrifice**.
- **Step 2:** All sacrifices flow into **one account**: Capital Reduction Account, **60**. "No new company. No cash leaves. The sacrifices are the gain."
- **Step 3:** The machinery row lights up. The account writes off the **loss 40** and the **overvalued machinery 20**.
- **Step 4:** The balance sheet re-appears cleaned up (100 each side) with **"and Reduced"** added to the name.
- **Mention only (footer):** alteration, variation of rights, reduction, compromise, surrender (Ch 14 topic 2).
- **Ask:** "Does any cash leave the company here?" (No.)

## Section 3 · The big fork

### [s03a] Does the target company die? — 4 steps
- **Open:** "Everything else in these chapters comes from one question."
- **Step 1:** "**Does the target company survive?**"
- **Step 2:** **DIES** column: external reconstruction, amalgamation, absorption. One-time event, one date, everything settled that day. **AS 14.**
- **Step 3:** **LIVES** column: subsidiary, associate, joint venture. An ongoing relationship; the target keeps trading and earning. **AS 21, 23, 27.**
- **Step 4:** "After every section we come back to this slide: **which column are we in?**"
- **Trap:** mixing up **acquisition** (target survives) with **amalgamation** (target dies).

### [s03b] Dies vs lives: the contrast — 6 steps
- **Steps 1 – 6**, one row per click: the target (wound up vs continues); the relationship (one-time vs ongoing); the relevant date (amalgamation vs acquisition); **pre/post split of reserves and profits (No vs Yes)**; **pre/post split of dividends (No vs Yes)**; **minority interest (No vs Yes if not wholly owned)**.
- **Ask:** "If a company is wound up tomorrow, can it declare a dividend next year?" (No: so no pre/post.)

## Section 4 · Target dies

### [s04a] Three forms: what physically happens — 6 steps
- **Open:** "Three ways a company can die into another." ICAI's wording, Ch 13 p13.3 – 13.5.
- **Step 1:** **External reconstruction.** A **new** company takes over the business of an existing, often loss-making company; the same shareholders carry on. (Ch 14 p14.3: "substantially the same shareholders".)
- **Step 2:** The old company fades.
- **Step 3:** **Amalgamation (A + B → C).** Two or more companies are wound up; a brand-new company takes over their business. "Two people leave their parents' homes and start a new home."
- **Step 4:** A and B fade.
- **Step 5:** **Absorption (A absorbs B).** An **existing** company takes over the business of an existing company. No new company.
- **Step 6:** B fades.
- **Footer:** "Buying **shares** is a different thing" (next sections).

### [s04b] The five conditions of AS 14 — 8 steps
- **Open:** "The **form** does not decide the method. Five conditions do."
- **Steps 1 – 5:** one condition per click: (1) all assets and liabilities come over; (2) 90% or more of the transferor's equity shareholders (by face value) become shareholders of the transferee; (3) consideration **wholly in equity shares** (cash only for fractions); (4) the business is intended to carry on; (5) **no adjustment to book values** (except for uniform accounting policies).
- **Step 6:** All five pass: **merger**, accounted by **pooling of interests**.
- **Step 7:** Even one fails: **purchase**, accounted by the **purchase method**.
- **Step 8:** The **two traps**: an absorption of your own subsidiary at book values can pass all five (a merger); an A + B → C where the new company **revalues** assets fails condition 5 (a purchase). "Marriage vs swallowing is a feeling. The five conditions are the test." ICAI itself says external reconstruction is essentially a merger (Ch 14 p14.3).
- **Ask:** "A swallows B, pays B's shareholders in A's equity shares only, keeps book values: merger or purchase?" (Merger, if all five pass.)
- **Trap:** equating the *form* (amalgamation/absorption) with the *method* (merger/purchase).

### [s04c] Merger vs purchase, and the words — 5 steps
- **Steps 1 – 4:** consideration (shares only vs any form); nature (share swap vs buy-out); **vendor's reserves** (all preserved vs revenue reserves not carried, only statutory reserves via the **Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve**); net assets vs consideration (nothing recognised vs goodwill or capital reserve).
- **Step 5:** The words: AS 14 says **"consideration for the amalgamation"** (topic 2.7, p9.28); in a purchase we usually say **"purchase consideration"**. **Mention only:** Realisation A/c (Ch 13 p13.21), the 4 purchase-consideration methods (lump sum, net payment, net assets, intrinsic value / share exchange; p13.7 – 13.8), transferee's entries (p13.29).

## Section 5 · Target lives: the ladder

### [s05a] The ownership ladder — 4 steps
- **Open:** "Think of your friends: a stranger, a friend, family." The investor is **Bhim Ltd**; the company it buys into is **Arjun Ltd**.
- **Step 1:** Bhim holds **10%**: **the stranger.** Just an investor. **AS 13**, shown at cost. (AS 18: under 20% of voting power, no significant influence is presumed.)
- **Step 2:** Bhim holds **40%**: **the friend.** **Associate, AS 23, equity method**: can influence, cannot command; the investment **moves with** Arjun's results. (AS 18: 20% or more is presumed significant influence.)
- **Step 3:** Bhim holds **80%**: **father and son.** **Subsidiary, AS 21, consolidation.** Control: Bhim reports Arjun's assets as if they were its own.
- **Step 4:** **Joint control** (shared by contract, not a percentage): **AS 27**: JCO, JCA, JCE. "Standalone books always show cost; the equity method and consolidation appear only in the **consolidated** statements."
- **Ask:** "At what percentage does an associate begin?" (20% presumed, per AS 18 / AS 23.)

### [s05b] Buying shares vs buying the business — 5 steps
- **Open:** "Buying **shares** does not kill a company. Buying the **business** does."
- **Step 1:** Bhim pays cash.
- **Step 2:** The **shares** travel from Arjun Ltd's shareholders to Bhim.
- **Step 3:** **Control →**: **Arjun Ltd stays standing: it is a subsidiary.** Acquisition (hostile or friendly): the target survives.
- **Step 4:** Now the other way: Bhim buys Arjun's **assets and business**.
- **Step 5:** **Arjun Ltd is wound up: absorption (AS 14).**
- **Ask:** "A hostile takeover: does the target company disappear?" (No.)

## Section 6 · Net assets

### [s06a] Arjun's company: what net assets really are — 4 steps
- **Open (step 0):** "Arjun starts a company with ₹50,000 of his own money." (₹ thousand; equity & liabilities on the **left**, assets on the **right**.)
- **Step 1:** Capital 50. He spends it: cash 5, inventory 10, investment 15, PPE 20. Assets 50. **Net assets = Assets − Liabilities = 50 − 0 = 50.**
- **Step 2:** One year of business. **Profit 50**: this is the **net results of operations**. Reserves & surplus 50; equity 100; trade payables 20; assets 120 (cash 10, inventory 20, trade receivables 20, investment 30, PPE 40). **Net assets = 120 − 20 = 100 = equity.** "A profit of 50 raised net assets by 50."
- **Step 3:** Arjun borrows **₹10 lakh (= 1,000)**, puts half into PPE (540) and half into cash (510). Both sides jump to **1,120**. **Net assets: 1,120 − 1,020 = 100. Still 100.**
- **Step 4:** The lesson (green card): **net assets rise only when net worth rises.** Growing assets and liabilities together makes the balance sheet bigger, not the owners richer.
- **Ask:** "If Arjun borrows another ₹10 lakh, what happens to net assets?" (No change.) "If he makes a loss of 20?" (Down by 20.)
- **Trap:** adding liabilities into the value of the company. "Creditors are paid first; owners own what is left."

### [s06b] Bhim Ltd meets Arjun Ltd — 3 steps
- **Open (step 0):** "Year 2 starts. Arjun Ltd on 1 April: net assets **100**." Show the reserves in parts: statutory 10, general 15, P&L 25.
- **Step 1:** Arjun Ltd will earn **40** this year, evenly.
- **Step 2:** It is now **1 January**. Nine months of profit (+30) have been earned: P&L 55, assets 150, **net assets 130** (capital 50 + reserves 80).
- **Step 3:** "Bhim Ltd wants shares in Arjun Ltd, on 1 January."
- **Trap:** "9 months" is pre-acquisition and "3 months" is post. Always 9 : 3, so nobody mixes up the two sides.

### [s06c] Bhim's price list — 5 steps
- **Open:** "Bhim pays ₹1.5 for every 1% of Arjun Ltd's shares."
- **Step 1:** **10%** for 15; share of net assets (130) is 13; premium 2. Stranger, AS 13, shown at cost.
- **Step 2:** **40%** for 60; share 52; premium **8**. Friend, AS 23; goodwill sits inside the carrying amount.
- **Step 3:** **80%** for 120; share 104; premium **16**. Father and son, AS 21: goodwill (cost of control) **plus** minority interest.
- **Step 4:** **100%** for 150; share 130; premium **20**. Wholly owned: goodwill, no minority interest.
- **Step 5:** "Same price per 1% in every row. What changes is **how much of Arjun Ltd Bhim controls**, and so **which standard** decides what Bhim's books show."

## Section 7 · Control

### [s07a] Control switches five things on — 8 steps
- **Open:** "Say the word **control** and five things switch on."
- **Step 1:** Bhim holds **10%**: the stranger, just an investor. **Step 2:** **40%**: the friend, can influence, cannot command. **Step 3:** **80%**: **CONTROL ON.** Father and son.
- **Step 4:** **Line by line**: "If I control it, I must report it." Arjun's assets and liabilities enter Bhim's statements.
- **Step 5:** **Cost of control**: goodwill or capital reserve appears.
- **Step 6:** **Pre- and post-acquisition**: profits and reserves are split at the acquisition date.
- **Step 7:** **Minority interest**, only if Bhim owns less than 100%.
- **Step 8:** **Intra-group adjustments**: dealings inside the group are cancelled.
- **Close:** "That is why the AS 21 chapter feels crowded: five ideas arriving together."

### [s07b] Control: AS 21 and the wider AS 18 — 4 steps
- **Step 1:** **AS 21 control:** (a) ownership, directly or indirectly, of **more than one-half** of the voting power; **or** (b) control of the composition of the board of directors **so as to obtain economic benefits** from its activities. (AS 21 p10.6, p10.11)
- **Step 2:** **AS 18 control:** (a) and (b) as above, **or** (c) …
- **Step 3:** …clause **(c)**: a **substantial interest in voting power** (20% or more) **and** the power to direct the financial and/or operating policies, by statute or agreement. "That clause is the addition: AS 18 is wider." (ICAI SM, Presentation & Disclosures Based Accounting Standards, topic 4.6, pp 4.82 – 4.83.)
- **Step 4:** Two notes: **exclusions from consolidation** (control intended to be temporary; severe long-term restrictions; AS 21 p10.12 – 10.13) and **syllabus limits** (one subsidiary; no interest acquired at different dates, no cross-holding, no disposal, no foreign subsidiary; AS 21 Note-1, p10.2).

### [s07c] Line-by-line is not adding every item — 5 steps
- **Open (step 0):** Three columns: Bhim's own books (investment in Arjun Ltd **120** for 80%); Arjun Ltd (assets 150, liabilities (20), net assets **130**); the consolidated statements (empty).
- **Step 1:** The investment lights up. "The 120 already **stands for** Bhim's 80% of Arjun's net assets (plus a premium)."
- **Step 2:** The investment line **dissolves**. Arjun's **assets 150** and **liabilities (20)** take its place, line by line. It is **not** added on top of anything.
- **Step 3:** Net assets 130 split: **Bhim's 104** (80% × 130) cancels the investment; **goodwill 16** (120 − 104); **minority interest 26** (20% × 130). Check on screen: 104 + 26 = 130; 120 = 104 + 16.
- **Step 4:** "Try it: what if Bhim bought **100%** for 150?"
- **Step 5:** The orange slice vanishes: 130 all belongs to Bhim; 150 = 130 + **goodwill 20**; **no minority interest**. "That is the **only** difference between wholly owned and partly owned."
- **Ask:** "Bhim owns 80%. Does it report 80% of Arjun's assets or all of them?" (All: it controls them; the other 20% appears as minority interest.)
- **Trap:** "consolidation = adding two balance sheets." It is **replacing one line**.

## Section 8 · Cost of control

### [s08a] Cost of control: goodwill or capital reserve — 4 steps
- **Open:** "Did Bhim pay more or less than what it received?"
- **Step 1:** Two pans: **cost of investment 120** and **Bhim's 80% of net assets 104**.
- **Step 2:** The left pan is heavier: **goodwill 16** (120 − 104). An **asset**: the premium paid for control.
- **Step 3:** Swap the cost to **90**: the other pan is heavier: **capital reserve 14** (104 − 90). A **reserve**: a discount, often a distressed target.
- **Step 4:** "Cost of control arises for **every** subsidiary, wholly or partly owned. The same idea sits in an AS 14 purchase (consideration vs net assets taken over)."
- **Ask:** "Bhim paid 90 for the same 80%. What is the difference called?" (Capital reserve 14.)
- **Trap:** saying cost of control arises only for wholly owned subsidiaries.

### [s08b] Cost of investment vs cost of control — 3 steps
- **Step 1:** Appears in: standalone statements (AS 13) vs consolidated statements (AS 21).
- **Step 2:** What it is: **total price paid (120)** vs **price paid minus Bhim's share of net assets (120 − 104 = 16)**.
- **Step 3:** In the consolidated statements the investment line **disappears**, and goodwill or capital reserve takes its place. "Cost of investment is the **gross input**; cost of control is the **net output**."

## Section 9 · Pre- and post-acquisition

### [s09a] The cut-off line: 9 months before, 3 months after — 4 steps
- **Open:** "Arjun Ltd was not born the day Bhim bought it."
- **Step 1:** The line drops at **1 January**, the acquisition date.
- **Step 2:** April to December (9 months) turn red: **PRE-acquisition, capital**. January to March (3 months) turn green: **POST, revenue**.
- **Step 3:** **₹30** earned before the date: Bhim **paid for it** when it bought the shares; it was inside the price. **₹10** earned while Bhim owns Arjun Ltd.
- **Step 4:** **Net results of operations**: Arjun Ltd's profit for the year is **₹40**, ₹30 pre and ₹10 post, taken as evenly earned.
- **Point to make aloud:** in amalgamation the target dies on one day, so there is no "after": no pre/post split.
- **Trap:** forgetting that the **current-year profit earned before the date** (the 30) is also capital.

### [s09b] The reserves split: who gets which slice — 5 steps
- **Open (step 0):** the stack is on screen: capital 50, statutory reserve 10, general reserve 15, P&L (25 brought forward + first 9 months 30) 55, P&L last 3 months 10. Bhim holds 80%.
- **Step 1:** The caption appears: "Arjun Ltd's equity at 31 March is **140** = capital 50 + reserves 90."
- **Step 2:** The **dashed line** is the acquisition date. Everything above it is **PRE: 130**. Only the last block, **10**, is **POST**.
- **Step 3:** The stack **splits by ownership**: **Bhim 80%** (blue) and **Minority 20%** (orange). Bhim's pieces: 40, 8, 12, 44 and 8. Minority's pieces: 10, 2, 3, 11 and 2.
- **Step 4:** The four resulting amounts fly to their destinations: **Bhim's PRE 104** → into the **cost-of-control** comparison (against the 120 paid → goodwill 16); it is **not** shown as a reserve. **Minority's PRE 26** → minority interest. **Bhim's POST 8** → consolidated reserves. **Minority's POST 2** → minority interest.
- **Step 5:** Checks: **minority interest = 26 + 2 = 28 = 20% × 140**; **Bhim = 104 + 8 = 112 = 80% × 140**.
- **Ask:** "Of Arjun's 30 earned before 1 January, who 'owns' it today?" (Bhim paid for it: it is capital to Bhim.)
- **Trap:** crediting a pre-acquisition dividend to P&L; treating post-acquisition profit as capital.

### [s09c] Three small rules that ride on the cut-off line — 4 steps
- **Step 1:** **Dividend rule:** from **pre**-acquisition profit, a return of the investment (credit Investment A/c); from **post**-acquisition profit, **income** (AS 21 p10.31 – 10.33; AS 13 p5.89).
- **Step 2:** **Time basis:** if control is acquired during the year, profit for the year is split pre/post **on the basis of time**, unless told otherwise (AS 21 p10.32).
- **Step 3:** **Bonus shares** by the subsidiary: the **source** of the reserves used (pre or post) decides the effect (AS 21 Note 5, p10.33).
- **Step 4:** "Same idea behind all three: profit earned **before** the date was paid for (capital); profit earned **after** belongs to the group (revenue)."

## Section 10 · Minority interest

### [s10] Minority interest: the outsiders' share — 5 steps
- **Open:** "If Bhim owns 80%, who owns the other 20%?"
- **Step 1:** Net assets on the acquisition date: **Bhim 104 | minority 26** (80 / 20 of 130).
- **Step 2:** Post-acquisition profit: **Bhim 8 | minority 2** (80 / 20 of 10).
- **Step 3:** **Minority interest = 26 + 2 = 28.** It is **not** a liability to be repaid: it is the outsiders' share of Arjun's net assets and profit, which the group includes because it controls **100%** of Arjun's assets. Shown on its own line after shareholders' funds.
- **Step 4:** "**Toggle:** what if Bhim had bought **100%** (for 150)?"
- **Step 5:** The orange slice vanishes. Bhim's bars are **130** and **10**; minority interest is **nil**. Cost of control still arises (goodwill 20).
- **Term:** ICAI's definition of minority interest mentions **both** the net results of operations and the net assets (AS 21 p10.6, p10.26).
- **Ask:** "Bhim buys 100%. Is there minority interest? Is there cost of control?" (No; yes.)
- **Trap:** treating MI as a loan.

## Section 11 · Statutory reserves

### [s11] Statutory reserves: three fates — 4 steps
- **Open:** "Arjun Ltd's reserves: statutory 10, general 15, P&L 55."
- **Step 1:** **Merger (pooling):** all reserves carried over **as they are** (AS 14 p9.29).
- **Step 2:** **Purchase:** only **statutory** reserves are carried, through the **Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve**; general reserve and P&L are not (AS 14 p9.30 – 9.31).
- **Step 3:** **Consolidation (target survives):** all three are **pre-acquisition**. Bhim's share is used in the cost-of-control comparison; the minority's share becomes minority interest. Nothing is kept as a "statutory reserve".
- **Step 4:** **Honest note:** the AS 21 chapter has **no specific rule** on statutory reserves. If a consolidation question includes one, follow the question and the pre/post logic. Statutory reserves matter in the **purchase method** of amalgamation.
- **Ask:** "Which method makes you worry about *statutory* reserves?" (Purchase method of amalgamation.)
- **Trap:** carrying revenue reserves under the purchase method; forgetting the Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve.

## Section 12 · Intra-group adjustments

### [s12] A group cannot make a profit by selling to itself — 6 steps
- **Open:** "Inside the dashed line, Arjun Ltd and Bhim Ltd are one economic family."
- **Step 1:** Arjun sells goods costing **80** to Bhim for **100**.
- **Step 2:** The goods move from Arjun to Bhim.
- **Step 3:** **Profit 20** is booked in Arjun's books.
- **Step 4:** Bhim **still holds the goods at year end**.
- **Step 5:** **"Unrealised: the group has not earned it."**
- **Step 6:** **Mutual debts cancel** (what Bhim owes Arjun and Arjun owes Bhim).
- **Mention only:** unrealised profit in inventory (AS 21 p10.68), non-current asset transfers (p10.70), mutual balances (p10.71); all in topic 1.14 section C (starts p10.67). Concept only today.
- **Ask:** "Arjun sells to Bhim at a profit; Bhim still has the goods at year end. Has the group earned that profit?" (No.)

## Section 13 · Associates and joint ventures

### [s13a] Same 40% stake, two treatments — 4 steps
- **Open:** "Bhim buys **40%** of Arjun Ltd for **60**: share of net assets 52 plus goodwill 8 (goodwill sits inside the carrying amount)." Bars are not to scale.
- **Step 1:** The caption explains the starting point: both bars are at **60**: **AS 13, cost method** (left) and **AS 23, equity method** (right).
- **Step 2:** Arjun Ltd earns **10** after the date. AS 13: **"Profit of 10 ignored."** Equity method: the bar **grows by 4** (40% × 10) to **64**.
- **Step 3:** Arjun Ltd pays a dividend of **5**; Bhim receives **2** (40% × 5). AS 13: **+ dividend income 2**. Equity method: the bar **shrinks by 2** to **62**: "a return of part of the investment, **not income**."
- **Step 4:** Closing: **AS 13 shows 60 and dividend income 2. Equity method shows 62 (= 40% × 135 + goodwill 8) and no dividend income.**
- **Anchor:** ICAI's own illustration (AS 23 p10.114 – 10.115): 45%, cost 15,00,000, + share of profit 1,35,000, − share of dividend 45,000 = **15,90,000**.
- **Say aloud:** "In the equity method, the investment is a **living number**: up with profits, down with dividends. If it ever hits zero, we stop."
- **Ask:** "Dividend received under the equity method: income or reduction of the investment?" (Reduction.)
- **Trap:** booking dividend as income under the equity method.

### [s13b] AS 13 vs equity method: key differences — 5 steps
- **Steps 1 – 5**, one row per click: **typical holding** (under 20% vs 20 – 50%); **initial recognition** (cost vs cost with goodwill / capital reserve identified); **later measurement** (long-term: cost less any permanent fall; current: lower of cost and fair value vs adjusted for the investor's share of post-acquisition change in net assets); **investee's profit or loss** (ignored vs recognised); **dividends** (income, pre-acquisition dividends reduce cost vs reduce the carrying amount, not income).

### [s13c] Joint ventures: JCO, JCA, JCE — 4 steps
- **Step 1:** **JCO**: each partner uses its **own** assets and books its **own share** of revenue and expenses. No new entity (AS 27 p10.138).
- **Step 2:** **JCA**: an asset owned jointly; each partner books **its share** of the asset and of the output (p10.143).
- **Step 3:** **JCE**: a separate company under joint control; each partner combines its **% of every line**: **proportionate consolidation** (p10.149; consolidation p10.154).
- **Step 4:** "**Equity method** = one line that grows and shrinks. **Proportionate consolidation** = your percentage of every line. **Full consolidation** = every line, then give the outsiders their share."
- **Footer:** AS 18: joint control is contractual and does not depend on voting power (p4.84).

## Section 15 · The map and the word-bank

### [s15] The master map: where each word is born — 8 steps
- **Open:** "Put each word where it is born." Left: **TARGET DIES, AS 14**. Right: **TARGET LIVES, AS 21 · 23 · 27**. Bottom strip: what sits at the bottom of every balance sheet.

### [s16] The eight words, in plain language — 3 steps
