## Answers

### Section A — MCQ Answer Key & Explanations

**1.** Correct Option: **(B)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) ₹2,27,250 — original cost = 3,000 shares × ₹100 + 1% brokerage = ₹3,03,000. The May 2024 bonus issue (1 for 3) adds 1,000 shares at nil cost, so the weighted-average cost across the enlarged 4,000-share holding remains ₹3,03,000, i.e. ₹75.75/share. On sale of 1,000 shares in September 2024, the cost removed = 1,000 × ₹75.75 = ₹75,750, leaving the carrying cost of the remaining 3,000 shares at ₹3,03,000 − ₹75,750 = ₹2,27,250.</p>

**2.** Correct Option: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) Nil — bonus shares are not separately costed; the existing cost of the original holding (₹3,03,000) is simply spread over the enlarged number of shares (original 3,000 plus 1,000 bonus = 4,000), so the bonus shares themselves carry no incremental cost.</p>

**3.** Correct Option: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) ₹34,250 — using the weighted-average cost of ₹75.75/share (see Q2(a)), the cost of the 1,000 shares sold = ₹75,750; sale proceeds = 1,000 × ₹110 = ₹1,10,000; profit = ₹1,10,000 − ₹75,750 = ₹34,250.</p>

**4.** Correct Option: **(A)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (A) ₹2,10,000 — the remaining 3,000 shares carry a weighted-average cost of ₹2,27,250 (see Q2(a)); at the December 2024 market price of ₹70/share, their fair value = 3,000 × ₹70 = ₹2,10,000. As per AS 13, a current investment is carried at the lower of cost and fair value, so the carrying amount is written down to ₹2,10,000.</p>

**5.** Correct Option: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) ₹4,68,000 — in Sun's separate financial statements, the investment is carried at cost. The dividend of ₹80,000 declared on 15.09.2023 relates to Moon's FY2022-23 profits, a pre-acquisition period (Sun acquired its stake on 01.07.2023); Sun's 40% share of this pre-acquisition dividend (₹32,000) is treated as a recovery of the cost of investment, not as income, reducing the carrying cost to ₹5,00,000 − ₹32,000 = ₹4,68,000.</p>

**6.** Correct Option: **(B)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) Current investment will be recorded at ₹1,000 and gain of ₹200 will be credited to profit and loss account — under AS 13, the investment was initially carried at the lower of cost (₹1,000) and fair value (₹800), i.e. ₹800. When fair value subsequently rises to ₹1,200, the carrying amount can be written back up, but only as far as the original cost (₹1,000) — never above it, even though the new fair value is higher. The write-back from ₹800 to ₹1,000 is a gain of ₹200 credited to profit and loss.</p>

**7.** Correct Option: **(B)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) — AS 13 requires long-term investments to be carried at cost, with a provision only for a decline that is <em>other than temporary</em>; classification as long-term alone does not exempt an entity from testing for a permanent diminution.</p>


### Section B — Descriptive Solutions

**Q1.**

<p>As per AS 13 (Revised) ‘Accounting for Investments’, where long-term investments are reclassified as current investments, transfers are made at the lower of cost and carrying amount at the date of transfer. And where investments are reclassified from current to long term, transfers are made at lower of cost and fair value on the date of transfer.</p>
<p>Accordingly, the re-classification will be done on the following basis:</p>
<ol type="i">
<li>In this case, carrying amount of investment on the date of transfer is less than the cost; hence this re-classified current investment should be carried at ₹9 lakhs in the books.</li>
<li>The carrying/book value of the long-term investment is same as cost i.e., ₹14 lakhs. Hence this long-term investment will be reclassified as current investment at book value of ₹14 lakhs only.</li>
<li>In this case, reclassification of current investment into long-term investments will be made at ₹12 lakhs as cost is less than its market value of ₹13.5 lakhs.</li>
<li>Market value of the investment is ₹16.5 lakhs, which is lower than its cost i.e., ₹18 lakhs. Therefore, the transfer to long term investments should be done in the books at the market value i.e., ₹16.5 lakhs.</li>
</ol>

> **Author's Note (Synthesized — not ICAI-sourced):** A common error is applying a single reclassification rule across all four cases rather than switching direction with the transfer – long-term-to-current uses the lower of cost and carrying amount (not fair value), while current-to-long-term uses the lower of cost and fair value (not carrying amount); mixing these up produces the wrong transfer value in at least two of the four cases.

**Q5.**

<div>
<p>As per AS 13 (Revised) "Accounting for Investments", for investment in shares, if the investment is purchased with an intention to hold for short-term period (less than one year), then it will be classified as current investment and to be carried at lower of cost and fair value, i.e. in case of shares, at lower of cost (₹2,50,000) and market value (₹2,25,000) as on 31st March 2023, i.e. ₹2,25,000.</p>
<p>If equity shares are acquired with an intention to hold for long term period (more than one year), then it should be considered as long-term investment to be shown at cost in the Balance Sheet of the company. However, provision for diminution should be made to recognise a decline, if other than temporary, in the value of the investments.</p>
<p>Gold and silver are generally purchased with an intention to hold it for long term period (more than one year) until and unless given otherwise. Hence, the investment in Gold and Silver (purchased on 1st March, 2020) should continue to be shown at cost (since there is no "other than temporary" diminution) as on 31st March, 2023, i.e. ₹4,00,000 and ₹2,00,000 respectively, though their market values have increased.</p>
</div>

> **Author's Note (Synthesized — not ICAI-sourced):** A common error is applying the same "lower of cost and market value" treatment to the gold and silver holdings as to the shares, without first checking the intended holding period — long-term investments (like the gold/silver, held since 2020) stay at cost even when market value rises, and are written down only for an other-than-temporary decline, never simply marked to the lower of cost and current market value the way current investments are.
