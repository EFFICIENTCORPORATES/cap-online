## Answers

### Section A: Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) Goodwill written off can be reversed only if certain conditions are met.</p>

**2.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) Apply both Bottom up test and Top down test if goodwill cannot be allocated to CGU (cash generating unit) under review.</p>

**3.** Correct Answer: **(B)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) The entity should review the remaining useful life, scrap value and method of depreciation and amortization for the purposes of AS 10.</p>

**4.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) ₹45 crores - AS 28 permits reversal of a prior impairment loss on Plant &amp; Machinery and Technical Knowhow (never on goodwill, whose ₹50 crore impairment is permanent), capped at what each asset's carrying amount would have been (net of depreciation/amortisation) had no impairment ever been recorded. Plant &amp; Machinery: ₹450cr (no-impairment hypothetical) − ₹427cr (actual current carrying amount) = ₹23cr reversible. Technical Knowhow: ₹405cr − ₹383cr = ₹22cr reversible. Total reversal = ₹23cr + ₹22cr = ₹45 crores.</p>

**5.** Correct Answer: **(D)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (iv) - AS 28 lists this as an internal-source impairment indicator. Options (i)-(iii) are all genuine AS 28 indicators, but each is drawn from external sources of information (market value decline, market interest rate movements, market capitalisation versus net assets) rather than internal sources, so they do not answer the question as asked.</p>

**6.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) ₹20,00,000 - per AS 28,
  once an asset's carrying amount (₹30 Lakhs) exceeds its recoverable amount (₹20 Lakhs), an
  impairment loss is recognised and the asset is written down to the recoverable amount itself, i.e.
  ₹20,00,000.</p>

**7.** Correct Answer: **(D)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (D) ₹4 Lakhs - total
  impairment loss = carrying amount ₹30 Lakhs − recoverable amount ₹20 Lakhs = ₹10 Lakhs.
  Per AS 28, an impairment loss on a revalued asset is first adjusted against that asset's own revaluation
  reserve (here ₹6 Lakhs, the upward revaluation done last year); only the excess, ₹10 Lakhs −
  ₹6 Lakhs = ₹4 Lakhs, is charged to the statement of profit and loss.</p>

**8.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) Impairment loss of ₹6,25,000 - carrying amount just before the 31 March 2023 impairment test = ₹44,00,000 less two years' revised depreciation at ₹4,87,500 (FY2021-22 and FY2022-23) = ₹44,00,000 − ₹9,75,000 = ₹34,25,000. Recoverable amount is given as ₹28,00,000. Impairment loss per AS 28 = ₹34,25,000 − ₹28,00,000 = ₹6,25,000, debited to the Profit &amp; Loss Account.</p>

**9.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) - a rise in interest rates makes competing risk-free investments relatively more attractive, pushing the market value of an unquoted equity holding like ANI Ltd downward. This is a classic external indicator of impairment under AS 28: it does not automatically write anything down, but it does trigger a duty to formally estimate the recoverable amount of the interest and test for impairment.</p>

**Q3.**

<p>The objective of AS 28 ‘Impairment of Assets’ is to prescribe the procedures that an enterprise applies to ensure that its assets are carried at no more than their recoverable amount. An asset is carried at more than its recoverable amount if its carrying amount exceeds the amount to be recovered through use or sale of the asset. If this is the case, the asset is described as impaired and this Standard requires the enterprise to recognize an impairment loss.  If carrying amount < = Recoverable amount : Asset is not impaired  If carrying amount > Recoverable amount : Asset is impaired Impairment Loss = Carrying Amount - Recoverable Amount Recoverable amount is the higher of net selling price and its value in use This standard should be applied in accounting for the impairment of all assets, other than (i) inventories (AS 2, Valuation of Inventories); (ii) assets arising from construction contracts (AS 7, Accounting for Construction Contracts); (iii) financial assets, including investments that are included in the scope of AS 13, Accounting for Investments; and (iv) deferred tax assets (AS 22, Accounting for Taxes on Income). AS 28 does not apply to inventories, assets arising from construction contracts, deferred tax assets or investments because other accounting standards applicable to these assets already contain specific requirements for recognizing and measuring the impairment related to these assets.</p>

**Q4.**

<div>
<p><strong>Treatment of Impairment Loss:</strong> As per AS 28 "Impairment of assets", if the recoverable amount (higher of net selling price and its value in use) of an asset is less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset should be reduced to its recoverable amount. In the given case, net selling price is ₹64.50 lakhs (₹67.50 lakhs − ₹3 lakhs) and value in use is ₹60 lakhs. Therefore, recoverable amount will be ₹64.50 lakhs. Impairment loss will be calculated as ₹10.50 lakhs [₹75 lakhs (Carrying Amount after revaluation - Refer Working Note) less ₹64.50 lakhs (Recoverable Amount)].</p>
<p>Thus impairment loss of ₹10.50 lakhs should be recognised as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss immediately since there was downward revaluation of asset which was already charged to Statement of Profit and Loss.</p>
