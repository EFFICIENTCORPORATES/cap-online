## Answers

### Section A: Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) Both (a) and (b).</p>

**2.** Correct Answer: **(D)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (D) Change in accounting estimate.</p>

**3.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) 1 - Not a change in accounting policy. 2 - Not a change in accounting policy. - per AS 5, the adoption of an accounting policy for a transaction/event that did not occur previously, or that previously did not exist, is not a change in accounting policy. Both scheme 1 (formalising ad hoc ex-gratia payments into a retirement gratuity scheme) and scheme 2 (introducing a pension scheme where none existed before) are first-time adoptions for a previously non-existent benefit, not a switch from one existing policy to another, so neither qualifies as a change in accounting policy.</p>


### Section B: Descriptive Questions

**Q1.**

<p>The decision of making provision for non-moving inventories on the basis of technical evaluation does not amount to change in accounting policy. Accounting policy of a company may require that provision for non-moving inventories should be made. The method of estimating the amount of provision may be changed in case a more prudent estimate can be made. In the given case, considering the total value of inventory, the change in the amount of required provision of non-moving inventory from ₹3.5 lakhs to ₹2.5 lakhs is also not material. The disclosure can be made for such change in the following lines by way of notes to the accounts in the annual accounts of ABC Ltd. for the year 2021-22:</p>
<p>“The company has provided for non-moving inventories on the basis of technical evaluation unlike preceding years. Had the same method been followed as in the previous year, the profit for the year and the corresponding effect on the year end net assets would have been lower by ₹1 lakh.”</p>

> **Common Mistake to Avoid:** A common error is treating any change in the basis of a provision estimate as automatically a change in accounting policy requiring restatement, without distinguishing a genuine policy change from a change in accounting estimate (moving to a more prudent estimation method for an existing policy of providing for non-moving inventory).

**Q2.**

<div>
<p><strong>Original scenario:</strong> Per AS 5, prior period items are income/expenses arising in the current period from errors or omissions in preparing prior periods' financial statements. The 3 months' salary (Jan-Mar 2024, ₹4,50,000) omitted from FY2023-24 is a <strong>prior period item</strong> in FY2024-25.</p>
<p><strong>Journal entry (FY2024-25):</strong> Salary A/c (₹1,50,000×6) Dr. 9,00,000; Prior Period Item A/c (₹1,50,000×3) Dr. 4,50,000; To Bank A/c 13,50,000.</p>
<p><strong>Variant (termination/reinstatement):</strong> AS 5 states "prior period items" does not include adjustments necessitated by circumstances that, though related to prior periods, are determined only in the current period. Since Sneha's reinstatement (with retrospective salary) was ordered by the court only in FY2024-25, the ₹4,50,000 (Jan-Mar 2024) is <strong>not</strong> a prior period item - the entire ₹13,50,000 is a current-year expense.</p>
<p><strong>Journal entry (variant):</strong> Salary A/c (₹1,50,000×9) Dr. 13,50,000; To Bank A/c 13,50,000.</p>
</div>

> **Examiner's Comment:** A majority of examinees were unable to determine whether the given item constituted a prior period item under AS 5 "Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies". Many of them also failed to pass the correct journal entry.

**Q3.**

<p>As per para 21 of AS 5, the preparation of financial statements involves making estimates based on circumstances existing when the statements are prepared; it may be necessary to revise an estimate in a subsequent period if circumstances change. Revision of an estimate, by its nature, does not bring the adjustment within the definitions of a prior period item or an extraordinary item. Change in Accounting estimate is not a change in accounting policy.</p>
<p>(i) Provision for doubtful debts rate change (3% to 5%): this is a change in accounting estimate, not a change in accounting policy. Per para 27 of AS 5, a change in estimate with a material effect in the current period should be disclosed and quantified; any change expected to have a material effect in later periods should also be disclosed and quantified.</p>
<p>(ii) As per AS 5, adopting an accounting policy for events or transactions that differ in substance from previously occurring events or transactions is not considered a change in accounting policy. Accordingly, introduction of a formal retirement gratuity scheme in place of ad hoc ex-gratia payments is not a change in accounting policy.</p>
<p>(iii) As per AS 2 and AS 5: the method of determining cost (FIFO/Weighted Average) is an accounting policy choice, and any change in it affects measurement of inventory and profit. Hence a change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories IS a change in Accounting Policy.</p>
<p>(iv) The adoption of a new accounting policy for events or transactions which did not occur previously, or that were immaterial, will not be treated as a change in accounting policy. Introducing a pension scheme where none previously existed is therefore not a change in accounting policy.</p>

> **Common Mistake to Avoid:** Many examinees classified the provision-rate change and the new gratuity/pension schemes as changes in accounting policy, not recognising AS 5's distinction between a genuine policy change (like the inventory cost-formula change) and a change in estimate or the adoption of a policy for a genuinely new type of transaction.
