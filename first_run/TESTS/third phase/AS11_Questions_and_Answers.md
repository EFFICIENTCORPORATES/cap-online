## Answers

### Section A: Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** Correct Answer: **(D)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (D) Premium on contract will be amortized over the life of the contract - the forward contract was entered into to hedge a foreign-currency risk, not for speculation. Per AS 11, the premium or discount on such a forward exchange contract (the difference between the forward rate and the spot rate at inception: here 83.10 − 83.02 = ₹0.08 per US$) is amortised as expense or income over the life of the contract, while any exchange difference on the contract itself is recognised in profit or loss in the period in which the exchange rate changes.</p>

**2.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) ₹7,34,383 approx - the trade receivable is a foreign-currency monetary asset, restated at the closing rate on the balance sheet date. USD principal = ₹7,00,000 / 79.4 = USD 8,815.82 (approx); at the closing rate of USD 1 = ₹83.3, the restated balance = 8,815.82 × 83.3 = ₹7,34,383 approx.</p>

**3.** Correct Answer: **(A)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (A) ₹1,50,000 - the residual exchange loss on the loan principal (₹5,00,000 total restatement loss) after the portion already absorbed within the AS 16 borrowing-cost cap in Q1 is separately recognised under AS 11.</p>

**4.** Correct Answer: **(A)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (A)</p>


### Section B: Descriptive Questions

**Q1.**

<ol type="i">
<li>Interest for the period 2022-23 = US $10 lakhs × 4% × ₹62 per US$ = ₹24.80 lakhs</li>
<li>Increase in the liability towards the principal amount = US $10 lakhs × ₹(62 − 56) = ₹60 lakhs</li>
<li>Interest that would have resulted if the loan was taken in Indian currency = US $10 lakhs × ₹56 × 10.5% = ₹58.80 lakhs</li>
<li>Difference between interest on local currency borrowing and foreign currency borrowing = ₹58.80 lakhs − ₹24.80 lakhs = ₹34 lakhs.</li>
</ol>
<p>Therefore, out of ₹60 lakhs increase in the liability towards principal amount, only ₹34 lakhs will be considered as the borrowing cost. Thus, total borrowing cost would be ₹58.80 lakhs being the aggregate of interest of ₹24.80 lakhs on foreign currency borrowings plus the exchange difference to the extent of difference between interest on local currency borrowing and interest on foreign currency borrowing of ₹34 lakhs.</p>
<p>Hence, ₹58.80 lakhs would be considered as the borrowing cost to be accounted for as per AS 16 and the remaining ₹26 lakhs (60 − 34) would be considered as the exchange difference to be accounted for as per AS 11.</p>

> **Common Mistake to Avoid:** A common error is treating the entire ₹60 lakh increase in the rupee liability as either wholly a borrowing cost under AS 16 or wholly an exchange difference under AS 11, instead of splitting it at the interest-rate-differential figure of ₹34 lakhs.

**Q4.**

<div>
<p>As per AS 11 'The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates', Monetary items are money held and assets and liabilities to be received or paid in fixed or determinable amounts of money.</p>
<p>Foreign currency monetary items should be reported using the closing rate at each balance sheet date. However, in certain circumstances, the closing rate may not reflect with reasonable accuracy the amount in reporting currency that is likely to be realised from, or required to disburse, a foreign currency monetary item at the balance sheet date. In such circumstances, the relevant monetary item should be reported in the reporting currency at the amount which is likely to be realised from or required to disburse, such item at the balance sheet date.</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Item</th><th>Classification</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Share capital</td><td>Non-monetary</td></tr>
<tr><td>Trade receivables</td><td>Monetary</td></tr>
<tr><td>Investments</td><td>Non-monetary</td></tr>
<tr><td>Fixed assets</td><td>Non-monetary</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

> **Common Mistake to Avoid:** A common error is classifying Investments as a monetary item because they are eventually realised in cash, missing that AS 11 tests whether the amount to be received is fixed or determinable in monetary terms at the reporting date, not merely whether the asset will ultimately convert to cash.
