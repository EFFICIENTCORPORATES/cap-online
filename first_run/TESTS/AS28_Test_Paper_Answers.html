1 Marks: 7 · Source: PYQ September 2025, Q3(b) of the original paper · AS 28: CGU impairment loss allocated first to goodwill, then pro-rata to other assets

(i) Value in Use: 1,000×0.909=909.00 + 800×0.826=660.80 + 700×0.751=525.70 + 800×0.683=546.40 + 600×0.621=372.60 + 500×0.564=282.00 = ₹3,296.50 lakhs, rounded to ₹3,297 lakhs.

Carrying amount at 31.3.2025 (2 years post-acquisition): Goodwill 2,000 − amortisation (2,000/5×2=800) = 1,200. Identifiable assets 6,000 − depreciation (6,000/8×2=1,500) = 4,500. Total CGU carrying amount = 5,700.

Recoverable amount = higher of Value in Use (3,297) and net selling price of identifiable assets (3,000, no CGU-wide fair-value-less-costs-to-sell given) = 3,297 (since 3,297 > 3,000).

(ii) Impairment loss = Carrying amount (5,700) − Recoverable amount (3,297) = ₹2,403 lakhs.

(iii) Allocation: per AS 28, impairment is allocated first to goodwill (written down to zero: full 1,200 absorbed), then the remainder (2,403−1,200=1,203) to identifiable assets pro-rata: revised carrying amount of identifiable asset = 4,500−1,203 = ₹3,297 lakhs (goodwill: Nil).

Examiner’s Comment (real ICAI-sourced): A majority of examinees were unable to compute the impairment loss and allocate the same appropriately among the assets of the CGU as per the requirements of AS 28 "Impairment of Assets". Many examinees failed to determine the value in use, impairment loss and the revised carrying amount of assets, evidencing an inadequate understanding of the impairment principles.Source: ICAI Examiner's Comment — September 2025, verbatim

Author’s Note (likely pitfall, not an official ICAI finding): Many examinees debited the entire ₹3,50,000 impairment loss to the profit and loss account, not applying AS 28's requirement (para 58) to first set off an impairment loss on a revalued asset against the remaining balance in that asset's revaluation reserve before charging the balance to P&L. Several also capitalised the ₹10,800 delay-related expenses instead of expensing them, since the asset was already ready for use on 1st October, 2022.
3 Marks: 4 · Source: MTP May 2024 Set 1, Q1(c) of the original paper · AS 28: Impairment loss = carrying amount less recoverable amount (higher of net selling price and value in use)

Treatment of Impairment Loss: As per AS 28 "Impairment of assets", if the recoverable amount (higher of net selling price and its value in use) of an asset is less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset should be reduced to its recoverable amount. In the given case, net selling price is ₹64.50 lakhs (₹67.50 lakhs − ₹3 lakhs) and value in use is ₹60 lakhs. Therefore, recoverable amount will be ₹64.50 lakhs. Impairment loss will be calculated as ₹10.50 lakhs [₹75 lakhs (Carrying Amount after revaluation – Refer Working Note) less ₹64.50 lakhs (Recoverable Amount)].

Thus impairment loss of ₹10.50 lakhs should be recognised as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss immediately since there was downward revaluation of asset which was already charged to Statement of Profit and Loss.

Working Note — Calculation of carrying amount of the Property, Plant and Equipment at the end of the fourth year on revaluation (₹ in lakhs):

Purchase price of a Property, Plant and Equipment150.00
Less: Depreciation for four years [(150 lakhs / 10 years) × 4 years](60.00)
Carrying value at the end of fourth year90.00
Less: Downward revaluation charged to profit and loss account(15.00)
Revalued carrying amount75.00
Author’s Note (likely pitfall, not an official ICAI finding): A common error is using net selling price or value in use alone instead of the HIGHER of the two as the recoverable amount, or crediting the impairment loss against a revaluation reserve balance that does not exist here (the earlier downward revaluation was already charged to profit and loss, leaving no reserve to absorb the fresh impairment loss).
