### Section B: Descriptive Questions

**Q1.**

<p><strong>(a)</strong> A non-company entity is an MSME only if <em>all</em> the following conditions are satisfied:</p><ol><li>its equity or debt securities are not listed, or in the process of listing, on any stock exchange in India or outside India;</li><li>it is not a bank, financial institution or an insurance company;</li><li>its turnover (excluding other income) did not exceed ₹250 crore in the immediately preceding accounting year;</li><li>it did not have borrowings in excess of ₹50 crore at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year; and</li><li>it is not a holding or subsidiary of an entity which is not itself an MSME.</li></ol><p>The conditions are tested as at the end of the relevant accounting period. A non-company entity that is not an MSME is a <strong>Large entity</strong> and must comply in full with all the Accounting Standards; MSMEs get the exemptions and relaxations in Annexure 1.</p><p><strong>(b)</strong> Turnover of ₹180 crore is within the ₹250 crore limit, and the entity is unlisted, not a financial institution and not a subsidiary. However, its borrowings of ₹55 crore exceed the limit of ₹50 crore. One of the conditions fails, so Om Finserv LLP <strong>cannot</strong> be classified as an MSME. It is a Large entity and must comply with all the Accounting Standards in full, with no MSME exemptions.</p><p><strong>(c)</strong> Turnover of ₹230 crore does not exceed ₹250 crore; borrowings of ₹45 crore do not exceed ₹50 crore; the firm is unlisted, is not a bank, financial institution or insurance company, and is neither a holding nor a subsidiary. Every condition is satisfied on the preceding-year figures, so Ravi Traders <strong>qualifies as an MSME</strong> for FY 2024-25 and may avail the exemptions and relaxations. If it avails them, it must disclose by way of a note that it is an MSME and has complied with the Accounting Standards insofar as they are applicable to an MSME.</p>

**Q2.**

<p><strong>1. Not applicable to any MSME in their entirety:</strong> AS 3 (Cash Flow Statements), AS 17 (Segment Reporting), AS 20 (Earnings per Share) and AS 24 (Discontinuing Operations).</p><p><strong>2. AS 18 (Related Party Disclosures) and AS 28 (Impairment of Assets)</strong> are not applicable in their entirety to an MSME which satisfies all of the following:</p><ul><li>its turnover (excluding other income) does not exceed ₹50 crore in the immediately preceding accounting year;</li><li>it does not have borrowings in excess of ₹10 crore at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year; and</li><li>it is not a holding or subsidiary of an MSME not covered by this exemption.</li></ul><p>An MSME that is otherwise not exempted from AS 28 may measure 'value in use' on a reasonable estimate instead of the present value technique, and need not disclose the information required by paragraph 121(g).</p><p><strong>3. AS 14 and AS 27:</strong> MSMEs generally have no transactions covered by AS 14 (Accounting for Amalgamations) or jointly controlled operations or assets under AS 27, so these standards are not applicable. However, if there are any such transactions, the entity must apply the requirements of the relevant standard.</p><p><strong>4. AS 21, AS 23, AS 25 and AS 27 (consolidation part):</strong> a non-company entity is not required to present consolidated financial statements or an interim financial report. The relevant standard applies only if the entity is required to, or elects to, prepare and present consolidated financial statements or an interim financial report.</p>

**Q3.**

<p><strong>Classification.</strong> For companies there are two levels: Small and Medium-sized Companies (SMCs), as defined in the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2021, and companies other than SMCs (Non-SMCs). Non-SMCs must comply with all the Accounting Standards in their entirety; certain exemptions and relaxations are given to SMCs.</p><p><strong>Not applicable to SMCs in its entirety:</strong> AS 17, Segment Reporting.</p><p><strong>Relaxations for SMCs:</strong></p><ul><li><strong>AS 15 (Employee Benefits):</strong> relaxation from recognition and measurement of short-term accumulating non-vesting compensated absences; from discounting of amounts falling due more than 12 months after the balance sheet date; and from the defined benefit plan and other long-term benefit provisions. However, the accrued liability must be actuarially determined using the Projected Unit Credit Method, with the discount rate based on market yields on government bonds at the balance sheet date (paragraph 78), and actuarial assumptions disclosed as per paragraph 120(l).</li><li><strong>AS 19 (Leases):</strong> certain disclosure paragraphs (22(c), (e), (f); 25(a), (b), (e); 37(a), (f); 46(b), (d)) are not applicable.</li><li><strong>AS 20 (Earnings Per Share):</strong> disclosure of diluted EPS (both including and excluding extraordinary items) is exempted.</li><li><strong>AS 28 (Impairment of Assets):</strong> 'value in use' may be measured on a reasonable estimate instead of the present value technique; the discount rate provisions do not apply and the information in paragraph 121(g) need not be disclosed.</li><li><strong>AS 29 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets):</strong> disclosure paragraphs 66 and 67 are not applicable.</li></ul><p><strong>AS 25 (Interim Financial Reporting)</strong> does not require a company to present an interim financial report; it applies only if the company is required or elects to present one (for example, quarterly results required by SEBI from certain Non-SMCs).</p>

**Q4.**

<p><strong>(a)</strong> An MSME that avails the exemptions or relaxations must disclose, by way of a note to its financial statements, the fact that it is an MSME and has complied with the Accounting Standards insofar as they are applicable to an MSME. If it opts not to avail the exemptions in respect of some, but not all, Standards, it must disclose the Standard(s) in respect of which it has availed the exemption or relaxation. If it opts not to avail an exemption, it must comply with the relevant requirements of that Standard. Partial exemption or relaxation must not be permitted to mislead the users of financial statements.</p><p><strong>(b)</strong> Where an MSME no longer qualifies, the relevant Standards or requirements become applicable from the current period. So ABC &amp; Co. must apply the full Accounting Standards in FY 2024-25. The corresponding previous-period figures need not be revised merely because it ceased to be an MSME. It must disclose in the notes that it was an MSME in the previous period and had availed the exemptions or relaxations, and that the previous-period figures have not been revised.</p><p><strong>(c)</strong> MNO Enterprises must disclose, by way of a note, that it is an MSME, and mention the Standard in respect of which it has availed the exemption (AS 3), since it has not availed it for all Standards. It need not revise any previous-year figures. An entity that was previously not an MSME and subsequently becomes one cannot claim MSME exemptions or relaxations until it remains an MSME for two consecutive years.</p>
