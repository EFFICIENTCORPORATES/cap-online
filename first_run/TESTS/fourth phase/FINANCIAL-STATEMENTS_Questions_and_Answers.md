## Answers

### Section A: Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** Correct Answer: **(D)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (D) Reserve and Surplus − ₹80 lakhs only. Schedule III does not permit an accumulated loss (a debit balance in the Statement of Profit and Loss) to be shown as an asset. It must instead be netted directly within the "Reserves and Surplus" line on the equity side: total credit-balance reserves (₹90 + ₹40 + ₹70 = ₹200 lakhs) less the accumulated loss (₹280 lakhs) = a net negative ₹80 lakhs, disclosed as "Reserves and Surplus − ₹80 lakhs" (a negative figure within Reserves and Surplus, not a positive asset-side item).</p>

**2.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) 1/10th of the sum of its paid-up share capital and free reserves.</p>

**3.** Correct Answer: **(A)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> the source's printed Answer Key states (A). Working: instalments of ₹50,000 each fall due Dec 2023, Jun 2024, Dec 2024 ... Jun 2028 (10 total). One instalment (Dec 2023) is paid before the 31 March 2024 balance sheet date, leaving ₹4,50,000 outstanding, of which the Jun 2024 and Dec 2024 instalments (₹1,00,000) fall due within the next 12 months and ₹3,50,000 remains due beyond 12 months.</p><p><strong>Flagging an independent-derivation discrepancy:</strong> per Note 6(A) to Schedule III's General Instructions for the Balance Sheet, "current maturities of long-term debt" are disclosed as a separate line item under <strong>'Other Current Liabilities'</strong>, not under 'Short-term borrowings' - which would point to option (D), not (A), for the ₹1,00,000 due within 12 months. This exact classification point ("current-vs-long-term loan classification") is even called out as a worked example in this repo's own AS-index for the Schedule III unit. We are not overriding the source's printed answer letter (A) - it is reproduced verbatim - but flagging this as a possible inconsistency in the source's own key for Pranav's judgment, per the never-silently-fix rule.</p>

**4.** Correct Answer: **(C)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (C) - the principal loan balance, net of the interest component (₹17,15,000 − ₹1,15,000 = ₹16,00,000), stays under Long-Term Borrowings (secured, hypothecation-backed). Interest accrued but not yet due is not itself a "borrowing" - per Schedule III it is disclosed separately under Other Current Liabilities, not lumped into long-term borrowings and not mislabelled as a short-term borrowing (which would imply it is itself a loan).</p>

**5.** Correct Answer: **(D)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (D)</p>

**6.** Correct Answer: **(B)**

<p><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) Current Asset - an asset held for sale (expected to be realised/converted to cash within the normal operating cycle or 12 months) is classified as a current asset under Schedule III, regardless of its original non-current classification while in use.</p>


### Section B: Descriptive Questions

**Q1.**

<p>In case of declaration of dividend out of free reserves, there are 3 conditions:</p>
<p><strong>(1) Dividend Rate &lt; Average Rate of last 3 years:</strong> 10% &lt; 12% [(12+14+10)/3] - Condition is Satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>(2) Dividend Distributed &lt; 10% of PUSC + Reserve and Surplus:</strong> 50,00,000 &lt; 59,50,000 [(5,00,00,000 + 1,00,00,000 − 5,00,000) × 10%] - Condition is Satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>(3) Reserves after dividend &gt; 15% of PUSC:</strong> 45,00,000 not &gt; 75,00,000 (5,00,00,000 × 15%) - Condition is Not Satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>(4)</strong> The closing balance of reserves after payment of dividend and set off of loss = ₹75,00,000. Therefore, amount that can be utilized = 20,00,000 (1,00,00,000 − 5,00,000 − 75,00,000). Thus, rate of dividend = (20,00,000/5,00,00,000) = <strong>4%</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Alternatively</strong>: To judge the recommendation of management, the satisfaction of all three conditions is to be checked:</p>
<p><strong>Condition I</strong>: The proposed dividend of 10% is less than the average rate of dividend being 12% (i.e.) (12+14+10)/3 = 12%. Hence, this condition is satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>Condition II</strong> - Amount to be withdrawn: 10% dividend on Equity share capital 50,00,000 + Loss of Current year 5,00,000 = Amount to be drawn from General Reserve 55,00,000. Maximum amount that can be withdrawn should not exceed 10% of paid-up share capital + free reserves = 10% of [₹500 lakhs + ₹100 lakhs] = ₹60,00,000. As the amount to be withdrawn is within the maximum limit, this condition is also satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>Condition III</strong>: Balance of reserves after withdrawal (100−55) = ₹45,00,000; 15% of paid-up capital = ₹75,00,000. As the balance of reserves should not be less than 15% of its paid-up share capital, but here the balance of reserves after withdrawal is less than 15% of paid-up share capital, this condition is not satisfied - hence 10% dividend cannot be declared.</p>
<p><strong>Maximum withdrawal of Reserve if condition III is to be satisfied:</strong> Opening balance of Reserves at the beginning of the year = ₹1,00,00,000; Less: Closing balance of reserves being 15% of paid-up capital = ₹75,00,000; Reserves available = ₹25,00,000.</p>
<p><strong>Maximum permissible Divisible Profits:</strong> Permissible withdrawal as above = ₹25,00,000; Less: Current Year's Loss = ₹5,00,000; Maximum permissible Divisible profit = ₹20,00,000.</p>
<p><strong>Actual permissible rate of Dividend</strong> = (₹20,00,000 / ₹5,00,00,000) x 100 = <strong>4%</strong>.</p>
<p>Therefore, the recommendation of management is not justified and a dividend only up to a rate of 4% can be declared.</p>

> **Examiner's Comment:** Most of the examinees wrote the first condition and its testing correctly but many of them got confused between the second and third condition. Additionally, a few examinees failed to deduct the loss when determining the amount available for the declaration of dividends.

**Q2.**

<p>Amount required for 10% dividend on ₹80,00,000 = ₹8,00,000. Profit available for the current year = ₹1,42,500. Amount to be utilized from reserves = ₹8,00,000 − ₹1,42,500 = <strong>₹6,57,500</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Conditions as per Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014:</strong></p>
<p><strong>Condition I:</strong> since the proposed rate (10%) is lower than the average rate of dividend over the last five years (12%), a 10% dividend can be declared.</p>
<p><strong>Condition II:</strong> the maximum amount that can be drawn from accumulated profits and reserves must not exceed 10% of paid-up capital plus free reserves = 10% × (₹80,00,000 + ₹25,00,000) = ₹10,50,000 - the required ₹6,57,500 is within this cap.</p>
<p><strong>Condition III:</strong> the balance of reserves after the drawal, ₹18,42,500 (₹25,00,000 − ₹6,57,500), must not fall below 15% of paid-up capital, i.e. ₹12,00,000 (15% of ₹80,00,000) - satisfied.</p>
<p>All three conditions being satisfied, XYZ Ltd. can withdraw <strong>₹6,57,500</strong> from the accumulated General Reserve. (Revaluation Reserve, not being a free reserve, is not counted in this computation.)</p>

> **Common Mistake to Avoid:** A common error here is including the Revaluation Reserve as part of free reserves when applying the three conditions, when it is explicitly excluded because it represents an unrealised, notional gain.

**Q3.**

<p><strong>Amount that can be drawn from reserves for 9% dividend:</strong> 9% dividend on ₹95,00,000 = ₹8,55,000; less current year profit available (₹3,75,000) = amount which can be utilised from reserves ₹4,80,000.</p>
<p><strong>Conditions per Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014:</strong></p>
<p><strong>Condition I:</strong> Since 9% is lower than the average rate of dividend (12.5%), 9% dividend can be declared - satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>Condition II:</strong> Maximum amount that can be drawn from accumulated profits and reserves must not exceed 10% of paid-up capital plus free reserves, i.e. ₹11,35,000 [10% of (95,00,000+18,50,000)] - satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>Condition III:</strong> The balance of reserves after drawal, ₹13,70,000 (₹18,50,000−₹4,80,000), must not fall below 15% of paid up capital, i.e. ₹14,25,000 (15% of ₹95,00,000) - NOT satisfied.</p>
<p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> Since Condition III is not satisfied, DAY Ltd cannot declare dividend @ 9%. For all three conditions to be satisfied, DAY Ltd. can maximum utilize ₹4,25,000 (bringing General Reserve down to exactly ₹14,25,000, the 15% floor) and pay dividend only to that extent. (Revaluation Reserve is not a Free Reserve and is excluded from this computation throughout.)</p>

> **Examiner's Comment:** Many examinees were unfamiliar with the three conditions for declaring dividends from accumulated profits. They failed to compute the dividend amount correctly. Some incorrectly considered Revaluation Reserves as Free Reserves, which led to wrong calculations of available profits and post-withdrawal reserve balances. As a result, they could not determine the permissible withdrawal from General Reserves under the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014.

**Q4.**

<div>
<p>Schedule III has come into force for the Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss Account prepared for the financial year commencing on or after 1st April, 2023. As per Part I of the Schedule III, a company should, inter alia, disclose in notes to accounts for the period of 5 years immediately preceding the balance sheet date (31st March, 2024 in the instant case) the aggregate number and class of shares allotted as fully paid-up bonus shares. Therefore, non-disclosure of source from which bonus shares have been issued does not violate the Schedule III to the Companies Act.</p>
