PYQ May 2026 · Q3(a) · Marks: 6 · Approx Time: 11 minutes (approx.) · M3_C11_U2 : Cash Flow Statement / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.4.2
Question:

Maharishi Ltd, gives you the following information for the year ended 31st March, 2026: (i) The company sells goods for cash only. Cost of goods sold was 60% of sales. Closing inventory was higher than opening inventory by ₹34,400. Trade payables on 31st March, 2026 exceed the balance of trade payables on 31st March, 2025 by ₹18,400. (ii) Net profit before taxation was ₹11,04,000. Tax paid amounted to ₹5,60,000. Depreciation on fixed assets for the year was ₹2,52,000. Outstanding expenses on 31st March, 2025 and 31st March, 2026 totalled ₹65,600 and ₹72,800 respectively. (iii) New machinery costing ₹8,22,000 was purchased during the year 2025-26. (iv) Issued Bonds of ₹5,00,000 at face value in exchange for plant assets on 30th March, 2026. (v) A right issue was made of 40,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹4 per share. The entire money was received with application. (vi) Investment costing ₹1,20,000 were sold at a profit of ₹20,000. (vii) Dividends paid during the year ₹3,31,200. (viii) Cash and cash Equivalents on 31st March, 2025 ₹1,71,040.

You are required to prepare a statement of cash flows as per AS-3 (Revised), using indirect method and ascertain the closing balance of cash and cash equivalent on 31st March, 2026. (6 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

Cash Flow Statement for the year ended 31 March, 2026 (Indirect Method as per AS-3)

(A) Cash Flow from Operating Activities₹
Net Profit before taxation11,04,000
Add: Depreciation2,52,000
Less: Profit on sale of investment(20,000)
Operating Profit before Working Capital changes13,36,000
Less: Increase in Inventory(34,400)
Add: Increase in Trade Payables18,400
Add: Increase in Outstanding Expenses (72,800−65,600)7,200
Cash generated from operations13,27,200
Less: Income Tax Paid(5,60,000)
Net Cash from Operating Activities (A)7,67,200
(B) Cash Flow from Investing Activities
Purchase of Machinery(8,22,000)
Sale of Investment (1,20,000 cost + 20,000 profit)1,40,000
Net Cash used in Investing Activities (B)(6,82,000)
(C) Cash Flow from Financing Activities
Proceeds from Rights Issue (40,000 shares × ₹14)5,60,000
Less: Dividend Paid(3,31,200)
Net Cash from Financing Activities (C)2,28,800
Net Increase in Cash and Cash Equivalents (A+B+C)3,14,000
Add: Cash and Cash Equivalents at the beginning (31.03.2025)1,71,040
Cash and Cash Equivalents at the end (31.03.2026)4,85,040

Note: The bonds issued at face value in exchange for plant assets (a non-cash financing/investing transaction) are correctly excluded from the cash flow statement itself, per AS 3's exclusion of non-cash transactions.

Author’s Note: Some examinees included the ₹5,00,000 bonds-for-plant-assets exchange as a cash flow line item, not recognising it as a non-cash financing and investing transaction that AS 3 requires to be disclosed separately (in notes) rather than within the cash flow statement itself.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. RTP September 2025 · Q7 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M3_C11_U2 : Cash Flow Statement / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.4.2
Question:

Kiran Ltd. Balance Sheets (2024/2025): Share capital 12,00,000/12,00,000; P&L A/c 8,50,000/10,00,000; Long Term Loans 10,00,000/10,60,000; Trade payables 3,50,000/4,00,000; Fixed Assets 17,00,000/20,00,000; Investment in shares 2,00,000/2,00,000; Inventory 6,80,000/7,00,000; Trade receivables 7,20,000/6,60,000; Cash 60,000/70,000; Bills Receivable 40,000/30,000. Income Statement 2025: Sales 40,80,000; Cost of sales 27,20,000; Gross Profit 13,60,000; Admin expenses 4,60,000; Depreciation 2,20,000; Operating Profit 6,80,000; Dividend received 50,000; Interest paid 1,40,000; PBT 5,90,000; Tax 2,60,000; PAT 3,30,000. Retained earnings: Opening 8,50,000 + Profit 3,30,000 − Dividend paid 1,80,000 = Closing 10,00,000. Prepare the Cash Flow Statement using the indirect method.

Answer / Solution:

(i) Cash flows from operating activities: PBT 5,90,000 + Depreciation 2,20,000 + Interest (non-operating) 1,40,000 = 9,50,000; less Dividend received (non-operating) (50,000) = Operating Profit before WC changes 9,00,000; add Decrease in Bills Receivable 10,000, Decrease in Trade receivables 60,000, Increase in Trade payables 50,000 = 10,20,000; less Increase in inventory (20,000) = Cash flow from operations before tax 10,00,000; less Tax paid (2,60,000) = Cash flow from operating activities: ₹7,40,000.

(ii) Cash flows from investing activities: Purchase of fixed assets (20,00,000+2,20,000 depreciation−17,00,000 opening = 5,20,000) outflow (5,20,000); Dividend on investments received 50,000 = Cash used in investing activities: ₹(4,70,000).

(iii) Cash flows from financing activities: Long term loan taken 60,000; Interest paid (1,40,000); Dividend paid (1,80,000) = Cash used in financing activities: ₹(2,60,000).

Net increase in cash: ₹10,000 (7,40,000−4,70,000−2,60,000). Opening cash 60,000 + 10,000 = Closing cash balance ₹70,000 (matches given).

Author’s Note: Some examinees computed the fixed-asset purchase figure without adding back the year's depreciation to the net book value movement, understating the actual cash outflow for the purchase.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q8 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M3_C11_U2 : Cash Flow Statement / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.6
Question:

Garden Ltd. acquired plant and machinery for ₹20 lakhs and sold furniture and fixtures for ₹5 lakhs in the same year. Can the company disclose net cash outflow towards purchase of fixed assets in the cash flow statement per AS 3?

Answer / Solution:

No. Per AS 3 para 21, major classes of gross cash receipts and gross cash payments from investing and financing activities must be reported separately, except for the specific items permitted to be reported net under paras 22 and 24 — acquisition and disposal of fixed assets is not among those permitted items. The company cannot net the ₹20 lakh purchase against the ₹5 lakh disposal; both must be shown as separate gross cash flow lines.

Author’s Note: Some examinees assumed netting was acceptable since both items relate to fixed assets, not checking the specific, limited list of items AS 3 actually permits to be reported net.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
