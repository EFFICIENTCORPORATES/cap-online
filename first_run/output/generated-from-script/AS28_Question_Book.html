MTP May 2024 Set 1 · Q1(c) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 7.7: Impairment loss = carrying amount less recoverable amount (higher of net selling price and value in use) Compulsory
Question:

X Ltd. purchased a Property, Plant and Equipment four years ago for ₹150 lakhs and depreciates it at 10% p.a. on straight line method. At the end of the fourth year, it has revalued the asset at ₹75 lakhs and has written off the loss on revaluation to the profit and loss account. However, on the date of revaluation, the market price is ₹67.50 lakhs and expected disposal costs are ₹3 lakhs. What will be the treatment in respect of impairment loss on the basis that fair value for revaluation purpose is determined by market value and the value in use is estimated at ₹60 lakhs?

Answer / Solution:

Treatment of Impairment Loss: As per AS 28 "Impairment of assets", if the recoverable amount (higher of net selling price and its value in use) of an asset is less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset should be reduced to its recoverable amount. In the given case, net selling price is ₹64.50 lakhs (₹67.50 lakhs − ₹3 lakhs) and value in use is ₹60 lakhs. Therefore, recoverable amount will be ₹64.50 lakhs. Impairment loss will be calculated as ₹10.50 lakhs [₹75 lakhs (Carrying Amount after revaluation – Refer Working Note) less ₹64.50 lakhs (Recoverable Amount)].

Thus impairment loss of ₹10.50 lakhs should be recognised as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss immediately since there was downward revaluation of asset which was already charged to Statement of Profit and Loss.

Working Note — Calculation of carrying amount of the Property, Plant and Equipment at the end of the fourth year on revaluation (₹ in lakhs):

Purchase price of a Property, Plant and Equipment150.00
Less: Depreciation for four years [(150 lakhs / 10 years) × 4 years](60.00)
Carrying value at the end of fourth year90.00
Less: Downward revaluation charged to profit and loss account(15.00)
Revalued carrying amount75.00
Author’s Note: A common error is using net selling price or value in use alone instead of the HIGHER of the two as the recoverable amount, or crediting the impairment loss against a revaluation reserve balance that does not exist here (the earlier downward revaluation was already charged to profit and loss, leaving no reserve to absorb the fresh impairment loss).

PYQ May 2025 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 2] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 7.7: Impairment loss on a previously-revalued asset set off first against the remaining revaluation reserve balance, before charging any excess to profit and loss
Question:

A machine was acquired by Zest Ltd. on 01/04/2019 for ₹60 lakhs. It had a useful life of 6 years. The machine is depreciated on straight line basis and does not carry any residual value. On 01/04/2022, the carrying value of the machine was reassessed at ₹36 lakhs. The surplus arising out of the revaluation being credited to revaluation reserve.

For the year ended March 2024, conditions indicating an impairment of the existed machine and the amount recoverable ascertained to be ₹9 lakhs.

You are required to calculate the loss on impairment of the machine and show how this loss is to be treated in the books of Zest Ltd. The company had followed the policy of writing down the revaluation surplus by the increased charge of depreciation resulting from the revaluation. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

Statement Showing Impairment Loss (₹ in lakhs):

Carrying amount of the machine as on 1st April, 201960
Depreciation for 3 years i.e. 2019-2020 to 2021-2022 (60/6 × 3)(30)
Carrying amount as on 31.03.202230
Add: Upward Revaluation (credited to Revaluation Reserve account)6
Carrying amount of the machine as on 1st April, 2022 (revalued)36
Less: Depreciation for 2 years i.e. 2022-2023 & 2023-2024 (36/3 × 2)(24)
Carrying amount as on 31.03.202412
Less: Recoverable amount(9)
Impairment loss3
Less: Balance in revaluation reserve as on 31.03.2024 (6), less enhanced depreciation met from revaluation reserve 2022-2023 & 2023-2024 [(12−10)×2 years] (4)(2)
Impairment loss set off against revaluation reserve balance as per para 58 of AS 28 "Impairment of Assets"(2)
Impairment Loss to be debited to profit and loss account1
Author’s Note: A common error here is treating the entire impairment loss as a straight debit to the Profit and Loss Account without first checking for, and setting off against, any remaining balance in the Revaluation Reserve relating to the same asset — only the loss in excess of that reserve balance should reach the Profit and Loss Account.

PYQ May 2026 · Q1(b) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.8: Cost of installation - directly attributable labour/supervisor/testing costs; deferred-credit interest excluded  +  Topic 7.13/7.16: Impairment loss on a revalued asset - first set off against the revaluation reserve balance, remainder to P&L Compulsory
Question:

On 1 April, 2022, Sky Limited purchased a machinery for ₹7,35,000 (inclusive of GST @5%, input credit available for entire amount). Other installation expenses: Cost of preparation of site ₹16,500; Total Labour charges ₹75,000 (200 of 500 total men hours spent on installation); Total salary of supervisor ₹34,000 (25% of time spent on installation); Interest charges paid to supplier for deferred credit ₹8,000; Test run and experimental production expenses ₹28,200; Consultancy charges to architect for plant set up ₹18,800; Depreciation on assets used for installation ₹8,000. The machine was ready for use on October 1, 2022 but was put to use from February 1, 2023 (further expenses of ₹10,800 incurred due to this delay). Estimated useful life 10 years, SLM, no residual value.

On September 30, 2025, the company revalued the machine at ₹7 lakhs, surplus credited to revaluation reserve. For the year ended March 31, 2026, conditions indicating impairment existed and the recoverable amount was ascertained to be only ₹3 Lakhs.

You are required to: (i) Calculate the value at which the machine should be capitalized and the amount of depreciation to be charged for the year ending March 31, 2023 in the books of Sky Limited. (ii) Calculate the loss on impairment of the machine and show how this loss is to be treated in the books of Sky Limited. The company had followed the policy of writing down the revaluation surplus by the increased charge of depreciation resulting from the revaluation. (5 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

(i) Calculation of Cost of Machinery:

Purchase Price7,35,000
Add: Site Preparation Cost16,500
Labour charges (75,000 × 200/500)30,000
Supervisor's Salary (25% of 34,000)8,500
Test run and experimental production charges28,200
Architect Fees for set up18,800
Depreciation on assets used for installation8,000
Total Cost of Asset8,45,000
Less: GST credit receivable(35,000)
Value to be capitalized8,10,000
Depreciation to be charged on 31.3.2023 (for 6 months, SLM 10 year)40,500

Note: (i) Further expenses of ₹10,800 from 1.10.2022 to 1.2.2023 are charged to profit and loss A/c, as the plant was ready for production on 1.10.2022 (already capitalised) – the delay in actual use does not extend capitalisation. (ii) Interest charges of ₹8,000 paid on deferred credit terms to the supplier of the plant (not a qualifying asset) are not directly attributable costs and are written off to the Statement of Profit and Loss in the period incurred.

(ii) Statement showing impairment loss and its treatment:

Carrying amount of the machine as on 01/10/20228,10,000
Less: Depreciation for 3 years (8,10,000 x 3/10)(2,43,000)
Carrying amount as on 30/09/20255,67,000
Add: Upward revaluation (credited to revaluation reserve)1,33,000
Carrying amount of the machine as on 30/09/2025 (post-revaluation)7,00,000
Less: Depreciation for 6 months up to 31/03/2026 (7,00,000 x 0.5/7)(50,000)
Carrying amount as on 31/03/20266,50,000
Less: Recoverable amount(3,00,000)
Impairment Loss3,50,000
Less: balance in revaluation reserve as on 31/03/2026 [1,33,000 − (50,000−40,500)](1,23,500)
Impairment Loss to be debited to Profit & Loss Account2,26,500

Depreciation for 01/10/2025 to 31/03/2026 (7,00,000 x 0.5/7) = ₹50,000. Depreciation set off against Revaluation Reserve = (1,33,000 x 0.5/7) = ₹9,500 (Impairment loss set off against revaluation reserve balance as per para 58 of AS 28 "Impairment of Assets").

Author’s Note: Many examinees debited the entire ₹3,50,000 impairment loss to the profit and loss account, not applying AS 28's requirement (para 58) to first set off an impairment loss on a revalued asset against the remaining balance in that asset's revaluation reserve before charging the balance to P&L. Several also capitalised the ₹10,800 delay-related expenses instead of expensing them, since the asset was already ready for use on 1st October, 2022.

PYQ September 2025 · Q3(b) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · Topic 7.6: CGU impairment loss allocated first to goodwill, then pro-rata to other assets
Question:

Grace Limited acquired the business (a cash-generating unit) of Venus Limited on 31st March 2023 for ₹8,000 lakhs: Fair value of identifiable assets ₹6,000 lakhs, Goodwill (amortised over 5 years) ₹2,000 lakhs. Anticipated useful life of acquired assets: 8 years, nil residual value, straight-line. On 31st March 2025, significant decline in production estimated due to government policy changes; net selling price of identifiable assets ₹3,000 lakhs. Cash flow forecast for next 6 years (₹ lakhs): 1,000/800/700/800/600/500. Discount rate 10% (PV factors: 0.909, 0.826, 0.751, 0.683, 0.621, 0.564). Calculate: (i) Value in use at 31 March 2025; (ii) Impairment loss for year ended 31 March 2025; (iii) Revised carrying amount of asset.

Answer / Solution:

(i) Value in Use: 1,000×0.909=909.00 + 800×0.826=660.80 + 700×0.751=525.70 + 800×0.683=546.40 + 600×0.621=372.60 + 500×0.564=282.00 = ₹3,296.50 lakhs, rounded to ₹3,297 lakhs.

Carrying amount at 31.3.2025 (2 years post-acquisition): Goodwill 2,000 − amortisation (2,000/5×2=800) = 1,200. Identifiable assets 6,000 − depreciation (6,000/8×2=1,500) = 4,500. Total CGU carrying amount = 5,700.

Recoverable amount = higher of Value in Use (3,297) and net selling price of identifiable assets (3,000, no CGU-wide fair-value-less-costs-to-sell given) = 3,297 (since 3,297 > 3,000).

(ii) Impairment loss = Carrying amount (5,700) − Recoverable amount (3,297) = ₹2,403 lakhs.

(iii) Allocation: per AS 28, impairment is allocated first to goodwill (written down to zero: full 1,200 absorbed), then the remainder (2,403−1,200=1,203) to identifiable assets pro-rata: revised carrying amount of identifiable asset = 4,500−1,203 = ₹3,297 lakhs (goodwill: Nil).

Examiner’s Comment: A majority of examinees were unable to compute the impairment loss and allocate the same appropriately among the assets of the CGU as per the requirements of AS 28 "Impairment of Assets". Many examinees failed to determine the value in use, impairment loss and the revised carrying amount of assets, evidencing an inadequate understanding of the impairment principles.

RTP January 2026 · Q19 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 7.4: Impairment loss = carrying amount less recoverable amount (higher of net selling price and value in use)
Question:

X Ltd. purchased a fixed asset four years ago for ₹150 lakhs, depreciating at 10% p.a. SLM. At the end of the fourth year, it revalued the asset to ₹75 lakhs, writing off the revaluation loss to P&L. On the revaluation date, market price is ₹67.50 lakhs, expected disposal costs ₹3 lakhs, and value in use is estimated at ₹60 lakhs. What is the treatment of impairment loss, given fair value for revaluation is market-value-based?

Answer / Solution:

W.N. — Carrying amount at revaluation: Purchase price 150.00, less depreciation for 4 years (150/10 × 4) 60.00 = carrying value before revaluation 90.00, less downward revaluation charged to P&L (90−75) 15.00 = revalued carrying amount 75.00 lakhs.

Recoverable amount = higher of net selling price and value in use. Net selling price = market price 67.50 − disposal costs 3.00 = 64.50 lakhs. Value in use = 60 lakhs. Recoverable amount = ₹64.50 lakhs (the higher of the two).

Impairment loss = carrying amount (75.00) − recoverable amount (64.50) = ₹10.50 lakhs, recognised as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss immediately (since the prior downward revaluation was already charged to P&L rather than to a revaluation reserve, there is no reserve balance to absorb this further impairment against).

Author’s Note: Some examinees used the market price directly as net selling price without deducting disposal costs, or used net selling price as recoverable amount without comparing it against value in use to find the higher of the two.

RTP May 2026 · Q16 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 7.8: Identification of the cash-generating unit - independence of cash inflows
Question:

A publisher owns 150 magazine titles of which 70 were purchased and 80 were self-created. The price paid for a purchased magazine title is recognised as an intangible asset. The costs of creating magazine titles and maintaining the existing titles are recognised as an expense when incurred. Cash inflows from direct sales and advertising are identifiable for each magazine title. Titles are managed by customer segments. The level of advertising income for a magazine title depends on the range of titles in the customer segment to which the magazine title relates. Management has a policy to abandon old titles before the end of their economic lives and replace them immediately with new titles for the same customer segment. Whether it will be a cash-generating unit as per AS 28?

Answer / Solution:

It is likely that the recoverable amount of an individual magazine title can be assessed. Even though the level of advertising income for a title is influenced, to a certain extent, by the other titles in the customer segment, cash inflows from direct sales and advertising are identifiable for each title. In addition, although titles are managed by customer segments, decisions to abandon titles are made on an individual title basis. Therefore, it is likely that individual magazine titles generate cash inflows that are largely independent one from another and that each magazine title is a separate cash-generating unit.

Author’s Note: Some examinees identified the customer segment as the cash-generating unit because titles are managed and reported at that level, without recognising that CGU identification turns on independence of cash inflows, not on the level at which management happens to monitor performance.
