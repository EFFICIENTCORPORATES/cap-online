RTP January 2026 · Q4 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U1 : AS 4 — Contingencies and Events occurring after the Balance Sheet Date / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.5
Question:

Zenith Industries Ltd., preparing FY2024-25 financial statements. After year-end: (a) Fire on 10th April, 2025 destroyed uninsured stock worth ₹10,00,000, salvage recovered ₹2,00,000. (b) Legal claim filed 18th April, 2025 alleging misleading advertisement, damages claimed ₹20,00,000. (c) Cheques in Hand: company proposes recognising all cheques dated 31st March, 2025 or earlier received from stockists as "Cheques in Hand" under Cash and Cash Equivalents, reducing Trade Receivables — but two categories exist: (i) cheques physically collected by marketing personnel on/before 31st March, 2025; (ii) cheques sent by courier on/before 31st March but received only in April 2025. All cheques were deposited and realized in April 2025. Comment on the accounting treatment for each.

Answer / Solution:

(a) Fire and loss of uninsured stock: occurred after the balance sheet date and does not relate to any condition existing on 31st March, 2025 — a non-adjusting event. Given its materiality (uninsured stock, future risk exposure), the fire and uninsured position must be disclosed, including its nature, estimated financial effect, and future vulnerability if determinable.

(b) Legal suit: initiated after the balance sheet date, no additional evidence of a pre-existing condition — also non-adjusting. Per AS 4 para 16, disclose as a contingent liability, including the nature of the contingency, estimated financial effect, and outcome uncertainties.

(c)(i) Cheques collected by marketing personnel: physically collected on or before 31st March, 2025 — since marketing personnel act as company representatives, this constitutes receipt by the company itself, discharging the stockist's liability. This is an adjusting event: reduce Trade Receivables and recognise as Cheques in Hand under Cash and Cash Equivalents, with appropriate accounting-policy disclosure.

(c)(ii) Cheques sent by courier, received only in April: though dated on/before 31st March, they were not in the company's possession until after the balance sheet date — a non-adjusting event. Account for these in the period actually received (April 2025), regardless of the cheque date; no disclosure needed since the event does not materially affect the financial position.

Author’s Note: Some examinees treated all cheques dated on or before 31st March as adjusting events based on the cheque date alone, missing that physical possession by the company (or its representative) by the balance sheet date, not the cheque's date, is what actually determines adjusting vs. non-adjusting treatment.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q9(i) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U1 : AS 4 — Contingencies and Events occurring after the Balance Sheet Date / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.5
Question:

While preparing its financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2025, B Limited has come across the following events which took place after 31st March, 2025. The financial statements of the company were approved by the Board of Directors on 30th May, 2025: B Limited started negotiations with S Limited during 2024-25 to acquire their Consumer Durables Business for ₹400 lakhs. The deal was finalized on 20th April, 2025 and B Limited invested ₹275 lakhs before 30th May, 2025. You are required to state with reasons, whether the aforesaid event is an adjusting or non-adjusting event as per the provisions of the relevant AS.

Answer / Solution:

AS 4 (Revised) defines “Events Occurring after the Balance Sheet Date” as those significant events, both favourable and unfavourable, that occur between the balance sheet date and the date on which the financial statements are approved by the Approving Authority in the case of a company.

The acquisition of Consumer Durables Business of S Limited by B Limited is an event occurring after the balance sheet date. However, it is a non-adjusting event as it does not affect the determination and the condition of the amounts stated in the financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2025. The disclosure should be made in the report of the approving authority about the investment of ₹275 lakhs to enable the users of financial statements to make proper evaluations and decisions.

Author’s Note: Some examinees classified the acquisition as an adjusting event on the basis that negotiations had begun during 2024-25, not recognising that the deal was only finalised after the balance sheet date and so does not reflect conditions existing at 31st March, 2025.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q9(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U1 : AS 4 — Contingencies and Events occurring after the Balance Sheet Date / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.4
Question:

During the course of audit for the year ended 31st March, 2025, the auditor discovered a fraud of ₹1,25,000 done by the accountant of the company. The fraud was discovered on 20th April, 2025. The accountant of the company had left the job and absconded on 29th March, 2025. You are required to state with reasons, whether the aforesaid event is an adjusting or non-adjusting event as per the provisions of the relevant AS.

Answer / Solution:

This is an adjusting event. If a fraud of the accounting period is detected after the balance sheet date but before approval of the financial statements, it is necessary to recognize the loss of ₹1,25,000 and adjust the accounts of the company for the year ended 31st March, 2025.

Author’s Note: Many examinees treated the fraud's discovery date (after year end) as decisive and classified it as non-adjusting, rather than recognising that the fraud itself related to the accounting period under audit and so required recognition in that period's accounts.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q9(iii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U1 : AS 4 — Contingencies and Events occurring after the Balance Sheet Date / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.5
Question:

A fire engulfed the warehouse of B Limited on 13th April, 2025 and destroyed inventory worth ₹15 lakhs lying there. You are required to state with reasons, whether the aforesaid event is an adjusting or non-adjusting event as per the provisions of the relevant AS.

Answer / Solution:

This is a non-adjusting event. The destruction of warehouse due to fire did not exist on the balance sheet date i.e. 31st March, 2025. Therefore, loss occurred due to fire is not to be recognized in the financial year 2024-25. Considering that the going concern assumption is still valid, the fact of fire together with a loss of ₹15 lakhs should be disclosed in the report of the approving authority for 2024-25 to enable the users of financial statements to make proper evaluations and decisions.

Author’s Note: A few examinees recognised the fire loss directly in the 2024-25 financial statements instead of treating it as a non-adjusting, disclosure-only event, incorrectly assuming any post-year-end loss of this size must be adjusted for.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
