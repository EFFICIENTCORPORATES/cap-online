RTP January 2026 · Q9(i) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.5
Question:

Machinery purchased 1st April, 2020 for ₹10 lakhs, useful life 5 years, residual value (at 2020 prices) ₹10 lakhs. Calculate depreciation for the year ended 31st March, 2025 per AS 10 (Revised).

Answer / Solution:

Nil. Since the residual value (₹10 lakhs) equals the cost (₹10 lakhs), the depreciable amount is nil; depreciation is correctly zero throughout the asset's life.

Author’s Note: Some examinees computed depreciation on the full cost, ignoring the stated residual value entirely.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP January 2026 · Q9(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.5
Question:

The company owns a piece of land acquired for ₹50 lakhs, held for use in its factory operations. Calculate depreciation for the year ended 31st March, 2025.

Answer / Solution:

Nil. Land has an unlimited useful life (unless it is a wasting asset such as a quarry) and is therefore not depreciated.

Author’s Note: A basic but recurring error is depreciating land alongside buildings without separating the two components.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP January 2026 · Q9(iii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.6
Question:

Zenith Ltd. constructed a machine for its own use, total cost ₹5,00,000, completed 1st April, 2024, useful life 10 years; the company did not commence using the machine until 31st March, 2025. Calculate depreciation for the year ended 31st March, 2025.

Answer / Solution:

₹50,000 (₹5,00,000/10 years). Depreciation begins from the date the machine is ready for use (1st April, 2024), regardless of when actual use commenced — the fact that it sat idle for a period after being ready is not relevant to when depreciation begins.

Author’s Note: Some examinees charged no depreciation at all, reasoning the machine was never actually used during the year, missing that "ready for use" (not actual use) triggers depreciation.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP January 2026 · Q9(iv) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.9
Question:

Another machine was purchased 1st April, 2022, for ₹50,000, useful life 5 years, nil residual value. On 1st April, 2024, management decided the machine would now be used for only two more years. Calculate depreciation for the year ended 31st March, 2025.

Answer / Solution:

Original SLM depreciation = ₹50,000/5 = ₹10,000/year. Net book value on 1st April, 2024 (after 2 years) = 50,000−20,000 = ₹30,000. With the revised remaining useful life of 2 years, the unamortised cost is spread over the revised remaining life: 30,000/2 = ₹15,000/year for the next 2 years.

Author’s Note: Some examinees continued the original ₹10,000/year rate, not recognising that a revised useful-life estimate requires recomputing the annual charge over the remaining life going forward.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q12(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.8
Question:

On 1st December 2024, Precision Tools Ltd. replaced the motor of an old machine with a new one costing ₹1,20,000, improving output capacity by 15%. The old motor had cost ₹50,000 and accumulated depreciation ₹35,000. Calculate the amount to be capitalized.

Answer / Solution:

Carrying amount of the old motor = 50,000−35,000 = ₹15,000, which is derecognised (written off) since it is being replaced. The new motor's acquisition cost of ₹1,20,000 is capitalised as a separate component (component accounting under AS 10). Net effect: ₹1,20,000 capitalised, ₹15,000 carrying amount of the old motor removed/expensed.

Author’s Note: Some examinees simply added the new motor's cost to the machine's carrying value without also derecognising the replaced motor's remaining carrying amount.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q12(iii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.4
Question:

On 15th March 2025, Precision Tools Ltd. shifted a machine from one factory to another, incurring: Dismantling cost ₹12,000; Transport and installation ₹18,000; Loss due to damage in transit ₹6,000. Calculate the amount to be capitalized.

Answer / Solution:

Nil is capitalised — costs of relocating or reorganising part or all of an enterprise's operations are not included in the carrying amount of PPE under AS 10. The entire ₹36,000 (12,000+18,000+6,000) is charged off to the Profit and Loss Account.

Author’s Note: Some examinees capitalised the transport and installation cost, reasoning it was directly attributable to the machine, missing AS 10's specific exclusion of relocation costs regardless of their connection to a particular asset.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ May 2026 · Q1(b) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.8  +  M2_C5_U7 : AS 28 — Impairment of Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 7.13/7.16 Compulsory
Question:

On 1 April, 2022, Sky Limited purchased a machinery for ₹7,35,000 (inclusive of GST @5%, input credit available for entire amount). Other installation expenses: Cost of preparation of site ₹16,500; Total Labour charges ₹75,000 (200 of 500 total men hours spent on installation); Total salary of supervisor ₹34,000 (25% of time spent on installation); Interest charges paid to supplier for deferred credit ₹8,000; Test run and experimental production expenses ₹28,200; Consultancy charges to architect for plant set up ₹18,800; Depreciation on assets used for installation ₹8,000. The machine was ready for use on October 1, 2022 but was put to use from February 1, 2023 (further expenses of ₹10,800 incurred due to this delay). Estimated useful life 10 years, SLM, no residual value.

On September 30, 2025, the company revalued the machine at ₹7 lakhs, surplus credited to revaluation reserve. For the year ended March 31, 2026, conditions indicating impairment existed and the recoverable amount was ascertained to be only ₹3 Lakhs.

You are required to: (i) Calculate the value at which the machine should be capitalized and the amount of depreciation to be charged for the year ending March 31, 2023 in the books of Sky Limited. (ii) Calculate the loss on impairment of the machine and show how this loss is to be treated in the books of Sky Limited. The company had followed the policy of writing down the revaluation surplus by the increased charge of depreciation resulting from the revaluation. (5 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

(i) Calculation of Cost of Machinery:

Purchase Price7,35,000
Add: Site Preparation Cost16,500
Labour charges (75,000 × 200/500)30,000
Supervisor's Salary (25% of 34,000)8,500
Test run and experimental production charges28,200
Architect Fees for set up18,800
Depreciation on assets used for installation8,000
Total Cost of Asset8,45,000
Less: GST credit receivable(35,000)
Value to be capitalized8,10,000
Depreciation to be charged on 31.3.2023 (for 6 months, SLM 10 year)40,500

Note: (i) Further expenses of ₹10,800 from 1.10.2022 to 1.2.2023 are charged to profit and loss A/c, as the plant was ready for production on 1.10.2022 (already capitalised) – the delay in actual use does not extend capitalisation. (ii) Interest charges of ₹8,000 paid on deferred credit terms to the supplier of the plant (not a qualifying asset) are not directly attributable costs and are written off to the Statement of Profit and Loss in the period incurred.

(ii) Statement showing impairment loss and its treatment:

Carrying amount of the machine as on 01/10/20228,10,000
Less: Depreciation for 3 years (8,10,000 x 3/10)(2,43,000)
Carrying amount as on 30/09/20255,67,000
Add: Upward revaluation (credited to revaluation reserve)1,33,000
Carrying amount of the machine as on 30/09/2025 (post-revaluation)7,00,000
Less: Depreciation for 6 months up to 31/03/2026 (7,00,000 x 0.5/7)(50,000)
Carrying amount as on 31/03/20266,50,000
Less: Recoverable amount(3,00,000)
Impairment Loss3,50,000
Less: balance in revaluation reserve as on 31/03/2026 [1,33,000 − (50,000−40,500)](1,23,500)
Impairment Loss to be debited to Profit & Loss Account2,26,500

Depreciation for 01/10/2025 to 31/03/2026 (7,00,000 x 0.5/7) = ₹50,000. Depreciation set off against Revaluation Reserve = (1,33,000 x 0.5/7) = ₹9,500 (Impairment loss set off against revaluation reserve balance as per para 58 of AS 28 "Impairment of Assets").

Author’s Note: Many examinees debited the entire ₹3,50,000 impairment loss to the profit and loss account, not applying AS 28's requirement (para 58) to first set off an impairment loss on a revalued asset against the remaining balance in that asset's revaluation reserve before charging the balance to P&L. Several also capitalised the ₹10,800 delay-related expenses instead of expensing them, since the asset was already ready for use on 1st October, 2022.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q10 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U2 : AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.8  +  M2_C5_U4 : AS 16 — Borrowing Costs / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 4.4/4.9
Question:

On 1st April, 2024, Y Limited purchased an industrial plot of land for ₹50,00,000 for the purpose of constructing a new factory. This cost of ₹50,00,000 included legal cost of ₹2,50,000 incurred for the purpose of acquisition of this plot. The construction of the factory commenced on 1st May, 2024. Y Limited provides you with the following details of the costs incurred in relation to the above construction:

ParticularsAmount (₹)
Preparation and levelling of the land1,60,000
Employment costs of construction workers (per month)58,000
Purchase of Raw materials for the construction42,48,000
Cost of relocating the workers to new factory for work75,000
Costs of inaugural function of the new factory on 1st January, 202595,000
Overhead costs incurred directly on the construction of the factory (per month)50,000

The construction of the factory was completed on 31st December, 2024 and production could commence on 1st February, 2025. The overall useful life of the factory building was estimated at 40 years from the date of completion. However, it was estimated that the roof would need to be replaced 20 years after the date of completion. The cost of replacing the roof at current prices would be 25% of the total cost of the building. The construction of the factory was partly financed by a loan of ₹56,00,000 raised from State Bank of India on 1st April, 2024 at an interest rate of 9% per annum. During the period when the loan proceeds had been fully utilized to finance the construction, Y Limited earned an income of ₹50,000 from the temporary investment of the proceeds. Assume that all of the net finance cost needs to be allocated to the cost of factory (excluding land) and interest cost to be capitalized based on eight months' period.

You are required to compute the carrying amount (i.e. value after charging depreciation) of the Factory in the books of Y Limited as at 31 March, 2025 as per the provisions of the relevant Accounting Standard.

Answer / Solution:

Cost of construction of the factory

Cost of Industrial Plot50,00,000
Preparation and levelling of the land1,60,000
Employment costs of construction workers (₹58,000 per month) for 8 months4,64,000
Purchase of Raw materials for the construction42,48,000
Overhead costs incurred directly on the construction of the factory (₹50,000 per month) for 8 months4,00,000
Interest on ₹56,00,000 for 9 months @ 9% = ₹3,78,000 less ₹50,0003,28,000
Total Cost (A)1,06,00,000
Depreciation (B) (W.N.)43,750
Carrying amount of the factory (A−B)1,05,56,250

Working Note: Calculation of Depreciation

On land of ₹50,00,000: Nil. On remaining cost of Building i.e. ₹1,06,00,000 − ₹50,00,000 = ₹56,00,000:

  • On Building: ₹56,00,000 × 75% / 40 yrs × 3/12 = ₹26,250
  • On roof: ₹56,00,000 × 25% / 20 yrs × 3/12 = ₹17,500
  • Total Depreciation charged = ₹43,750

Cost of relocating the workers to new factory for work and cost of inaugural function of the new factory is not directly attributable to construction cost, hence not considered.

Author’s Note: Many examinees included the relocation cost and the inauguration-function cost in the capitalised cost of the factory, failing to apply AS 10's distinction between costs necessary to bring an asset to its working condition and costs merely associated with commissioning it. Several also capitalised the gross interest of ₹3,78,000 without netting the ₹50,000 temporary-investment income against it, as AS 16 requires.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
