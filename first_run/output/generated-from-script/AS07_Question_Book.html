RTP January 2026 · Q7 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U1 : AS 7 — Construction Contracts / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.4
Question:

A construction contractor entered a fixed-price contract of ₹18,000 lacs to construct a bridge within 3 years, with provisions for variations. (₹ in lacs): Year 1/2/3 — Initial revenue agreed 18,000/18,000/18,000; Variation in revenue (+)400/400 (Years 2,3); Contract costs incurred to date 4,186/12,336*/16,200**; Estimated profit for whole contract 1,900/2,000/2,000. *includes ₹200 lacs standard materials stored at site for use in Year 3. **excludes ₹200 lacs standard material brought forward from Year 2. Year 2's variation was approved by the customer. Compute year-wise: revenue recognised, expenses recognised, contract costs to complete, and profit/loss recognised.

Answer / Solution:

Working — degree of completion each year (costs incurred, excluding unused stored materials, ÷ estimated total cost): Year 1: revenue after variations 18,000, less estimated profit 1,900 = estimated total cost 16,100; actual cost incurred 4,186; degree of completion = 4,186/16,100 = 26%. Year 2: revenue 18,400, less profit 2,000 = total cost 16,400; cost incurred (excluding the ₹200 lacs stored materials not yet used) 12,336−200=12,136; degree of completion = 12,136/16,400 = 74%. Year 3: revenue 18,400, total cost 16,400; cost incurred 16,200+200 (bringing forward the previously-excluded stored material, now used) = 16,400; degree of completion 100%.

Contract costs to complete the remaining work: Year 1: 16,100−4,186=11,914. Year 2: 16,400−12,136=4,264 (plus the ₹200 lacs stored materials earmarked for Year 3, held separately). Year 3: Nil (contract complete).

RTP September 2025 · Q10 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U1 : AS 7 — Construction Contracts / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.5
Question:

A company took a construction contract for ₹100 lakhs in January 2024; 80% completed at cost ₹92 lakhs by 31.3.2025; further ₹23 lakhs estimated to complete; expected loss ₹15 lakhs. Can the company recognise the loss in the FY2024-25 financial statements?

Answer / Solution:

Yes. Per AS 7 paras 31 and 35, an expected loss on a construction contract must be recognised as an expense immediately, regardless of (i) whether work has commenced; (ii) the stage of completion; or (iii) profits expected on other contracts. The company must recognise the ₹15 lakh loss immediately in the year ended 31.3.2025.

Author’s Note: Some examinees deferred the loss recognition to future periods or spread it across the remaining completion percentage, missing AS 7's requirement for immediate full recognition.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
