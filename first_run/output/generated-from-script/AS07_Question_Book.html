MTP September 2025 Set 1 · Q3(a) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.6/1.9/1.10: Percentage-of-completion method with a mid-contract variation changing total contract value and estimated cost
Question:

Constructions Limited is engaged in the business of constructing Flyovers and Railway over bridges. It obtained a contract from Railway Authorities to construct a railway over bridge for ₹400 crores. The construction of the railway over bridge is expected to be completed in 4 years.

At the outset of the contract, it was estimated that the total costs to be incurred will be ₹370 crores but by the end of year 1, this estimate stands revised to ₹375 crores.

During year 3, the Construction Limited has requested for a variation in the contract which is approved by Railway Authorities and accordingly the total contract value will increase by ₹10 crores and costs will increase by ₹7 crores.

The Constructions Limited decided to measure the stage of completion on the basis of the proportion of contract costs incurred to the total estimated contract costs. Contract costs incurred at the end of each year is: Year 1 ₹98.8 crores; Year 2 ₹202.4 crores; Year 3 ₹310 crores (including unused material of 3 crores); Year 4 ₹382 crores.

You are required to: (1) Calculate stage of completion of contract for each year; (2) Profit to be recognised for each year.

(i) Stage of completion = Costs incurred to date / Total estimated costs

RTP January 2025 · Q6 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.6: Percentage of completion method - stage of completion, revenue and profit recognition, updated for a contract-price escalation clause
Question:

Rose Constructions undertake to construct a bridge for the Government of Uttar Pradesh. The construction commenced during the financial year ending 31.03.2024 and is likely to be completed by the next financial year. The contract is for a fixed price of ₹12 crore with an escalation clause. You are given the following information for the year ended 31.03.2024:

Cost incurred upto 31.03.2024₹4 crore
Cost estimated to complete the contract₹6 crore

Escalation in cost was by 5%. Hence, the contract price is also increased by 5%.

You are required to ascertain the stage of completion and compute the amount of revenue and profit to be recognized for the year as per AS 7.

Answer / Solution:
Cost of construction of bridge incurred upto 31.3.2024₹4.00 crore
Add: Estimated future cost₹6.00 crore
Total estimated cost of construction₹10.00 crore
Contract Price (12 crore × 1.05)₹12.60 crore

Stage of completion: Percentage of completion till date to total estimated cost of construction = (4/10)×100 = 40%.

Revenue and Profit to be recognized for the year ended 31st March, 2024 as per AS 7:

Proportion of total contract value recognized as revenue = Contract price × percentage of completion = ₹12.60 crore × 40% = ₹5.04 crore.

Profit for the year ended 31st March, 2024 = ₹5.04 crore − ₹4 crore = ₹1.04 crore.

Author’s Note: A common error is computing the percentage of completion using the original (pre-escalation) contract price or cost estimates instead of updating both cost incurred/estimated-to-complete and contract price for the 5% escalation before applying AS 7's percentage of completion formula.

RTP May 2025 · Q9(i) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.3: Conditions under which one contract document covering multiple assets is treated as separate construction contracts
Question:

AP Ltd., a construction contractor, undertakes the construction of a commercial complex for Kay Ltd. AP Ltd. submitted separate proposals for each of 3 units of the commercial complex. A single agreement is entered into between the two parties. The agreement lays down the value of each of the 3 units, i.e. ₹50 Lakh, ₹60 Lakh and ₹75 Lakh respectively. The agreement also lays down the completion time for each unit. Comment, with reference to AS-7, whether AP Ltd. should treat it as a single contract or three separate contracts.

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 7 'Construction Contracts', when a contract covers a number of assets, the construction of each asset should be treated as a separate construction contract when: (a) separate proposals have been submitted for each asset; (b) each asset has been subject to separate negotiation and the contractor and customer have been able to accept or reject that part of the contract relating to each asset; and (c) the costs and revenues of each asset can be identified.

In the given case, AP Ltd. submitted separate proposals for each of the 3 units, the agreement lays down a separate value and separate completion time for each unit, and the costs/revenues of each unit are identifiable. Therefore, AP Ltd. is required to treat the construction of each unit as a separate construction contract, as the above-mentioned conditions of AS 7 are fulfilled in the given case – notwithstanding that a single agreement document was used.

Author’s Note: A common error is concluding it must be a single contract simply because only one agreement document was signed, missing that AS 7 looks at the three specific conditions (separate proposals, separate negotiability, identifiable costs/revenues) rather than the number of physical documents.

RTP May 2025 · Q9(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.6: Percentage-of-completion revenue recognition together with immediate recognition of the entire foreseeable loss on a loss-making contract
Question:

On 1st December, 2023, GR Construction Co. Ltd. undertook a contract to construct a building for ₹45 lakhs. On 31st March, 2024, the company found that it had already spent ₹32.50 lakhs on the construction. Additional cost of completion is estimated at ₹15.10 lakhs. What amount should be charged to revenue in the final accounts for the year ended 31st March, 2024 as per provisions of AS-7?

Answer / Solution:
₹ in lakhs
Cost of construction incurred till date32.50
Add: Estimated future cost15.10
Total estimated cost of construction47.60

Percentage of completion till date to total estimated cost of construction = (32.50/47.60) × 100 = 68.28%.

Proportion of total contract value recognised as revenue for the year ended 31st March, 2024 per AS 7 (Revised) = Contract price × percentage of completion = ₹45 lakh × 68.28% = ₹30.73 lakhs.

₹ in lakhs
Total cost of construction47.60
Less: Total contract price(45.00)
Total foreseeable loss to be recognized as expense2.60

According to AS 7, when it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue, the expected loss should be recognized as an expense immediately (in full, not spread by percentage of completion).

Author’s Note: A common error is recognising only the current period's proportionate share of the ₹2.60 lakh foreseeable loss (via the 68.28% completion rate) rather than the entire foreseeable loss immediately, once it becomes probable that total costs will exceed total contract revenue.

RTP September 2025 · Q10 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.5: Expected loss on a construction contract recognised immediately in full, regardless of stage of completion
Question:

A company took a construction contract for ₹100 lakhs in January 2024; 80% completed at cost ₹92 lakhs by 31.3.2025; further ₹23 lakhs estimated to complete; expected loss ₹15 lakhs. Can the company recognise the loss in the FY2024-25 financial statements?

Answer / Solution:

Yes. Per AS 7 paras 31 and 35, an expected loss on a construction contract must be recognised as an expense immediately, regardless of (i) whether work has commenced; (ii) the stage of completion; or (iii) profits expected on other contracts. The company must recognise the ₹15 lakh loss immediately in the year ended 31.3.2025.

Author’s Note: Some examinees deferred the loss recognition to future periods or spread it across the remaining completion percentage, missing AS 7's requirement for immediate full recognition.
