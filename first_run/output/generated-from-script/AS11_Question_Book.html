MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q1(a) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U3 : AS 11 — Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.6 Compulsory
Question:

Jared Limited purchased a machine for US$20,000 on 31st December 2024, payment due after four months. The company entered a four-month forward contract at ₹78.85 per US$; the spot rate on 31st December 2024 was ₹77.50 per US$. (i) How should Jared recognise the profit or loss on the forward contract for the year ended 31st March, 2025? (ii) Trade payables include an amount due to Sterling Limited of ₹9,75,000, recorded at US$1=₹75.00 on the purchase date. Exchange rate on balance-sheet date (31st March 2025): US$1=₹79.00. Payment made 1st May 2025 at US$1=₹78.30. Calculate the exchange difference as at 31st March 2025 and on settlement (1st May 2025), and explain the accounting treatment per AS 11.

Answer / Solution:

(i) Forward contract premium/loss: Forward rate 78.85 − Spot rate 77.50 = premium of ₹1.35/US$. Total loss on contract = 20,000 × 1.35 = ₹27,000. Contract period 4 months, of which 3 months fall in the year ended 31st March, 2025: loss to be recognised = 27,000 × 3/4 = ₹20,250 in the year ended 31st March, 2025 (the balance ₹6,750 recognised in the following year).

(ii) Exchange difference on trade payable to Sterling Ltd.: Initial recognition: US$13,000 (9,75,000/75) at ₹75. Balance sheet date rate ₹79 — exchange loss = 13,000 × (79−75) = ₹52,000, charged to the Statement of Profit & Loss for the year ended 31st March, 2025 (per AS 11, monetary item restatement differences are recognised as they arise). Settlement date rate ₹78.30 — exchange gain on settlement = 13,000 × (79−78.30) = ₹9,100, credited to the Statement of Profit & Loss for the year ended 31st March, 2026 (the year of settlement).

Author’s Note: Some examinees recognised the full forward-contract loss in the year of inception rather than time-apportioning it over the contract period falling in each accounting year, or netted the balance-sheet-date exchange loss against the later settlement-date gain instead of recognising each in its own accounting period.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

MTP May 2026 Set 1 · Q1(b) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U3 : AS 11 — The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.4/3.5 Compulsory
Question:

Karna Ltd., an Indian Company, has the following foreign currency transactions during the financial year 2025-26:
(i) On 1st July, 2025, imported goods from Try Ltd., a German based company, amounting to ₹30,96,000.
(ii) On 1st October, 2025, imported plant and machinery from Lucy Ltd., a German based company, for €18,500. The amount was paid on the date of import itself. (Ignore depreciation).
(iii) On 1st December, 2025, exported good on credit to Cream Ltd., a German based company, amounting to ₹50,40,000.
All the above transactions were recorded in the books of account at the prevailing exchange rate on the date of the transactions. Ignore taxes and duty on the above transactions.
Payment due from Cream Ltd. and payment due to Try Ltd. is outstanding as on 31st March, 2026.
Rate of exchange between reporting currency (₹) and foreign currency (€) on different dates are as under: On 1st July, 2025, 1€ = ₹86; On 1st October, 2025, 1€ = ₹88; On 1st December, 2025, 1€ = ₹84; On 31st March, 2026, 1€ = ₹90.
You are required, as per AS-11: (i) To show value at which above items will appear in Balance sheet as on 31st March, 2026; (ii) To calculate the amount of gain/loss on each of above transactions on account of exchange differences, if any. (5 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 11 “The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates”, Foreign currency monetary items should be reported using the closing rate. Non-monetary items which are carried in terms of historical cost denominated in a foreign currency should be reported using the exchange rate at the date of the transaction. Exchange differences arising on the settlement of monetary items or on reporting an enterprise's monetary items at rates different from those at which they were initially recorded during the period, or reported in previous financial statements, should be recognised as income or as expenses in the period in which they arise.

(i) Items given in the question will appear in the Balance Sheet at the following values:
Trade Payables (30,96,000/86 = 36,000 German Currency) x ₹90 = ₹32,40,000
Plant and Machinery 18,500 German Currency x ₹88 = ₹16,28,000
Trade Receivables (50,40,000/84 = 60,000 German Currency) x ₹90 = ₹54,00,000

(ii) Amount of gain/loss on each transaction on account of exchange difference:
Exchange loss on Transaction of import of goods from Try Ltd. = ₹(1,44,000) [36,000 German Currency x ₹4 (i.e. 90−86)]
Exchange gain on Transaction of export of goods to Cream Ltd = ₹3,60,000 [60,000 German Currency x ₹6 (i.e. 90−84)]

Author’s Note: A significant number of examinees used the transaction-date rate instead of the closing rate to restate the outstanding trade payable and trade receivable at year end under AS 11, and some failed to translate the plant and machinery paid for on the date of import at the transaction-date rate.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ May 2026 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 2] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U3 : AS 11 — The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.13
Question:

ABC Ltd, borrowed 10,000 US $ in foreign currency on 1st April, 2025 at 6% p.a. annual interest and acquired a depreciable asset. Exchange rates: 01-04-2025 ₹85.00/US$; 31-03-2026 ₹90.00/US$. (Assume interest accrued on 31st March, 2026 is paid on the same date.)

You are required to: (i) Pass the journal entries in case the option under Para 46A of AS 11 is availed by the company. (ii) Explain the difference in treatment of exchange difference if Para 46A of AS 11 is not availed. (iii) Would your answer be different regarding treatment of exchange difference if the loan was taken to finance the operations of the entity, not to procure a depreciable asset, and option under Para 46A of AS 11 is availed by the company? (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

(i) Option under Para 46A availed – Journal Entries in the Books of ABC Ltd.:

DateParticulars₹ Dr.₹ Cr.
2025 Apr. 01Bank Account (10,000×85) Dr.
  To Foreign Loan Account		8,50,0008,50,000
2026 Mar 31Finance Cost (USD 10,000×6%×₹90) Dr.
  To Bank Account		54,00054,000
2026 Mar 31Foreign Exchange Difference Account Dr.
  To Foreign Loan Account [10,000×(90−85)]		50,00050,000
2026 Mar 31Property, Plant and Equipment Dr.
  To Foreign Exchange Difference Account		50,00050,000

Since the option under Para 46A is availed, the Exchange Loss of ₹50,000 is capitalized in the cost of PPE, indirectly recognized in the P&L by way of increased depreciation over the remaining useful life of the asset.

(ii) If Para 46A is NOT availed: the Exchange Loss of ₹50,000 is recognised as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ending 31 March 2026 instead.

(iii) If the loan financed operations (not a depreciable asset) and Para 46A is availed: the Exchange Loss of ₹50,000 is accumulated in the FCMITD A/c, subsequently spread over and debited to P&L over the tenure of the loan (since there is no qualifying asset to capitalise it against).

Author’s Note: Many examinees applied the same asset-capitalisation treatment to part (iii) as part (i), not recognising that Para 46A's asset-linked capitalisation route is only available when the borrowing financed a depreciable asset; where it instead financed operations, the FCMITD accumulation-and-amortisation route applies.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
