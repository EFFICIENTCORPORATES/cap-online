MTP January 2026 Set 1 · Q4(a) · Marks: 6 · Approx Time: 11 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.4: Accumulating compensated absences recognised as a provision in the year earned, not spread flat
Question:

Mr. Suresh works for Raina Ltd. Annual salary ₹30,00,000 in both 2023-24 and 2024-25; 300 working days each year; leave allowed 10 days each year; leave taken 7 days (2023-24) and 13 days (2024-25); leave carried forward to next year: 3 days (2023-24), Nil (2024-25). Raina Ltd. assumes Mr. Suresh will avail the unutilized 3 days of 2023-24 in 2024-25, and contends it will record ₹30,00,000 as employee benefit expense in each year. Comment on the accounting treatment and pass journal entries for both years.

Answer / Solution:

Salary cost per day = ₹30,00,000 / 300 = ₹10,000/day.

Particulars2023-242024-25
Annual salary30,00,00030,00,000
Leave carried forward/(utilised)3 days(3 days)
Expense to be recognised30,30,00029,70,000

Raina Ltd.'s contention (₹30,00,000 flat in both years) is not correct under AS 15 — the accumulating compensated absence for the unused 3 days of 2023-24 must be recognised as an additional expense/provision in that year itself (when earned), not spread evenly.

Journal entries: 2023-24: Employee benefit expense Dr. 30,30,000; To Cash 30,00,000; To Provision for Leave Encashment 30,000. 2024-25: Provision for Leave Encashment Dr. 30,000; Employee benefit expense Dr. 29,70,000; To Cash 30,00,000.

Author’s Note: Some examinees accepted the company's flat ₹30,00,000-per-year treatment without challenging it, missing that AS 15 requires the accumulating leave benefit to be recognised as a provision in the period it is earned, regardless of when it is expected to be used.

MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q6(c) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.9: Expected vs actual return on plan assets, time-weighted for mid-year contributions and benefit payments
Question:

Pendora Ltd.'s employee benefit pension plan: fair value of plan assets 01-04-2024 ₹5,00,000; benefits paid 30-11-2024 ₹63,000; inward contributions received 30-09-2024 ₹1,42,000; fair value of plan assets 31-03-2025 ₹7,50,000. Estimates made 01.04.2024: interest and dividend income (after tax) 10.50%; realised gains 2.00%; fund administrative costs (2.00)%; expected rate of annual return 10.50% (compounded annually). Find the expected and actual returns on plan assets as on 31.03.2025 as per AS 15.

Answer / Solution:

Expected return on plan assets: Return on opening ₹5,00,000 held 12 months @10.50% = ₹52,500. Return on ₹1,42,000 contribution held 6 months (from 30.9.24 to 31.3.25) @10.50% = ₹7,455. Less: loss of interest on benefits paid (₹63,000 for the 4 months it was no longer earning return, 30.11.24–31.3.25) @10.50% = (₹2,205). Expected return for 2024-25 = ₹57,750.

Actual return on plan assets: Fair value 31.3.25 (7,50,000) less fair value 1.4.24 (5,00,000) less contributions received (1,42,000) = 1,08,000; add back benefits paid (63,000, since paying out benefits reduces the fair value but isn't a return-related reduction) = Actual return ₹1,71,000.

Author’s Note: Some examinees computed expected return on the full-year balances without time-weighting the mid-year contribution and benefit payment, or confused expected return (an estimate based on opening assumptions) with actual return (derived from the actual movement in fair value).

MTP September 2024 Set 1 · Q6(b) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.10: Actuarial gains/losses recognised immediately in profit and loss, never spread or deferred
Question:

Synergy Ltd., is in engineering industry. The company received an actuarial valuation for the first time for its pension scheme which revealed a surplus of ₹6 lakhs. It wants to spread the same over the next 2 years by reducing the annual contribution to ₹2 lakhs instead of ₹5 lakhs. The average remaining life of the employee is estimated to be 6 years. You are required to advise the company.

Answer / Solution:

According to AS 15 (Revised) "Employee Benefits", actuarial gains and losses should be recognized immediately in the statement of profit and loss as income or expense. Therefore, the surplus of ₹6 lakhs in the pension scheme on its actuarial valuation is required to be credited to the profit and loss statement of the current year. Hence, Synergy Ltd. cannot spread the actuarial gain of ₹6 lakhs over the next 2 years by reducing the annual contributions to ₹2 lakhs instead of ₹5 lakhs. It has to contribute ₹5 lakhs annually for its pension scheme, and recognise the full ₹6 lakh surplus immediately.

Author’s Note: A common error is agreeing with a company's proposal to spread an actuarial gain or loss over future periods, missing that AS 15 requires immediate recognition of the entire actuarial gain or loss in the period it arises, with no deferral option.

PYQ January 2025 · Q1(c) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.7: Accounting treatment - defined benefit asset/liability recognition, capped at the lower of the computed amount and the present value of available future refunds Compulsory
Question:

What is the difference between Defined Contribution Plan and Defined Benefit Plan? From the following information calculate the amount of defined benefit liability / asset:

Particulars₹ in lakhs

Answer / Solution:

Difference between Defined Contribution Plan and Defined Benefit Plan:

S.No.Defined Contribution PlanDefined Benefit Plan
1Fixed Contributions are paid by the employer into a separate fund and will have no obligation to pay further contributions.Detailed actuarial calculation is performed to determine the charge.
2The employer has no obligation to pay further contributions if the fund does not hold sufficient assets to pay all employee benefits and the employee has to bear the investment and actuarial risk.The employer ensures that sufficient funds are available to meet the promised benefits regardless of fund performance and the actuarial and investment risk fall on the employer.

Computation of defined benefit liability/Asset:

Present value of the defined benefit obligation as on 31.3.202436.00
Less: Past service cost not yet recognized(7.50)
28.50
Less: The fair value of plan assets(38.50)
Defined benefit Asset10.00

In case where fair value of plan assets is high, it may so happen that the net amount under defined benefit liability turns negative (giving rise to net assets). As per AS 15 the enterprise, in such a situation, should measure the resulting asset at the lower of: (i) the amount so determined, i.e. ₹10 lakh; and (ii) the present value of available future refunds from the plan i.e. ₹6 lakh. Therefore, defined benefit asset will be recognised at ₹6 lakhs.

The company also has a policy of giving lump sum payment of 30% of the last drawn annual salary of the employee for each completed year of service if the employee retires after completing minimum 5 years of service. Since the company has inducted Mr. A in the beginning of the year and it is expected that he will complete the minimum five-year term before retiring, he will get 5 yearly increments.

RTP May 2024 · Q13 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.10: Actuarial gains and losses are recognised immediately in the statement of profit and loss, not spread/deferred over future periods
Question:

Hello Limited belongs to the manufacturing industry. The company received an actuarial valuation for the first time for its pension scheme which revealed a surplus of ₹12 lakhs. It wants to spread the same over the next 2 years by reducing the annual contribution to ₹4 lakhs instead of ₹10 lakhs. The average remaining life of the employees is estimated to be 6 years. You are required to advise the company on the following items from the viewpoint of finalization of accounts, taking note of the mandatory accounting standards.

Answer / Solution:

According to AS 15 (Revised 2005) 'Employee Benefits', actuarial gains and losses should be recognized immediately in the statement of profit and loss as income or expense. Therefore, a surplus amount of ₹12 lakhs is required to be credited to the profit and loss statement of the current year.

Author’s Note: A common error here is accepting the company's proposal to spread the actuarial surplus over future years by reducing annual contributions, rather than recognising the full surplus immediately in the current year's profit and loss as AS 15 requires.
