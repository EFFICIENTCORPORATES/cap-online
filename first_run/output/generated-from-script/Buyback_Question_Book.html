MTP January 2026 Set 1 · Q2(b) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M3_C12_U0 : Buy-back of Securities / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1
Question:

Aerodots Ltd. has the following capital structure as on 31.03.2025 (₹ in thousands): Equity Share Capital (shares of ₹10 each) 600; General Reserve 540; Securities Premium 200; Profit & Loss 100; Revaluation Reserve 30; Investment Allowance Reserve (Statutory Reserve) 75; Infrastructure Development Reserve 25; Loan Funds 2000. On 1st April, 2025 the company wants to buy back 14,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹30 per share, duly authorized. You are required to calculate the maximum permissible number of equity shares that can be bought back.

Answer / Solution:
TestShares (thousands)
Shares Outstanding Test (25% of 60,000 shares)15
Resources Test (25% of Shareholders' funds ÷ ₹30 buyback price)12
Debt Equity Ratio Test11
Maximum buy-back permitted [least of the above]11

Thus, the lowest being 11,000 shares, the company cannot buy back the requested 14,000 shares.

W.N.1 Shares Outstanding Test: outstanding 60,000 shares; 25% = 15,000.

W.N.2 Resources Test: Paid-up capital 600 + Free reserves (540+200+100=840) = Shareholders' funds 1,440; 25% = 360; ÷ buy-back price ₹30 = 12,000 shares.

W.N.3 Debt-Equity Ratio Test: Loan funds 2,000; minimum equity to maintain (2:1) = 1,000; present equity funds 1,440; future equity funds (after 110 transferred to CRR, solved simultaneously) = 1,330; maximum permitted buy-back of equity = 1,330−1,000 = 330; ‷ ₹30/share = 11,000 shares.

Author’s Note: Some examinees included statutory reserves (Investment Allowance Reserve) or the Infrastructure Development Reserve in "free reserves" for the Resources Test, when only reserves freely available for distribution should be counted.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

MTP May 2026 Set 1 · Q5(b) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M3_C12_U0 : Companies Act 2013, s.68 — Buy-back of Securities / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1
Question:

The following is the extract of Balance Sheet of Yellow Limited as on 31.03.2025:

4,00,000 Equity shares of ₹10 each40,00,000
General Reserve48,00,000
Profit & Loss Account10,00,000
Securities Premium18,00,000
Secured Loans60,00,000
Unsecured Loans32,00,000
Current Liabilities28,00,000
Total2,36,00,000
Property, Plant and Equipment90,00,000
Investments18,00,000
Current Assets1,28,00,000
Total2,36,00,000

The company intends to buy-back 80,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at a premium of 150%. You are required to state whether the company can buy back equity shares. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

Determination of Buy-back of maximum no. of shares as per the Companies Act, 2013

1. Shares Outstanding Test

Number of shares outstanding4,00,000
25% of the shares outstanding1,00,000

2. Resources Test: Maximum permitted limit 25% of Equity paid up capital + Free Reserves

Paid up capital (₹)40,00,000
Free reserves (₹) (48,00,000 + 18,00,000 + 10,00,000)76,00,000
Shareholders' funds (₹)1,16,00,000
25% of Shareholders' fund (₹)29,00,000
Buy-back price per share₹25
Number of shares that can be bought back (shares)1,16,000
Actual Number of shares for buy-back80,000

3. Debt Equity Ratio Test: Loans cannot be in excess of twice the Equity Funds post Buy-Back

Debt Equity ratio of the company should not exceed 2:1 after such buy-back. In this case, the debt is ₹92,00,000 (60,00,000 + 32,00,000)* and equity after such buy back will be ₹96,00,000 (1,16,00,000 − 20,00,000). Thus, the debt equity ratio is 0.96:1, which is less than 2:1.

Company qualifies all tests for buy-back of shares and came to the conclusion that it can buy 80,000 equity shares @ ₹25.

* Total debt may be considered (i.e. including current liability).

MTP May 2026 Set 2 · Q6(c) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M3_C12_U0 : Companies Act 2013, s.70 — Buy-back of Securities / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2
Question:

Alpha Chemicals Ltd., a listed company, proposes a buy-back of 12% of its equity share capital from its free reserves under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. On scrutiny of its records, the following details were observed: 1. The company intends to route the buy-back through its wholly-owned subsidiary. 2. The company had defaulted on repayment of a term loan to a public sector bank, which was fully repaid 27 months ago. 3. The company has not filed its Annual Return and Financial Statements for the preceding financial year. 4. The company had declared a dividend in the last year, but the amount remains unpaid for 35 days from the date of declaration.

You are required to: Examine the compliance of Alpha Chemicals Ltd. with the requirements of Section 70 of the Companies Act, 2013 and state whether the company is eligible to proceed with the proposed buy-back. (5 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

As per Section 70 of the Companies Act, 2013, the company cannot buy back its own shares under the following conditions, all of which apply to Alpha Chemicals Ltd.:

1. Buy-back through Subsidiary – Prohibited. Section 70(1)(a): A company shall not buy back shares through any subsidiary, including wholly owned subsidiaries.

2. Default in Term Loan – Three-Year Cooling Off Required. Section 70(1)(c): A company that has defaulted in repayment of a term loan can buy back shares only after 3 years from the cessation of default. Alpha repaid the loan only 27 months ago — still short of the required 36-month period. Hence, not eligible.

3. Non-Filing of Annual Return and Financial Statements. Section 70(2): Buy-back is prohibited if the company has not complied with Sections 92 (Annual Return) and 129 (Financial Statements). Alpha has failed to file both which disqualifies it from buy-back.

4. Unpaid Dividend for More Than 30 Days. Section 127 read with Section 70(2): Non-distribution of declared dividend within 30 days is a punishable offence. Dividend unpaid for 35 days, this is a default.

Alpha Chemicals Ltd. is not eligible to proceed with the buy-back under Section 70 due to multiple violations.

Author’s Note: Many examinees identified only one or two of the four independent Section 70 violations, most often missing the wholly-owned-subsidiary prohibition since it is easy to assume a wholly-owned subsidiary is functionally the same entity as its parent.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ January 2026 · Q3(a) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M3_C12_U0 : Companies Act 2013, s.68 — Buy-back of Securities / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1
Question:

Balance Sheet of P Limited as at 31st March, 2025: Share Capital ₹1,500 lakh (150 lakh Equity Shares of ₹10 each); Reserves and Surplus ₹2,750 lakh (Capital Reserve 380, Securities Premium 300, Revaluation Reserve 120, General Reserve 1,800, Surplus 150); Long Term Borrowings ₹3,450 lakh (12% Debentures 2,250, Unsecured Loans 1,200); Short Term Borrowings (Current Maturities) ₹1,980 lakh; Trade Payables ₹1,500 lakh; Total ₹11,180 lakh. Assets: PPE ₹5,675 lakh; Inventories ₹1,005 lakh; Trade Receivables ₹1,500 lakh; Cash and Cash Equivalents ₹3,000 lakh; Total ₹11,180 lakh.

Considering the large surplus funds, the company decides to buy back 30 lakh Equity Shares on 1st April, 2025. Prevailing market value ₹16/share; to induce shareholders, offer price is 25% over market. Buy-back is duly authorized by Articles and necessary resolution passed.

Comment with your calculations, whether buy-back of shares by the company is within the provisions of the Companies Act, 2013. If yes, pass necessary journal entries towards buy-back of shares. (7 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

Determination of Buy-back of maximum no. of shares as per the Companies Act, 2013:

1. Shares Outstanding Test: Number of shares outstanding 150 lakh; 25% of shares outstanding = 37.5 lakh.

2. Resources Test: Paid up capital ₹1,500 lakh; Free reserves (300+1,800+150) ₹2,250 lakh; Shareholders' funds ₹3,750 lakh; 25% of Shareholders' funds ₹937.5 lakh; Buy-back price per share ₹20 (₹16 × 1.25); Number of shares that can be bought back = 46.875 lakh.

3. Debt Equity Ratio Test (loans cannot exceed twice the equity funds post buy-back): Loan funds (3,450+1,980) = 5,430 lakh; Minimum equity to be maintained after buy-back (2:1 ratio, a/2) = 2,715 lakh; Present equity/shareholders' funds = 3,750 lakh. Using the simultaneous-equation method (amount transferred to CRR = x, maximum permitted buy-back of equity = y): (3,750−x) − 2,715 = y, and y/20×10 = x (i.e. y=2x); solving gives x=345 lakh, y=690 lakh. Maximum number of shares at ₹20/share = 34.50 lakh shares.

Summary: Shares Outstanding Test 37.5 lakh; Resources Test 46.875 lakh; Debt Equity Ratio Test 34.50 lakh. Maximum number of shares that can be bought back (least of the three) = 34.50 lakh. Company qualifies all tests; it can buy a maximum of 34.5 lakh shares, but wants to buy back only 30 lakh equity shares @₹20 – therefore, the buy-back of 30 lakh shares is within the provisions of the Companies Act, 2013.

Note on CRR: if one share is bought back, ₹10 face value is reduced from share capital, ₹10 premium (20−10) is reduced from Securities Premium, and an amount equal to the face value (₹10) must be transferred from free reserves to CRR, since CRR itself is not a free reserve available for dividend distribution.

Examiner’s Comment: Several examinees were unable to determine the permissible amount of buy-back in accordance with the relevant provisions. Errors were also observed in the computation of the Capital Redemption Reserve and in passing the necessary journal entries. While many examinees correctly applied two of the required tests, they failed to correctly apply the Debt-Equity ratio test.Real ICAI Examiner’s Comment, quoted from the official document for this sitting.
