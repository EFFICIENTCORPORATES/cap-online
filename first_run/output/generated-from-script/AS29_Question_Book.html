RTP January 2026 · Q20 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C6_U2 : AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.5
Question:

An oil company has been contaminating land for several years, not cleaning up since no legislation currently requires it. At 31st March, 2025, it is virtually certain that a law requiring clean-up of contaminated land will be enacted shortly after the year end. Is provisioning presently necessary?

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 29 para 29, a past event creates a present obligation only when the enterprise has no realistic alternative to settling it. Environmental contamination alone does not create an obligation to remedy it unless a law requires the existing damage to be rectified — and where a proposed law's details are not yet finalised, an obligation arises only once enactment is virtually certain.

Here, it is virtually certain that a clean-up law will be enacted. The oil company must therefore provide for the clean-up cost in the year in which the legislation's enactment becomes virtually certain (the year ended 31st March, 2025) — not deferred until the law is actually enacted.

Author’s Note: Some examinees concluded no provision was needed since the law had not yet been enacted at the balance sheet date, missing that "virtually certain" enactment is itself sufficient to trigger the obligation under AS 29.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q17(i) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C6_U2 : AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.15
Question:

Galaxy Limited is in the process of finalization of its accounts for the year ended 31st March, 2025 and needs your advice on the following issue in line with the provisions of AS 29: Galaxy Limited is selling cosmetic products under its brand name “Angel” but is getting these products manufactured from X Limited. It has an understanding (enforceable agreement) with X Limited that if the company becomes liable for any damage claims, due to any injury or harm to the customer of the cosmetic products, 40% shall be reimbursed to it by X Limited. During the year 2024-25, an estimate of the claim of ₹25 lakhs may be payable to the customers by Galaxy Limited. How should Galaxy Limited account for the claim that becomes payable?

Answer / Solution:

Since the understanding results in an enforceable agreement, the reimbursement of ₹10,00,000 (40% of ₹25,00,000) shall be recognized as a reimbursement right and provision will be recognized for the full amount of the liability of ₹25,00,000. The reimbursement right shall be treated as a separate asset and shall not be offset with the provision. In the Statement of Profit and Loss, the expense may be presented as ₹15,00,000 after offsetting the reimbursement right.

Author’s Note: Many examinees recognised only the net ₹15,00,000 exposure as a provision, instead of recognising the full ₹25,00,000 provision and a separate ₹10,00,000 reimbursement asset, contrary to AS 29's requirement that a reimbursement never be offset against the provision on the balance sheet even though it may be netted in the profit and loss presentation.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q17(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C6_U2 : AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.9
Question:

Galaxy Limited has been sued by one of its competitor for alleged infringement of trademark and a notice for levy of penalty of ₹50 lakhs has been received by it. The company has appointed a law firm to defend its case for a fee of ₹5 lakhs. 60% of the fees has been paid in advance and the rest 40% would be paid after finalization of the case. There are 70% chances that the penalty may not be imposed.

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 29, an obligation is a present obligation if, based on the evidence available, its existence at the balance sheet date is considered probable, i.e. more likely than not. Liability is a present obligation of the enterprise arising from the past events, the settlement of which is expected to result in an outflow from the enterprise of resources embodying economic benefits. In the given case, there are 70% chances that the penalty may not be levied. Accordingly, Galaxy Limited should not make the provision for liability. The matter is disclosed as a contingent liability unless the probability of any outflow is considered as remote. However, a provision should be made for the remaining 40% fees of the law firm amounting to ₹2,00,000 in the financial statements for 2024-25.

Author’s Note: Several examinees recognised a provision for the trademark-suit penalty itself, treating a 30% chance of levy as sufficient to be "probable," when AS 29 requires the outflow to be more likely than not before a provision (rather than mere contingent-liability disclosure) is warranted; many also missed the separate, unconditional obligation to provide for the remaining unpaid law-firm fee.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
