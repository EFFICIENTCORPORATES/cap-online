MTP January 2026 Set 1 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 1] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U5 : AS 19 — Leases / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 5.3
Question:

Colour Limited leased a Machine to Red Limited on 1st April, 2021: Cost of machine ₹18,00,000; Lease term 3 years; Fair market value ₹18,00,000; Unguaranteed residual value as on 31.3.2024 ₹2,00,000; Internal rate of return 12%. Expected useful life 5 years; machine reverts to Colour Limited on termination. Lease payments at the end of each year, in 3 equal parts. PV of ₹1 due at end of 3rd year at 12% = ₹0.7118. PV of annuity of ₹1 for 3 years at 12% = ₹2.4018. Analyze whether the lease constitutes a finance lease. Also calculate unearned finance income, if any.

Answer / Solution:

Computation of Annual Lease Payment: PV of unguaranteed residual value = 2,00,000 × 0.7118 = 1,42,360. PV of Lease Payments to recover = 18,00,000 − 1,42,360 = 16,57,640. Annual Lease Payment = 16,57,640 / 2.4018 = ₹6,90,166 (approx).

Classification: PV of lease payments (₹16,57,640) is 92.09% of fair value (₹18,00,000) — substantially covers the fair value of the leased asset. Lease term (3 years) covers the major part of the asset's economic life (5 years). Both indicators point to a finance lease.

Unearned Finance Income: Total Lease Payments (6,90,166 × 3) 20,70,498 + Unguaranteed residual value 2,00,000 = 22,70,498; less Net investment (PV of payments + residual = 1,42,360+16,57,640 = 18,00,000) = Unearned Finance Income ₹4,70,498 (approx, matching source's ₹4,70,495 to rounding).

Author’s Note: Some examinees computed unearned finance income using the guaranteed residual value formula, not adjusting for the fact that this residual value is unguaranteed (still included in the lessor's net investment, but the classification/income mechanics require care in distinguishing guaranteed vs unguaranteed treatment).Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ January 2026 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 2] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U5 : AS 19 — Leases / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 5.8.1
Question:

S Limited took an equipment on lease from R Limited, fair value ₹9,50,000. Economic life and lease term both 3 years. At the end of each year, lessee pays ₹4,00,000 to lessor. S Limited has guaranteed a residual value of ₹35,000 on lease expiry to R Limited; R Limited estimates residual value will be only ₹25,000. Implicit rate of return 15% p.a.; PV factors at 15%: 0.869, 0.756, 0.657 (years 1-3).

You are required to ascertain the value at which the equipment would be considered by S Limited and the finance charges in each year. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

As per para 11 of AS 19, the lessee should recognize the lease as an asset and a liability at the inception of a finance lease, at an amount equal to the fair value of the leased asset, or, if lower, the present value of minimum lease payments.

Present value of minimum lease payments = Annual lease rental × PVIF + Present value of guaranteed residual value = ₹4,00,000 × (0.869+0.756+0.657) + ₹35,000 × 0.657 = ₹9,12,800 + ₹22,995 = ₹9,35,795. Fair value of leased asset = ₹9,50,000. Recognition is at the lower of the two: ₹9,35,795.

RTP January 2026 · Q15 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U5 : AS 19 — Leases / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 5.9
Question:

On 1st April, 2024, Mansi Ltd. sold a plant for ₹8,52,800 (carrying amount ₹1,80,000) as part of a sale-and-leaseback package with Akash Ltd., leasing the asset back for an 8-year term. Economic life of the asset is 8 years. Minimum lease rents ₹1,60,000/year, payable annually from 31st March, 2025. Mansi Ltd.'s incremental borrowing rate is 10% p.a. Calculate the net effect on the Statement of Profit and Loss in Mansi Ltd.'s (the lessee's) books.

Answer / Solution:

Lease classification: lease term (8 years) covers the entire economic life (8 years). PV of minimum lease payments = ₹1,60,000 × 5.335 (annuity factor @10% for 8 years) = ₹8,53,600, which substantially covers the fair value/sale price (₹8,52,800). This is therefore a finance lease.

Per AS 19 para 48, where a sale-and-leaseback results in a finance lease, the profit on sale (₹8,52,800−₹1,80,000 = ₹6,72,800) is not recognised immediately — it is deferred and amortised over the lease term in proportion to the depreciation of the leased asset.

Assuming straight-line depreciation: Depreciation = ₹8,52,800/8 = ₹1,06,600/year. Deferred profit amortised = ₹6,72,800/8 = ₹84,100/year. Net effect on Statement of P&L = ₹1,06,600 − ₹84,100 = ₹22,500 debit per annum, for each of the 8 years of the lease term.

Author’s Note: Some examinees recognised the full sale profit immediately in the year of sale, not applying AS 19's specific deferral-and-amortisation rule for a sale-and-leaseback that results in a finance lease.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q14 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U5 : AS 19 — Leases / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 5.9
Question:

Explain, in brief, the accounting treatment for an asset given under Operating Lease in the books of the Lessor.

Answer / Solution:

The accounting treatment for an asset given under Operating Lease in the books of the lessor is given as under:

  1. The Lessor should present an asset given under Operating Lease as PPE in its balance sheet.
  2. Lease income from operating leases should be recognized in the Statement of Profit and Loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is more representative of the time pattern in which benefit derived from the use of the leased asset is diminished.
  3. Depreciation should be recognized in the books of the lessor. The depreciation of leased assets should be on a basis consistent with the normal accounting policy of the lessor for similar assets, and the depreciation charge should be calculated on the basis set out in AS 10.
  4. The impairment loss on assets given on operating leases are determined and treated as per AS 28.
Author’s Note: Many examinees conflated lessor and lessee treatments, describing finance-lease-style derecognition of the asset instead of the lessor retaining the asset as PPE and depreciating it under AS 10, resulting in partial credit loss.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
