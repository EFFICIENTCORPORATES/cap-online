PYQ May 2026 · Q6(b) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U2 : AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.5
Question:

The accountant of Moon Limited provides you the following information: (i) Provision for doubtful debts was created @3% till 31st March, 2025; from FY 2025-26, the rate has been changed to 5%. (ii) During the year ended 31st March, 2026 the management has introduced a formal gratuity scheme in place of ad-hoc ex-gratia payments to employees on retirement. (iii) During 2025-26, there was change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories. (iv) Management decided to pay pension to employees who have retired after completing 5 years of service (₹25,000 per month); earlier there was no such pension scheme.

You are required to advise the accountant of Moon Limited, with valid reasons, whether the above transactions would be treated as change in accounting policy or not, for the year ended 31st March, 2026 in the context of AS 5. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

As per para 21 of AS 5, the preparation of financial statements involves making estimates based on circumstances existing when the statements are prepared; it may be necessary to revise an estimate in a subsequent period if circumstances change. Revision of an estimate, by its nature, does not bring the adjustment within the definitions of a prior period item or an extraordinary item. Change in Accounting estimate is not a change in accounting policy.

(i) Provision for doubtful debts rate change (3% to 5%): this is a change in accounting estimate, not a change in accounting policy. Per para 27 of AS 5, a change in estimate with a material effect in the current period should be disclosed and quantified; any change expected to have a material effect in later periods should also be disclosed and quantified.

(ii) As per AS 5, adopting an accounting policy for events or transactions that differ in substance from previously occurring events or transactions is not considered a change in accounting policy. Accordingly, introduction of a formal retirement gratuity scheme in place of ad hoc ex-gratia payments is not a change in accounting policy.

(iii) As per AS 2 and AS 5: the method of determining cost (FIFO/Weighted Average) is an accounting policy choice, and any change in it affects measurement of inventory and profit. Hence a change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories IS a change in Accounting Policy.

(iv) The adoption of a new accounting policy for events or transactions which did not occur previously, or that were immaterial, will not be treated as a change in accounting policy. Introducing a pension scheme where none previously existed is therefore not a change in accounting policy.

Author’s Note: Many examinees classified the provision-rate change and the new gratuity/pension schemes as changes in accounting policy, not recognising AS 5's distinction between a genuine policy change (like the inventory cost-formula change) and a change in estimate or the adoption of a policy for a genuinely new type of transaction.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ September 2025 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 1] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U2 : AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.3
Question:

Sneha, an employee of Omre Limited, went on paid maternity leave for 9 months, 1st January 2024 to 30th September 2024, monthly pay ₹1,50,000. While preparing FY2023-24 financial statements, her salary for 3 months (Jan–Mar 2024) was omitted. When she joined 1st October 2024, the whole 9-month salary was paid. With reference to AS-5, determine if this is a prior period item and pass the FY2024-25 journal entry. Variant: Suppose Sneha was terminated 1st January 2024 and reinstated by court order on 30th September 2024, with the 9-month salary paid 1st October 2024 — what is the AS-5 treatment here, with journal entry?

Answer / Solution:

Original scenario: Per AS 5, prior period items are income/expenses arising in the current period from errors or omissions in preparing prior periods' financial statements. The 3 months' salary (Jan–Mar 2024, ₹4,50,000) omitted from FY2023-24 is a prior period item in FY2024-25.

Journal entry (FY2024-25): Salary A/c (₹1,50,000×6) Dr. 9,00,000; Prior Period Item A/c (₹1,50,000×3) Dr. 4,50,000; To Bank A/c 13,50,000.

Variant (termination/reinstatement): AS 5 states "prior period items" does not include adjustments necessitated by circumstances that, though related to prior periods, are determined only in the current period. Since Sneha's reinstatement (with retrospective salary) was ordered by the court only in FY2024-25, the ₹4,50,000 (Jan–Mar 2024) is not a prior period item — the entire ₹13,50,000 is a current-year expense.

Journal entry (variant): Salary A/c (₹1,50,000×9) Dr. 13,50,000; To Bank A/c 13,50,000.

Examiner’s Comment: A majority of examinees were unable to determine whether the given item constituted a prior period item under AS 5 "Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies". Many of them also failed to pass the correct journal entry.Real ICAI Examiner’s Comment, quoted from the official document for this sitting.

RTP January 2026 · Q5 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U2 : AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.3
Question:

Identify each of the following as Change in Accounting Policy / Change in Accounting Estimate / Extraordinary Item / Prior Period Item / Ordinary Activity: (i) Non-provision for salary already due in an earlier year. (ii) Attachment of the property of the enterprise. (iii) Introduction of a new pension scheme for employees. (iv) Change in Reserve for obsolete inventory. (v) Settlement of a litigation case. (vi) Legislative changes with long-term retrospective application. (vii) Change from Cost Model to Revaluation Model for PPE measurement. (viii) Government-sanctioned grant received this year for expenses incurred in a previous year.

Answer / Solution:
  1. Prior period item.
  2. Extraordinary item.
  3. Ordinary activity (not a change in accounting policy).
  4. Change in accounting estimate.
  5. Ordinary activity, but requires separate disclosure.
  6. Extraordinary item requiring separate disclosure.
  7. Change in accounting policy.
  8. Prior period item.

RTP January 2026 · Q6 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U2 : AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.4
Question:

Best Ltd., a consultancy services provider, received a GST department demand notice of ₹2,50,000 on consultancy services provided; the company paid the demand and recorded it as extraordinary expenditure. Can this payment of a tax demand be recognised as an extraordinary item?

Answer / Solution:

No. Per AS 5, extraordinary items arise from events clearly distinct from ordinary activities and are not expected to recur frequently or regularly. Providing consultancy services is Best Ltd.'s ordinary business activity, and the GST liability arises directly from these ordinary transactions. Paying a tax demand raised by the taxation authority is part of normal business operations. Recording it as extraordinary is therefore not correct — it should be recognised as part of profit or loss from ordinary activities.

Author’s Note: Some examinees accepted the company's own extraordinary-item classification uncritically, not testing it against AS 5's actual definition (an event clearly distinct from, and not part of, the ordinary business activity).Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q9(a) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U2 : AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.3
Question:

When can an item qualify to be a prior period item as per AS 5?

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 5 para 16, prior period items are income or expenses that arise in the current period as a result of errors or omissions in the preparation of financial statements of one or more prior periods. The term does not include other adjustments necessitated by circumstances that, though related to prior periods, are determined only in the current period (e.g. arrears payable to workers from a retrospective wage revision).

Author’s Note: A direct definitional question; the key nuance examinees often miss is the exclusion for items only determinable in the current period despite relating to an earlier one.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q9(b) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C7_U2 : AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 2.4
Question:

A Ltd. finds that the stock sheets of 31.3.2024 did not include two pages containing details of inventory worth ₹20 lakhs. How should this be dealt with in the accounts for the year ended 31st March, 2025, per AS 5?

Answer / Solution:

This omission is a prior period item under AS 5 para 16. Per para 19, prior period items are normally included in determining the current period's net profit or loss — so ₹20 lakhs should be added to the opening stock as at 1.4.2024. Alternatively, such items may be shown in the statement of profit and loss after the current period's net profit/loss has been determined. Either presentation must clearly indicate the effect of the item on the current period's profit or loss.

Author’s Note: Some examinees treated the omission as a current-year adjustment to closing stock rather than correcting the prior year's opening stock figure, misplacing the correction in the wrong period.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
