MTP May 2023 Set 1 · Q1(d) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.5: Change in the method of estimating a provision is not a change in accounting policy, and the required disclosure note when the change is nonetheless made Compulsory
Question:

ABC Ltd. was making provision for non-moving inventories based on no issues for the last 12 months up to 31.3.2021.

The company wants to provide during the year ending 31.3.2022 based on technical evaluation:

Particulars₹
Total value of inventory100 lakhs
Provision required based on 12 months issue3.5 lakhs
Provision required based on technical evaluation2.5 lakhs

Does this amount to change in Accounting Policy? Can the company change the method of provision? (5 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

The decision of making provision for non-moving inventories on the basis of technical evaluation does not amount to change in accounting policy. Accounting policy of a company may require that provision for non-moving inventories should be made. The method of estimating the amount of provision may be changed in case a more prudent estimate can be made. In the given case, considering the total value of inventory, the change in the amount of required provision of non-moving inventory from ₹3.5 lakhs to ₹2.5 lakhs is also not material. The disclosure can be made for such change in the following lines by way of notes to the accounts in the annual accounts of ABC Ltd. for the year 2021-22:

“The company has provided for non-moving inventories on the basis of technical evaluation unlike preceding years. Had the same method been followed as in the previous year, the profit for the year and the corresponding effect on the year end net assets would have been lower by ₹1 lakh.”

Author’s Note: A common error is treating any change in the basis of a provision estimate as automatically a change in accounting policy requiring restatement, without distinguishing a genuine policy change from a change in accounting estimate (moving to a more prudent estimation method for an existing policy of providing for non-moving inventory).

PYQ May 2026 · Q6(b) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.5: Distinguishing a change in accounting estimate, a policy for new/immaterial transactions, and a genuine change in accounting policy
Question:

The accountant of Moon Limited provides you the following information: (i) Provision for doubtful debts was created @3% till 31st March, 2025; from FY 2025-26, the rate has been changed to 5%. (ii) During the year ended 31st March, 2026 the management has introduced a formal gratuity scheme in place of ad-hoc ex-gratia payments to employees on retirement. (iii) During 2025-26, there was change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories. (iv) Management decided to pay pension to employees who have retired after completing 5 years of service (₹25,000 per month); earlier there was no such pension scheme.

You are required to advise the accountant of Moon Limited, with valid reasons, whether the above transactions would be treated as change in accounting policy or not, for the year ended 31st March, 2026 in the context of AS 5. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

As per para 21 of AS 5, the preparation of financial statements involves making estimates based on circumstances existing when the statements are prepared; it may be necessary to revise an estimate in a subsequent period if circumstances change. Revision of an estimate, by its nature, does not bring the adjustment within the definitions of a prior period item or an extraordinary item. Change in Accounting estimate is not a change in accounting policy.

(i) Provision for doubtful debts rate change (3% to 5%): this is a change in accounting estimate, not a change in accounting policy. Per para 27 of AS 5, a change in estimate with a material effect in the current period should be disclosed and quantified; any change expected to have a material effect in later periods should also be disclosed and quantified.

(ii) As per AS 5, adopting an accounting policy for events or transactions that differ in substance from previously occurring events or transactions is not considered a change in accounting policy. Accordingly, introduction of a formal retirement gratuity scheme in place of ad hoc ex-gratia payments is not a change in accounting policy.

(iii) As per AS 2 and AS 5: the method of determining cost (FIFO/Weighted Average) is an accounting policy choice, and any change in it affects measurement of inventory and profit. Hence a change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories IS a change in Accounting Policy.

(iv) The adoption of a new accounting policy for events or transactions which did not occur previously, or that were immaterial, will not be treated as a change in accounting policy. Introducing a pension scheme where none previously existed is therefore not a change in accounting policy.

Author’s Note: Many examinees classified the provision-rate change and the new gratuity/pension schemes as changes in accounting policy, not recognising AS 5's distinction between a genuine policy change (like the inventory cost-formula change) and a change in estimate or the adoption of a policy for a genuinely new type of transaction.

PYQ September 2025 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 1] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.3: Prior period item vs current-year expense - depends on whether the underlying adjustment was determinable in the prior period or only becomes so via a later event
Question:

Sneha, an employee of Omre Limited, went on paid maternity leave for 9 months, 1st January 2024 to 30th September 2024, monthly pay ₹1,50,000. While preparing FY2023-24 financial statements, her salary for 3 months (Jan–Mar 2024) was omitted. When she joined 1st October 2024, the whole 9-month salary was paid. With reference to AS-5, determine if this is a prior period item and pass the FY2024-25 journal entry. Variant: Suppose Sneha was terminated 1st January 2024 and reinstated by court order on 30th September 2024, with the 9-month salary paid 1st October 2024 — what is the AS-5 treatment here, with journal entry?

Answer / Solution:

Original scenario: Per AS 5, prior period items are income/expenses arising in the current period from errors or omissions in preparing prior periods' financial statements. The 3 months' salary (Jan–Mar 2024, ₹4,50,000) omitted from FY2023-24 is a prior period item in FY2024-25.

Journal entry (FY2024-25): Salary A/c (₹1,50,000×6) Dr. 9,00,000; Prior Period Item A/c (₹1,50,000×3) Dr. 4,50,000; To Bank A/c 13,50,000.

Variant (termination/reinstatement): AS 5 states "prior period items" does not include adjustments necessitated by circumstances that, though related to prior periods, are determined only in the current period. Since Sneha's reinstatement (with retrospective salary) was ordered by the court only in FY2024-25, the ₹4,50,000 (Jan–Mar 2024) is not a prior period item — the entire ₹13,50,000 is a current-year expense.

Journal entry (variant): Salary A/c (₹1,50,000×9) Dr. 13,50,000; To Bank A/c 13,50,000.

Examiner’s Comment: A majority of examinees were unable to determine whether the given item constituted a prior period item under AS 5 "Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies". Many of them also failed to pass the correct journal entry.

RTP January 2025 · Q5 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.2: Prior period items arise only from errors or omissions in preparing earlier financial statements - a provision write-back from a genuinely unusual event is an extraordinary item instead
Question:

During the course of the last three years, a company owning and operating Helicopters lost four Helicopters. The company's accountant felt that after the crash, the maintenance provision created in respect of the respective helicopters was no longer required, and proposed to write it back to the Profit and Loss account as a prior period item.

Is the company's proposed accounting treatment correct? Discuss.

Answer / Solution:

The balance amount of maintenance provision written back to profit and loss account, no longer required due to crash of the helicopters, is not a prior period item because there was no error in the preparation of previous periods' financial statements. The term "prior period items", as defined in AS 5 (revised) "Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies", refer only to income or expenses which arise in the current period as a result of errors or omissions in the preparation of the financial statements of one or more prior periods. The balance amount left in the provision created earlier is not as a result of error in the past. So it will not be considered as a prior period item. Such write back of provision is not an ordinary feature of the business; it shall be considered as an extra-ordinary item.

As per paragraph 8 of AS 5, extraordinary items should be disclosed in the Statement of Profit and Loss as a part of net profit or loss for the period. The nature and the amount of each extraordinary item should be separately disclosed in the Statement of Profit and Loss in a manner that its impact on current profit or loss can be perceived. Hence, the amount so written-back (if material) should be disclosed as an extraordinary item as per AS 5 rather than as a prior period item.

Author’s Note: A common error is treating the reversal of a no-longer-required provision as a prior period item merely because the original provision was created in an earlier year, missing AS 5's requirement that prior period items arise specifically from errors or omissions in preparing earlier financial statements — a provision correctly created and later found unnecessary due to a genuinely unusual event is an extraordinary item, not a prior period correction.

RTP January 2026 · Q5 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.3: Classification exercise across accounting policy changes, estimate changes, extraordinary items, and prior period items
Question:

Identify each of the following as Change in Accounting Policy / Change in Accounting Estimate / Extraordinary Item / Prior Period Item / Ordinary Activity: (i) Non-provision for salary already due in an earlier year. (ii) Attachment of the property of the enterprise. (iii) Introduction of a new pension scheme for employees. (iv) Change in Reserve for obsolete inventory. (v) Settlement of a litigation case. (vi) Legislative changes with long-term retrospective application. (vii) Change from Cost Model to Revaluation Model for PPE measurement. (viii) Government-sanctioned grant received this year for expenses incurred in a previous year.

Answer / Solution:
  1. Prior period item.
  2. Extraordinary item.
  3. Ordinary activity (not a change in accounting policy).
  4. Change in accounting estimate.
  5. Ordinary activity, but requires separate disclosure.
  6. Extraordinary item requiring separate disclosure.
  7. Change in accounting policy.
  8. Prior period item.
Author’s Note: Some examinees classified the change from Cost to Revaluation Model as a change in estimate rather than a change in accounting policy, and conversely treated the pension-scheme introduction as a policy change rather than recognising it as a new ordinary business decision, not a change to an existing policy.

RTP January 2026 · Q6 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.4: Tax demands from ordinary business activity are not extraordinary items
Question:

Best Ltd., a consultancy services provider, received a GST department demand notice of ₹2,50,000 on consultancy services provided; the company paid the demand and recorded it as extraordinary expenditure. Can this payment of a tax demand be recognised as an extraordinary item?

Answer / Solution:

No. Per AS 5, extraordinary items arise from events clearly distinct from ordinary activities and are not expected to recur frequently or regularly. Providing consultancy services is Best Ltd.'s ordinary business activity, and the GST liability arises directly from these ordinary transactions. Paying a tax demand raised by the taxation authority is part of normal business operations. Recording it as extraordinary is therefore not correct — it should be recognised as part of profit or loss from ordinary activities.

Author’s Note: Some examinees accepted the company's own extraordinary-item classification uncritically, not testing it against AS 5's actual definition (an event clearly distinct from, and not part of, the ordinary business activity).

RTP May 2025 · Q8 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.5: Classification exercise distinguishing accounting-estimate changes, non-policy-changes (new events), and genuine accounting-policy changes
Question:

The Accountant of Heera Ltd. has sought your opinion with relevant reasons, whether the following transactions will be treated as change in Accounting Policy or not for the year ended 31st March, 2024. Please advise him in the following situations in accordance with the provisions of relevant Accounting Standard:

  1. Till the previous year the furniture was depreciated on straight line basis over a period of 5 years. From current year, the useful life of furniture has been changed to 3 years.
  2. Management decided to pay pension to those employees who have retired after completing 5 years of service in the organization. Such employees will get pension of ₹20,000 per month. Earlier there was no such scheme of pension in the organization.
  3. During the year ended 31st March, 2024, there was change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories.
Answer / Solution:
  1. Change in useful life of furniture from 5 years to 3 years is a change in accounting estimate, not a change in accounting policy.
  2. Adoption of a new accounting policy for events or transactions which did not occur previously (there was no pension scheme before) should not be treated as a change in an accounting policy. The introduction of the new pension scheme is therefore not a change in accounting policy.
  3. Change in the cost formula used in measuring the cost of inventories is a change in accounting policy.
Author’s Note: A common error is classifying the new pension scheme as a change in accounting policy simply because it is a new accounting treatment being adopted, missing AS 5's specific rule that adopting a policy for an event that did not previously occur is not a change in an existing policy.

RTP September 2024 · Q6 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.5: Adoption of a policy for events/transactions that did not previously occur is not a change in accounting policy
Question:

Explain whether the following will constitute a change in accounting policy or not as per AS 5:

  1. Introduction of a formal retirement gratuity scheme by an employer in place of ad hoc ex-gratia payments to employees on retirement.
  2. Management decided to pay pension to those employees who have retired after completing 5 years of service in the organistaion. Such employees will get pension of ₹20,000 per month. Earlier there was no such scheme of pension in the organization.
Answer / Solution:

As per para 31 of AS 5 ‘Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies’, the adoption of an accounting policy for events or transactions that differ in substance from previously occurring events or transactions, will not be considered as a change in accounting policy.

  1. Accordingly, introduction of a formal retirement gratuity scheme by an employer in place of ad hoc ex-gratia payments to employees on retirement is not a change in an accounting policy.
  2. Similarly, the adoption of a new accounting policy of paying pension to retired employees is a policy for events or transactions which did not occur previously. Hence, it will not be treated as a change in an accounting policy.
Author’s Note: A common error is treating both scenarios as changes in accounting policy simply because the amount charged to profit or loss will differ going forward, without checking whether the underlying event or transaction itself is genuinely new rather than merely a change in the treatment of the same recurring event.

RTP September 2025 · Q9(a) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.3: Definition of a prior period item - errors/omissions in prior financial statements, not later-determined adjustments
Question:

When can an item qualify to be a prior period item as per AS 5?

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 5 para 16, prior period items are income or expenses that arise in the current period as a result of errors or omissions in the preparation of financial statements of one or more prior periods. The term does not include other adjustments necessitated by circumstances that, though related to prior periods, are determined only in the current period (e.g. arrears payable to workers from a retrospective wage revision).

Author’s Note: A direct definitional question; the key nuance examinees often miss is the exclusion for items only determinable in the current period despite relating to an earlier one.

RTP September 2025 · Q9(b) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.4: Correcting an omitted-inventory error from a prior period, added to opening stock as a prior period item
Question:

A Ltd. finds that the stock sheets of 31.3.2024 did not include two pages containing details of inventory worth ₹20 lakhs. How should this be dealt with in the accounts for the year ended 31st March, 2025, per AS 5?

Answer / Solution:

This omission is a prior period item under AS 5 para 16. Per para 19, prior period items are normally included in determining the current period's net profit or loss — so ₹20 lakhs should be added to the opening stock as at 1.4.2024. Alternatively, such items may be shown in the statement of profit and loss after the current period's net profit/loss has been determined. Either presentation must clearly indicate the effect of the item on the current period's profit or loss.

Author’s Note: Some examinees treated the omission as a current-year adjustment to closing stock rather than correcting the prior year's opening stock figure, misplacing the correction in the wrong period.

MTP January 2025 Set 2 · Q1(a) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.4: Adjusting versus non-adjusting events after the balance sheet date, across five distinct fact patterns  +  Topic 2.2: Extraordinary item discovered after financial statements are approved, treated in the subsequent period Compulsory
Question:

State with reasons, how the following events would be dealt with in the financial statements of Hari Ltd. for the year ended 31st March, 2024 (accounts were approved on 25th July, 2024):

  1. Negotiations with another company for acquisition of its business was started on 21st January, 2024. Hari Ltd. invested ₹40 lakh on 22nd April, 2024.
  2. The company made a provision for bad debts @ 4% of its total debtors (as per trend followed from the previous years). In the second week of March 2024, a debtor for ₹2,50,000 had suffered heavy loss due to an earthquake; the loss was not covered by any insurance policy. In May, 2024 the debtor became bankrupt.
  3. During the year 2023-24, Hari Ltd. was sued by a competitor for ₹13 lakhs for infringement of a trademark. Based on the advice of the company's legal counsel, Hari Ltd. provided for a sum of ₹8 lakhs in its financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2024. On 26th May, 2024, the Court decided in favour of the party alleging infringement of the trademark and ordered Hari Ltd. to pay the aggrieved party a sum of ₹12 lakhs.
  4. Cashier of Hari Ltd. embezzled cash amounting to ₹3,00,000 during March, 2024. However the same comes to the notice of Company management during August, 2024.
  5. Cheques dated 31st March, 2024 collected in the month of April, 2024. All cheques are presented to the bank in the month of April, 2024 and are also realized in the same month in the normal course after deposit in the bank.
Answer / Solution:
  1. As per AS 4 'Contingencies and Events Occurring After the Balance Sheet Date', disclosure should be made in the report of the approving authority of those events occurring after the balance sheet date that represent material changes and commitments affecting the financial position of the enterprise; the investment of ₹40 lakhs in April, 2024 in the acquisition of another company should be disclosed in the report of the Board of Directors to enable users of financial statements to make proper evaluations and decisions.
  2. As per AS 4, adjustment to assets and liabilities are required for events occurring after the balance sheet date that provide additional information materially affecting the determination of the amounts relating to conditions existing at the Balance Sheet date. A debtor for ₹2,50,000 suffered heavy loss due to earthquake in the second week of March, 2024 which was not covered by insurance. This information with its implications was already known to the company. The fact that he became bankrupt in May, 2024 (after the balance sheet date) is only an additional information related to the existing condition on the balance sheet date. Accordingly, full provision for bad debts amounting ₹2,50,000 should be made, to cover the loss arising due to the insolvency of a debtor, in the final accounts for the year ended 31st March 2024.
  3. As per AS 4, adjustments to assets and liabilities are required for events occurring after the balance sheet date that provide additional information materially affecting the determination of the amounts relating to conditions existing at the balance sheet date. In the given case, since Hari Ltd. was sued by a competitor for infringement of a trademark during the year 2023-24 for which the provision was also made by it, the decision of the Court on 26th May, 2024, for payment of the penalty will constitute as an adjusting event because it is an event occurred before approval of the financial statements. Therefore, Hari Ltd. should adjust the provision upward by ₹4 lakhs to reflect the award decreed by the Court to be paid by them to its competitor.
  4. As the embezzlement of cash comes to the notice of company management only after approval of financial statements by board of directors of the company, then the treatment will be done as per the provisions of AS 5 "Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies" and the same will not be adjusted in the financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2024. This being an extra-ordinary item should be disclosed in the statement of profit and loss as a part of loss for the year ending March, 2025, in a manner, that its impact on current profit or loss can be perceived.
  5. Collection of cheques after balance sheet date is not an adjusting event even if the cheques bear the date of 31st March. Recognition of cheques in hand is therefore not consistent with requirements of AS 4. Moreover, the collection of cheques after balance sheet date does not represent any material change or commitments affecting financial position of the enterprise and no disclosure of such collections in the Directors' Report is necessary.
Author’s Note: A common error is treating all five items uniformly as either "always adjust" or "always disclose only", rather than separately testing each event against AS 4's adjusting-versus-non-adjusting distinction on its own facts — in particular, confusing the Court's post-year-end decision (an adjusting event, since it relates to a liability that already existed and was already provided for at the balance sheet date) with the cashier's embezzlement discovered after the accounts were approved (a subsequent-period item under AS 5, not an adjustment to the year already closed).
