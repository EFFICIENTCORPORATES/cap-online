MTP September 2025 Set 2 · Q1(b) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · Topic 4.3/4.4: Related-party status ceasing mid-year, directors serving in an individual professional capacity, and sole-customer economic dependence not amounting to related-party status Compulsory
Question:

Answer the following with respect to AS 18: (i) ABC Ltd. sold goods of ₹2,00,000 to its associate company for the 1st quarter ending 30.06.2025. After that the related party relationship ceased to exist. However, goods were supplied to any other ordinary customer. Decide whether transactions of the entire year have to be disclosed as related party transaction. (ii) If the majority of directors of Arjun Ltd. constitute the majority of the Board of another Company Bheem Ltd. in their individual capacity as professionals (and not by virtue of their being Directors in Arjun Ltd.). Are both the companies related? (iii) Asha Ltd. sells all the manufactured furniture of ₹1,00,00,000 to Sasha Ltd, as per agreement. Sasha Ltd. is the only customer to Asha Ltd. In the financial statements, Asha Ltd. wants to present Sasha company as a related party. Comment on the disclosure requirement.

Answer / Solution:

(i) As per AS 18, parties are considered related if at any time during the reporting period one party has the ability to control the other or exercise significant influence over the other. Transactions of ABC Ltd. with its associate company for the first quarter ending 30.06.2025 only are required to be disclosed as related party transactions, as the company had the ability to exercise significant influence only until 30.6.2025. The transactions for the period in which the related party relationship did not exist need not be reported.

(ii) Arjun Ltd. cannot be said to control the composition of the board of directors of Bheem Ltd., as the directors have been appointed in their individual capacity as professionals and not by virtue of their being directors in Arjun Ltd. Hence, it cannot be concluded that the companies are related merely because the majority of the directors of one company became the majority of the directors of the second in their individual capacity as professionals.

(iii) In the context of AS 18, a single customer, supplier, franchiser, distributor, or general agent with whom an enterprise transacts a significant volume of business cannot be construed as a Related Party Relationship merely by virtue of the resulting economic dependence. There is economic dependence between the companies, but no one controls or exercises significant influence on the other. Asha Ltd. need not report Sasha Company as its related party in its financial statements.

Author’s Note: A common error here is over-extending "related party" to any relationship of economic significance — mere economic dependence on a sole customer, or directors overlapping only in an unrelated individual professional capacity, does not by itself create control or significant influence under AS 18.

You are required to: (i) Identify the related parties for the reporting entities – AX Limited, CX Limited and EX Limited. (ii) If DX Limited would have sold its investment in EX Limited on 1st October, 2023, but goods were continued to be supplied by DX Limited to EX Limited throughout the year, will this scenario change your answer with respect to any of the reporting entity mentioned in point (i)? Give reasons for your answer as per AS 18. (4 Marks)

RTP January 2025 · Q13 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 4.6: A related party transaction requires disclosure whether or not a price is actually charged for the transfer of resources or obligations
Question:

Will transactions with related parties, for services provided/received free of cost, be required to be disclosed?

A Limited has a corporate communications department, which centralises the public relations function for the whole group of A Limited and its subsidiaries. No charges are, however, levied by A Limited on its subsidiaries and accordingly, these transactions are not given accounting recognition. Would these constitute related party transactions requiring disclosure under AS 18 in the standalone financial statements of A Limited?

Answer / Solution:

These transactions would require disclosure under AS 18 in the standalone financial statements of A Limited. As per paragraph 10 of AS 18, a related party transaction is "a transfer of resources or obligations between related parties, regardless of whether or not a price is charged". In the given situation, there is a transfer of resources from A Limited to its subsidiaries, though no price is charged for the same. Hence, it will constitute a related party transaction and will require disclosure in the financial statements of A Ltd.

Author’s Note: A common error is concluding that a related party transaction requires disclosure under AS 18 only if a price is actually charged, missing that the standard's own definition explicitly covers a transfer of resources or obligations between related parties regardless of whether or not a price is charged.

RTP January 2026 · Q14 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 4.6: Expense reimbursement to a KMP is not remuneration and does not require AS 18 disclosure
Question:

Mr. Rajiv, a KMP of Raijada Limited, received a cheque of ₹70,000 towards reimbursement of expenses incurred on hotel stay, conference expenses, etc. while attending a meeting on behalf of the company. Is this transaction covered under AS-18?

Answer / Solution:

Mr. Rajiv, as a KMP, is a related party of Raijada Limited under AS 18. However, a reimbursement of expenses actually incurred on the company's behalf is not in the nature of remuneration or a related-party transaction requiring disclosure — it is simply the company bearing its own cost, routed through the KMP. Hence, this reimbursement need not be disclosed as a related-party transaction under AS 18.

Author’s Note: Some examinees concluded disclosure was required simply because Mr. Rajiv is a related party, without separately testing whether the specific transaction (a cost reimbursement, not remuneration) actually falls within AS 18's disclosure scope.

RTP May 2024 · Q7(i) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 4.3: Related-party status requires either a voting-power interest giving control/significant influence, or the ability to direct/instruct the board (key management personnel) - day-to-day management alone under a fee contract does not qualify
Question:

A Ltd. enters into an agreement with Mr. Bhola for running a business for a fixed amount payable to the later every year. The contract states that the day-to-day management of the business will be handled by Mr. Bhola, while all financial and operating policy decisions are taken by the Board of Directors of the Company. Mr. Bhola does not have any voting power in A Limited. You are required to suggest how this transaction will be treated as at the closing date i.e. on 31st March, 2023 for the purposes of AS 18 'Related Party Disclosures'.

Answer / Solution:

Mr. Bhola will not be considered as a related party of A Ltd. in view of provisions of AS 18 "Related Party Disclosures" which states, "individuals owning, directly or indirectly, an interest in the voting power of the reporting enterprise that gives them control or significant influence over the enterprise, and relatives of any such individual are related parties". In the given case, in the absence of share ownership, Mr. Bhola would not be considered to exercise significant influence on A Limited, even though there is an agreement giving him the power to manage the company. Further, the fact that Mr Bhola does not have the ability to direct or instruct the board of directors does not qualify him as a key management personnel.

Author’s Note: A common error here is treating anyone contractually responsible for day-to-day management as automatically a related party, without checking for an actual voting-power interest or the ability to control the board's financial and operating policy decisions.

RTP May 2024 · Q7(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 4.7: Related-party disclosure applies if the relationship existed at any time during the reporting period, even if it has since ended before year-end
Question:

Shri Manoj a relative of key management personnel received remuneration of ₹3,50,000 for his services in the company for the period from 1st April, 2022 to 30th June, 2022. On 1st July, 2022, he left the service. You are required to suggest how this transaction will be treated as at the closing date i.e. on 31st March, 2023 for the purposes of AS 18 'Related Party Disclosures'.

Answer / Solution:

According to AS 18 on 'Related Party Disclosures', parties are considered to be related if at any time during the reporting period one party has the ability to control the other party or exercise significant influence over the other party in making financial and/or operating decisions. Hence, Shri Manoj, a relative of key management personnel should be identified as related party for disclosure in the financial statements for the year ended 31.3.2023 as he received remuneration for his services in the company for the period from 1st April, 2022 to 30th June, 2022.

Author’s Note: A common error here is excluding the transaction from disclosure on the reasoning that Shri Manoj was no longer employed as at the balance sheet date, missing that AS 18's "at any time during the reporting period" test is satisfied by the relationship having existed for even part of the year.

RTP May 2025 · Q17 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · A day-to-day hotel manager with no voting power and no control over financial/operating policy decisions is not a related party under AS 18
Question:

SP Hotels Limited enters into an agreement with Mr. A for running its hotel for a fixed return payable to the latter every year. The contract involves the day-to-day management of the hotel, while all financial and operating policy decisions are taken by the Board of Directors of the company. Mr. A does not own any voting power in SP Hotels Limited. Would he be considered as a related party of SP Hotels Limited?

Answer / Solution:

Mr. A will not be considered as a related party of SP Hotels Limited, in view of paragraph 3(c) of AS 18, which states: "individuals owning, directly or indirectly, an interest in the voting power of the reporting enterprise that gives them control or significant influence over the enterprise, and relatives of any such individual." In the given case, in the absence of share ownership, Mr. A would not be considered to exercise significant influence over SP Hotels Limited, even though there is an agreement giving him the power to manage the day-to-day operations of the company. Further, the fact that Mr. A does not have the ability to direct or instruct the Board of Directors (which retains all financial and operating policy decisions) means he does not qualify as a key management personnel either.

Author’s Note: A common error is treating Mr. A as a related party simply because he has day-to-day management authority over the hotel, without separately testing whether he has the voting power or board-level control that AS 18 actually requires for related-party or key-management-personnel status.

RTP September 2025 · Q18 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 4.4: Only transactions during the period the related party relationship actually existed require disclosure
Question:

X Ltd. sold goods to its associate company for the 1st quarter ending 30.6.2024, priced as it would for any ordinary customer. After that quarter, the related party relationship ceased to exist. Decide whether transactions for the entire year have to be disclosed as related party transactions.

Answer / Solution:

No. Per AS 18 para 23, only the transactions during the period the related party relationship actually existed — here, the first quarter ending 30.6.2024 — need be disclosed as related party transactions. Transactions in the remaining periods, after the relationship ceased, need not be disclosed as such.

Author’s Note: Some examinees required disclosure for the whole year merely because a related party relationship existed at some point during it, not applying the period-specific test AS 18 actually uses.
