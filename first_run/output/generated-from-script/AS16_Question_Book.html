MTP January 2026 Set 1 · Q1(b) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U4 : AS 16 — Borrowing Costs / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 4.5 Compulsory
Question:

On 1st April, 2024, A Limited started the construction of an Office Building (qualifying asset; land is a separate asset, not part of the qualifying asset). For construction, the company raised a specific loan of ₹14 lakhs from a Bank at 12% p.a. Interest income of ₹15,000 was earned on this loan while held in anticipation of payments. The company's other outstanding loans on 1st April, 2024: ₹20,00,000 at 15%; ₹30,00,000 at 8%. Construction started 1st April, 2024, completed 31st January, 2025 (ready for intended use). Payments to the contractor: 1st April 2024 ₹4,00,000; 1st August 2024 ₹10,00,000; 1st December 2024 ₹25,00,000; 31st January 2025 ₹5,00,000. Life of building 20 years, straight line depreciation. Required: (i) borrowing cost to be capitalized; (ii) initial journal entry (interest paid at year end); (iii) depreciation for the year ending 31st March, 2025; (iv) carrying value of building as on 31st March, 2025.

Answer / Solution:

(i) Weighted average accumulated expenses and borrowing cost capitalized:

1 Aug 202410,00,000Specific10,00,000 × 12% × 10/121,00,000

Less: interest income on specific borrowing(15,000)

Working note — weighted average rate on general borrowings: (20,00,000×15%=3,00,000) + (30,00,000×8%=2,40,000) = 5,40,000 on 50,00,000 = 10.8%.

(ii) Journal entry (31.1.2025): Building A/c Dr. 45,70,000; To Bank A/c 44,00,000; To Interest Payable A/c (borrowing cost) 1,70,000. (Cost of building = 44,00,000 payments + 1,70,000 capitalised interest.)

(iii) Depreciation for year ended 31.3.2025 = 45,70,000 / 20 years × 2/12 (Feb–Mar, post-completion) = ₹38,083.33.

MTP May 2026 Set 1 · Q1(a) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U4 : AS 16 — Borrowing Costs / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 4.4/4.6/4.7 Compulsory
Question:

Glen Ltd. began construction of a new building on 1st January, 2025. On 1st April, 2025, following two loans were obtained to fund the construction cost:
(i) Loan of ₹60,00,000 from Data Bank Ltd. was taken at interest rate of 8% per annum. This loan was fully utilized for construction of the new building.
(ii) Loan of ₹20,00,000 from Satya Bank Ltd. Out of this, loan amount of ₹6,00,000 was utilized for working capital purpose. Total interest of ₹1,92,000 were paid to Satya Bank Ltd. for the financial year 2025-26.
Construction of the new building was completed on 31st January, 2026 and was ready for its intended use on the same date.
None of the loan was repaid during the year. The building is a qualifying asset for the purpose of AS-16.
Out of loan from Data Bank Ltd., surplus funds were temporarily invested for the short period of time. This temporary investment earned interest of ₹30,000.
You are required to calculate the amount of interest (a) to be capitalized, (b) to be charged to profit and loss account from the total interest incurred as borrowing cost during the year 2025-26 (as per AS-16). (5 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

According to AS 16 “Borrowing Costs”, borrowing costs that are directly attributable to the acquisition, construction or production of a qualifying asset should be capitalized as part of the cost of that asset. The amount of borrowing costs eligible for capitalization should be determined in accordance with this Standard. Other borrowing costs should be recognised as an expense in the period in which they are incurred.

The standard also states that to the extent that funds are borrowed specifically for the purpose of obtaining a qualifying asset, the amount of borrowing costs eligible for capitalization on that asset should be determined as the actual borrowing costs incurred on that borrowing during the period less any income on the temporary investment of those borrowings.

Thus, eligible borrowing cost on Loan of data bank to be capitalized:
= ₹(60,00,000 x 8%) x 10/12 − ₹30,000
= ₹(4,80,000 x 10/12) = ₹4,00,000
= ₹4,00,000 − ₹30,000
= ₹3,70,000

LoanParticularsNature of assetsInterest to be Capitalized (₹)Interest to be charged to Profit & Loss Account (₹)
Data bankConstruction of factory buildingQualifying Asset3,70,000(4,80,000 − 4,00,000) = 80,000
Satya BankConstruction of factory buildingQualifying Asset(1,92,000 x 14/20) x 10/12 = 1,12,000(1,92,000 x 14/20) x 2/12 = 22,400
Satya BankWorking CapitalNot a Qualifying AssetNIL(1,92,000 x 6/20) = ₹57,600
Total₹4,82,000₹1,60,000

Note: Loan from Satya bank is considered to be specific borrowings.

MTP May 2026 Set 2 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 2] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U4 : AS 16 — Borrowing Costs / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 4.11
Question:

When capitalisation of borrowing cost should cease as per Accounting Standard 16? Explain in brief. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

Capitalization of borrowing costs should cease when substantially all the activities necessary to prepare the qualifying asset for its intended use or sale are complete. An asset is normally ready for its intended use or sale when its physical construction or production is complete even though routine administrative work might still continue. If minor modifications such as the decoration of a property to the user's specification, are all that are outstanding, this indicates that substantially all the activities are complete. When the construction of a qualifying asset is completed in parts and a completed part is capable of being used while construction continues for the other parts, capitalization of borrowing costs in relation to a part should cease when substantially all the activities necessary to prepare that part for its intended use or sale are complete.

Author’s Note: Several examinees described only the overall-completion cessation rule and omitted the part-completion exception, despite this being the same principle tested numerically elsewhere in this paper's Case Scenario I.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP January 2026 · Q12 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U4 : AS 16 — Borrowing Costs / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 4.2
Question:

Can interest be included in the cost of inventories?

Answer / Solution:

As a general rule, AS 2 (para 12) provides that interest and other borrowing costs are usually not related to bringing inventories to their present location and condition, and are therefore usually excluded from inventory cost.

However, AS 16 (para 6) requires borrowing costs directly attributable to acquisition/construction/production of a qualifying asset to be capitalised, and para 5 specifically includes "inventories that require a substantial period of time to bring them to a saleable condition" as an example of a qualifying asset (e.g. liquor requiring 12+ months of maturation, per ASI 1). It is only in such exceptional cases — where time is a major factor in changing the condition of the inventory — that borrowing costs are included in inventory valuation. Routinely/repetitively manufactured inventory over a short period is never a qualifying asset, so its borrowing costs are never capitalised.

Author’s Note: Some examinees answered with a flat "no", missing the exceptional qualifying-asset case (e.g. maturing liquor) where AS 16 does require capitalisation into inventory cost.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q16 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U4 : AS 16 — Borrowing Costs / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 4.3
Question:

Is it permissible to capitalise borrowing costs incurred on assets necessary for the construction of qualifying assets? A company constructing a large manufacturing plant in a backward area also purchased a residential building (funded by a long-term loan) to house workers engaged in the plant's construction, intending to dispose of the building once construction completes. If the manufacturing plant is a qualifying asset, are the borrowing costs on the residential-building loan eligible for capitalisation?

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 16 para 3, a qualifying asset "necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale"; para 5 clarifies that assets ready for use when acquired are not qualifying assets. Prima facie, the residential building appears ready for use on acquisition, suggesting no capitalisation.

However, since providing housing is necessary for the construction of the plant, the borrowing costs on the building loan are, in substance, directly attributable to the construction of the manufacturing plant. The company should therefore capitalise these borrowing costs as part of the cost of the manufacturing plant, up to the period the plant is ready for its intended use.

Author’s Note: Some examinees stopped at the observation that the residential building is itself "ready for use" and concluded no capitalisation applies, missing the substance-over-form link to the main construction project's necessity.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
