MTP January 2025 Set 1 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 2] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.5: Schedule III bonus-share disclosure requirement - source of a bonus issue is not mandated to be disclosed
Question:

In the financial statements of the financial year 2023-2024, Alpha Ltd. has mentioned in the notes to accounts that during financial year, 24,000 equity shares of ₹10 each were issued as fully paid bonus shares. However, the source from which these bonus shares were issued has not been disclosed. Is such non-disclosure a violation of the Schedule III to the Companies Act? Comment.

Answer / Solution:

Schedule III has come into force for the Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss Account prepared for the financial year commencing on or after 1st April, 2023. As per Part I of the Schedule III, a company should, inter alia, disclose in notes to accounts for the period of 5 years immediately preceding the balance sheet date (31st March, 2024 in the instant case) the aggregate number and class of shares allotted as fully paid-up bonus shares. Schedule III does not require a company to disclose the source from which bonus shares have been issued. Therefore, non-disclosure of source from which bonus shares have been issued does not violate the Schedule III to the Companies Act.

Author’s Note: A frequent misconception is assuming Schedule III requires disclosure of the specific reserve a bonus issue was funded from, when only the aggregate number and class of bonus shares over the preceding five years must be disclosed.

MTP January 2026 Set 1 · Q6(b) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1.6: Declaration of dividend out of accumulated reserves - three-condition test under the Dividend Rules
Question:

Z Limited as on 31st March, 2024: Equity shares of ₹100 each ₹500 lakhs; General Reserve ₹100 lakhs; Loss for the year ₹5 lakhs. Due to absence of profits, management recommends a 10% dividend on equity share capital out of general reserve. Rates of equity dividend for the last 5 years preceding 2023-24: 2022-23 12%, 2021-22 14%, 2020-21 10%, 2019-20 10%, 2018-19 7%. As an accountant, suggest whether the recommendation is justified; if not, suggest the rate of dividend.

Answer / Solution:

Three conditions govern declaring dividend out of free reserves (Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules): (1) Rate ≤ average rate of last 3 years: 10% < 12% [(12+14+10)/3] — satisfied. (2) Amount drawn ≤ 10% of (paid-up capital + free reserves): 55,00,000 drawn (10% dividend 50,00,000 + loss 5,00,000) vs. maximum 60,00,000 [10% of (500+100) lakhs] — satisfied. (3) Reserves after withdrawal ≥ 15% of paid-up capital: balance after withdrawal (100−55=45 lakhs) is less than 75 lakhs (15% of 500 lakhs) — not satisfied.

Since condition (3) fails, the 10% recommendation is not justified. Maximum permissible withdrawal = opening reserves (100 lakhs) − minimum reserves to retain (75 lakhs, i.e. 15% of paid-up capital) = 25 lakhs; less current year's loss (5 lakhs) = 20 lakhs maximum divisible profit. Permissible dividend rate = 20,00,000 / 5,00,00,000 = 4%.

Author’s Note: Some examinees checked only the rate-cap and amount-cap conditions, missing the third condition (post-withdrawal reserves must remain at least 15% of paid-up capital), which is the one that actually fails here.

Answer / Solution:
