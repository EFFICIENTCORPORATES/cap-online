MTP January 2026 Set 1 · Q2(a) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U1 : AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.10
Question:

Happy Limited is a Textile Manufacturing Company producing Polyester (P) and Nylon (N); a by-product Fiber (F) is also produced. Cost of production: Raw material for 30,000 units ₹3,50,000; Wages ₹1,60,000; Fixed overheads ₹1,20,000; Variable overheads ₹60,000. Output: Polyester 12,500 units, Nylon 10,000 units, Fiber 3,200 units. Closing inventory: Polyester 1,600 units, Nylon 400 units. Average market price: Polyester ₹100/unit, Nylon ₹60/unit; by-product Fiber sold @₹40/unit, profit of ₹8,000 on sale of by-product after separate processing expenses ₹10,000 and packing charges ₹9,000. ₹5,000 realized from sale of scrap. Compute the value of closing inventory of Polyester and Nylon.

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 2 'Valuation of Inventories', most by-products, being immaterial, are measured at net realizable value, deducted from the cost of the main product.

W.N.1 — NRV of by-product Fiber: Selling price (3,200 × ₹40) 1,28,000 − separate processing charges 10,000 − packing charges 9,000 = ₹1,09,000.

W.N.2 — Joint cost allocation: Raw material 3,50,000 + Wages 1,60,000 + Fixed OH 1,20,000 + Variable OH 60,000 = 6,90,000; less NRV of Fiber (1,09,000) and scrap sale value (5,000) = Joint cost to allocate: 5,76,000.

MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 2] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U1 : AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.9
Question:

"In determining the cost of inventories, it is appropriate to exclude certain costs and recognise them as expenses in the period in which they are incurred." Provide examples of such costs as per AS 2 (Revised) 'Valuation of Inventories'.

Answer / Solution:

Examples of costs excluded from inventory cost and expensed as incurred: (a) abnormal amounts of wasted materials, labour, or other production costs; (b) storage costs, unless required by the production process itself; (c) administrative overheads that do not contribute to bringing the inventories to their present location and condition; (d) selling and distribution costs.

Author’s Note: Some examinees included all administrative overheads as excluded costs, without distinguishing those that do (production-related) vs. do not (general admin) contribute to bringing inventory to its present location and condition.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ September 2025 · Q1(b) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U1 : AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.14 Compulsory
Question:

SR Limited manufactures finished goods 'MP' from raw material 'RP'. Year ended 31st March 2025: Opening Inventory MP 4,000 units ₹1,20,000; RP 4,400 units ₹52,800. Purchase of RP 40,000 units ₹4,80,000. Labour ₹3,23,200. Fixed Overheads ₹3,15,000. Sales 40,200 units ₹11,20,000. Closing Inventory: MP 4,200 units; RP 4,000 units. Expected production 45,000 units of MP. Due to fall in market demand, sales price of MP fell to ₹22/unit; replacement cost of RP ₹11.50/unit on closing day. Calculate the value of Closing Stock of 'MP' and 'RP' as on 31st March 2025.

Answer / Solution:

W.N.1 Actual production: Sales 40,200 + Closing FG 4,200 − Opening FG 4,000 = 40,400 units.

W.N.2 RP consumed: Opening RP 4,400 + Purchases 40,000 = 44,400 available; less Closing RP 4,000 = 40,400 units consumed (1 unit RP per unit MP produced).

W.N.3 Cost per unit RP (weighted average): (Opening value 52,800 + Purchases 4,80,000) / 44,400 units = 5,32,800/44,400 = ₹12.00/unit.

Cost of Material Consumed (40,400 × ₹12)4,84,800
Direct Labour3,23,200
Fixed Overhead (3,15,000 × 40,400/45,000 — based on normal/expected production, not actual)2,82,800
Cost of Production10,90,800
Cost per unit (10,90,800/40,400)₹27
Net Realisable Value per unit (selling price)₹22

Since NRV (₹22) is less than cost (₹27), the closing MP inventory is valued at NRV. Since finished goods NRV is below cost, the raw material RP feeding it is valued at replacement cost (₹11.50), per AS 2's write-down rule.

Value of closing inventory: MP (4,200 × ₹22) = ₹92,400. RP (4,000 × ₹11.50) = ₹46,000.

Examiner’s Comment: The performance of examinees in this part was not satisfactory. A majority of them were unable to determine the value of closing inventory of finished goods (MP) and raw materials (RP) in accordance with AS 2 "Valuation of Inventories". Several examinees failed to compute the cost of production correctly, particularly where certain items required proportionate allocation. Many could ascertain only the labour cost of ₹3,23,200, while the remaining elements of cost were incorrectly computed. Further, a large number of examinees valued the closing stock of finished goods (on NRV basis) and raw materials (on replacement cost basis) incorrectly.Real ICAI Examiner’s Comment, quoted from the official document for this sitting.

RTP January 2026 · Q3 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U1 : AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.3
Question:

A private limited company manufacturing fancy terry towels had valued its closing inventory of finished goods at realisable value, inclusive of profit and export cash incentives. Firm contracts had been received and goods were packed for export, but ownership had not been transferred to the foreign buyers. Comment on the valuation of the inventories by the company.

Answer / Solution:

AS 2 requires inventories to be valued at the lower of historical cost and net realisable value. AS 9 permits valuing goods at net realisable value only in specific industries where performance is substantially complete prior to the transaction generating revenue — e.g. harvested agricultural crops or extracted mineral ores, where a sale is assured under forward contract/government guarantee or a ready market exists with negligible risk of failure to sell.

Fancy terry towels do not fall into the agricultural-crop or mineral-ore category. Since ownership had not transferred to the foreign buyers, the sale was not yet complete. The closing inventory should therefore have been valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value, not at net realisable value. Further, export cash incentives are recognised only in the year the export sale actually takes place, not accrued into inventory valuation ahead of the sale. The company's valuation policy is therefore not correct.

Author’s Note: Some examinees accepted the NRV-inclusive-of-profit valuation on the basis of firm export contracts alone, not recognising that the AS 9 exception is limited to specific industries (agricultural/mineral) and does not extend to manufactured goods merely because a sale contract exists.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q6 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U1 : AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.10
Question:

Zing Ltd., 2024-25: Raw Material closing stock 700 units, cost ₹35/unit, replacement cost ₹20/unit. Finished products FP1/FP2: Production 3,000/1,600 units; Closing stock 500/300 units. Material consumed ₹3,20,000; Direct labour ₹1,60,000; Direct expenses ₹78,000 (these three cost figures pertain to combined joint production). Fixed overhead ₹95,000, including ₹15,000 godown rent (for storing finished products, not a production cost). 1,000 units of by-product BY also emerged, sold @₹12/unit after ₹2,500 expense; ₹4,800 realized from scrap sale. Average market price: FP1 ₹160/unit, FP2 ₹100/unit. Calculate the value of closing stock per AS 2.

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 2, most by-products/scrap are measured at NRV, deducted from the cost of the main product.

NRV of by-product BY: Selling price (1,000×₹12) 12,000, less separate processing charges 2,500 = ₹9,500.

Joint cost to allocate: Raw material 3,20,000 + Labour 1,60,000 + Direct expenses 78,000 + Fixed overhead (95,000−15,000 godown rent, excluded as unrelated to production) 80,000 = 6,38,000; less NRV of BY (9,500) and scrap sale value (4,800) = ₹6,23,700 to allocate between FP1 and FP2.

Since both FP1 (₹155.93 cost vs ₹160 selling price) and FP2 (₹97.45 vs ₹100) sell above cost, both finished goods are valued at cost, and the raw material feeding them is valued at its own cost (₹35/unit, not written down to the ₹20 replacement cost).

Closing inventory: FP1 (500×₹155.93) = ₹77,965. FP2 (300×₹97.45) = ₹29,235. Raw material (700×₹35) = ₹24,500. Total closing inventory = ₹77,965 + ₹29,235 + ₹24,500 = ₹1,31,700.
