MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q6(a) [OR-alt 1] · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.6
Question:

Indicate in each case whether revenue can be recognized and when, as per AS-9: (i) Delivery is delayed at buyer's request but buyer takes title and accepts billing. (ii) Instalment Sales. (iii) Trade discounts and volume rebates. (iv) Insurance agency commission for rendering services. (v) Advertising commission.

Answer / Solution:

(i) Revenue is recognised notwithstanding the delayed physical delivery, so long as delivery is expected and the goods are on hand, identified, and ready for delivery at the time of sale recognition (not merely an intention to acquire/manufacture in time).

(ii) When consideration is receivable in instalments, revenue attributable to the sale price (excluding interest) is recognised at the date of sale; the interest element is recognised as revenue proportionately to the unpaid balance.

(iii) Trade discounts and volume rebates received are not revenue (they reduce cost); those given are deducted in determining revenue.

(iv) Insurance agency commissions are recognised on the effective commencement or renewal dates of the related policies.

(v) Advertising commission is recognised when the service is completed — media commission when the advertisement appears before the public (with the agency's intimation received); production commission when the project is completed.

Author’s Note: Some examinees deferred revenue on the delayed-delivery sale entirely, missing that the goods being on-hand and ready is what matters, not the physical transfer date itself.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ September 2025 · Q1(c-i) · Marks: 1 · Approx Time: 2 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.4 Compulsory
Question:

AB Limited purchased goods on credit from XY Limited for ₹150 lakhs for export. The export order was cancelled; AB Limited decided to sell the same goods locally with a price discount. XY Limited agreed to a 12% price discount requested by AB Limited. XY's chief accountant wants to adjust the sales figure to the extent of the discount. Discuss whether this treatment is as per the relevant Accounting Standard.

Answer / Solution:

Correct. Per AS 9, trade discounts given should be deducted in determining revenue. The 12% discount should be deducted from the turnover figure for revenue recognition purposes — XY Limited's adjustment of the sales figure to the extent of the discount is correct as per AS 9.

PYQ September 2025 · Q1(c-ii) · Marks: 1 · Approx Time: 2 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.5 Compulsory
Question:

XY Limited recognized dividend income of ₹10 lakhs on accrual basis on securities of face value ₹100 lakhs held at 31st March 2025. The dividend (10%) was declared on 30th April 2025, having been proposed on 30th March 2025. Comment.

Answer / Solution:

Incorrect. Dividends from investments in securities are not recognised in the Statement of Profit and Loss until the right to receive payment is established. Here, the dividend is proposed 30th March 2025 but declared only 30th April 2025 — the right to receive payment is established only on 30th April 2025. Recognising ₹10 lakhs on accrual basis at 31st March 2025 is not correct as per AS 9.

PYQ September 2025 · Q1(c-iii) · Marks: 1 · Approx Time: 2 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.6 Compulsory
Question:

XY Limited sold goods of ₹1,50,000 on approval basis on 15th December 2024; approval period 3 months, after which goods are considered sold. Buyer approved 60% of goods by 31st January 2025; no approval/disapproval received for the remainder by 31st March 2025. The accountant recognised ₹1,50,000 as revenue on 15th December, 2024. Comment.

Answer / Solution:

Incorrect as recognised. For goods sold on approval, revenue should not be recognised until formal acceptance, an act adopting the transaction, or expiry of the rejection period. Revenue for ₹90,000 (60%) should be recognised on 31st January, 2025 (date of approval); the remaining ₹60,000 should be recognised on 15th March, 2025, when the 3-month approval period lapsed without response (deemed acceptance) — not the full ₹1,50,000 on 15th December, 2024 as the accountant did.

PYQ September 2025 · Q1(c-iv) · Marks: 1 · Approx Time: 2 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.7 Compulsory
Question:

In 2024-2025, XY Limited supplied goods worth ₹80,000 on consignment basis to ABC (a retail outlet). ABC sold goods worth ₹60,000 during the year; the rest remains in its store. XY recognizes ₹60,000 revenue in its books for 2024-2025. Comment.

Answer / Solution:

Correct. Unsold consignment goods (₹20,000) remain XY Limited's closing inventory; revenue on a consignment sale is recognised only when the goods are sold to a third party by the consignee. XY's recognition of ₹60,000 revenue is correct as per AS 9.

RTP January 2026 · Q8 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.2
Question:

Class Ltd., a real-estate developer, purchased land for ₹225 crore in FY2025 intending a residential complex, then sold it a few months later for ₹360 crore due to a strategic reallocation and favourable market conditions. All legal formalities completed. Advise on revenue recognition for the year ended 31st March, 2025.

Answer / Solution:

AS 9 recognises revenue when earned with reasonable certainty of collection, arising from the enterprise's ordinary activities. Class Ltd. is in the business of buying and selling properties; the land forms part of its inventory. The ₹360 crore sale is therefore a transaction arising from ordinary activities, and revenue is recognised when the sale is completed and collection is reasonably certain.

Per AS 5, this is not an extraordinary item, since it arises in the ordinary course of business — though given its size, its nature and amount should be separately disclosed if relevant to explaining the enterprise's performance. The ₹360 crore is recognised as revenue in the year the sale is completed.

Author’s Note: Some examinees treated the large, unusual sale as an extraordinary item given its size and the company's stated change in strategy, missing that dealing in land is Class Ltd.'s core ordinary business, so scale alone does not make a transaction extraordinary.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q11(i) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.7
Question:

Tonk Tanners (leather shoes): On 31st December, 2024, shoes worth ₹3,20,000 were sent to Mohan Shoes for sale on consignment; 25% remained unsold with Mohan Shoes as on 31st March, 2025. Advise when revenue is recognised in 2024-25 per AS 9.

Answer / Solution:

Mohan Shoes is a consignee (agent), not a buyer — risk and reward remain with Tonk Tanners until Mohan Shoes sells to a third party. Revenue is recognised only for the goods actually sold by the consignee: ₹3,20,000 × 75% = ₹2,40,000 recognised as revenue in 2024-25; the remaining 25% (unsold) stays as Tonk Tanners' closing inventory.

Author’s Note: Some examinees recognised the full ₹3,20,000 upon dispatch to the consignee, treating the consignment shipment itself as a completed sale.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q11(ii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.8
Question:

On 10th January, 2025, Tonk Tanners supplied shoes worth ₹4,50,000 to Shani Shoes and concurrently agreed to repurchase the same goods on 11th April, 2025. Advise on revenue recognition.

Answer / Solution:

A sale with a concurrent agreement to repurchase the same goods is, in substance, a financing arrangement, not a genuine sale. The resulting cash inflow is not revenue and should not be recognised as revenue in 2024-25.

Author’s Note: Some examinees recognised the ₹4,50,000 as ordinary sales revenue, not recognising the repurchase commitment as converting the transaction into a financing arrangement in substance.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q11(iii) · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C8_U2 : AS 9 — Revenue Recognition / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 1.4
Question:

On 21st March, 2025, shoes worth ₹1,60,000 were sold to Shoe Shine, but due to their showroom refurbishment, delivery was made on 12th April, 2025 at their request. Advise on revenue recognition.

Answer / Solution:

If, on 21st March 2025, Shoe Shine took title and accepted billing for the goods, the sale is complete and all risks/rewards of ownership have transferred, even though physical delivery was delayed at the buyer's own request. Provided no significant uncertainty exists over the consideration, revenue is recognised in 2024-25, regardless of the delayed physical delivery.

Author’s Note: Some examinees deferred revenue recognition to the actual delivery date (April 2025), not recognising that title transfer and billing acceptance, not physical delivery, is what matters here.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
