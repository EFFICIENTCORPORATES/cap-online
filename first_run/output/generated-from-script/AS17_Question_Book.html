MTP May 2026 Set 1 · Q6(c) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · M1_C4_U3 : AS 17 — Segment Reporting / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.9
Question:

Garnet Limited has 4 operating segments. The total revenue (internal and external) and assets are set out as below:

SegmentInter Segment SalesExternal SalesTotal Assets
Fan3,20010,90023,700
Light2001,40013,200
Lamp01,5004,200
Printer1,1002003,400
TOTAL4,50014,00044,500

How many reportable segments does Garnet Limited have as per the Revenue and Assets criteria given in AS 17? State Reasons for your answer. (4 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 17 ‘Segment Reporting’, a business segment or geographical segment should be identified as a reportable segment if: its revenue from sales to external customers and from other transactions with other segments is 10% or more of the total revenue – external and internal of all segments; or its segment assets are 10% or more of the total assets of all segments.

If the total external revenue attributable to reportable segments constitutes less than 75% of total enterprise revenue, additional segments should be identified as reportable segments even if they do not meet the 10% thresholds until at least 75% of total enterprise revenue is included in reportable segments. This is not applicable in the given case. In the given case 75% of External Revenue is ₹10,500 Lakhs (₹14,000 × 75%) and the total External Revenue from Reportable segments is ₹12,300 Lakhs. So, no need to add Reportable segments.

On the basis of turnover criteria segment Fan is reportable segment as its sales are more than 1,850 lakhs (10% of ₹18,500 lakhs). Moreover, total external revenue attributable to reportable segment is also more than 75% of the total enterprise revenue.

On the basis of asset criteria, Fan and Light are reportable segments as their assets are more than 4,450 lakhs (10% of ₹44,500 lakhs).

Author’s Note: Many examinees applied only the 10% revenue or asset threshold test and omitted to check the 75% cumulative external-revenue test for identifying reportable segments under AS 17.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP January 2026 · Q13 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M1_C4_U3 : AS 17 — Segment Reporting / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.3
Question:

Garnet Limited has 4 operating segments (₹ in appropriate units): Fan — Inter-segment sales 3,200, External sales 10,900, Total assets 23,700; Light — 200, 1,400, 13,200; Lamp — 0, 1,500, 4,200; Printer — 1,100, 200, 3,400; Total — 4,500, 14,000, 44,500. How many reportable segments does Garnet Limited have per the Revenue and Assets criteria in AS 17? State reasons.

Answer / Solution:

A segment is reportable if its revenue (external + inter-segment) is ≥10% of total revenue (external + internal, all segments), OR its assets are ≥10% of total assets.

Total revenue basis (external 14,000 + internal 4,500 = 18,500): 10% threshold = 1,850. Fan's total revenue (3,200+10,900=14,100) far exceeds 1,850 — Fan is reportable. (Light: 200+1,400=1,600, below threshold; Lamp: 0+1,500=1,500, below; Printer: 1,100+200=1,300, below.)

Assets basis (total 44,500): 10% threshold = 4,450. Fan (23,700) and Light (13,200) both exceed this — both are reportable on the assets criterion.

75% external-revenue test: reportable segments' external revenue must cover ≥75% of total external revenue (75% × 14,000 = 10,500). Fan and Light's combined external revenue (10,900+1,400=12,300) already exceeds 10,500, so no additional segments need to be added to meet the 75% threshold.

Conclusion: Garnet Limited has 2 reportable segments — Fan and Light (Fan qualifies on both revenue and assets; Light qualifies on assets only, but that alone is sufficient).

Author’s Note: Some examinees tested the 10% revenue threshold against external sales alone, omitting inter-segment sales from the revenue base used for that specific test.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP May 2026 · Q13 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M1_C4_U3 : AS 17 — Segment Reporting / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.5
Question:

Whether interest expense relating to overdrafts and other operating liabilities identified to a particular segment should be included in the segment expense or not?

Answer / Solution:

The interest expense relating to overdrafts and other operating liabilities identified to a particular segment should not be included as a part of the segment expense unless the operations of the segment are primarily of a financial nature or unless the interest is included as a part of the cost of inventories.

Author’s Note: Some examinees included all interest expense indiscriminately in segment expense, overlooking the two exceptions (segment primarily financial in nature, or interest forming part of inventory cost) under which such interest is included.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q17 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M1_C4_U3 : AS 17 — Segment Reporting / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.3
Question:

Cotton Garments Limited, 5 segments (₹ Crore): Segment Assets A/B/C/D/E 40/15/10/10/5 (Total 80); Segment Results (95)/5/5/(5)/15 (Total −75); Segment Revenue 310/40/30/40/30 (Total 450). The Chief Accountant believes only segment "A" should be reported. Examine this opinion per AS 17.

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 17 para 27, a segment is reportable if: (i) its revenue (external+internal) is ≥10% of total revenue; or (ii) its result (profit or loss) is ≥10% of the combined result of all profit-making segments or all loss-making segments, whichever is greater in absolute amount; or (iii) its assets are ≥10% of total assets. If reportable segments cover <75% of total external revenue, additional segments must be added until 75% is reached.

(a) Revenue test (10% of 450 = 45): only A (310) qualifies.

(b) Result test: combined profit-segments (B+C+E = 5+5+15=25) vs. combined loss-segments (A+D = 95+5=100, in absolute terms); greater = 100; 10% = 10. Segments with |result| ≥10: A (95) and E (15) qualify.

(c) Assets test (10% of 80 = 8): A, B, C, D (40,15,10,10) qualify; only E (5) falls short.

Since every segment (A through E) satisfies at least one of the three tests, all five segments must be reported per AS 17. The Chief Accountant's opinion that only segment A is reportable is therefore wrong.

Author’s Note: Some examinees checked only the revenue test (which favours only segment A) and stopped there, missing that the result and assets tests bring in the remaining segments too.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
