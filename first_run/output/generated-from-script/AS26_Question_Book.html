MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q1(b) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U6 : AS 26 — Intangible Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 6.5 Compulsory
Question:

As per provisions of AS-26, how would you deal with the following situations: (1) ₹23,00,000 paid by a manufacturing company to the legal advisor for defending the patent of a product, treated as capital expenditure. (2) During 2024-2025, a company spent ₹7,00,000 on publicity and research expenses on a new consumer product, marketed the same year but proved a failure. (3) A company spent ₹25,00,000 over three years to develop a product, charged to P&L since AS-26 capitalization criteria were not met; in the current year approval was received and the company wishes to capitalize ₹25,00,000 as a prior period item. (4) A company (turnover ₹200 crores, annual advertising budget ₹50,00,000) debited ₹50,00,000 to P&L for an extensive special initial advertisement campaign for a new product expected to generate ₹20 crore turnover.

Answer / Solution:

(1) The legal expenses of ₹23,00,000 to defend the patent should not be capitalized — subsequent expenditure on an intangible asset is expensed unless it enables the asset to generate future economic benefits in excess of its originally assessed performance and can be reliably measured and attributed. Defending an existing right does not meet this test; charge to P&L.

(2) The full ₹7,00,000 must be expensed in the year ended 31st March, 2025, since the product failed and no future economic benefit will arise.

(3) AS 26 explicitly prohibits reinstating expenditure on an intangible item that was previously recognised as an expense — the company cannot capitalize the ₹25,00,000 even with the current-year approval; it remains an expense already recognised, not a prior period item to be restated.

(4) Advertising and promotional expenditure is always charged to the Profit and Loss Account as incurred, regardless of expected future revenue benefit — the company's treatment of debiting the full ₹50,00,000 is correct.

Author’s Note: Some examinees allowed the ₹25,00,000 to be capitalized once approval was received, missing AS 26's explicit bar on reinstating expenditure once it has been recognised as an expense in an earlier period.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q6(b) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U6 : AS 26 — Intangible Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 6.7
Question:

Record Journal Entries for amortization in the books of Huge Ltd. for the year ended 31st March, 2025 with reference to AS-26: Patent Rights acquired for ₹340 lakhs on 01.04.2023, estimated product life 4 years, amortization decided in the ratio of estimated future cash flows: Year 1 ₹140 lakhs, Year 2 ₹350 lakhs, Year 3 ₹280 lakhs, Year 4 ₹420 lakhs.

Answer / Solution:

Amortization for the second year (2024-25, the year in question): ₹340 lakhs × 350/(140+350+280+420) = 340 × 350/1,190 = ₹100 lakhs.

Journal entry (31.3.2025): Amortization A/c Dr. 100 lakhs; To Patent Rights A/c 100 lakhs. P&L A/c Dr. 100 lakhs; To Amortization A/c 100 lakhs.

Author’s Note: Some examinees amortized the patent on a straight-line basis (₹85 lakhs/year) instead of following the cash-flow-ratio method the question specifically directs.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP January 2026 · Q17 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U6 : AS 26 — Intangible Assets / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 6.7
Question:

Swift Limited acquired patent rights to manufacture Solar Roof Top Panels for ₹600 lacs. Product life cycle estimated at 5 years; amortization decided in the ratio of future cash flows: Year 1–5: 300, 300, 300, 150, 150 (₹ lacs). After Year 3, estimated balance future life revised to 3 years, with expected cash flow after Year 5 of ₹75 lacs. Determine the amortization cost of the patent for each year per AS 26.

Answer / Solution:

Years 1–3, amortized in the ratio of the originally estimated future cash flows 300:300:300:150:150 (total 1,200): each of Years 1, 2, 3 = ₹600 × 300/1,200 = ₹150 lacs/year.

Unamortized patent cost after Year 3 = 600−450 = ₹150 lacs, now amortized over the revised remaining life of 3 years (Years 4, 5, 6) in the ratio of the revised estimated future cash flows: 150:150:75 (i.e. 2:2:1). Year 4 = 150×2/5=₹60 lacs; Year 5 = ₹60 lacs; Year 6 = 150×1/5=₹30 lacs.

Total amortized over the patent's full (revised) life: 150+150+150+60+60+30 = ₹600 lacs, fully recovering the original cost.

Author’s Note: Some examinees continued amortizing the remaining balance using the original 5-year cash-flow ratio, not re-basing the calculation on the revised 3-year remaining life and its own revised cash-flow estimates.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
