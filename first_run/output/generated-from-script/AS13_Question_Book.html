MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q2(a) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U3 : AS 13 — Accounting for Investments / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.7
Question:

Prem Ltd. holds 2,000, 15% Debentures of ₹100 each in Mehar Ltd. as on 1st April, 2024 at a cost of ₹2,50,000. Interest payable 30th June and 31st December each year. Transactions during 2024-25: 1st May 2024, 1,000 debentures purchased cum-interest at ₹1,05,000; 1st November 2024, 1,200 debentures sold ex-interest at ₹1,28,200; 30th November 2024, 500 debentures purchased ex-interest at ₹54,500; 31st December 2024, 900 debentures sold cum-interest for ₹1,18,000. Prepare the Investment Account showing holdings on 31st March, 2025 at cost, using FIFO.

Answer / Solution:

15% Debentures (Investment) Account, FIFO basis — closing balance as on 31.3.25: 1,400 debentures, face value ₹1,40,000, cost ₹1,44,500 (900 from the opening 2,000 + 500 purchased 30.11.24: opening lot after the 1.11.24 sale of 1,200 leaves 800 of the original 2,000 plus the 1,000 bought 1.5.24 minus what's sold, reconciled via FIFO issue order; net closing units 1,400 as shown in the working note below).

W.N.1 — Loss on sale 1.11.24: Cost of 1,200 sold (FIFO, from opening lot) = 2,50,000/2,000 × 1,200 = ₹1,50,000. Sale proceeds ₹1,28,200. Loss = ₹21,800, charged to P&L.

W.N.2 — Profit on sale 31.12.24: Cost of 900 sold (FIFO: 800 remaining from opening lot + 100 from the 1.5.24 purchase) = (2,50,000/2,000 × 800) + (1,00,000/1,000 × 100) = 1,00,000 + 10,000 = ₹1,10,000. Sale proceeds (cum-interest, less accrued interest component) net to ₹1,11,250 principal. Profit = ₹1,250.

W.N.3 — Closing balance: 900 debentures remaining from the 1.5.24 purchase (1,000−100 sold) at ₹90,000 (proportionate cost) + 500 debentures from the 30.11.24 purchase at ₹54,500 = 1,400 debentures, ₹1,44,500.

Author’s Note: Some examinees applied weighted-average cost instead of the FIFO method the question specifically requires, or failed to separate the interest component from cum-interest purchase/sale prices before computing the capital gain/loss on disposal.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

MTP May 2026 Set 2 · Q1(b) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U3 : AS 13 — Accounting for Investments / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.5 Compulsory
Question:

On 1st April, 2025 Mr. Zoom had an opening balance of 1,000 equity shares of P Ltd. ₹1,20,000 (face value ₹100 each). On 5.04.2025 he further purchased 200 cum-right shares for ₹135 each. On 8.04.2025 the director of P Ltd announced right issue in the ratio of 1:6. Mr. Zoom waived off 100% of his entitlement of right issue in the favour of Mr. X at the rate of ₹20 each. All the shares held by Mr. Zoom had been acquired on cum right basis and the total market price (ex-right) of all these shares after the declaration of rights got reduced by ₹3,400. On 10.10.2025 Zoom sold 350 shares for ₹140 each. 31.03.2026 the market price of each share is ₹125 each.

You are required to prepare the Investment account in the books of Mr. Zoom for the year ended 31.03.2026 assuming that the shares are being valued at average cost. (7 Marks)

Answer / Solution:

In the books of Mr. Zoom for the year ending on 31.3.2026 (Scrip: Equity Shares of P Limited)

DateParticularsQtyAmountDateParticularsQtyAmount
1.4.2025To Balance b/d10001,20,0008.04.2025By Bank A/c (W.N.1) (sale of rights)4,000
5.04.2025To Bank (200 x ₹135)20027,00010.10.2025By Bank A/c (350 x ₹140)35049,000

Total12001,54,117Total12001,54,117

Working Notes:

1. Sale of Rights ₹4,000. The market price of all shares of P Ltd after shares becoming ex-rights has been reduced by ₹3,400. Out of sale proceeds of ₹4,000, ₹3,400 may be applied to reduce the carrying amount to the market value and ₹600 would be credited to the profit and loss account.

2. Profit on sale of 350 shares: Sale price of 350 shares (350 × ₹140) = ₹49,000. Less: Cost of 350 shares [(1,20,000+27,000−3,400) × 350]/1200 = ₹41,883. Profit = ₹7,117.

3. Valuation of 850 shares as on 31.03.2026: Cost price of 850 shares [(1,20,000+27,000−3,400) × 850/1,200] = ₹1,01,717. Fair Value as on 31.03.2026 (850 × ₹125) = ₹1,06,250. Cost price or fair value whichever is less = ₹1,01,717.

Author’s Note: Several examinees credited the entire ₹4,000 rights-renunciation proceeds directly to the profit and loss account, not applying AS 13's rule that such proceeds first reduce the carrying cost of the existing holding to the extent the market value has fallen, with only the balance taken to profit and loss.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

PYQ January 2026 · Q3(b) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U3 : AS 13 — Accounting for Investments / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.5
Question:

Mr. AB acquires 28,000 equity shares of ₹10 each in F Ltd. for ₹3,36,000 inclusive of brokerage and stamp duty on 1st April, 2024. F Ltd. announces: (i) 30th September, 2024, bonus declared 2:7. (ii) 30th November, 2024, rights issue 3:8 @₹15/share. Mr. AB took up 2/3 of right shares and sold the remaining rights for ₹6/share. On 18 January, 2025, Mr. AB sold 40% of his shareholdings at ₹17/share to a broker, who charged 2% brokerage.

You are required to prepare Investment Account of Mr. AB for the year ended 31st March, 2025 assuming that the shares are being valued at average cost. (7 Marks)

Working Notes: (1) Bonus Shares = (28,000/7)×2 = 8,000 shares. (2) Right Shares = (28,000+8,000)×3/8 = 13,500 shares. (6) Sale of rights amounting ₹27,000 (₹6×4,500 shares) is not shown in the Investment A/c but taken directly to the P&L statement.

RTP January 2026 · Q11 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U3 : AS 13 — Accounting for Investments / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.6
Question:

Mr. Rohan acquired 200 equity shares of Zeta Technologies Ltd. on a cum-right basis for ₹1,40,000 as a long-term investment. The company announced a 1:1 rights issue at ₹214/share. Mr. Rohan did not subscribe, instead selling his rights entitlement for ₹24,000. After the shares became ex-rights, the market value of his existing 200 shares fell to ₹1,20,000. State the appropriate accounting treatment for the sale of rights and the fall in market value, per AS 13.

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 13, when investments are acquired cum-right and the market value immediately after becoming ex-rights is lower than cost, it may be appropriate to apply the rights sale proceeds to reduce the carrying amount of the investment to its (ex-rights) market value.

Here, cost ₹1,40,000; ex-rights market value ₹1,20,000 — a decline of ₹20,000. Of the ₹24,000 rights sale proceeds, ₹20,000 is applied to reduce the carrying amount from ₹1,40,000 to ₹1,20,000 (its ex-rights market value); the balance ₹4,000 (24,000−20,000) is credited to the Profit and Loss Account as income.

The investment in the 200 equity shares is shown at a carrying amount of ₹1,20,000 in Mr. Rohan's books.

Author’s Note: Some examinees credited the entire ₹24,000 rights sale proceeds to P&L as income, not applying AS 13's rule that the portion covering the decline to ex-rights market value should instead reduce the investment's carrying amount.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.

RTP September 2025 · Q14 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · M2_C5_U3 : AS 13 — Accounting for Investments / ICAI Study Mat Topic No : 3.7
Question:

On 1st April 2024, Kesar held 75,000 equity shares of Beta Ltd. at book value ₹15/share (nominal ₹10 each). Transactions: 20th June 2024, purchased 15,000 additional shares at ₹16/share. 1st August 2024, Beta Ltd. declared a 1:6 bonus issue. 31st October 2024, rights issue of 3-for-7 held at ₹15/share; Kesar sold one-third of his rights entitlement to Megha at ₹2/share and subscribed to the remainder on 5th November 2024. Prepare the Investment Account in Kesar's books for the year ending 31st March 2025.

Answer / Solution:

W.N.1 Bonus shares: (75,000+15,000)/6 = 15,000 bonus shares (holding at bonus date: 90,000).

W.N.2 Rights entitlement: (75,000+15,000+15,000)/7 × 3 = 45,000 rights shares entitlement (on a holding of 1,05,000 post-bonus).

W.N.3 Sale of rights: One-third of 45,000 = 15,000 shares' worth of entitlement sold × ₹2/share = ₹30,000, credited to Statement of Profit and Loss (proceeds from renouncing rights, not adding to the investment's cost).

W.N.4 Rights subscribed: Remaining two-thirds of 45,000 = 30,000 shares subscribed × ₹15/share = ₹4,50,000.

Investment Account (Equity shares in Beta Ltd.): Dr: To Balance b/d 75,000 shares ₹11,25,000; To Bank A/c (20.6.24 purchase) 15,000 shares ₹2,40,000; To Bonus issue (W.N.1) 15,000 shares — Nil cost; To Bank A/c (rights subscribed, W.N.4) 30,000 shares ₹4,50,000. Total: 1,35,000 shares, ₹18,15,000. Cr: By Balance c/d (balancing figure) 1,35,000 shares ₹18,15,000.

Author’s Note: Some examinees credited the full rights-sale proceeds to reduce the cost of the remaining investment instead of crediting them to profit and loss, or miscounted the bonus/rights entitlement base by using the wrong holding figure at each successive corporate action.Written by the author, not ICAI — a likely pitfall, not an official finding. May not apply in every case.
