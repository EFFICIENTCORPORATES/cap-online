MTP January 2026 Set 2 · Q2(a) · Marks: 7 · Approx Time: 13 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.7: Investment account with cum-interest/ex-interest purchases and sales, FIFO cost basis
Question:

Prem Ltd. holds 2,000, 15% Debentures of ₹100 each in Mehar Ltd. as on 1st April, 2024 at a cost of ₹2,50,000. Interest payable 30th June and 31st December each year. Transactions during 2024-25: 1st May 2024, 1,000 debentures purchased cum-interest at ₹1,05,000; 1st November 2024, 1,200 debentures sold ex-interest at ₹1,28,200; 30th November 2024, 500 debentures purchased ex-interest at ₹54,500; 31st December 2024, 900 debentures sold cum-interest for ₹1,18,000. Prepare the Investment Account showing holdings on 31st March, 2025 at cost, using FIFO.

Answer / Solution:

15% Debentures (Investment) Account, FIFO basis — closing balance as on 31.3.25: 1,400 debentures, face value ₹1,40,000, cost ₹1,44,500 (900 from the opening 2,000 + 500 purchased 30.11.24: opening lot after the 1.11.24 sale of 1,200 leaves 800 of the original 2,000 plus the 1,000 bought 1.5.24 minus what's sold, reconciled via FIFO issue order; net closing units 1,400 as shown in the working note below).

W.N.1 — Loss on sale 1.11.24: Cost of 1,200 sold (FIFO, from opening lot) = 2,50,000/2,000 × 1,200 = ₹1,50,000. Sale proceeds ₹1,28,200. Loss = ₹21,800, charged to P&L.

W.N.2 — Profit on sale 31.12.24: Cost of 900 sold (FIFO: 800 remaining from opening lot + 100 from the 1.5.24 purchase) = (2,50,000/2,000 × 800) + (1,00,000/1,000 × 100) = 1,00,000 + 10,000 = ₹1,10,000. Sale proceeds (cum-interest, less accrued interest component) net to ₹1,11,250 principal. Profit = ₹1,250.

W.N.3 — Closing balance: 900 debentures remaining from the 1.5.24 purchase (1,000−100 sold) at ₹90,000 (proportionate cost) + 500 debentures from the 30.11.24 purchase at ₹54,500 = 1,400 debentures, ₹1,44,500.

Author’s Note: Some examinees applied weighted-average cost instead of the FIFO method the question specifically requires, or failed to separate the interest component from cum-interest purchase/sale prices before computing the capital gain/loss on disposal.

MTP May 2024 Set 1 · Q1(a) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.4/3.6: Current investment (held under one year, at lower of cost and fair value) vs long-term investment (at cost, absent other-than-temporary decline) Compulsory
Question:

Innovative Garments Manufacturing Company Limited invested in the shares of another company on 1st October, 2022 at a cost of ₹2,50,000. It also earlier purchased Gold of ₹4,00,000 and Silver of ₹2,00,000 on 1st March, 2020. Market value as on 31st March, 2023 of above investments are as follows:

Particulars₹
Shares2,25,000
Gold6,00,000
Silver3,50,000

How above investments will be shown in the books of accounts of Innovative Garments Manufacturing Company Limited for the year ending 31st March, 2023 as per the provisions of Accounting Standard 13 "Accounting for Investments"?

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 13 (Revised) "Accounting for Investments", for investment in shares, if the investment is purchased with an intention to hold for short-term period (less than one year), then it will be classified as current investment and to be carried at lower of cost and fair value, i.e. in case of shares, at lower of cost (₹2,50,000) and market value (₹2,25,000) as on 31st March 2023, i.e. ₹2,25,000.

If equity shares are acquired with an intention to hold for long term period (more than one year), then it should be considered as long-term investment to be shown at cost in the Balance Sheet of the company. However, provision for diminution should be made to recognise a decline, if other than temporary, in the value of the investments.

Gold and silver are generally purchased with an intention to hold it for long term period (more than one year) until and unless given otherwise. Hence, the investment in Gold and Silver (purchased on 1st March, 2020) should continue to be shown at cost (since there is no "other than temporary" diminution) as on 31st March, 2023, i.e. ₹4,00,000 and ₹2,00,000 respectively, though their market values have increased.

Author’s Note: A common error is applying the same "lower of cost and market value" treatment to the gold and silver holdings as to the shares, without first checking the intended holding period — long-term investments (like the gold/silver, held since 2020) stay at cost even when market value rises, and are written down only for an other-than-temporary decline, never simply marked to the lower of cost and current market value the way current investments are.

MTP May 2024 Set 2 · Q1(c) · Marks: 4 · Approx Time: 8 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.9: Reclassification transfers - LT to current at lower of cost/carrying amount; current to LT at lower of cost/fair value Compulsory
Question:

On 15th June, 2024, Y limited wants to re-classify its investments in accordance with AS 13 (revised). Decide and state the amount of transfer, based on the following information: (1) A portion of long term investments purchased on 1st March, 2023 are to be re-classified as current investments. The original cost of these investments was ₹14 lakhs but had been written down by ₹2 lakhs (to recognise 'other than temporary' decline in value). The market value of these investments on 15th June, 2024 was ₹11 lakhs. (2) A portion of current investments purchased on 15th March, 2024 for ₹7 lakhs are to be re-classified as long term investments, as the company has decided to retain them. The market value of these investments on 31st March, 2024 was ₹6 lakhs and fair value on 15th June 2018 was ₹8.5 lakhs.

Answer / Solution:

As per AS 13 (Revised) 'Accounting for Investments', where long-term investments are reclassified as current investments, transfers are made at the lower of cost and carrying amount at the date of transfer; and where investments are reclassified from current to long term, transfers are made at lower of cost and fair value on the date of transfer. Accordingly, the re-classification will be done on the following basis:

(i) In this case, carrying amount of investment on the date of transfer is less than the cost; hence this re-classified current investment should be carried at ₹12 lakhs in the books (cost ₹14 lakhs less the ₹2 lakh write-down = ₹12 lakhs carrying amount, which is lower than the ₹14 lakh original cost).

(ii) In this case, reclassification of current investment into long-term investments will be made at ₹7 lakhs as cost is less than its fair value of ₹8.5 lakhs on the date of transfer.

Author’s Note: A common error here is applying the same transfer rule in both directions — e.g. using "lower of cost and fair value" for the long-term-to-current reclassification too, instead of the correct "lower of cost and carrying amount" test that applies specifically to that direction.

MTP November 2023 Set 1 · Q1(a) · Marks: 5 · Approx Time: 9 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.9: Reclassification of investments - long-term to current at lower of cost and carrying amount; current to long-term at lower of cost and fair value Compulsory
Question:

Kunal Securities Ltd. wants to reclassify its investments in accordance with AS-13 (Revised). State the values, at which the investments have to be reclassified in the following cases:

  1. Long term investment in Company A, costing ₹10.5 lakhs is to be re-classified as current investment. The company had reduced the value of these investments to ₹9 lakhs to recognize a permanent decline in value. The fair value on the date of reclassification is ₹9.3 lakhs.
  2. Long term investment in Company B, costing ₹14 lakhs is to be re-classified as current investment. The fair value on the date of reclassification is ₹16 lakhs and book value is ₹14 lakhs.
  3. Current investment in Company C, costing ₹12 lakhs is to be re-classified as long term investment as the company wants to retain them. The market value on the date of reclassification is ₹13.5 lakhs.
  4. Current investment in Company D, costing ₹18 lakhs is to be re-classified as long term investment. The market value on the date of reclassification is ₹16.5 lakhs.
Answer / Solution:

As per AS 13 (Revised) ‘Accounting for Investments’, where long-term investments are reclassified as current investments, transfers are made at the lower of cost and carrying amount at the date of transfer. And where investments are reclassified from current to long term, transfers are made at lower of cost and fair value on the date of transfer.

Accordingly, the re-classification will be done on the following basis:

  1. In this case, carrying amount of investment on the date of transfer is less than the cost; hence this re-classified current investment should be carried at ₹9 lakhs in the books.
  2. The carrying/book value of the long-term investment is same as cost i.e., ₹14 lakhs. Hence this long-term investment will be reclassified as current investment at book value of ₹14 lakhs only.
  3. In this case, reclassification of current investment into long-term investments will be made at ₹12 lakhs as cost is less than its market value of ₹13.5 lakhs.
  4. Market value of the investment is ₹16.5 lakhs, which is lower than its cost i.e., ₹18 lakhs. Therefore, the transfer to long term investments should be done in the books at the market value i.e., ₹16.5 lakhs.
Author’s Note: A common error is applying a single reclassification rule across all four cases rather than switching direction with the transfer – long-term-to-current uses the lower of cost and carrying amount (not fair value), while current-to-long-term uses the lower of cost and fair value (not carrying amount); mixing these up produces the wrong transfer value in at least two of the four cases.

RTP January 2026 · Q11 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.6: Rights sale proceeds applied to reduce carrying amount to ex-rights market value, balance credited to P&L
Question:

Mr. Rohan acquired 200 equity shares of Zeta Technologies Ltd. on a cum-right basis for ₹1,40,000 as a long-term investment. The company announced a 1:1 rights issue at ₹214/share. Mr. Rohan did not subscribe, instead selling his rights entitlement for ₹24,000. After the shares became ex-rights, the market value of his existing 200 shares fell to ₹1,20,000. State the appropriate accounting treatment for the sale of rights and the fall in market value, per AS 13.

Answer / Solution:

Per AS 13, when investments are acquired cum-right and the market value immediately after becoming ex-rights is lower than cost, it may be appropriate to apply the rights sale proceeds to reduce the carrying amount of the investment to its (ex-rights) market value.

Here, cost ₹1,40,000; ex-rights market value ₹1,20,000 — a decline of ₹20,000. Of the ₹24,000 rights sale proceeds, ₹20,000 is applied to reduce the carrying amount from ₹1,40,000 to ₹1,20,000 (its ex-rights market value); the balance ₹4,000 (24,000−20,000) is credited to the Profit and Loss Account as income.

The investment in the 200 equity shares is shown at a carrying amount of ₹1,20,000 in Mr. Rohan's books.

Author’s Note: Some examinees credited the entire ₹24,000 rights sale proceeds to P&L as income, not applying AS 13's rule that the portion covering the decline to ex-rights market value should instead reduce the investment's carrying amount.

RTP September 2024 · Q11 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 3.6: Long-term investments carried at cost unless there is a decline other than temporary; a strategic-benefit premium forms part of cost and is not separately accounted for or amortised
Question:

A company is engaged in the business of refining, transportation and marketing of petroleum products. During the financial year ended 31st March, 2024, the company acquired controlling interest from Government of India in another public sector undertaking @ ₹1,551 per share as against the book value of ₹192.58 per share and market value of ₹876 per share as on 18th February, 2024.

Thus, the strategic premium of ₹675 per share has been paid considering various tangible and intangible factors.

The above investment in the shares of the acquired company has been considered as long term strategic investment and, therefore, has been accounted for at cost, i.e. at ₹1,551 per share in the financial statements. No provision for diminution in value has been made in the books of account.

As per the requirement of Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013, the aggregate market value of the quoted shares has been properly reflected in the financial statements.

On 28th March, 2024, the acquired shares were quoted at ₹880 per share on BSE and the current market price as on 18th July was around ₹300.

Considering the tangible and intangible benefits the Management is of the view that there is no permanent diminution in the value of the strategic investment in the acquired company, as the same has been considered as a long-term investment. Therefore, there is no need for provision for diminution in the value of the shares of the acquired company.

Required:

  1. Whether the accounting treatment 'at cost' under the head ‘Long Term Investments’ without providing for any diminution in value is correct and in accordance with the provisions of AS 13.
  2. If any provision for diminution in the value is to be made, whether such provision should be charged to the profit and loss account or whether same can be considered as deferred expenditure and amortised over a period of 5 years. Whether it is open for the company to charge off such diminution in the value in the books of account instead of creating provision.
  3. Whether the premium paid for strategic benefits for investment described in facts of the case, can be accounted for separately in the books of account keeping in view that AS 13 specifies that long term investments should be recorded at cost and there is no specific provision in the standard in respect of accounting for premium paid for strategic benefits.
Answer / Solution:
  1. The accounting treatment 'at cost' under the head ‘Long Term Investment’ in the separate financial statements of the company without providing for any diminution in value is correct and is in accordance with the provisions of AS 13 provided that there is no decline, other than temporary, in the value of investment.
  2. The provision for diminution in the value of investment should be a charge to the profit and loss statement. As per the requirements of AS 13, the diminution in the value of investment can neither be accounted for as deferred revenue expenditure nor it can be written off in the statement of profit and loss.
  3. The long-term investments should be carried at cost as per the requirements of AS 13. The amount paid over and above the market price should be treated as cost and cannot be accounted for separately.
Author’s Note: A common error is proposing to amortise the diminution in value as deferred revenue expenditure over several years, rather than recognising that AS 13 requires any genuine, other-than-temporary decline to be charged immediately to profit and loss, with no deferred-expenditure treatment available.
