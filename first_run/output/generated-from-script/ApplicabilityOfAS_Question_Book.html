RTP May 2024 · Q5 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1: Status of Accounting Standards
Question:

Based upon criteria for rating of non-corporate entity, categorize the following as Level I, Level II, Level III and Level IV entities for the purpose of compliance of Accounting Standards in India.

  1. Rama Textiles whose turnover (excluding other income) exceeds ten crore but does not exceed rupees fifty crore in the immediately preceding accounting year.
  2. Star Industries is having borrowings (including public deposits) in excess of rupees two crore but not in excess of rupees ten crore at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year.
  3. Newman Industries is having borrowings (including public deposits) less than rupees fifty lakh at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year.
  4. SS Finance is a financial institution carrying its business in India since last 10 years.
  5. DD Finance, holding company of SS Finance. (Entity mentioned at Point (v) above)
  6. Reliable Co-op Bank, a co-operative bank, carrying banking operations since last 15 years.
Answer / Solution:
  1. Level III Entity – Rama textiles, whose turnover (excluding other income) exceeds rupees ten crore but does not exceed rupees fifty crore in the immediately preceding accounting year.
  2. Level III Entity – Star industries is having borrowings (including public deposits) in excess of rupees two crore but not in excess of rupees ten crore at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year.
  3. Level IV Entity – Newman Industries is having borrowings (including public deposits) of less than rupees fifty lakhs at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year.
  4. Level I Entity – SS is a financial institution carrying its business in India since last 10 years.
  5. Level I Entity – DD finance, holding company of SS finance (Entity mentioned in point (d) above).
  6. Level I Entity – Reliable co-operative banks carrying on banking business for the last 15 years.
Author’s Note: A common error here is missing that a holding company of a Level I entity is itself automatically Level I regardless of its own size, and that banks/financial institutions/co-operative banks are Level I entities by their nature, not by a turnover or borrowings threshold.

RTP May 2026 · Q7 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 2.1: Revised MSME classification criteria - turnover/borrowings thresholds
Question:

Virat Traders, an unlisted partnership firm, had a turnover of ₹249.99 crore and borrowings of ₹25.01 crore during the financial year 2023-24. It is not a bank, financial institution, or insurance company, and also not a subsidiary of any other entity. The firm wishes to avail MSME exemptions for FY 2024-25. Is it eligible?

Answer / Solution:

Company qualifies as an MSME if following conditions are satisfied:

  1. whose equity or debt securities are not listed or are not in the process of listing on any stock exchange, whether in India or outside India;
  2. which is not a bank, financial institution or an insurance company;
  3. whose turnover (excluding other income) does not exceed 250 crore rupees in the immediately preceding accounting year;
  4. which does not have borrowings in excess of 50 crore rupees at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year; and
  5. which is not a holding or subsidiary of an entity which is not a MSME entity.

Yes, Virat Traders qualifies as an MSME since it meets all five criteria.

Further, it has been assumed that Virat Traders is not newly qualifying as an MSME in FY 2024-25 because a non-company entity that newly qualifies as an MSME can avail exemptions only after it remains as MSME for two consecutive years.

Until then, full AS compliance is required.

Author’s Note: Some examinees applied the five MSME tests mechanically and concluded eligibility without addressing the two-consecutive-year assumption for newly-qualifying entities, missing part of what the question was testing.

RTP September 2025 · Q4 · Marks: Not stated in source · Approx Time: 10–20 minutes (approx.) · Topic 1: Status of Accounting Standards
Question:

As per the revised scheme effective from accounting periods commencing on or after 1st April, 2024, classify non-company entities for the purpose of applicability of Accounting Standards. Briefly explain the criteria for each category.

Answer / Solution:

Non-company entities are classified into two categories: Micro, Small and Medium Sized Entities (MSMEs) and Large entities.

A non-company entity qualifies as an MSME if it satisfies all of: (i) its equity/debt securities are not listed or in the process of listing on any stock exchange (in India or abroad); (ii) it is not a bank, financial institution, or insurance company; (iii) turnover (excluding other income) does not exceed ₹250 crore in the immediately preceding accounting year; (iv) it does not have borrowings exceeding ₹50 crore at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year; and (v) it is not a holding or subsidiary of an entity that is not itself an MSME. These conditions are assessed as at the end of the relevant accounting period. A Large entity is any non-company entity that does not meet the MSME definition, and must comply in full with all Accounting Standards; MSMEs receive certain exemptions/relaxations.

Author’s Note: A direct recall question from the announcement text; common gaps include omitting one of the five MSME conditions, especially the holding/subsidiary condition.
