# EXAMINERS' COMMENTS ON THE PERFORMANCE OF THE EXAMINEES

##### PAPER – 1: ADVANCED ACCOUNTING

### Specific Comments

Question 1.(a) Most of the examinees failed to calculate the weighted average number of shares required for determining Basic EPS. As a result, they were unable to compute EPS accurately in accordance with AS 20, "Earnings per Share."

(b) Many examinees calculated the unearned finance income correctly, some failed to include the unguaranteed residual value in the total lease payments to determine the gross investment. Consequently, they could not ascertain the unearned finance income as per AS 19, "Leases."

(c) Most of the examinees were unable to calculate the correct amount of Defined Benefit Asset / Liability to be recognized for the year.

Question 2. A Large number of examinees were not able to prepare the Balance sheet and Notes to accounts in compliance with Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013, using the given information. Additionally, many failed to pass journal entries correctly for the settlement of preference shareholders and debenture holders' claims. As a result, they could not determine the correct balance for Capital Reduction/Internal Reconstruction to be transferred to Capital Reserve or the number of debentures issued to meet outstanding liabilities. The calculation of 12% Debentures required to settle liabilities and maintain the cash balance was also incorrect in most cases.

Question 3.(a) Several examinees calculated the provision amount incorrectly by determining the period from the date of the invoice instead of the balance period. This led to errors in computing the provision amounts for both years as per AS 29, "Provisions, Contingent Liabilities, and Contingent Assets."

(b) Some examinees successfully prepared the Cash Flow Statement. However, a few mistakenly added the net interest amount (after payments) instead of the provisioned interest while calculating cash flow from operating activities. This miscalculation also affected cash flow from financing activities.

Question 4. Many examinees were unable to prepare the Balance Sheet and Notes to Accounts as per Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. While most correctly prepared the Realization Account and Shareholders' Account of

Light Ltd., they made errors in Bright Ltd.'s Realization Account by failing to account for assets sold before the acquisition. This resulted in incorrect profit calculations in the Realization Account, which further impacted the Shareholders' Account entries. Most examinees failed to compute the net assets of Bright Ltd., leading to errors in the calculation of Goodwill/Capital Reserve.

Question 5. A few examinees did not prepare the Notes to Accounts separately as mandated by Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. Additionally, some misclassified post-acquisition profits as pre-acquisition profits, leading to incorrect calculations of the cost of control.

Question 6.(a) Some examinees failed to correctly identify the related parties for the three reporting entities (b) Only a small number of examinees failed to pass the correct journal entries for the buyback of shares. (c) Most examinees correctly recorded the journal entries. However, some mistakenly credited the respective expense account instead of the bank account. A few included the expense name along with the Head Office account, and some omitted narrations in their journal entries.

##### PAPER-3: TAXATION

##### SECTION A: INCOME TAX LAW

### Specific Comments

Question 1 Many examinees failed to compute the interest deduction on housing loan for reconstruction of self occupied property correctly. They were also not aware that the depreciation is not allowable on the capital asset for which payment in excess of ₹ 10,000 has been made in cash. Additionally, most of the examinees did not deduct the actual expenditure incurred for power pack up from the power back up charges computed under the head "Income from other sources". Consequently, total income and tax liability went wrong.

Question 2.(b) The question requires examining the applicability of TDS provisions on the different nature of payments made by Mr. Ganesh to CA. Rajesh. One of the payments made by Mr. Ganesh to CA Rajesh is for use of Computer Software and another is for Fees for teaching accountancy to his Son. Many examinees failed to appreciate these transactions and could not answer them. No tax is deductible at source by Individuals and HUFs on royalty payment or payment for personal purposes. Moreover, teaching does not fall within the scope of professional services.

Question 3.(a) Part (A) requires to define the meaning of Micro and Small Enterprise. Most of the examinees were not aware of the threshold limits to classify an enterprise as micro and small. In Part (B), few examinees could not compute the depreciation amount correctly due to lack of knowledge of applicable depreciation rate.

(b) Most of the examinees were not aware of the provisions that dining table and chairs are personal effects, hence, excluded from the definition of capital asset and consequently, there will not be capital gain on sale of those assets. They also wrongly treated income from dairy farming on rural agricultural land as agricultural income.

Question 4.(a) Many examinees incorrectly set off the share of loss from the firm against other business income.

# SECTION B: GOODS AND SERVICES TAX

### Specific Comments

Question 5.(a) i. Majority of the examinees failed to state the reason for the taxability of laptop supplied to Managing director's friend at transaction value. They did not mention that since the supply was made to an unrelated person and the price was the sole consideration, the value of supply should be based on the transaction value.

ii. Many examinees did not consider the supply of printed letter cards as supply of goods being a composite supply. As a result, they failed to apply the correct GST rate applicable to goods.

ii. Some examinees wrongly considered the transportation of passenger services by an omnibus provided through Electronic Commerce Operator (ECO) as an exempt supply. However, tax is payable by Galaxy Cube Private Ltd. and not by the ECO, since the service provider is a company.

iv. Most of the examinees did not provide valid reason for non-availability of ITC in case of goods purchased from composition dealer.

v. In large number of cases, examinees were unaware that tax on renting of residential dwelling to a registered person is payable by such registered person under reverse charge, in cash since ITC can only be utilised for paying the output tax.

vi. Also, place of supply was not correctly determined in many cases.

vii. Some examinees did not ascertain the time of supply in case of residential house taken on rent by MD of the company.

Due to these errors, incorrect calculations of the minimum net GST payable in cash were made.

(b) The examinees failed to explain that the combo supply of cinema tickets and snacks was a composite supply and it attracted GST at the rate applicable to principal supply i.e. service of exhibition of cinema. Rather examinees gave definition of Composite and Mixed supply ending up with conclusion that it was Mixed supply.

Question 6.(a) Most of the examinees were unable to mention that remuneration paid to director for professional services subjected to TDS under

section 194J of the Income-tax Act is outside the scope of Schedule III of the CGST Act, 2017. Consequently, tax is payable under reverse charge by the recipient company in this case. Also, rent paid to Indian Railways was wrongly considered under reverse charge by some examinees although same was taxable under forward charge. Resultantly, total GST payable under reverse charge was wrongly calculated.

(b) (i) Some examinees failed to understand that the place of supply of service in relation to annual event provided to the registered person shall be the location of the recipient. Instead, they wrongly ascertain the place of supply in this case as the place where the event was actually held.

(ii) Many examinees were unable to give accurate explanation related to “Bill to Ship to” Model. They did not correctly state that when goods are delivered by supplier to recipient at the direction of a third person, the place of supply is the principal place of business of such third person. Furthermore, they failed to determine the place of supply for the second activity involving the movement of goods.

Question 7.(a) Some examinees mentioned 30 days instead of 1 month while mentioning timeline within which revised tax invoice need to be issued.

(b) Most of the examinees incorrectly explained the provisions related to rectification of errors or omissions in returns filed under Section 39 of the CGST Act, 2017. They confused the actual filing date with the due date for filing the annual return while stating the time limit for making such rectifications.

Question 8.(a) The majority of examinees did not attempt this question. Whosoever attempted exhibited lack of adequate knowledge of the relevant circular and hence were unable to elaborate the clarification in relation to time of supply of services of construction of road and maintenance thereof of National Highway Projects of National Highway Authority of India (NHAI) in Hybrid Annuity Mode (HAM) model. Since this service falls under continuous supply, the answer should have been framed based on Section 31(5) of the CGST Act, 2017, along with the relevant clarification.

(b)(ii) Majority of the examinees mistakenly listed six States instead of the six State taxes that were subsumed under the GST regime.
