# CA Intermediate Advanced Accounting

## Examiner-Comments-Based Accounting Standards Attempt Guide

### Scope of this guide

This guide consolidates the specific examiner comments on Paper 1, Advanced Accounting, from the following CA Intermediate examinations:

- May 2024
- September 2024
- January 2025
- May 2025
- September 2025
- January 2026

It covers AS 2, AS 4, AS 5, AS 7, AS 9, AS 10, AS 12, AS 13, AS 15, AS 16, AS 19, AS 26, AS 28 and AS 29.

The first section contains deduplicated general lessons applicable across Accounting Standard questions. The second section contains only concept-specific pointers arising from the particular questions discussed by the examiners. A point is not repeated in both sections unless the AS-specific application would otherwise be unclear.

# Part I — General Points for Attempting Accounting Standard Questions

## 1. Identify the governing Accounting Standard from the transaction

Do not select an Accounting Standard merely because the question uses a familiar word such as *capitalisation*, *provision*, *return* or *value*. First identify:

1. What asset, liability, income or expense is involved?
2. What event or transaction has occurred?
3. Which recognition or measurement issue is being tested?

Applying the provisions of a related but incorrect Accounting Standard can make the entire answer incorrect even when the calculations are neat.

## 2. State the applicable rule before applying it

Begin each independent requirement with a short statement of the relevant recognition, measurement or presentation rule. Then apply that rule to the facts.

A useful answer sequence is:

1. **Applicable provision**
2. **Application to the given facts**
3. **Working/calculation**
4. **Accounting treatment or conclusion**

This prevents an answer from becoming a collection of unexplained figures.

## 3. Read dates, periods and cut-off points carefully

Before calculating interest, amortisation, provision, revenue or profit, mark:

- commencement date;
- capitalization or recognition date;
- balance-sheet date;
- number of months involved;
- whether the figure is for the current year or cumulative to date; and
- whether compounding is annual, half-yearly or based on another period.

Write the applicable period beside the rate before multiplying.

## 4. Separate the components of the calculation

Do not jump directly to one net figure. Prepare separate workings for each component—for example, cost elements, specific and general borrowings, obligation and plan assets, contract revenue and contract cost, or carrying amount and recoverable amount.

This makes the answer easier to verify and protects consequential marks if one component is wrong.

## 5. Use the correct measurement basis

Underline the measurement basis required by the Accounting Standard, such as:

- cost;
- net realisable value;
- replacement cost;
- present value/value in use;
- carrying amount;
- fair value; or
- expected value/best estimate.

Do not interchange these merely because the question provides all of them.

## 6. Distinguish opening, current-year, closing and cumulative figures

Where a question covers more than one period, use a reconciliation or year-wise table. Do not treat a cumulative figure as the amount belonging entirely to the current year.

## 7. Use every relevant fact given in the question

Before finalising the answer, scan the question again and tick off facts such as:

- residual value;
- temporary investment income;
- revised estimates;
- proportionate allocation instructions;
- special dates;
- tax rate;
- compounding frequency; and
- whether the transaction relates to an earlier period.

Examiner comments repeatedly show that omission of one fact changes all later calculations.

## 8. Show structured working notes

Give each material working a title and show the formula or logic used. Avoid placing unexplained additions and deductions in the main answer.

Recommended labels include:

- Working Note 1 — Relevant period
- Working Note 2 — Amount eligible for recognition
- Working Note 3 — Measurement of asset/liability
- Working Note 4 — Amount charged to Statement of Profit and Loss

## 9. Complete the required accounting treatment

A correct numerical answer is incomplete when the question also requires:

- a journal entry;
- treatment in the Statement of Profit and Loss;
- treatment in the Balance Sheet;
- revised carrying amount; or
- a disclosure/conclusion.

After completing the calculation, return to the requirement and finish every verb used in it: *calculate, pass, present, disclose, comment* or *advise*.

## 10. Label figures precisely

Do not label tax expense as tax payable, contract revenue as contract profit, gross investment as lease payments, or an asset as a liability. Correct figures under incorrect labels can still demonstrate incorrect conceptual understanding.

## 11. Answer independent situations separately

For case-based questions containing several situations, use a separate mini-answer for each case. Do not give one broad conclusion covering all situations.

## 12. End with an explicit conclusion

End each part with a sentence such as:

> Accordingly, ₹___ shall be recognised/capitalised/charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss/carried as an asset or liability as at ___.

The conclusion should state the amount, treatment and relevant date wherever applicable.

# Part II — AS-Specific Concept Pointers

## AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Joint products, by-products and scrap
- Allocation of conversion/production cost
- Valuation of finished goods at cost or NRV
- Valuation of raw materials when finished goods may be sold below cost

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Create the joint-cost pool only after giving the prescribed treatment to by-product NRV and scrap value.
2. Do not deduct an assumed profit from by-product NRV unless the facts and applicable method specifically require it.
3. Allocate joint/common production cost using the basis given in the question; where an item is used only partly, use proportionate allocation.
4. Build the cost of production component by component. Do not stop after calculating the most visible component, such as labour cost.
5. For finished goods, compare cost with NRV and take the lower amount.
6. For raw materials, do not automatically take replacement cost. First determine whether the finished goods in which they will be incorporated are expected to be sold at or above cost.
7. If finished goods are expected to be sold below cost, replacement cost may be the best available measure of raw-material NRV, subject to the AS 2 rule.
8. Show separate valuation workings for finished goods and raw materials because the measurement logic may differ.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Incorrect deduction of by-product NRV and scrap value.
- Unnecessary deduction of profit while determining by-product NRV.
- Incorrect proportionate allocation of cost elements.
- Incorrect cost of production.
- Incorrect NRV-based valuation of finished goods.
- Incorrect replacement-cost-based valuation of raw materials.

## AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies

### Concept covered in the examiner comments

- Identification and accounting treatment of a prior-period item

### Concept-specific pointers

1. First determine whether the error or omission arose in preparing the financial statements of one or more prior periods.
2. Check whether the relevant information was available when those prior-period financial statements were prepared.
3. Do not classify every expense relating to an earlier year as a prior-period item. The reason the item was not recognised earlier is crucial.
4. Distinguish a prior-period error from a change in accounting estimate and a current-period adjustment.
5. State the classification before calculating or recording the amount.
6. Pass the journal entry using the account affected by the correction; identification without accounting treatment is incomplete.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Failure to decide whether the item constituted a prior-period item.
- Failure to pass the correct journal entry after deciding the treatment.

## AS 7 — Construction Contracts

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Stage of completion over multiple years
- Revision of estimated contract expenditure
- Contract revenue, contract cost and profit recognised each year

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Use the latest revised estimate of total contract cost available at each reporting date.
2. Calculate the **cumulative stage of completion** at each year-end; do not confuse it with the percentage of work performed only during that year.
3. Apply the stage of completion to contract revenue to determine cumulative revenue recognised.
4. Determine cumulative cost/profit under the applicable AS 7 method.
5. Deduct amounts recognised up to the previous year to obtain the current-year revenue, cost and profit.
6. Keep contract revenue and contract profit in separate rows. Revenue is not profit.
7. Use a year-wise table containing revised estimated cost, stage of completion, cumulative amount and current-year amount.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Ignoring revised estimated expenditure while calculating Year 1 completion.
- Calculating yearly percentage completion instead of cumulative stage of completion.
- Treating contract revenue as contract profit.
- Using cumulative figures as current-year figures.
- Not separately calculating current-year contract revenue, cost and profit.

## AS 9 — Revenue Recognition

### Concept covered in the examiner comments

- Timing and basis of revenue recognition in multiple independent situations

### Concept-specific pointers

For each independent situation, identify:

1. the source/type of revenue;
2. the event that ordinarily triggers recognition;
3. whether performance is complete;
4. whether collection or measurement is subject to significant uncertainty;
5. whether recognition should occur, be postponed or be adjusted; and
6. the amount and accounting period in which revenue is recognised.

Do not give the same recognition rule for sale of goods, services, interest, royalty and dividend income. Each category has its own recognition trigger.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Incorrect treatment of independent situations.
- Lack of clarity about when revenue should be recognised.
- Lack of clarity about the basis on which revenue should be measured or recognised.

## AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Elements included in the cost of PPE
- Relocation of production facilities
- Computation of land cost
- Boundary between AS 10 and AS 16

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Start with purchase price and add only directly attributable costs necessary to bring the asset to the location and condition required for its intended operation.
2. Test each expenditure independently against the recognition criterion; do not capitalise an expense merely because it is connected with a larger capital project.
3. Relocation or reorganisation costs are not part of PPE cost merely because production facilities are being moved.
4. Separate expenditure relating directly to acquisition/preparation of land from general administrative, relocation or operating expenditure.
5. Apply AS 16 only to eligible borrowing costs. It does not determine whether a non-interest relocation expense forms part of PPE cost.
6. Present the land-cost working item by item, showing included and excluded expenditure with reasons.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Applying AS 16 instead of AS 10 to relocation expenditure.
- Incorrectly capitalising expenses connected with relocation of production facilities.
- Incorrect calculation of the cost of land.

## AS 13 — Accounting for Investments

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Valuation of investments at the reporting date
- Profit on sale of investments
- Sale of rights entitlement
- Presentation and explanation of the accounting treatment

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Classify the investment as current or long-term before applying the valuation rule.
2. Maintain a quantity-and-cost reconciliation when there are purchases, sales, bonus shares, rights shares or rights entitlements.
3. Calculate the carrying amount of the units sold using the method applicable to the question before calculating profit on sale.
4. Do not treat cash received from sale of rights as a reduction of the Investment Account when AS 13 requires recognition in the Statement of Profit and Loss.
5. After recording a sale, reconcile the remaining quantity and carrying amount before valuing the closing investment.
6. State the AS 13 rule supporting the year-end valuation and treatment of sale proceeds.
7. Use a structured investment account or table rather than scattered calculations.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Incorrect investment value at year-end.
- Incorrect profit on sale of shares.
- Failure to support the treatment with AS 13 provisions.
- Crediting proceeds from sale of rights to the Investment Account instead of the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Calculation errors and unstructured presentation.

## AS 15 — Employee Benefits

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Expected return on plan assets
- Actual return on plan assets
- Actuarial gains and losses
- Defined-benefit asset/liability
- Half-yearly compounding

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Prepare separate reconciliations for the defined-benefit obligation and plan assets.
2. Calculate expected return using the opening/fair value of plan assets and the applicable timing of contributions and benefits paid.
3. Calculate actual return independently from the movement in plan assets and the cash flows affecting them.
4. Do not treat expected return and actual return as interchangeable figures.
5. Determine the difference between actual and expected return before identifying its effect on actuarial gain/loss.
6. Apply half-yearly compounding when expressly required; do not substitute simple interest or annual compounding.
7. Arrive at the closing net defined-benefit asset or liability only after completing both reconciliations.
8. Clearly label whether the final balance is an asset or a liability.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Calculating actual return but not expected return.
- Incorrect expected return on plan assets.
- Incorrect defined-benefit asset/liability.
- Failure to apply half-yearly compounding.
- Confusion between actuarial gains/losses and actual return on plan assets.
- Errors in employee-benefit liability computation.

## AS 16 — Borrowing Costs

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Qualifying and non-qualifying assets
- Specific borrowings
- General borrowings and weighted-average capitalization rate
- Capitalization period
- Temporary investment income
- Amount charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Identify each qualifying asset separately. Do not assume that every asset under construction or every expenditure qualifies.
2. Mark the commencement and cessation/suspension dates of capitalization before calculating interest.
3. For specific borrowings, calculate the actual borrowing cost for the eligible period and give the prescribed treatment to income from temporary investment of those funds.
4. Do not calculate a full year's interest when the qualifying asset was ready for use earlier.
5. For general borrowings, determine the weighted-average capitalization rate from eligible general borrowings.
6. Apply the general-borrowing rate only to qualifying expenditure not already financed through specific borrowings.
7. Separate borrowing cost capitalised from borrowing cost charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
8. Carry the corrected capitalised cost into subsequent depreciation and carrying-amount calculations.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Calculating interest for the entire year instead of up to the capitalization date.
- Incorrect specific borrowing cost.
- Incorrect weighted-average rate of interest.
- Incorrect allocation between qualifying and non-qualifying assets.
- Failure to distinguish capitalised interest from interest charged to profit or loss.
- Consequential errors in depreciation, carrying amount and journal entries.

## AS 19 — Leases

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Finance-lease versus operating-lease classification
- Gross investment in the lease
- Unguaranteed residual value
- Unearned finance income
- Annual lease payment

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Classify the lease from the substance of the arrangement and the indicators given in the question; do not rely only on ownership transfer.
2. In a lessor-oriented finance-lease calculation, include the unguaranteed residual value in gross investment.
3. Keep minimum lease payments and unguaranteed residual value as separate components before totaling gross investment.
4. Calculate the present value/net investment using the interest rate implicit in the lease where required.
5. Determine unearned finance income as the difference between gross investment and net investment.
6. Recheck whether the annual lease payment has been derived using the correct annuity factor, timing and residual-value treatment.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Misclassification between finance and operating lease.
- Incorrect annual lease payment.
- Repeated omission of unguaranteed residual value from gross investment/total lease amount.
- Incorrect unearned finance income as a consequence of that omission.

## AS 26 — Intangible Assets

### Concept covered in the examiner comments

- Amortisation and the related journal entry

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Establish the depreciable/amortisable amount, useful life and date from which the intangible asset is available for use.
2. Calculate amortisation only for the applicable period.
3. After calculating amortisation, record the corresponding debit to amortisation expense and credit to accumulated amortisation/intangible asset, according to the presentation followed.
4. Do not stop after arriving at the correct amortisation amount when journal entries are required.
5. Ensure the post-entry carrying amount agrees with cost less accumulated amortisation and impairment, if any.

### Examiner-identified mistake

- Correct amortisation calculation followed by an incorrect journal entry.

---

## AS 28 — Impairment of Assets

### Concepts covered in the examiner comments

- Value in use
- Recoverable amount
- Impairment loss of a cash-generating unit
- Allocation of impairment loss
- Revised carrying amounts

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Determine the carrying amount of the CGU and the relevant assets before measuring impairment.
2. Calculate value in use from the appropriate cash-flow and discounting information.
3. Compare value in use with net selling price/fair value less costs of disposal, as applicable, to determine recoverable amount.
4. Calculate impairment loss as the excess of carrying amount over recoverable amount.
5. Allocate the loss in the prescribed sequence—first to goodwill, where applicable, and then to other assets on the required basis.
6. Observe the floor applicable to an individual asset while allocating the loss; do not reduce an asset below the permitted amount.
7. Reconcile the total allocated loss with the CGU impairment loss.
8. Present the revised carrying amount of every affected asset.

### Examiner-identified mistakes

- Incorrect value in use.
- Incorrect impairment loss.
- Incorrect allocation of impairment loss among CGU assets.
- Incorrect revised carrying amounts.
- Inadequate understanding of the sequence of impairment calculations.

---

## AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets

### Concept covered in the examiner comments

- Time-based measurement of a provision over two reporting years

### Concept-specific pointers

1. Confirm that a present obligation from a past event exists before measuring a provision.
2. Identify the balance period relevant to each reporting date instead of mechanically calculating from the original invoice date.
3. Prepare separate calculations for each year when the remaining period changes.
4. Distinguish the provision required at year-end from the expense or movement recognised during the year.
5. Where discounting or a time-linked rate applies, use the remaining period relevant to the obligation at that reporting date.
6. Reconcile opening provision, additional provision/unwinding, utilisation or reversal, and closing provision.

### Examiner-identified mistake

- Measuring the period from the invoice date instead of the relevant balance period, resulting in incorrect provisions for both years.

- [ ] I identified the transaction and the correct Accounting Standard.

- [ ] I marked the relevant dates and period.
- [ ] I used the correct measurement basis.
- [ ] I separated material components in working notes.
- [ ] I distinguished current-year and cumulative amounts.

- [ ] I labelled figures according to their real accounting meaning.
