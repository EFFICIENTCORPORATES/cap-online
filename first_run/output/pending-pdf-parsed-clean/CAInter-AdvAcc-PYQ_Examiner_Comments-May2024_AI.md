# EXAMINERS' COMMENTS ON THE PERFORMANCE OF THE EXAMINEES

# PAPER – 1: ADVANCED ACCOUNTING

## Specific Comments

Question 1.(a) Most examinees accurately calculated the weighted average interest rate on general borrowings but made common mistakes elsewhere. Many calculated interest for the entire year instead of up to the capitalization date, leading to errors, though they usually correctly deducted interest income from temporary investments. Many of them made mistakes in calculating specific borrowing costs resulting in incorrect total borrowing costs, affecting subsequent calculations and journal entries. These errors led to incorrect depreciation and carrying values for the building.

(b) Most examinees correctly calculated the cost of conversion for allocation between joint products, Polyester and Nylon, but made errors in deducting the NRV of the by-product fiber and sale value of scrap. Additionally, some deducted profit when calculating the net realizable value of the by-product fiber, leading to incorrect answers.

Question 2. Most of the examinees provided the correct journal entries for reducing share capital and revaluing assets and liabilities. However, many failed to correctly journalize the sale and cancellation of own debentures. Additionally, instead of debiting the Capital Reduction Account, they incorrectly debited the Preference Dividend and Penalty accounts for these payments. As a result, the amount transferred to the Capital Reserve account was incorrect. Despite these errors, most examinees correctly posted entries to the Capital Reduction and Bank Accounts.

Question 3.(a) Most examinees showed a lack of knowledge of the provisions of AS 7 Construction Contracts. They were required to calculate the percentage of stage of completion for each of the four years. While many did this correctly, some made errors in Year 1 by not considering the revised estimated expenditure, leading to incorrect percentages. A few examinees calculated the percentage completion for each year instead of the stage of completion and failed to calculate the profit to be recognized each year. Some mistook contract revenue for contract profit, and others incorrectly used cumulative figures to determine annual profit without separately calculating contract revenue and contract cost for each year.

(b) Some examinees could not prepare the cash flow statement and the provision for taxation account correctly. They got confused by adding back the provision for taxation initially and then deducting the income tax paid at the end, sometimes interchanging these figures. Others skipped the preparation of the provision for taxation account and simply deducted the amount of provision for taxation. Some examinees failed to make the required adjustments for investment-related transactions or incorrectly adjusted for increases in investments. Additionally, most examinees did not follow the prescribed format for the cash flow statement, instead adding and subtracting figures randomly without proper classification.

Question 4. Few examinees were unable to prepare the balance sheet as per Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. Additionally, the notes to accounts were not prepared in the prescribed format. While many examinees correctly computed the net assets, they made errors in computing the number of debentures. As a result, the total purchase consideration was incorrect, leading to an incorrect calculation of goodwill.

Question 5. A few examinees were unable to prepare the consolidated balance sheet as per Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. Some examinees misclassified main and sub-heads. Many did not correctly account for revaluation loss and depreciation savings, leading to incorrect analysis of profit and its allocation between the holding company and minority interest. Additionally, some examinees did not compute minority interest and capital reserve (cost of control) correctly.

Question 6.(a) A few examinees made errors in classifying leases as either finance or operating leases based on the given parameters. They also miscalculated the annual lease payment and unearned finance income. Additionally, many examinees forgot to include the unguaranteed residual value in the total lease amount. In AS 20 question, some examinees could not calculate the number of shares and also made mistake in calculating net profit.

(b) Most of the examinees wrote the first condition and its testing correctly but many of them got confused between the second and third condition. Additionally, a few examinees failed to deduct the loss when determining the amount available for the declaration of dividends.

(c) Some examinees failed to correctly convert the trial balance from USD to Indian currency, with the exception of inventory Q. Consequently, the exchange difference at the end was also calculated incorrectly.

Question 6.(b)(i) Most of the examinees could not correctly answer the question relating to signing of the audit report in case of proprietorship concern or the firm of the auditors and dealing of qualifications in the audit report as per the provisions of section 145 of the Companies Act, 2013.

(ii) Most of the examinees could not correctly answer the provisions relating to the expert's consent included in the prospectus to be issued in India by the companies incorporated outside India as per section 388 of the Companies Act, 2013.

## PAPER-3: TAXATION

## SECTION A: INCOME TAX LAW

## Specific Comments

Question 1. The question requires computing the total income and tax liability of Mr. Sahil. Many examinees failed to check the applicability of the presumptive provisions under section 44AD. Additionally, they did not restrict the interest deduction under section 24(b) for self-occupied property to ₹ 2,00,000 and failed to provide the deduction under section 80EE of ₹ 50,000, leading to incorrect calculations of total income and tax liability.

Question 2.(b) The question requires to examine the applicability of TDS/ TCS in two different situations. In sub-part (i), some of the examinees got confused between section 194-I and section 194-IB. They have wrongly answered based on the provisions of section 194-IB instead of 194-I. In sub-part (ii), the applicability of TCS needs to be examined on sale of two cars, one having value less than ₹ 10 lakhs and another above ₹ 10 lakhs. Many examinees answered wrongly by applying the provisions of section 206C(1H) instead of section 206C(1F).

Question 3.(a)(i) Some examinees failed to appreciate the concept of deemed dividend under section 2(22)(e) and mentioned the threshold limit for constituting substantial interest as 20% of the voting power instead of 10%.

(ii) Many examinees could not correctly compute deduction under section 35 in respect of revenue expenditure and capital expenditure towards scientific research.

Question 4.(a) Some examinees incorrectly set off the brought-forward loss of house property against other heads of income, even though it can only be set off against income from house property. Additionally, some examinees incorrectly clubbed the interest on fixed deposits received by major son in the hands of Mr. Joshi, the parent.

(b) In the first alternative, in sub-part (i) to determine whether Mr. Aneesh is required to file a return of income, if he is paying tax as per the default tax regime, the applicable basic exemption limit is ₹ 3,00,000. However, many examinees considered the basic exemption limit as ₹ 2,50,000, leading to incorrect answers. In the second alternative, most examinees were not aware of the conditions for furnishing a return of income specified in Notification No. 37/2022 dated 21.4.2022 and instead answered based on the conditions specified in the seventh proviso to section 139(1).

## SECTION B: GOODS AND SERVICES TAX

## Specific Comments

Question 5.(a) Majority of the examinees did not provide reason for the inclusion of freight charges in the principal supply while computing value of intra-State supply of goods to Vaidehi Enterprises. They did not mention that such supply is a composite supply. Similarly, while computing value of inter-State supply to Calc. Exim, they did not give proper reasoning of inclusion of municipal tax in the value. Municipal tax is includible in value since it is a tax levied under a law other than GST law and is charged separately. Additionally, most of them were unable to identify the nature of supply as inter-State / intra -State in the Bill to Ship Model in case of supply to Raghu Enterprise. Furthermore, they did not adequately explain the manner of utilization of input tax credit with proper working notes.

(b) In some cases, examinees failed to provide correct reasoning for taxability or non-taxability of remuneration received as a chief selector of the hockey team and rental income from warehousing of sugarcane. Most of the examinees were ignorant that consideration received against western music dance performance in an event is taxable as the exemption is available for performance in folk or classical art forms of music or dance. if the consideration charged for such performance is not more than ₹ 1,50,000.

Question 6.(a) Majority of the examinees did not state the legal provision of availability of input tax credit in case of voluntary registration. They failed to mention that a person taking voluntary registration can avail ITC on inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods held in stock on the day immediately preceding the date of grant of registration, provided it is within one year from the date of issue of the tax invoice by the supplier.

(b) Some examinees incorrectly considered the onward and return journey as one journey instead of two separate journeys while determining the place of supply of passenger transportation service to Mr. Nitin. However, the place of supply of passenger transportation service to an unregistered person is place where the passenger embarks on the conveyance for a continuous journey wherein the return journey is treated as a separate journey, even if the tickets for onward and return journey is issued at the same time.

Question 7.(a) The majority of examinees incorrectly calculated the number of days when determining the interest amount. Some missed accounting for a leap year and did not round off the interest amount, even though both pieces of information were explicitly given in the question.

(b) In some cases, although examinees correctly mentioned that every electronic commerce operator (ECO) is liable to collect tax at source, they failed to state that every ECO, not being an agent, is liable to collect TCS.

Question 8.(a) Most of the examinees did not mention the condition of linking of discount with relevant invoices for availing deduction of post supply discounts from the value of supply as per the provisions of section 15(3) of the CGST Act, 2017.
