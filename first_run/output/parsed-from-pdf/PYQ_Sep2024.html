PYQ Sep 2024 · Part I – Case Scenario 1 · Q1 · Marks: 2

What would be the value of closing stock of Raw Material X and Finished Goods as on 31st March 2024?

  1. Closing Stock of Raw Material X ₹50,000 and closing stock of Finished Goods ₹3,50,000
  2. Closing Stock of Raw Material X ₹50,000 and closing stock of Finished Goods ₹3,00,000
  3. Closing Stock of Raw Material X ₹62,500 and closing stock of Finished Goods ₹3,50,000
  4. Closing Stock of Raw Material X ₹62,500 and closing stock of Finished Goods ₹3,00,000

Answer: (B) Closing Stock of Raw Material X ₹50,000 and closing stock of Finished Goods ₹3,00,000 — of the 4,000 units of raw material purchased, 2,000 units went into the 1,000 finished-goods units and 1,500 units into the self-constructed asset, leaving 500 units as closing raw-material stock. Finished-goods cost = (2,000 units × ₹125 material) + ₹1,00,000 labour/overheads = ₹3,50,000 for 1,000 units, i.e. ₹350/unit, against an NRV of only ₹300/unit — since the finished goods will be sold below cost, AS 2's rule that "materials are not written down below cost if the finished products they will be incorporated into are expected to be sold at or above cost" no longer protects the raw material: it must instead be written down to its replacement cost of ₹100/unit as the best available measure of its NRV. Closing Raw Material = 500 × ₹100 = ₹50,000. Closing Finished Goods = 1,000 × lower of cost (₹350) and NRV (₹300) = 1,000 × ₹300 = ₹3,00,000.

AS 2 (1.14)
Many examinees valued the closing raw material at its original purchase cost of ₹125 per unit, without recognising that AS 2 requires raw material to be written down to replacement cost once the finished goods it will produce are expected to sell below their own cost.

What is the amount to be recognized as Revenue as per AS 9 in the books of Kay Ltd. as on 31 March, 2024?

  1. ₹23,50,000
  2. ₹1,50,000
  3. ₹23,00,000
  4. ₹1,00,000

Answer: (A) ₹23,50,000 — the sale to Mr. Ravi Kumar (₹22,00,000) qualifies as a "bill and hold" sale: delivery was deferred only at the buyer's own request, the goods were identified and ready for delivery, so revenue is recognised in full despite the delayed handover. On the approval sale to Sheetal Enterprises (₹2,00,000), the 3-month approval period runs from 1st January to 31st March, i.e. it has elapsed by the year end: the 50% (₹1,00,000) explicitly approved is revenue, the 25% (₹50,000) explicitly disapproved is not, and per AS 9 the remaining 25% (₹50,000) on which the buyer never responded is also recognised as revenue because the time period allowed for rejection has expired without rejection. P Ltd. has 60% voting right in Q Ltd. Q Ltd. has 20% voting right in R Ltd. Also, P Ltd. directly enjoys voting right of 14% in R Ltd. R Ltd. is a Listed Company and regularly supplies goods to P Ltd. The Management of R Ltd. has not disclosed its relationship with P Ltd. While preparing Financial Statements of P Ltd., which entities would you disclose as related parties with reference to AS-18?

  1. Q Ltd.
  2. R Ltd.
  3. Q Ltd. and R Ltd.
  4. Neither of Q Ltd. or R Ltd.

Answer: (C) Q Ltd. and R Ltd. — P Ltd. holds 60% of Q Ltd., making Q Ltd. a subsidiary and hence a related party by control. Since P Ltd. controls Q Ltd., Q Ltd.'s 20% voting power in R Ltd. is aggregated with P Ltd.'s own direct 14% holding: 20% + 14% = 34% combined voting power in R Ltd., well above the 20% threshold that gives rise to significant influence, making R Ltd. a related party too. R Ltd.'s own management not having disclosed the relationship does not change P Ltd.'s disclosure obligation, since AS 18 places the disclosure duty on the reporting enterprise regardless of the counterparty's conduct.

AS 18 (4.3)

  1. ₹30,000
  2. ₹60,000
  3. ₹50,000
  4. ₹36,000

Answer: (B) ₹60,000 — as a dealer/manufacturer lessor, the fair value (normal selling price) built into the lease is cost plus the stated profit margin: ₹3,00,000 × 1.20 = ₹3,60,000. PYQ Sep 2024 · Part I – MCQs · Q15 · Marks: 2

"Fixed Asset held for sale" will be classified in the Balance Sheet as per Schedule III of the Companies Act as:

  1. Deferred Tax Assets
  2. Current Asset
  3. Non-Current Asset
  4. Long term Investments

Answer: (B) Current Asset — a fixed asset retired from active use and held for disposal is no longer part of the entity's productive non-current asset base; it is expected to be realised/converted to cash within the normal operating cycle, and is accordingly reclassified and shown as a current asset (at the lower of net book value and net realisable value) under Schedule III.
