As per AS 12, how the Government Grant of ₹30 Lakhs should be presented:

  1. It should be recognised in the profit and loss statement as per the related cost.
  2. It will be treated as capital reserve.
  3. It will be treated as deferred income.

Answer: (B) Capital reserve — this grant is expressed as a percentage of total investment needed for the factory (a promoter's-contribution-style grant), not tied to any specific fixed asset or revenue item; AS 12 requires such grants to be credited to Capital Reserve, neither treated as deferred income nor distributable as dividend.

A common error is treating this grant the same way as the machinery-linked ₹15 lakh grant (deferred income over useful life), missing that a grant calculated as a percentage of total investment rather than tied to a specific asset falls under AS 12's separate "promoters' contribution" rule.

MTP Sep 2024 Set 1 · Part I – Case Scenario II · Q2(c) · Marks: 2

As per AS 12, how the Government Grant of ₹15 Lakhs with a condition to purchase machinery may be presented as:

  1. Capital Reserve
  2. Shareholders Fund
  3. Deferred Income
  4. Income in statement of profit and loss as received.

A common error is recognising this grant fully in income as received, missing that a grant tied to a specific asset must be spread over the asset's useful life rather than taken to profit and loss immediately.

As per the requirements of AS 3, ‘Cash Flow Statements’, how the amount for purchase of machinery should be presented:

  1. ₹10 lakhs as ‘Cash flows from Investing Activities’ and ₹30,000 will simply be booked in profit and loss with no presentation in Cash Flow Statement.
  2. ₹10.30 lakhs as ‘Cash flows from Investing Activities’ as entire amount is spend on purchase of machinery.

A common error is treating the entire ₹10.30 lakh instalment total as a single investing outflow, missing that the ₹30,000 excess over the cash price is a finance charge requiring separate classification.

Gyan Ltd. borrowed ₹10 crore for construction of a plant at the rate of 10% per annum (interest paid annually ₹1 crore). The construction was being carried on and out of the borrowings, ₹4 crore was temporarily placed in a fixed deposit at the rate of 6% per annum (interest earned ₹24 lakh). At the year end, how much cost of borrowing Gyan Limited will capitalise?

  1. Interest paid on ₹10 crore i.e. ₹1 crore

AS 16 (4.5)
A common error is capitalising the full ₹1 crore interest paid without netting off the ₹24 lakh income earned on the temporarily-invested surplus funds.

MTP Sep 2024 Set 1 · Part I – General MCQ · Q5 · Marks: 2

Cost of current investment acquired was ₹1,00,000 but the fair value was ₹80,000. The Investment was recorded at ₹80,000. Now the fair value of Investment is ₹1,20,000. At what value should it be recorded and how much gain will be credited to profit and loss account.

  1. No change is required and it will continue at ₹80,000
  2. Current investment will be recorded at ₹1,00,000 and gain of ₹20,000 will be credited to profit and loss account.
  3. Current investment will be recorded at ₹1,20,000 and gain of ₹40,000 will be credited to profit and loss account.
  4. Current investment will be recorded at ₹1,20,000 but no gain will be credited to profit and loss account.

Answer: (B) Recorded at ₹1,00,000, gain of ₹20,000 credited to P&L — AS 13 carries current investments at the lower of cost and fair value; a rise in fair value above cost is never recognised (prudence), so the carrying amount can be written back up only as far as original cost (₹1,00,000), not to the full ₹1,20,000 fair value. The gain recognised (₹1,00,000 − ₹80,000 = ₹20,000) is simply the reversal of the earlier write-down, not an unrealised gain above cost.

AS 13 (3.6)
A common error is writing the investment back up to the full ₹1,20,000 fair value and crediting the entire ₹40,000 movement to profit and loss, missing that AS 13 never recognises a current investment above its original cost.
Question/options verbatim; answer key gives only "5. (b)", no working. Independently re-derived per AS 13's lower-of-cost-and-fair-value rule – confirmed matches option (B).
MTP Sep 2024 Set 1 · Part I – General MCQ · Q6 · Marks: 2

In determining the cost of inventories, it is appropriate to exclude certain costs and recognise them as expenses in the period in which they are incurred. Which of the following is not an example of such costs:

  1. Abnormal amounts of wasted materials, labour, or other production costs;
  2. Storage costs, unless the production process requires such storage;
  3. Raw Material cost
  4. Selling and distribution costs.

Answer: (C) Raw Material cost — per AS 2, abnormal wastage, unnecessary storage costs, and selling/distribution costs are all specifically excluded from the cost of inventories and expensed as incurred. Raw material cost, by contrast, is a normal, properly includible component of inventory cost – it is the one item among the four that is not an example of a cost to be excluded.

AS 2 (1.9)
A common error is misreading the question's "not an example" framing and selecting one of the genuinely-excluded cost types instead of raw material cost, which is the item that properly belongs in inventory cost.

On 15th June, 2024, Y limited wants to re-classify its investments in accordance with AS 13 (revised). Decide and state the amount of transfer, based on the following information:

  1. A portion of long term investments purchased on 1st March, 2023 are to be re-classified as current investments. The original cost of these investments was ₹14 lakhs but had been written down by ₹2 lakhs (to recognise 'other than temporary' decline in value). The market value of these investments on 15th June, 2024 was ₹11 lakhs.
  2. Another portion of long term investments purchased on 15th January, 2023 are to be re-classified as current investments. The original cost of these investments was ₹7 lakhs but had been written down to ₹5 lakhs (to recognize 'other than temporary' decline in value). The fair value of these investments on 15th June, 2024 was ₹4.5 lakhs.
  3. A portion of current investments purchased on 15th March, 2024 for ₹7 lakhs are to be re-classified as long term investments, as the company has decided to retain them. The market value of these investments on 31st March, 2024 was ₹6 lakhs and fair value on 15th June 2024 was ₹8.5 lakhs.
  4. Another portion of current investments purchased on 7th December, 2023 for ₹4 lakhs are to be re-classified as long term investments. The market value of these investments was: on 31st March, 2024 ₹3.5 lakhs; on 15th June, 2024 ₹3.8 lakhs.

As per AS 13 'Accounting for Investments', where long-term investments are reclassified as current investments, transfers are made at the lower of cost and carrying amount at the date of transfer; and where investments are reclassified from current to long term, transfers are made at the lower of cost and fair value on the date of transfer.

  1. Carrying amount (₹12 lakhs, i.e. ₹14 lakhs cost − ₹2 lakhs write-down) is less than cost; the reclassified current investment should be carried at ₹12 lakhs.
  2. Carrying amount (₹5 lakhs) is less than cost (₹7 lakhs); the reclassified current investment should be carried at ₹5 lakhs.
  3. Cost (₹7 lakhs) is less than fair value (₹8.5 lakhs) on the date of transfer; the reclassified long-term investment will be carried at ₹7 lakhs.
  4. Fair value on the date of transfer (₹3.8 lakhs) is lower than cost (₹4 lakhs); the reclassified long-term investment will be carried at ₹3.8 lakhs.
AS 13 (3.9)
A common error is applying the same rule direction to all four scenarios instead of switching between "lower of cost and carrying amount" (long-term to current) and "lower of cost and fair value" (current to long-term) depending on the direction of reclassification.

MTP Sep 2024 Set 1 · Part II · Q1(b) · Marks: 7 · Compulsory

The financial statements of PQ Ltd. for the year 2023-24 approved by the Board of Directors on 15th July, 2024. The following information was provided:

  1. A suit against the company's advertisement was filed by a party on 20th April, 2024, claiming damages of ₹25 lakhs.
  2. The terms and conditions for acquisition of business of another company have been decided by March, 2024. But the financial resources were arranged in April, 2024 and amount invested was ₹50 lakhs.
  3. Theft of cash of ₹5 lakhs by the cashier on 31st March, 2024 but was detected on 16th July, 2024.
  4. Company sent a proposal to sell an immovable property for ₹40 lakhs in March, 2024. The book value of the property was ₹30 lakhs on 31st March, 2024. However, the deed was registered on 15th April, 2024.
  5. A major fire has damaged the assets in a factory on 5th April, 2024. However, the assets are fully insured.

With reference to AS-4 "Contingencies and events occurring after the balance sheet date", state whether the above mentioned events will be treated as contingencies, adjusting events or non-adjusting events occurring after the balance sheet date.

  1. The suit is a contingent liability that did not exist as on the balance sheet date, since it was filed on 20th April, after the balance sheet date. Per AS 4, "contingencies" is restricted to conditions or situations existing at the balance sheet date. It will have no effect on the financial statements and is a non-adjusting event.
  2. The terms and conditions for acquisition of business were finalised and carried out before the closure of the books of accounts, but the transaction for payment of financial resources was effected in April 2024 – this is clearly an event occurring after the balance sheet date. Necessary adjustment to assets and liabilities for the acquisition of business is required in the financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2024.
  3. Only significant events occurring between the balance sheet date and the date the financial statements are approved may indicate a need for adjustment or disclosure. Here, the theft of cash was detected on 16th July, 2024, after the Board's approval of the financial statements on 15th July, 2024; hence no treatment is required.
  4. Adjustments to assets and liabilities are not appropriate for events occurring after the balance sheet date if they do not relate to conditions existing at the balance sheet date. Here, the sale of immovable property was only at the proposal stage (negotiations not started) on the balance sheet date. Therefore, no adjustment to assets for the sale of immovable property is required in the financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2024.
  5. The condition of the fire's occurrence was not existing on the balance sheet date. Only disclosure regarding the fire event and the fact that the loss is fully insured may be given in the report of the approving authority.
AS 4 (1.3-1.5)
A common error across items like these is treating every event occurring after the balance sheet date the same way, rather than checking each one individually against whether the underlying condition already existed at the balance sheet date (adjusting) or arose only afterwards (non-adjusting/disclosure-only), and whether it was even known before financial statement approval at all.

Synergy Ltd., is in engineering industry. The company received an actuarial valuation for the first time for its pension scheme which revealed a surplus of ₹6 lakhs. According to AS 15 (Revised) "Employee Benefits", actuarial gains and losses should be recognized immediately in the statement of profit and loss as income or expense. Therefore, the surplus of ₹6 lakhs in the pension scheme on its actuarial valuation is required to be credited to the profit and loss statement of the current year. Hence, Synergy Ltd. cannot spread the actuarial gain of ₹6 lakhs over the next 2 years by reducing the annual contributions to ₹2 lakhs instead of ₹5 lakhs. It has to contribute ₹5 lakhs annually for its pension scheme, and recognise the full ₹6 lakh surplus immediately.

AS 15 (1.10)
A common error is agreeing with a company's proposal to spread an actuarial gain or loss over future periods, missing that AS 15 requires immediate recognition of the entire actuarial gain or loss in the period it arises, with no deferral option.
