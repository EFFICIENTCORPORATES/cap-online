MTP May 2026 Set 2 · Part I – MCQs · Q15 · Marks: 2

P Ltd. has 60% voting right in Q Ltd. Q Ltd. has 20% voting right in R Ltd. Also, P Ltd. directly enjoys voting right of 14% in R Ltd. R Ltd. is a Listed Company and regularly supplies goods to P Ltd. The Management of R Ltd. has not disclosed its relationship with P Ltd. While preparing Financial Statements of P Ltd., which entities would you disclose as related parties with reference to AS-18?

  1. Q Ltd.
  2. R Ltd.
  3. Q Ltd. and R Ltd.
  4. Neither of Q Ltd. or R Ltd.

Answer: (c) Q Ltd. and R Ltd. — Q Ltd. is a subsidiary of P Ltd. (P Ltd. controls >50% of its voting power), so Q Ltd. is a related party outright. AS 18 defines both "control" and "substantial interest" as direct or indirect ownership of the relevant voting-power threshold — since P Ltd. controls Q Ltd. outright, Q Ltd.'s full 20% voting stake in R Ltd. is attributed to P Ltd.'s group interest without dilution (indirect holdings through a controlled subsidiary are not multiplied through the parent's ownership percentage). Combined with P Ltd.'s own direct 14%, the group holds 34% of R Ltd. — well past AS 18's 20% substantial-interest/significant-influence threshold — making R Ltd. a related party too, regardless of R Ltd. management's own non-disclosure.

AS 18 (4.3)
Several examinees only counted P Ltd.'s direct 14% holding in R Ltd. and concluded it fell short of the 20% substantial-interest threshold, without also attributing Q Ltd.'s full 20% stake to the group given P Ltd.'s control over Q Ltd.
Question/options verbatim from CAInter-AdvAcc-MTP-May2026-Set2-Q.pdf; the Ans.pdf answer key gives only "15. (c)" with no working. The explanation is Claude-authored. Corrected 2026-07-26: the original explanation computed the indirect holding as a diluted 60%×20%=12% (giving a combined 26%), which is not how AS 18 aggregates a holding through a controlled subsidiary — verified against books/concept-book/raw_icai_study_materials/M1_C4_U4_ Accounting Standard 18 Related Party Disclosures.md, whose "control"/"substantial interest" definitions use direct-or-indirect ownership without dilution through an intermediate controlled entity. MTP May 2026 Set 2 · Part II · Q6(a), alternative 2 of 2 (OR) · Marks: 4

When capitalisation of borrowing cost should cease as per Accounting Standard 16? Explain in brief. (4 Marks)

Capitalization of borrowing costs should cease when substantially all the activities necessary to prepare the qualifying asset for its intended use or sale are complete. An asset is normally ready for its intended use or sale when its physical construction or production is complete even though routine administrative work might still continue. If minor modifications such as the decoration of a property to the user's specification, are all that are outstanding, this indicates that substantially all the activities are complete. When the construction of a qualifying asset is completed in parts and a completed part is capable of being used while construction continues for the other parts, capitalization of borrowing costs in relation to a part should cease when substantially all the activities necessary to prepare that part for its intended use or sale are complete.

AS 16 (4.11)
Several examinees described only the overall-completion cessation rule and omitted the part-completion exception, despite this being the same principle tested numerically elsewhere in this paper's Case Scenario I.

MTP May 2026 Set 2 · Part II · Q6(c) · Marks: 5

Alpha Chemicals Ltd., a listed company, proposes a buy-back of 12% of its equity share capital from its free reserves under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. On scrutiny of its records, the following details were observed: 1. The company intends to route the buy-back through its wholly-owned subsidiary. 2. The company had defaulted on repayment of a term loan to a public sector bank, which was fully repaid 27 months ago. 3. The company had declared a dividend in the last year, but the amount remains unpaid for 35 days from the date of declaration.

You are required to: Examine the compliance of Alpha Chemicals Ltd. with the requirements of Section 70 of the Companies Act, 2013 and state whether the company is eligible to proceed with the proposed buy-back. (5 Marks)

As per Section 70 of the Companies Act, 2013, the company cannot buy back its own shares under the following conditions, all of which apply to Alpha Chemicals Ltd.:

1. Buy-back through Subsidiary – Prohibited. Section 70(1)(a): A company shall not buy back shares through any subsidiary, including wholly owned subsidiaries.

2. Default in Term Loan – Three-Year Cooling Off Required. Section 70(1)(c): A company that has defaulted in repayment of a term loan can buy back shares only after 3 years from the cessation of default. Alpha repaid the loan only 27 months ago — still short of the required 36-month period. Hence, not eligible.

3. Non-Filing of Annual Return and Financial Statements. Section 70(2): Buy-back is prohibited if the company has not complied with Sections 92 (Annual Return) and 129 (Financial Statements). Alpha has failed to file both which disqualifies it from buy-back.

4. Unpaid Dividend for More Than 30 Days. Section 127 read with Section 70(2): Non-distribution of declared dividend within 30 days is a punishable offence. Dividend unpaid for 35 days, this is a default.

Alpha Chemicals Ltd. is not eligible to proceed with the buy-back under Section 70 due to multiple violations.
