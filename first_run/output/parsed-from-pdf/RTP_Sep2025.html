RTP Sep 2025 · Part II · Q4

As per the revised scheme effective from accounting periods commencing on or after 1st April, 2024, classify non-company entities for the purpose of applicability of Accounting Standards. Briefly explain the criteria for each category.

Non-company entities are classified into two categories: Micro, Small and Medium Sized Entities (MSMEs) and Large entities.

A non-company entity qualifies as an MSME if it satisfies all of: (i) its equity/debt securities are not listed or in the process of listing on any stock exchange (in India or abroad); (ii) it is not a bank, financial institution, or insurance company; (iii) turnover (excluding other income) does not exceed ₹250 crore in the immediately preceding accounting year; (iv) it does not have borrowings exceeding ₹50 crore at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year; and (v) it is not a holding or subsidiary of an entity that is not itself an MSME. These conditions are assessed as at the end of the relevant accounting period. A Large entity is any non-company entity that does not meet the MSME definition, and must comply in full with all Accounting Standards; MSMEs receive certain exemptions/relaxations.

RTP Sep 2025 · Part II · Q5

Lion Ltd. (manufacturing/construction contracts), FY ended 31st March 2025, follows historical cost, FIFO, and percentage-of-completion. During the year, management changes: (i) inventory valuation FIFO→Weighted Average (citing raw-material price volatility); (ii) depreciation SLM→WDV (citing better usage reflection). The company discloses the inventory-method change in notes but not the depreciation-method change (calling it immaterial), and has not disclosed its accounting policy on government grants despite receiving a significant subsidy this year. (a) Identify AS 1 violations, if any. (b) Critically evaluate whether "materiality" justifies non-disclosure of a change in accounting policy. (c) Is the FIFO→Weighted Average change an estimate or policy change under AS 1?

(a) AS 1 requires all significant accounting policies to be disclosed, and any material change in policy to be disclosed along with its nature and reasons (with the effect indicated if ascertainable). Applying this: (1) Inventory method change (FIFO→Weighted Average) is a change in accounting policy, correctly disclosed with justification — compliant, provided the quantified effect is also disclosed or noted as unascertainable. (2) Depreciation method change (SLM→WDV) is technically a change in accounting estimate under AS 10, but AS 1 still requires disclosure of material items affecting the financial statements — depreciation affects profit, asset values, and return ratios; non-disclosure on unsubstantiated "immateriality" grounds is not appropriate without clear quantitative evidence. (3) Government grants policy non-disclosure: AS 1 requires disclosure of all significant accounting policies, including for government grants received — non-disclosure here is a violation of AS 1.

(b) Materiality is assessed by an item's potential to influence users' economic decisions. A depreciation-method change affects profit, asset values, and tax, and even a quantitatively modest impact may carry qualitative significance for comparability/trend analysis. The company's blanket reliance on "materiality" to justify non-disclosure is not valid unless supported by concrete evidence that the impact is immaterial both quantitatively and qualitatively.

(c) The change from FIFO to Weighted Average is a change in accounting policy (not a change in accounting estimate) — it is a change in the cost formula used to measure inventory, and its nature and financial impact should be properly disclosed.

RTP Sep 2025 · Part II · Q9(a)

When can an item qualify to be a prior period item as per AS 5?

Per AS 5 para 16, prior period items are income or expenses that arise in the current period as a result of errors or omissions in the preparation of financial statements of one or more prior periods. The term does not include other adjustments necessitated by circumstances that, though related to prior periods, are determined only in the current period (e.g. arrears payable to workers from a retrospective wage revision).

RTP Sep 2025 · Part II · Q16

Is it permissible to capitalise borrowing costs incurred on assets necessary for the construction of qualifying assets? A company constructing a large manufacturing plant in a backward area also purchased a residential building (funded by a long-term loan) to house workers engaged in the plant's construction, intending to dispose of the building once construction completes. If the manufacturing plant is a qualifying asset, are the borrowing costs on the residential-building loan eligible for capitalisation?

Per AS 16 para 3, a qualifying asset "necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale"; para 5 clarifies that assets ready for use when acquired are not qualifying assets. Prima facie, the residential building appears ready for use on acquisition, suggesting no capitalisation.

However, since providing housing is necessary for the construction of the plant, the borrowing costs on the building loan are, in substance, directly attributable to the construction of the manufacturing plant. The company should therefore capitalise these borrowing costs as part of the cost of the manufacturing plant, up to the period the plant is ready for its intended use.

RTP Sep 2025 · Part II · Q17

Cotton Garments Limited, 5 segments (₹ Crore): Segment Assets A/B/C/D/E 40/15/10/10/5 (Total 80); Segment Results (95)/5/5/(5)/15 (Total −75); Segment Revenue 310/40/30/40/30 (Total 450). The Chief Accountant believes only segment "A" should be reported. Examine this opinion per AS 17.

Per AS 17 para 27, a segment is reportable if: (i) its revenue (external+internal) is ≥10% of total revenue; or (ii) its result (profit or loss) is ≥10% of the combined result of all profit-making segments or all loss-making segments, whichever is greater in absolute amount; or (iii) its assets are ≥10% of total assets. If reportable segments cover <75% of total external revenue, additional segments must be added until 75% is reached.

(a) Revenue test (10% of 450 = 45): only A (310) qualifies.

(b) Result test: combined profit-segments (B+C+E = 5+5+15=25) vs. combined loss-segments (A+D = 95+5=100, in absolute terms); greater = 100; 10% = 10. Segments with |result| ≥10: A (95) and E (15) qualify.

(c) Assets test (10% of 80 = 8): A, B, C, D (40,15,10,10) qualify; only E (5) falls short.

Since every segment (A through E) satisfies at least one of the three tests, all five segments must be reported per AS 17. The Chief Accountant's opinion that only segment A is reportable is therefore wrong.

AS 17 (3.3)
Some examinees checked only the revenue test (which favours only segment A) and stopped there, missing that the result and assets tests bring in the remaining segments too.

X Ltd. sold goods to its associate company for the 1st quarter ending 30.6.2024, priced as it would for any ordinary customer. After that quarter, the related party relationship ceased to exist. Decide whether transactions for the entire year have to be disclosed as related party transactions.

No. Per AS 18 para 23, only the transactions during the period the related party relationship actually existed — here, the first quarter ending 30.6.2024 — need be disclosed as related party transactions. Transactions in the remaining periods, after the relationship ceased, need not be disclosed as such.

RTP Sep 2025 · Part II · Q19

On 1st April, 2024, Mansi Ltd. sold a plant for ₹8,52,800 (carrying amount ₹1,80,000) as part of a sale-and-leaseback package with Akash Ltd., leasing the asset back for an 8-year term. Economic life 8 years. Minimum lease rents ₹1,60,000/year, payable annually from 31st March, 2025. Incremental borrowing rate 10% p.a. Calculate the net effect on the Statement of Profit and Loss in Mansi Ltd.'s (lessee's) books.

Lease classification: lease term (8 years) = entire economic life (8 years). PV of minimum lease payments = ₹1,60,000 × 5.335 (annuity factor @10% for 8 years) = ₹8,53,600, substantially covering the fair value/sale price (₹8,52,800). This is a finance lease.

Per AS 19 para 48, where a sale-and-leaseback results in a finance lease, the profit on sale (₹8,52,800−₹1,80,000 = ₹6,72,800) is not recognised immediately — deferred and amortised over the lease term in proportion to the leased asset's depreciation.

Assuming straight-line depreciation: Depreciation = ₹8,52,800/8 = ₹1,06,600/year. Amortised deferred profit = ₹6,72,800/8 = ₹84,100/year. Net effect on Statement of P&L = ₹1,06,600 − ₹84,100 = ₹22,500 debit per annum, for each of the 8 years of the lease term.

Some examinees recognised the full sale profit immediately in the year of sale, not applying AS 19's deferral-and-amortisation rule for a sale-and-leaseback resulting in a finance lease.
