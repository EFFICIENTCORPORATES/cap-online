PYQ Sep 2025 · Part II · Q6(a), alternative 2 of 2 (EITHER) · Marks: 4

What are the key elements of financial statements? Explain.

  1. Assets: a resource controlled by the enterprise from past events, expected to bring future economic benefits (e.g. plant, patents, receivables).
  2. Liabilities: a present obligation arising from past events, settlement of which is expected to result in an outflow of economic benefits (e.g. borrowings, provisions).
  3. Equity: the residual interest in assets after deducting all liabilities — owners' claim (capital, reserves), distinct from liabilities (claims of parties other than owners); changes through contributions/distributions or income/expenses.
  4. Income/Gain: increases in economic benefits through inflow of assets or reduction of liabilities, increasing equity (includes revenue and gains).
  5. Expenses/Loss: decreases in economic benefits through outflows/depletion of assets or incurrence of liabilities, decreasing equity (other than distributions to owners) — expenses arise in the ordinary course (e.g. wages); losses may or may not (e.g. loss on PPE disposal), and are shown separately to aid performance assessment.
Framework (1.2)
A direct theory recall question; common gaps include omitting the equity definition's distinction from liabilities, or failing to note that losses (unlike ordinary expenses) may fall outside the enterprise's normal activities.
