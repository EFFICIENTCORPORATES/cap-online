PYQ May 2026 · Part I – MCQs · Q10 · Marks: 2

Volt Tech Limited manufactures electric Vehicle (EV) batteries. The accountant showed Profit Before Tax (PBT) of ₹14,00,000 for the third quarter ending 31st December, 2025 after incorporating: (i) sales promotion expenses of ₹50,000 during the quarter, 80% deferred to the fourth quarter as sales in last quarter is high; (ii) gain on sale of investments in the first quarter of ₹1,60,000, apportioned equally to the four quarters; (iii) additional depreciation of ₹80,000 from a change in depreciation method, entirely debited in the third quarter though the third quarter's own share is only ₹20,000. What amount should be reported as adjusted Profit Before Tax for third quarter?

  1. ₹13,20,000
  2. ₹13,80,000
  3. ₹14,60,000
  4. ₹13,60,000

Answer: (A) ₹13,20,000, from ₹14,00,000 reported − ₹80,000 net correction: (i) −₹40,000 — sales promotion expense does not meet the definition of an asset at an interim date, so AS 25 does not allow deferring 80% of it to Q4 merely because Q4's sales are expected to be higher; the full ₹50,000 must be expensed in Q3, so the ₹40,000 the accountant deferred is expensed now. (ii) −₹40,000 — a one-time gain on sale of investments should not be anticipated or spread across quarters (AS 25 treats such occasional gains the same way as seasonal/occasional revenue — recognised when they arise), so the ₹40,000 apportioned to Q3 is reversed; the full ₹1,60,000 belongs in Q1. (iii) Nil adjustment — the cumulative effect of a mid-year change in depreciation method is recognised entirely in the quarter of change, not spread over the remaining quarters, so the full ₹80,000 correctly stays in Q3 as reported; the stated "₹20,000 own share" is the distractor here, not something to adjust for.

AS 25 (7.10/7.11/7.12)
Many examinees left the deferred 80% sales promotion expense and the one-time investment gain's artificially equal apportionment unadjusted. A more subtle error also seen: incorrectly reversing the excess depreciation charge as well, spreading it across quarters when AS 25 actually requires the full amount to stay in the quarter of change — treating every "quarter's own share" framing as something to adjust for, when here it is testing the opposite.

Oasis Limited runs a famous chain of super markets and restaurants. Restaurant division constitutes 35% of total group revenue. The management of the company decides to close the business of restaurants as the company has been experiencing declining profitability due to increased competition. On 1st April, 2025, the management announced its intention to discontinue the restaurant business and concentrate on Super market segment only. The decision was made through strategic review and the management started actively seeking buyers for the restaurant division. During the year 2025-26, the company has sold 5 restaurants out of 12 restaurants so far as part of the sale.

Comment, whether it will be considered as discontinuing operation or not as per the provisions of AS-24? (10 Marks)

As per AS 24, a discontinuing operation is a component of an enterprise: that the enterprise, pursuant to a single plan, is disposing of substantially in its entirety, or disposing of piecemeal, or terminating through abandonment; and which represents a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations.

Hence the restaurant division's closure by Oasis Limited will be considered a discontinuing operation as per the provisions of AS 24. This is due to the fact that the assets are sold off as part of a single plan, and that the business represents a separate major line of business, and can be distinguished both operationally and for financial reporting purposes.

AS 24 (6.2)
Some examinees concluded this was not a discontinuing operation because the disposal was piecemeal (5 of 12 restaurants sold rather than the whole division at once), not recognising that AS 24 explicitly includes piecemeal disposal pursuant to a single plan within its definition.

PYQ May 2026 · Part II · Q6(a), alternative 1 of 2 (OR) · Marks: 4

Explain the key aspects which required the need for convergence towards global standards of financial reporting. (4 Marks)

A few key aspects which required the need for convergence are as under:

1. Raising funds from international markets: Each country has its own set of rules and regulations for accounting and financial reporting. Therefore, when an enterprise decides to raise capital from markets other than the country in which it is located, the rules and regulations of that other country will apply, requiring the enterprise to understand the differences between the rules governing financial reporting in the foreign country as compared to its own.

2. Comparability of Financial Statements: International analysts and investors would like to compare financial statements based on similar ASs, leading to a growing need for an internationally accepted set of ASs for cross-border filings, raising investor confidence generally.

3. Uniformity, Comparability, Transparency etc.: A strong need was felt to bring about uniformity, rationalisation, comparability, transparency and adaptability in financial statements. A multiplicity of AS systems worldwide produces different reported numbers for the same events, creating confusion and encouraging error or fraud. The cure is a single set of global standards, of the highest quality, set in the public interest.

4. Global Investment: Convergence contributes to the free flow of global investment, improves investors' ability to compare investments globally (lowering risk of errors of judgment), facilitates accounting/reporting for companies with global operations, eliminates costly requirements like reinstatement of financial statements, creates a new standard of accountability and transparency, and reduces operational challenges for accounting firms.

PYQ May 2026 · Part II · Q6(b) · Marks: 4

The accountant of Moon Limited provides you the following information: (i) Provision for doubtful debts was created @3% till 31st March, 2025; from FY 2025-26, the rate has been changed to 5%. (ii) During the year ended 31st March, 2026 the management has introduced a formal gratuity scheme in place of ad-hoc ex-gratia payments to employees on retirement. (iii) During 2025-26, there was change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories. (iv) Management decided to pay pension to employees who have retired after completing 5 years of service (₹25,000 per month); earlier there was no such pension scheme.

You are required to advise the accountant of Moon Limited, with valid reasons, whether the above transactions would be treated as change in accounting policy or not, for the year ended 31st March, 2026 in the context of AS 5. (4 Marks)

As per para 21 of AS 5, the preparation of financial statements involves making estimates based on circumstances existing when the statements are prepared; it may be necessary to revise an estimate in a subsequent period if circumstances change. Revision of an estimate, by its nature, does not bring the adjustment within the definitions of a prior period item or an extraordinary item. Change in Accounting estimate is not a change in accounting policy.

(i) Provision for doubtful debts rate change (3% to 5%): this is a change in accounting estimate, not a change in accounting policy. Per para 27 of AS 5, a change in estimate with a material effect in the current period should be disclosed and quantified; any change expected to have a material effect in later periods should also be disclosed and quantified.

(ii) As per AS 5, adopting an accounting policy for events or transactions that differ in substance from previously occurring events or transactions is not considered a change in accounting policy. Accordingly, introduction of a formal retirement gratuity scheme in place of ad hoc ex-gratia payments is not a change in accounting policy.

(iii) As per AS 2 and AS 5: the method of determining cost (FIFO/Weighted Average) is an accounting policy choice, and any change in it affects measurement of inventory and profit. Hence a change in cost formula in measuring the cost of inventories IS a change in Accounting Policy.

(iv) The adoption of a new accounting policy for events or transactions which did not occur previously, or that were immaterial, will not be treated as a change in accounting policy. Introducing a pension scheme where none previously existed is therefore not a change in accounting policy.

AS 5 (2.5)
Many examinees classified the provision-rate change and the new gratuity/pension schemes as changes in accounting policy, not recognising AS 5's distinction between a genuine policy change (like the inventory cost-formula change) and a change in estimate or the adoption of a policy for a genuinely new type of transaction.
